If you’re wondering how Alex Ridha’s (aka Boys Noize) collaboration with Nine Inch Nails influenced his new record 7LLVSION, the answer is simple: It didn’t. That’s not to say Ridha didn’t learn anything from the virtuosic production team of Trent Reznor and Atticus Ross, but most of the songs on 7LLVSION had been knocking around for some time. And as Ridha is quick to point out, these are not rock songs built for live performance; they’re “battle tested” for clubs and DJ sets.

如果你想知道 Alex Ridha（即 Boys Noize）与九寸钉乐队（Nine Inch Nails）的合作是如何影响他新专辑《7LLVSION》的，答案很简单：并没有。这并不是说 Ridha 没有从 Trent Reznor 和 Atticus Ross 那支技艺精湛的制作团队身上学到东西，而是《7LLVSION》中的大多数歌曲早已创作完成。正如 Ridha 很快指出的那样，这些并非为现场演出而打造的摇滚歌曲，而是经过俱乐部和 DJ 现场“实战检验”的作品。

“I was kind of back on the usual kind of thing,” he tells The Verge. “I do the same I kind of always do. I go make coffee, go in my room, put on the machines, and I’ll start messing around with sounds and build one sound to another and there comes a record,” he says. Like previous Boys Noize releases, a lot of 7LLVSION starts with sound design. “It’s kind of tech-driven, in a way,” he says, “because I always look for the newest machines.”

“我算是回归了往常的状态，”他告诉《The Verge》。“我做着我一贯做的事情。去煮杯咖啡，走进房间，打开机器，开始摆弄各种声音，将一个声音叠加到另一个声音上，一张唱片就这样诞生了。”和 Boys Noize 之前的作品一样，《7LLVSION》的许多创作始于声音设计。“从某种程度上说，这是一种技术驱动，”他说，“因为我总是在寻找最新的机器。”

On 7LLVSION, Ridha frequently turned to an obscure Italian semimodular synth, the Grp A4. “It’s just so powerful, and it sounds so big,” he says. The routing options, dual filters (which he uses in parallel), and built-in sequencer kept leading him to new and inspiring places. Because of that, the A4 ended up on a number of tracks, including “Shut It Down,” “1+1+1,” and “Ycore.”

在《7LLVSION》中，Ridha 频繁使用了一款鲜为人知的意大利半模块化合成器 Grp A4。“它非常强大，音色极其宏大，”他说。其路由选项、双滤波器（他将其并行使用）以及内置音序器不断带他进入充满灵感的新领域。正因如此，A4 被应用到了多首曲目中，包括《Shut It Down》、《1+1+1》和《Ycore》。

“The exciting part is always to, you know, not repeat myself, even with the drum sounds, and try something new that I haven’t tried before,” Ridha says. For a few tracks, that meant getting away from the rigid, quantized grooves of techno and opting for a more dynamic, live feeling on tracks like “Shut It Down” and “1+1+1” especially. “Live drums are the new 808,” he says.

“最令人兴奋的部分始终是——你知道的——不重复自己，即使是在鼓点音色上，也要尝试我从未尝试过的新东西，”Ridha 说道。对于几首曲目来说，这意味着要摆脱 Techno 音乐中僵硬、量化的律动，转而追求更具动态感和现场感的氛围，尤其是在《Shut It Down》和《1+1+1》中。“现场鼓点就是新的 808，”他说。

He looked to German bands of the ’70s and ’80s for inspiration, like Can, Amon Düül, and Deutsch Amerikanische Freundschaft. He also called out Conny Plank, who produced landmark records by Neu!, Kraftwerk, and Eurythmics. But in general he says, “My goal is to not sound like anything you can point to. You know, I don’t want to be a direct copy.” Instead, he was chasing the sound of “electronic punk.” That means aggressive basslines, driving drums, and stripped-down arrangements that avoid overstuffing the mix with too many instruments.

他从 70 和 80 年代的德国乐队中汲取灵感，如 Can、Amon Düül 和 Deutsch Amerikanische Freundschaft。他还提到了曾为 Neu!、Kraftwerk 和 Eurythmics 制作过里程碑式唱片的 Conny Plank。但总的来说，他说：“我的目标是不让音乐听起来像任何你能指出的东西。你知道，我不想成为直接的模仿者。”相反，他追求的是“电子朋克”的声音。这意味着激进的低音线条、强劲的鼓点，以及避免在混音中堆砌过多乐器的极简编曲。

But it all comes back to the club at the end of the day. “Environment has always been the main thing in my music. I need to have a record that I want to play at the party,” he says, and that meant testing the songs off 7LLVSION extensively in his DJ sets and letting the audience tell him what works and what doesn’t. “The club is the most open and objective situation you can have,” he says. “They don’t know what I’m playing, so they hear something for the first time, and you can just look at someone’s face, or someone’s there in the room to get a cool reaction, and that usually makes me feel good about something or not.”

但归根结底，一切还是要回到俱乐部。“环境一直是我音乐中的核心要素。我需要一张我想在派对上播放的唱片，”他说。这意味着他需要在 DJ 现场中广泛测试《7LLVSION》中的歌曲，让观众告诉他哪些有效，哪些无效。“俱乐部是你所能拥有的最开放、最客观的环境，”他说。“他们不知道我在放什么，所以他们是第一次听到这些音乐。你可以观察人们的表情，或者在现场看到某种很酷的反应，这通常能让我判断出作品的好坏。”
