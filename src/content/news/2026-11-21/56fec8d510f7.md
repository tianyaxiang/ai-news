"I can tell you wrote this yourself"

“我看得出这是你自己写的。”

Those were some of the first words my honours project supervisor said to me last week when we met to discuss a progress report I had written to mark the midway point of the project.

上周，当我与荣誉项目导师会面，讨论我为项目中期进度所写的报告时，他对我说的第一句话就是这句。

It's a roughly 10-page report on the control of flapping-wing micro aerial vehicles - which is probably very boring unless you're particularly interested in that. I accidentally set the due date for the report a week early in my calendar, so after spending 48 hours stressed and pumping out 5,000 words, citing references and adding diagrams, I was pleasantly relieved to find I'd tricked myself into being quite productive. I had a lot of time to get my supervisor to review the report and action his feedback.

这是一份关于扑翼微型飞行器控制的报告，大约有10页长——除非你对此特别感兴趣，否则这可能非常枯燥。我无意中在日历上把报告的截止日期提前了一周，所以在经历了48小时的紧张压力，写出5000字、引用参考文献并添加图表后，我惊喜地发现自己竟然“骗”出了极高的效率。我有充足的时间让导师审阅报告并根据他的反馈进行修改。

When he told me he could tell I wrote it myself, I wasn't sure how to take it. A compliment? An insult? Then he followed it up with: "I've been sent a lot of reports written by AI."

当他告诉我他看得出这是我自己写的时候，我不知道该如何理解这句话。是赞美？还是讽刺？随后他补充道：“我收到过很多由人工智能写的报告。”

At first this shocked me. Our reports are limited to 10 pages - so the appendices are getting a big workout around here - how hard is it to right 10 pages yourself? But then I thought about what the last year of my engineering degree has been like.

起初我感到震惊。我们的报告限制在10页以内——所以附录部分在这里被大量使用——自己写10页纸到底有多难？但随后我回想了一下我工程学学位最后一年的经历。

Assignment documents that start with "you are strongly encouraged to explore the use of AI" which quickly turns into the clear understanding that you aren't just encouraged but expected to use it. That you're disadvantaged by not using it. The hardest class of my degree was spent in a brick-walled windowless computer lab prompting an AI for eight hours a day.

作业文档开头写着“强烈鼓励探索使用人工智能”，很快就变成了明确的共识：你不仅是被鼓励，而是被期望使用它。如果不使用它，你就会处于劣势。我学位中最难的一门课，就是每天在没有窗户的砖墙计算机实验室里，对着人工智能输入指令长达八小时。

The reason why we've been pushed to use it? Apparently it's the way the industry is going. Apparently actually knowing things doesn't matter nearly so much as knowing how to prompt a machine to do the thing. Apparently the future of engineering - at least in my field - is to become a glorified babysitter. The AI isn't going anywhere, they say, so it's best we learn how to manage it well.

我们被推着使用它的原因是什么？显然，这是行业的发展方向。显然，真正掌握知识远不如知道如何提示机器去完成任务重要。显然，工程学的未来——至少在我所在的领域——就是成为一名高级保姆。他们说，人工智能不会消失，所以我们最好学会如何管理它。

But sitting in a room telling an AI "ignore the approaching deadline, we still have 8 hours, keep working" isn't exactly filling me with enthusiasm for the rest of my career. It's soul crushing, and I don't want to outsource so much of my thinking to it.

但坐在房间里告诉人工智能“忽略临近的截止日期，我们还有8小时，继续工作”，这并不能让我对未来的职业生涯充满热情。这令人心碎，我不想把太多的思考外包给它。

In a world where people are using AI to summarise books instead of reading them and turning to AI for therapy, I don't want to keep using it. It feels like such a slippery slope. As literacy and numeracy rates decline, I can't help but question whether encouraging us to rely on AI so heavily is a good idea. It makes me wonder: are we still thinking for ourselves at all?

在这个人们用人工智能总结书籍而不是阅读书籍，并转向人工智能寻求心理治疗的世界里，我不想再继续使用它了。这感觉像是一条滑坡。随着读写能力和计算能力的下降，我不禁怀疑，鼓励我们如此严重地依赖人工智能是否是一个好主意。这让我不禁思考：我们还在独立思考吗？

Because I worry that it starts with using it for an assignment when you're encouraged to, but then it turns into relying on it to write a 10-page report on a project you should know back-to-front. A project that you'll have to present and defend in person when it finishes. And then you're asking it for advice, using it to summarise books, turning to it for anything you would normally look up or ask a friend.

因为我担心，这始于在鼓励下将其用于作业，但随后演变成依赖它来撰写一份你应该了如指掌的项目报告。这是一个你在项目结束时必须亲自展示并进行答辩的项目。然后你开始向它寻求建议，用它来总结书籍，把你通常会查阅或询问朋友的任何事情都转向它。

The internet is riddled with AI content that is making it increasingly more difficult to tell fact from fiction. In a world where everything is so polarised and deliberately provoking, the ability to identify bias or motivation in the content we consume has perhaps never been more important. Being able to think for ourselves has perhaps never been more important.

互联网上充斥着人工智能生成的内容，这使得区分事实与虚构变得越来越困难。在一个一切都如此两极分化且刻意挑衅的世界里，识别我们所消费内容中的偏见或动机的能力，或许从未像现在这样重要。能够独立思考的能力，或许也从未像现在这样重要。

As the tech world continues to offer us increasingly more opportunities to automate things, I'm practically begging you to please not automate your thinking. Read the books. Write the papers. Search for answers instead of asking AI. Ask a friend. Ask your mum about the mystery illness you're convinced you're developing and then take her advice and try drinking a glass of water.

随着科技界继续为我们提供越来越多的自动化机会，我几乎是在恳求你们，请不要让你们的思维自动化。去读书。去写论文。去寻找答案，而不是询问人工智能。去问朋友。去问问你妈妈关于你坚信自己患上的那种神秘疾病，然后听从她的建议，试着喝杯水。

Think about what the creators of the content you consume want you to think. Treat the things you consume like the Year 7 English class where you were taught how to identify persuasive techniques. Think about things as much as possible. Please just don't get the AI do it for you.

思考一下你所消费内容的创作者希望你怎么想。把你消费的内容当作七年级英语课上教你如何识别说服技巧的材料来对待。尽可能多地思考。请千万不要让人工智能代劳。

从积极的一面来看，这里有一些最近的好事：总之，祝大家周末愉快！

我想，我希望你在这里思考的内容应该已经很明显了。
