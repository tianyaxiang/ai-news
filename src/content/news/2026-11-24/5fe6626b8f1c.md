# UQ researchers send an unblockable message using gravity

University of Queensland researchers have used gravity to transmit a message through a brick wall using an elevator and a sensitive gravimeter.

昆士兰大学的研究人员利用电梯和灵敏的重力仪，通过重力穿过砖墙发送了一条信息。

The unusual experiment took place in UQ’s Parnell Building, where the team of physicists sent the elevator back and forth between 2 levels, moving its 1.8-tonne counterweight between positions representing binary 0 and 1.

这项不同寻常的实验在昆士兰大学的帕内尔大楼（Parnell Building）进行。物理学家团队让电梯在两个楼层之间往返移动，通过改变其1.8吨重的配重位置来代表二进制的0和1。

A gravimeter in an adjacent bathroom detected the changes in the gravitational field and translated the message into a single word, ‘Gravity’.

放置在相邻洗手间内的一台重力仪探测到了引力场的变化，并将该信息翻译成了一个单词：“Gravity”（重力）。

Dr Charles Woffinden from the School of Mathematics and Physics said gravitational communication had an intriguing advantage. “Unlike the electromagnetic signals used in modern wireless communication, a gravitational signal cannot simply be screened out by placing ordinary material in its path,” Dr Woffinden said.

来自数学与物理学院的查尔斯·沃芬登（Charles Woffinden）博士表示，重力通信具有一个引人注目的优势。沃芬登博士说：“与现代无线通信中使用的电磁信号不同，重力信号无法通过在路径上放置普通材料来简单地屏蔽。”

“Anyone who has lost phone reception in a tunnel or struggled with Wi-Fi through a solid wall will understand the appeal. While the present system is far from practical, it demonstrates the possibility of communicating using a method that ordinary physical barriers cannot block.”

“任何在隧道中失去手机信号或因墙壁阻隔而难以连接Wi-Fi的人都会明白这种吸引力。虽然目前的系统还远未达到实用阶段，但它证明了使用普通物理屏障无法阻挡的方法进行通信的可能性。”

Dr Andrew Groszek said the idea came from a project to develop a contactless weighbridge. “We were aiming to determine the mass of trucks and other large vehicles by measuring their tiny gravitational pull,” Dr Groszek said. “Once we had shown that the idea worked, we realised the same physics could be used to encode and transmit information.”

安德鲁·格罗泽克（Andrew Groszek）博士表示，这个想法源于一个开发非接触式地磅的项目。格罗泽克博士说：“我们当时的目标是通过测量卡车和其他大型车辆微小的引力来确定它们的质量。一旦我们证明了这个想法可行，我们就意识到同样的物理原理可以用来编码和传输信息。”

Professor Andrew White said the biggest limitation to future gravity-based communication was its slow speed. “Our lift was snail-paced – it’s from the 1950s – so our experiment transmitted just 1 bit per minute, meaning it took an hour to send a single word,” Professor White said.

安德鲁·怀特（Andrew White）教授表示，未来基于重力的通信面临的最大限制是速度缓慢。怀特教授说：“我们的电梯速度像蜗牛一样慢——它是20世纪50年代的产物——所以我们的实验每分钟只能传输1比特的数据，这意味着发送一个单词需要一个小时。”

“The speed of gravity communication would come down to how big the mass is, how fast you can move it and the sensitivity of the detector. It took more than 100 years to go from the first telegraph system to the mobile phone, so while gravity communication is not practical today, it could be in the future.”

“重力通信的速度取决于质量的大小、移动速度以及探测器的灵敏度。从第一个电报系统到移动电话用了100多年，所以虽然重力通信在今天并不实用，但在未来可能会实现。”

Professor Matthew Davis said the UQ team had established an important underlying principle. “It’s not often that you get to demonstrate an entirely new way of communicating,” Professor Davis said. “We have shown experimentally that information can be encoded in the gravitational field and recovered at the other end.”

马修·戴维斯（Matthew Davis）教授表示，昆士兰大学团队确立了一个重要的基本原理。戴维斯教授说：“能够展示一种全新的通信方式并不常见。我们已经通过实验证明，信息可以被编码在引力场中，并在另一端被恢复。”

“Our experiment used a classical gravity sensor, but our modelling indicates that quantum sensors could, with rather ambitious assumptions, support ranges of up to about 500 metres and data rates of around 15 bits per minute.” Such sensors could eventually support communication in environments where radio waves, light and other conventional signals cannot penetrate.

“我们的实验使用了经典的重力传感器，但我们的模型显示，在相当大胆的假设下，量子传感器可以支持约500米的传输距离，数据传输速率约为每分钟15比特。”这种传感器最终可能支持在无线电波、光和其他常规信号无法穿透的环境中进行通信。
