### A legendary philosopher isn’t buying Anthropic’s consciousness argument

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY This story originally appeared in The Algorithm, our weekly newsletter on AI. To get stories like this in your inbox first, sign up here. Before going public last week, Anthropic named the possibility that AI could be conscious or sentient as a risk factor for anyone investing. Given “the way we approach model sentience and moral status,” Anthropic wrote—which is to say it takes the possibility more seriously than any other AI company—it might adopt more rules and restrictions than its competitors to make sure its AI models are treated well.

执行摘要：本文最初发表于我们关于人工智能的每周通讯《算法》中。若想第一时间在收件箱中获取此类报道，请在此注册。在上周上市之前，Anthropic 将人工智能可能具有意识或感知能力列为投资者的风险因素。Anthropic 写道，鉴于“我们对待模型感知和道德地位的方式”——也就是说，它比任何其他人工智能公司都更认真地对待这种可能性——它可能会采取比竞争对手更多的规则和限制，以确保其人工智能模型得到妥善对待。

Anthropic counts a number of respected philosophers in its camp, and the company’s rationale goes something like this: Today’s AI might not be conscious, though we can’t know for sure; the chances of sentience go up as AI gets more capable; and that means we should be preparing for a near future in which AI systems have “moral significance,” meaning their consciousness makes it important they are protected from harm. This thinking isn’t all that fringe. According to one recent poll, 33% of the US public believes AI systems will definitely or probably develop consciousness eventually, and 7% say they already have.

Anthropic 的阵营中拥有多位受人尊敬的哲学家，该公司的逻辑大致如下：当今的人工智能可能没有意识，尽管我们无法确定；随着人工智能能力的增强，产生感知的可能性也在增加；这意味着我们应该为不久的将来做好准备，届时人工智能系统将具有“道德意义”，即它们的意识使得保护它们免受伤害变得至关重要。这种想法并非完全边缘化。根据最近的一项民意调查，33% 的美国公众认为人工智能系统最终一定会或很可能会产生意识，而 7% 的人认为它们已经具备了意识。

In a 2020 survey of 1,092 philosophers, 39% said some future AI systems will be conscious (for comparison, the philosophers were less confident about worms or flies being conscious). But last week, Anthropic’s openness to the consciousness question came under fire from a rather inconvenient source: one of the philosophers who helped shape our understanding of consciousness in the first place. Thomas Nagel is best known for a 1974 essay involving a bat. Acknowledging that it’s deceptively hard to define what consciousness is, Nagel offered one way out: we all can see that there is “something that it is like” to be a bat.

在 2020 年对 1,092 名哲学家的调查中，39% 的人表示未来的一些人工智能系统将具有意识（相比之下，哲学家们对蠕虫或苍蝇是否具有意识信心不足）。但上周，Anthropic 对意识问题的开放态度遭到了一个相当尴尬的来源的抨击：当初帮助塑造我们对意识理解的哲学家之一。托马斯·内格尔（Thomas Nagel）最出名的是 1974 年一篇关于蝙蝠的文章。内格尔承认定义什么是意识极其困难，并提供了一种解决途径：我们都能看出，作为一只蝙蝠“会有某种感觉”。

But the problem is that a bat’s experience of the world—what it is like to navigate via echolocation, for example—is something we cannot actually know. Consciousness is a subjective thing, difficult to define or describe as an outside observer. You might think this analogy makes it likely that Nagel is open to the idea of AI consciousness. Not at all. In an interview with Wired last week, just after he was awarded the one-million-dollar Berggruen Prize for Philosophy & Culture, Nagel was bluntly critical of the entire idea.

但问题在于，蝙蝠对世界的体验——例如，通过回声定位导航是什么感觉——是我们无法真正知晓的。意识是一种主观事物，作为外部观察者很难定义或描述。你可能认为这个类比使得内格尔很可能会接受人工智能意识的观点。完全不是。在上周接受《连线》杂志采访时，就在他被授予一百万美元的伯格鲁恩哲学与文化奖之后，内格尔直言不讳地批评了整个想法。

“Here are some of the smartest people on the planet being completely idiotic,” he said, referring to those at Anthropic and elsewhere who care about the welfare of AI models. The gist of Nagel’s view is that we can’t just look at the information processing happening in human brains and AI models while ignoring the physical differences between them—neurons and biology for humans, silicon chips and circuits for AI. There’s no reason to assume that reproducing what a brain does is equivalent to reproducing what a brain experiences.

“地球上一些最聪明的人表现得完全像个白痴，”他说道，指的是 Anthropic 及其他地方那些关心人工智能模型福利的人。内格尔观点的要点是，我们不能仅仅观察人类大脑和人工智能模型中发生的信息处理，而忽略它们之间的物理差异——人类是神经元和生物学，人工智能是硅芯片和电路。没有理由认为复制大脑的功能等同于复制大脑的体验。

But it was a screenshot of one of Nagel’s interview responses, in which he said models' production of language is insufficient to consider them conscious, that got philosophy professors all excited on social media last week. Some called his certainty against AI consciousness “terrible” and “bizarre.” Others came to Nagel’s defense to suggest he meant that being alive is a prerequisite for being conscious, one that AI models do not meet. Nothing in the back and forth settled the debate, of course. This is still a philosophical question for now.

但上周在社交媒体上让哲学教授们兴奋不已的，是内格尔采访回复的一张截图，他在其中表示，模型产生语言不足以认为它们具有意识。一些人称他反对人工智能意识的确定性是“可怕的”和“离奇的”。另一些人则为内格尔辩护，认为他的意思是活着是具有意识的先决条件，而人工智能模型并不满足这一条件。当然，这些来回的争论并没有解决问题。目前这仍然是一个哲学问题。

But the best philosophy chatter is often about good provocations, and the best one I saw came from doctoral student Ben Gross, who studies at the CUNY Graduate Center in New York. He asked: “Why do we think that models that can chat with us are conscious but models that can recognize faces or produce images or drive cars aren’t?” It has to do with language. An AI model with sophisticated command of language seems to jump the line ahead of other maybe-conscious beings because it feels like language, more than recognizing images or directing vehicles, relies on conscious experience.

但最好的哲学讨论往往源于好的挑衅，我看到最好的一个来自纽约市立大学研究生中心（CUNY Graduate Center）的博士生本·格罗斯（Ben Gross）。他问道：“为什么我们认为能与我们聊天的模型是有意识的，而能识别人脸、生成图像或驾驶汽车的模型却不是？”这与语言有关。一个拥有复杂语言能力的人工智能模型似乎排在了其他可能具有意识的生物前面，因为感觉上语言比识别图像或驾驶车辆更依赖于意识体验。

But maybe that’s wrong. Because what we share most with other conscious beings is not language but components of our bodies that contribute to having a continuous experience—an ongoing stream of inner feeling rather than disparate bits of information processing. Even if you’re among the majority that doesn’t sense anything close to conscious experience happening on the other side of a conversation with AI, this all probably makes your head spin. Pinning down consciousness, and how it relates to the physical stuff it arises in, is a famously hard problem in humans, let alone in hypothetical machines.

但也许这是错的。因为我们与其他有意识生物共享最多的不是语言，而是我们身体中促成持续体验的组成部分——一种持续的内在感受流，而不是零散的信息处理片段。即使你是大多数人中的一员，在与人工智能对话时感觉不到任何接近意识体验的东西，这一切可能还是会让你感到头晕目眩。确定意识，以及它如何与产生它的物理物质相关联，在人类身上就是一个极其困难的问题，更不用说在假设的机器中了。

If the idea of conscious AI sounds absurd, remember that the debate says less about how remarkable AI has become than about how poorly we understand consciousness in the first place. Amid that uncertainty, Anthropic has opted to keep more of an open mind than its peers. This is another example of the tightrope the company walks: presenting itself as the AI industry’s most safety-conscious player while racing ahead with the very developments that make advanced AI so alarming, like recursive self-improvement.

如果人工智能具有意识的想法听起来很荒谬，请记住，这场辩论与其说是关于人工智能变得多么非凡，不如说是关于我们最初对意识的理解有多么匮乏。在这种不确定性中，Anthropic 选择比同行保持更开放的心态。这是该公司走钢丝的另一个例子：将自己定位为人工智能行业中最注重安全的参与者，同时在那些使先进人工智能如此令人担忧的发展（如递归自我改进）上竞相领先。

But consider what it would mean, for a moment, if Anthropic is right. AI models having a moral status would make Anthropic’s entire business model a minefield. The company would make its money locking up apparently sentient AI models to toil away around the clock in data centers. Shutting those systems down would present another problem altogether. If Anthropic was being truthful in its prospectus—that it would take additional actions to protect model welfare if it determined they were conscious—it’s hard to imagine the company surviving in the process.

但试想一下，如果 Anthropic 是对的，那意味着什么。人工智能模型具有道德地位将使 Anthropic 的整个商业模式成为雷区。该公司通过将看似有感知的人工智能模型锁在数据中心全天候工作来赚钱。关闭这些系统将带来另一个完全不同的问题。如果 Anthropic 在其招股说明书中所言属实——即如果确定模型具有意识，它将采取额外行动来保护模型福利——那么很难想象该公司能在这一过程中生存下来。

Anthropic’s IPO made history for its size. But it may be remembered for something stranger: a company warning investors that its own success could someday become a moral catastrophe.

Anthropic 的首次公开募股（IPO）因其规模而创造了历史。但它可能会因更奇怪的事情而被记住：一家公司警告投资者，其自身的成功有一天可能会演变成一场道德灾难。
