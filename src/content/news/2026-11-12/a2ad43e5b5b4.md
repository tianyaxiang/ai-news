Buckle Up: Is the U.S.S. Enterprise About to Take Off? I recently came across a post by the AI Futures Project with a rather unsettling title: Q3.5 Timelines Update: Looks Like the Intelligence Explosion Will Happen in 2027 After All. The basic idea is that we could be much closer than previously expected to AI systems capable of doing AI research better than the best human researchers. And, if that happens, those systems could help build even more capable AI, potentially accelerating the whole process.

系好安全带：联邦星舰“进取号”要起飞了吗？我最近偶然看到“人工智能未来项目”（AI Futures Project）发布的一篇文章，标题相当令人不安：《第三季度（3.5版）时间线更新：看来智能爆炸终究会在2027年发生》。其核心观点是，我们可能比预想中更快地迎来能够比顶尖人类研究员更出色地进行人工智能研究的系统。如果这种情况发生，这些系统将有助于构建能力更强的人工智能，从而可能加速整个进程。

I honestly don't know how to feel about this. I've always been a fan of science fiction. I've loved imagining different versions of the future, whether it's the terrifying world of Terminator 2, the mind-bending reality of The Matrix, or the optimistic, technology-driven universe of Star Trek. But one particular science-fiction story has stayed with me more than any other: Isaac Asimov's short story The Last Question. It's a fascinating story that follows humanity and increasingly advanced computers across an almost unimaginable stretch of time.

老实说，我不知道该对此作何感想。我一直是个科幻迷。我喜欢构想未来的不同版本，无论是《终结者2》中令人恐惧的世界、《黑客帝国》中颠覆认知的现实，还是《星际迷航》中乐观且由技术驱动的宇宙。但有一个科幻故事比其他任何故事都更令我难忘：艾萨克·阿西莫夫的短篇小说《最后的问题》。这是一个引人入胜的故事，讲述了人类和日益先进的计算机在一段几乎无法想象的时间跨度里的历程。

Throughout the story, humans repeatedly ask a computer whether the universe's entropy can ever be reversed. Each time, the computer becomes more sophisticated, but its answer remains the same: there isn't enough information to answer the question yet. Eventually, the story reaches a conclusion that is difficult to forget. What struck me about the story wasn't simply the idea of an incredibly powerful computer. It was the scale of what intelligence might eventually become, and how far beyond our current understanding it could take us.

在整个故事中，人类反复询问一台计算机宇宙的熵是否可以逆转。每一次，计算机都变得更加复杂，但它的回答始终如一：目前还没有足够的信息来回答这个问题。最终，故事迎来了一个令人难以忘怀的结局。这个故事打动我的不仅仅是关于一台极其强大的计算机的想法，而是智能最终可能达到的规模，以及它能将我们带到多么超越当前认知的地方。

It made me think about a future in which technological progress reaches a point where we can no longer meaningfully predict what comes next. That's closely related to an idea known as the technological singularity. The term is borrowed from physics, where a singularity describes a point at which our existing theories can no longer adequately explain what is happening. In technology, it's used as a metaphor for a hypothetical point when progress becomes so rapid, and its consequences so profound, that our ability to predict what comes next breaks down.

这让我思考起这样一个未来：技术进步达到一个临界点，以至于我们无法再有意义地预测接下来会发生什么。这与一个被称为“技术奇点”的概念密切相关。这个术语借用自物理学，在物理学中，奇点描述的是一个现有的理论无法再充分解释正在发生之事的点。在技术领域，它被用作一个假设点的隐喻，即当进步变得如此迅速、后果如此深远时，我们预测未来的能力就会失效。

But how could we get there? One proposed path is something known as an intelligence explosion. Imagine AI becoming capable enough to meaningfully contribute to developing the next generation of AI. That next generation would be even better at research and engineering, allowing it to build an even more capable generation after that. Each improvement could make the next improvement happen faster. In theory, this creates a feedback loop: AI helps improve AI, which makes AI even better at improving itself.

但我们该如何到达那里呢？一种被提出的路径被称为“智能爆炸”。想象一下，人工智能变得足够强大，能够为开发下一代人工智能做出实质性贡献。那下一代将更擅长研究和工程，从而能够构建出更强大的后续一代。每一次改进都可能使下一次改进发生得更快。从理论上讲，这会形成一个反馈循环：人工智能帮助改进人工智能，这使得人工智能在自我改进方面变得更加出色。

If this process becomes sufficiently powerful, the pace of technological progress could accelerate dramatically. This is the intelligence explosion: a hypothetical period of extremely rapid growth in machine intelligence. And if that growth becomes powerful enough to fundamentally transform the pace and direction of technological development, it could lead to what we call the technological singularity. In other words, the intelligence explosion is a possible route to the singularity. The singularity is the hypothetical point beyond which our ability to predict technological progress breaks down.

如果这个过程变得足够强大，技术进步的步伐可能会急剧加速。这就是智能爆炸：一个机器智能极速增长的假设时期。如果这种增长变得足够强大，能够从根本上改变技术发展的速度和方向，它就可能导致我们所说的技术奇点。换句话说，智能爆炸是通往奇点的一条可能路径。奇点是一个假设的临界点，越过它，我们预测技术进步的能力就会失效。

It's an extraordinary possibility. And it's also difficult to get our heads around. Why is exponential growth so difficult to understand? Part of the problem is that exponential growth is not something humans are naturally good at understanding. We're pretty good at imagining one thing getting a little bigger, then a little bigger again. We're much worse at imagining something doubling over and over. The numbers look small at first, then suddenly become ridiculous.

这是一种非凡的可能性，但也令人难以理解。为什么指数增长如此难以理解？部分原因是人类天生不擅长理解指数增长。我们很擅长想象某样东西变大一点，然后再变大一点。但我们很不擅长想象某样东西不断翻倍。数字起初看起来很小，然后突然变得荒谬。

Perhaps that's because most of the things we encounter in everyday life don't behave this way. A tree grows gradually. A house gets built one brick at a time. Our salaries, savings and everyday experiences tend to change in ways that feel relatively linear. We rarely encounter something that appears almost insignificant at first and then grows to an unimaginable size in a surprisingly short period. But exponential growth can do exactly that.

也许这是因为我们在日常生活中遇到的大多数事物并非如此。树木是逐渐生长的，房子是一砖一瓦盖起来的。我们的工资、储蓄和日常经历往往以一种相对线性的方式变化。我们很少遇到起初看起来微不足道，却在极短时间内增长到无法想象规模的事物。但指数增长恰恰能做到这一点。

There's a famous legend that makes this easy to picture. A king offers a reward to the person who invents chess. The inventor asks for one grain of rice on the first square of the chessboard, two on the second, four on the third, and so on—doubling the amount on every square. The king thinks this sounds like a modest request. It isn't. A chessboard has 64 squares, so the total is: 1 + 2 + 4 + 8 + ... + 2^63 = 2^64 - 1. That's 18,446,744,073,709,551,615 grains of rice. Around 18 quintillion.

有一个著名的传说让这一点变得容易想象。一位国王要奖励国际象棋的发明者。发明者要求在棋盘的第一个格子里放一粒米，第二个格子里放两粒，第三个格子里放四粒，以此类推——每一格的米粒数量都翻倍。国王认为这听起来是一个微不足道的请求。事实并非如此。棋盘有64个格子，总数是：1 + 2 + 4 + 8 + ... + 2^63 = 2^64 - 1。那是18,446,744,073,709,551,615粒米，大约1800京（quintillion）。

Suppose, very roughly, that each grain weighs 25 milligrams. The total would weigh about 461 billion metric tonnes. That's roughly 1.3 million Empire State Buildings' worth of rice, using a rough building-weight estimate of 365,000 metric tonnes. The first few squares barely matter. Almost all the rice is on the last few. That's the part of exponential growth our intuition misses: the ending can be so much bigger than the beginning that it's hard to believe they belong to the same process.

假设每粒米重25毫克，总重量约为4610亿公吨。按照每栋帝国大厦约36.5万公吨的粗略估算，这相当于约130万栋帝国大厦重量的米。前几个格子几乎无关紧要，几乎所有的米都在最后几个格子里。这就是我们直觉所忽略的指数增长的部分：结尾可能比开头大得多，以至于很难相信它们属于同一个过程。

And this is why discussions about an AI intelligence explosion are so difficult to evaluate intuitively. If AI development starts feeding into its own improvement, we might be looking at a process whose consequences are much harder to imagine than the technologies we're used to seeing develop around us.
