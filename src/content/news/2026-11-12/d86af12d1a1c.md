# AlphaGenome 2: Deciphering how cells read DNA to better understand disease

November 11, 2026 ScienceAlphaGenome 2: Deciphering how cells read DNA to better understand diseaseAdam Gayoso, Natasha Latysheva, Pushmeet Kohli and Žiga Avsec Read the paper ShareOur next-generation DNA sequence model bridges single-cell biology and human genetics, predicting how genetic variants affect hundreds of human cell types.

2026年11月11日，《科学》杂志发布了“AlphaGenome 2：破译细胞如何读取DNA以更好地理解疾病”。作者包括Adam Gayoso、Natasha Latysheva、Pushmeet Kohli和Žiga Avsec。我们的下一代DNA序列模型架起了单细胞生物学与人类遗传学之间的桥梁，能够预测遗传变异如何影响数百种人类细胞类型。

Today, we are announcing AlphaGenome 2, our next-generation model that predicts how DNA sequences and their variations impact specific human cell types, giving scientists a powerful new tool to uncover the cellular drivers of disease. Cells are the building blocks of life. Every cell in our body carries the same DNA blueprint, yet each cell type and state—like a heart muscle cell or an immune cell fighting an infection—switches different genes on or off to do its job.

今天，我们宣布推出AlphaGenome 2，这是我们的下一代模型，旨在预测DNA序列及其变异如何影响特定的人类细胞类型，为科学家提供了一种强大的新工具来揭示疾病的细胞驱动因素。细胞是生命的基石。我们体内的每个细胞都携带相同的DNA蓝图，但每种细胞类型和状态——例如心肌细胞或正在对抗感染的免疫细胞——都会开启或关闭不同的基因来执行其功能。

Global efforts like the Human Cell Atlas are mapping this gene expression for millions of cells, but it is still an unsolved challenge to predict how subtle DNA variations affect this expression in cells, which is a key driver of human traits and disease. Last year, we launched AlphaGenome to predict how DNA variants affect molecular activity across broad tissues. We expanded its reach with AlphaGenome Atlas, a platform containing predictions for all 9 billion possible single-letter changes in the human genome.

像“人类细胞图谱”这样的全球性项目正在为数百万个细胞绘制基因表达图谱，但预测细微的DNA变异如何影响细胞内的这种表达仍然是一个未解的挑战，而这正是人类性状和疾病的关键驱动因素。去年，我们推出了AlphaGenome，用于预测DNA变异如何影响广泛组织中的分子活性。我们通过AlphaGenome Atlas扩展了其覆盖范围，该平台包含了人类基因组中所有90亿种可能的单字母变化的预测。

These tools help researchers prioritize and interpret variant effects, driving discoveries in rare diseases, complex traits, and fundamental biology. By zooming in from broad tissue averages to individual cell types and cell states, and redesigning the model’s architecture, AlphaGenome 2 represents a step-change improvement in variant predictive performance.

这些工具帮助研究人员优先处理和解读变异效应，推动了罕见病、复杂性状和基础生物学领域的发现。通过从广泛的组织平均水平缩小到单个细胞类型和细胞状态，并重新设计模型架构，AlphaGenome 2在变异预测性能方面实现了跨越式的提升。

Importantly, it allows researchers to predict variant effects in disease-relevant cells that would be missed in broad-tissue models, as well as predictions for cell and disease states that the model hasn’t seen before. AlphaGenome 2 identifies nearly twice as many variants that change gene expression in cells, compared to our previous state-of-the-art model.

重要的是，它使研究人员能够预测在广泛组织模型中会被忽略的疾病相关细胞中的变异效应，并能对模型此前未见过的细胞和疾病状态进行预测。与我们之前最先进的模型相比，AlphaGenome 2识别出的改变细胞基因表达的变异数量几乎翻了一番。

Trained on more than 100 million individual cell profiles across 68 tissues, the model turns static cell atlases into a predictive engine for human genetics. AlphaGenome 2 is available today for non-commercial research via the AlphaGenome API, and in private preview for commercial research.

该模型在涵盖68种组织的超过1亿个个体细胞图谱上进行了训练，将静态细胞图谱转化为人类遗传学的预测引擎。AlphaGenome 2即日起可通过AlphaGenome API用于非商业研究，并以私有预览形式提供给商业研究使用。

Redesigning AlphaGenome for specific cell types. Comparing how AlphaGenome 1 and 2 treat cell types. AlphaGenome produces fixed parallel outputs of bulk tissues and cell lines from a 1-million-letter DNA sequence. AlphaGenome 2 is a conditional model, taking a specific biological condition alongside an input DNA sequence, either chosen from a reference catalog of cell types or generated on the fly from a scientist’s own single-cell experiment via our cell encoder.

为特定细胞类型重新设计AlphaGenome。比较AlphaGenome 1和2处理细胞类型的方式。AlphaGenome从100万个字母的DNA序列中产生批量组织和细胞系的固定并行输出。AlphaGenome 2是一个条件模型，它在输入DNA序列的同时接收特定的生物学条件，这些条件既可以从参考细胞类型目录中选择，也可以通过我们的细胞编码器从科学家自己的单细胞实验中即时生成。

The first AlphaGenome model took a million-letter DNA sequence and predicted properties across thousands of experiments from many tissues all at once. While efficient, this parallel approach spread the model’s capacity thin and made it hard to capture subtle differences between closely related cell types. To improve this, we fundamentally redesigned how cell types are treated by the model.

第一个AlphaGenome模型接收100万个字母的DNA序列，并同时预测来自许多组织的数千个实验的属性。虽然这种并行方法很有效，但它分散了模型的能力，使其难以捕捉密切相关的细胞类型之间的细微差异。为了改进这一点，我们从根本上重新设计了模型处理细胞类型的方式。

Instead of predicting in parallel, AlphaGenome 2 is a conditional model that predicts DNA effects for one condition - defined as a tissue, cell type or state - at a time. Alongside the million-letter DNA sequence, it uses the target biological condition as input at every stage of its architecture. This allows it to learn how the same stretch of DNA is read differently across cell types.

AlphaGenome 2不再进行并行预测，而是一个条件模型，每次预测一种条件（定义为组织、细胞类型或状态）下的DNA效应。除了100万个字母的DNA序列外，它在架构的每个阶段都将目标生物学条件作为输入。这使它能够学习同一段DNA在不同细胞类型中是如何被不同地读取的。

A conditional model also allows cross-modality imputation, enabling AlphaGenome 2 to predict missing experimental data. The model separates a cell’s biological state from its outputs, so if a scientist inputs just one measurement, like single-cell gene activity, AlphaGenome 2 can then predict unmeasured properties, such as chromatin accessibility. This can then help researchers prioritize which experiments to do next.

条件模型还允许跨模态插补，使AlphaGenome 2能够预测缺失的实验数据。该模型将细胞的生物学状态与其输出分离开来，因此如果科学家仅输入一项测量数据（如单细胞基因活性），AlphaGenome 2就可以预测未测量的属性（如染色质可及性）。这进而可以帮助研究人员确定接下来进行哪些实验的优先级。

We trained AlphaGenome 2 on both bulk and single-cell data from human and mouse samples, including over 107 million individual cell profiles. Moving beyond the bulk tissue averages of the original model, AlphaGenome 2 can resolve activity inside specialized cells—such as distinct heart muscle or blood immune cells—to help uncover crucial DNA variants that act only in specific cell types.

我们在来自人类和小鼠样本的批量和单细胞数据上训练了AlphaGenome 2，其中包括超过1.07亿个个体细胞图谱。超越了原始模型的批量组织平均水平，AlphaGenome 2能够解析专门细胞内的活性——例如特定的心肌细胞或血液免疫细胞——以帮助发现仅在特定细胞类型中起作用的关键DNA变异。

The model predicts the same core experimental readouts of DNA function as AlphaGenome — such as gene expression, histone modifications and chromatin accessibility — but also DNA methylation, a widely requested readout.

该模型预测与AlphaGenome相同的DNA功能核心实验读数——如基因表达、组蛋白修饰和染色质可及性——同时也包括DNA甲基化，这是一种被广泛要求的读数。

"What makes AlphaGenome 2 exciting is how it adapts directly to new single-cell data without retraining, predicting how DNA variants act in newly profiled cell states from even a small sample of cells." — Professor Sarah Teichmann, Co-founder of the Human Cell Atlas consortium and Professor of Stem Cell Medicine at the University of Cambridge.

“AlphaGenome 2令人兴奋之处在于它无需重新训练即可直接适应新的单细胞数据，即使是从少量细胞样本中，也能预测DNA变异在最新分析的细胞状态中如何发挥作用。”——Sarah Teichmann教授，人类细胞图谱联盟联合创始人，剑桥大学干细胞医学教授。

State-of-the-art cell-type predictions and variant recall. AlphaGenome 2 is significantly better at predicting gene regulation in specific cells, helping researchers pinpoint disease-relevant variants. Improved cell type specificity: On genome regions outside its training data, AlphaGenome 2 explains significantly more of how gene expression differs across cell types within a tissue—tracking continuous changes in gene expression, such as when human blood stem cells mature into red blood cells and immune lineages.

最先进的细胞类型预测和变异召回。AlphaGenome 2在预测特定细胞中的基因调控方面表现显著更好，帮助研究人员精确定位疾病相关变异。改进的细胞类型特异性：在训练数据之外的基因组区域，AlphaGenome 2能更显著地解释组织内不同细胞类型之间基因表达的差异——追踪基因表达的连续变化，例如人类造血干细胞成熟为红细胞和免疫谱系的过程。

Improved variant effect prediction: On the TenK10K dataset, a large single-cell map of 28 primary immune cell types, AlphaGenome 2 reliably identified nearly twice as many causal variants (30% vs. 16%) and directional gene effects (43% vs. 23%) as the original AlphaGenome fine-tuned on single-cell data and other specialized tools. This precision helps researchers find overlooked candidate variants for new discoveries.

改进的变异效应预测：在TenK10K数据集（一个包含28种原代免疫细胞类型的庞大单细胞图谱）上，AlphaGenome 2可靠地识别出的因果变异（30%对16%）和定向基因效应（43%对23%）几乎是原始AlphaGenome（经单细胞数据微调）及其他专业工具的两倍。这种精确度有助于研究人员发现被忽视的候选变异，从而实现新的发现。
