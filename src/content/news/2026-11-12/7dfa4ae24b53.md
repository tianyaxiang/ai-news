EXECUTIVE SUMMARY It’s that time of year again: Thousands of delegates, lobbyists, and journalists are gathered for the annual UN climate talks, taking place this time in Antalya, Turkey. COP31 officially kicked off on November 8, marking the beginning of negotiations on one of the biggest stages of the climate world. Turkey and Australia are cohosting this year’s talks, which are scheduled to wrap up on November 20. This annual gathering can often serve as an indicator of where the world stands on addressing and adapting to the climate crisis. And this year’s talks are sure to be a doozy—expect a lot of back and forth on fossil fuels and climate finance in particular. Here’s what you should know about COP31.

执行摘要：又到了每年的这个时候：成千上万的代表、游说者和记者齐聚一堂，参加在土耳其安塔利亚举行的年度联合国气候谈判。第31届缔约方会议（COP31）于11月8日正式开幕，标志着气候领域最大舞台之一的谈判拉开帷幕。土耳其和澳大利亚共同主办了今年的会议，会议定于11月20日结束。这一年度聚会通常可以作为衡量世界在应对和适应气候危机方面所处位置的指标。今年的谈判注定会非常棘手——预计在化石燃料和气候融资方面会有大量的反复讨论。以下是关于COP31你需要了解的内容。

In recent years, the tension always feels high when COP rolls around. (COP stands for “Conference of the Parties.”) But this year feels especially charged: The world is still grappling with the ongoing war in Iran, which has disrupted the Middle East and sent economic shock waves around the world as energy prices have spiked. This year’s COP will be the second without a formal delegation from the US. President Trump formally withdrew the country from the Paris Agreement earlier this year and is in the process of withdrawing from the UN Framework Committee on Climate Change. That’s the treaty that allows formal participation in negotiations.

近年来，每当COP（缔约方会议的缩写）临近时，紧张气氛总是很高。但今年感觉格外紧绷：世界仍在努力应对伊朗持续不断的战争，这场战争扰乱了中东局势，并随着能源价格飙升向全球发送了经济冲击波。今年的COP将是第二次没有美国正式代表团参加的会议。特朗普总统今年早些时候正式让美国退出了《巴黎协定》，并正在退出《联合国气候变化框架公约》。该公约是允许正式参与谈判的条约。

Meanwhile, things aren’t looking good for the Paris Agreement, which was established at UN climate talks 11 years ago, setting a goal of limiting warming to 1.5 °C over preindustrial levels. In a report published in September, the UN Environment Program acknowledged that global warming is set to exceed that 1.5 °C goal, “likely within the next few years.” So as climate talks kick off, there’s an obvious, urgent question looming about how to quickly cut emissions to limit warming.

与此同时，《巴黎协定》的前景并不乐观。该协定于11年前在联合国气候谈判中达成，设定了将升温幅度限制在工业化前水平1.5摄氏度以内的目标。联合国环境规划署在9月发布的一份报告中承认，全球变暖注定会超过1.5摄氏度的目标，“很可能在未来几年内”就会发生。因此，随着气候谈判的开始，一个显而易见且紧迫的问题浮出水面：如何迅速削减排放以限制升温。

There’s also the major issue of how to pay for efforts to address emissions and to help a warming world endure the inevitable damage. Two years ago, talks ended in a goal of mobilizing $300 billion per year by 2035 to help pay to reduce emissions and adapt to climate change. (Rich nations provided $136.7 billion in 2024, according to the Organization for Economic Cooperation and Development.) And last year, the COP30 agreement included a goal of tripling the money for adaptation over that period. (Such funding has been quite flat over the past few years.)

此外，还有一个重大问题是如何为应对排放的努力买单，并帮助变暖的世界承受不可避免的损害。两年前，谈判达成了一个目标，即到2035年每年筹集3000亿美元，用于支付减排和适应气候变化的费用。（根据经济合作与发展组织的数据，富裕国家在2024年提供了1367亿美元。）去年，COP30协议包含了一个目标，即在此期间将适应资金增加两倍。（此类资金在过去几年中一直相当平稳。）

The world got a tragic reminder of just how high the stakes are earlier this year, when a glacier collapse caused devastating flooding in the Himalayas. The flood killed approximately 1,500 people, left over 5,000 missing, and caused nearly $5 billion in damage. The UN loss and damages fund committed $20 million to helping Nepal recover (less than 1% of the total estimated cost to the country).

今年早些时候，喜马拉雅山脉发生冰川崩塌引发了毁灭性的洪水，世界得到了一个悲剧性的提醒，即风险有多高。洪水造成约1500人死亡，5000多人失踪，并造成近50亿美元的损失。联合国损失与损害基金承诺提供2000万美元帮助尼泊尔恢复（不到该国估计总成本的1%）。

Another key trend to watch is how the formal negotiations engage with the topic of fossil fuels. Three years ago, the final agreement text explicitly called for a transition away from them. But it felt as if things took a step back last year, when the final agreement didn’t even mention the phrase “fossil fuels” once. This will be an interesting conversation, especially because cohosts Turkey and Australia have both faced criticism over their relationships with these fuels—Australia is a major exporter of coal and natural gas, and Turkey’s grid relies heavily on fossil fuels, including coal.

另一个值得关注的关键趋势是正式谈判如何处理化石燃料这一议题。三年前，最终协议文本明确呼吁摆脱化石燃料。但去年似乎有所倒退，最终协议甚至一次都没有提到“化石燃料”这个词。这将是一场有趣的对话，尤其是因为共同主办国土耳其和澳大利亚都因其与这些燃料的关系而面临批评——澳大利亚是煤炭和天然气的主要出口国，而土耳其的电网严重依赖包括煤炭在内的化石燃料。

Outside the formal negotiations, COP31 president Murat Kurum, Turkey’s environment minister, is spearheading a side agenda, including two informal initiatives that will be worth watching. These wouldn’t be part of the official agreement text, but countries could sign on to signal support. One is a pledge to make AI more sustainable; this is almost comically squishy, even as voluntary COP pledges go. The text includes explicit wording that it creates no new obligations for countries, so don’t expect anything resembling enforcement there.

在正式谈判之外，COP31主席、土耳其环境部长穆拉特·库鲁姆（Murat Kurum）正在牵头一项侧重议程，包括两项值得关注的非正式倡议。这些倡议不会成为正式协议文本的一部分，但各国可以签署以表示支持。其中一项是关于使人工智能更具可持续性的承诺；即使以COP自愿承诺的标准来看，这也显得有些滑稽且空洞。文本中明确写道，它不会为各国创造新的义务，因此不要指望那里会有任何类似强制执行的内容。

The COP leadership is also pushing to make electricity a larger part of member countries’ energy use. Today, only about 23% of global energy is used in the form of electricity. Fossil fuels, like gasoline in vehicles or natural gas for heating, make up the bulk of the rest. Shifting to more electricity use, and relying on cleaner sources for that electricity, could help drive down emissions. The new pledge’s target is for electricity to make up 35% of total energy consumption by 2035.

COP领导层还在推动提高电力在成员国能源使用中的占比。目前，全球只有约23%的能源以电力的形式使用。其余大部分是化石燃料，如车辆使用的汽油或用于供暖的天然气。转向更多地使用电力，并依靠更清洁的能源来发电，有助于降低排放。新承诺的目标是到2035年使电力占总能源消耗的35%。

With leadership billing this as an “implementation COP,” it would be great to see some real, tangible progress on the global challenge of addressing climate change.
