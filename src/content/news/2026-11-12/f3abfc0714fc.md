# Loot boxes, influencers, and addictive features face an EU crackdown

Gaming, social media, and e-commerce platforms are facing a fresh wave of sweeping European Union rules that aim to make “your online experience fairer.” The newly proposed Digital Fairness Act (DFA) published by the EU Commission on Wednesday is designed to tackle practices that exploit consumers into spending more of their time and money online, including misleading prices or availability, addictive design features like gaming loot boxes, undisclosed influencer marketing, and more.

游戏、社交媒体和电子商务平台正面临欧盟新一轮全面规则的冲击，旨在让“您的在线体验更公平”。欧盟委员会周三发布的《数字公平法案》（DFA）草案旨在解决诱导消费者在网上花费更多时间和金钱的行为，包括误导性的价格或库存信息、游戏战利品箱等令人上瘾的设计功能、未披露的网红营销等。

“The Act will protect consumers of all ages shopping and browsing for goods or services online,” the EU Commission said in a news bulletin. “It will also mean fairer competition for businesses, which are expecting to save EU consumers up to €13.7 billion ($15.1 billion) a year.”

欧盟委员会在新闻简报中表示：“该法案将保护所有年龄段在网上购物和浏览商品或服务的消费者。这也意味着企业之间将展开更公平的竞争，预计每年将为欧盟消费者节省高达 137 亿欧元（约合 151 亿美元）的开支。”

Here’s an overview of what the DFA proposal includes: A ban on manipulative marketing practices and so-called “dark patterns” that push consumers into making decisions they might not otherwise make, such as fake claims about a product almost being sold out. A requirement to give consumers the choice to turn off addictive features, such as persistent prompts, endless scrolling, and autoplay of videos. Restrictions around addictive design also apply to “AI companions and general conversational chatbots.”

以下是 DFA 提案内容的概述：禁止操纵性营销行为和所谓的“黑暗模式”，这些手段会诱导消费者做出本不会做出的决定，例如虚假宣称某产品即将售罄。要求赋予消费者关闭上瘾功能的权利，例如持续的提示、无限滚动和视频自动播放。针对上瘾设计的限制也适用于“人工智能伴侣和通用对话聊天机器人”。

Restrictions on unfair practices that encourage overspending, such as banning gambling-like features (pay-to-win and pay-to-progress, for example) for minors. Requiring influencers to clearly disclose advertising content, and restrictions to prevent them from exploiting the trust of children. Restrictions on unfair personalised ads and prices that target minors or take advantage of sensitive data.

限制鼓励过度消费的不公平行为，例如禁止向未成年人提供类似赌博的功能（如付费获胜和付费推进）。要求网红明确披露广告内容，并限制其利用儿童的信任。限制针对未成年人或利用敏感数据的各类不公平个性化广告和定价。

Making subscriptions and digital contracts easier to cancel, with prompts to remind customers about upcoming automatic renewals and free trial periods coming to an end. Requiring clear and transparent pricing, especially around dynamic pricing and purchases using virtual in-app currency. When consumers wait in virtual queues for cultural and sports event tickets, companies will have to show the current price in real time and at the highest possible price. Giving consumers the right to communicate with a human customer service representative, and clear rights using an AI agent to make purchases.

简化订阅和数字合同的取消流程，并设置提示以提醒客户即将到来的自动续费和免费试用期结束。要求价格清晰透明，特别是在动态定价和使用应用内虚拟货币购买时。当消费者在虚拟排队购买文化和体育赛事门票时，公司必须实时显示当前价格及最高可能价格。赋予消费者与人工客服沟通的权利，以及在使用人工智能代理进行购买时的明确权利。

The scope of the restrictions being proposed is broad, and could have a significant impact on major parts of the online digital landscape, from the “rabbit hole” algorithms across YouTube, TikTok, and Meta platforms, to AI chatbots and apps like DuoLingo that relentlessly nudge users to keep interacting with them. Some gaming organizations have already slammed the DFA proposal, describing its restrictions around virtual currencies, loot boxes, and offering micro-transactions to minors as being “unworkable, unjustified and disproportionate.”

拟议的限制范围很广，可能会对在线数字领域的多个主要部分产生重大影响，从 YouTube、TikTok 和 Meta 平台上的“兔子洞”算法，到人工智能聊天机器人以及像多邻国（DuoLingo）这样不断促使用户保持互动的应用程序。一些游戏组织已经抨击了 DFA 提案，称其对虚拟货币、战利品箱以及向未成年人提供微交易的限制是“不可行、不合理且不成比例的”。

Given this is a draft proposal, the DFA will undergo various approvals through the European Parliament and the Council of the European Union before being passed into law, and its current measures could be changed or removed entirely as part of that process. When (or if) it does come into force, the EU Commission says companies who violate the regulations could see fines of up to six percent of their worldwide annual turnover.

鉴于这只是草案，DFA 在成为法律之前将经历欧洲议会和欧盟理事会的各项审批，其现有措施可能会在该过程中被修改或完全删除。欧盟委员会表示，一旦（如果）该法案生效，违反规定的公司可能面临高达其全球年度营业额 6% 的罚款。

“Our consumer protection rules will help ensure that businesses compete on the merits of their products and services - not on their ability to exploit consumers or evade the rules. We are not making it harder to do business. We are making it harder to do business unfairly,” EU Commissioner Michael McGrath said in remarks following the DFA draft publication. “We cannot have 21st-century consumers protected by rules that have not kept pace with the 21st-century economy.”

欧盟委员迈克尔·麦格拉思（Michael McGrath）在 DFA 草案发布后的讲话中表示：“我们的消费者保护规则将有助于确保企业在产品和服务的优劣上竞争，而不是靠剥削消费者或规避规则的能力。我们并不是在增加经商难度，我们是在增加不公平经商的难度。我们不能让 21 世纪的消费者受到那些跟不上 21 世纪经济发展的规则的保护。”
