Avery has been writing fan fiction for about six years, now mainly focused on the gay hockey romance Heated Rivalry. Until last month, it all lived in one place—Archive of Our Own. Then, on October 29 the fan fiction behemoth went dark due to a “security incident.” Within days, Avery was alarmed to find her works on mirror sites she hadn’t posted them to.

Avery 创作同人小说已有六年左右，目前主要专注于男同性恋冰球题材小说《Heated Rivalry》。直到上个月，她的所有作品都只存放在一个地方——Archive of Our Own (AO3)。然而，10 月 29 日，这个同人小说巨头因“安全事件”陷入瘫痪。几天之内，Avery 惊恐地发现自己的作品出现在了她从未授权的镜像网站上。

“To me it’s a security risk,” Avery, who did not want her real name used due to fears of backlash on social media, tells WIRED via DM. “One bad actor could put malware in that drive.” She emailed the sites—Squidgeworld and AO3.Life—and the stories were removed, though she’ll never know if she has found every copy online. (AO3.Life has since been taken down, and Walter Hopgood, president of Squidge.org, which hosts Squidgeworld, told WIRED it removes works that are posted without an author’s permission.)

“对我来说，这是一种安全风险，”Avery 通过私信告诉《连线》杂志，由于担心社交媒体上的反弹，她不愿透露真实姓名。“一个恶意行为者可能会在那个云端硬盘里植入恶意软件。”她给这些网站——Squidgeworld 和 AO3.Life——发了邮件，随后这些故事被删除了，但她永远无法确定是否找出了网上所有的副本。（AO3.Life 此后已被关闭，而托管 Squidgeworld 的 Squidge.org 总裁 Walter Hopgood 告诉《连线》，他们会删除未经作者许可发布的作品。）

Avery’s problem is one shared with many fanfic authors since the team behind AO3 announced the site will remain offline for up to four weeks. Hosting more than 18.8 million fan works across hundreds of thousands of fandoms, AO3 is among the most visited websites in the world. When it went down, some fans started freaking out over the “AO3 apocalypse.”

自 AO3 团队宣布网站将持续离线长达四周以来，Avery 面临的问题也是许多同人小说作者共同的困扰。AO3 托管了跨越数十万个同人圈的超过 1880 万部同人作品，是世界上访问量最大的网站之一。当它瘫痪时，一些粉丝开始对这场“AO3 末日”感到恐慌。

Within days, readers who had saved stories on their own device placed them onto shared libraries on Google Drives. But finding alternatives has caused friction in the community, as some authors are unhappy with the way their works are being distributed without their permission, breaking fanfic norms and exacerbating fears about AI scraping.

几天之内，那些在自己设备上保存了故事的读者将它们上传到了 Google 云端硬盘的共享库中。但寻找替代方案在社区内引发了摩擦，因为一些作者对自己的作品在未经许可的情况下被分发感到不满，这打破了同人小说的规范，并加剧了人们对人工智能抓取内容的担忧。

One of the many drives to pop up was the Archive Salvation Google Drive project, a collective of fan fiction downloaded from AO3. It faced criticism after launching, though a spokesperson tells WIRED it offered ways for authors to ask for their work to be removed. But it’s since been hacked and is no longer accessible, according to a November 3 statement on a moderator’s TikTok, though the group behind it is still sharing fanfics in a closed Discord server.

涌现出的众多云端硬盘项目之一是“Archive Salvation”Google 云端硬盘项目，这是一个从 AO3 下载的同人小说合集。它在推出后遭到批评，尽管一位发言人告诉《连线》，他们提供了让作者申请删除作品的途径。但根据一位版主 11 月 3 日在 TikTok 上发布的声明，该项目此后遭到黑客攻击，已无法访问，不过其背后的团队仍在私密的 Discord 服务器中分享同人小说。

“We continue to acknowledge the controversies of such drives but also want to acknowledge the joy this has brought readers and the authors who submitted their fics to us,” the statement said, adding, “our drive was controversial and very public, so the hacker is most likely someone who stands against drives like this in general.”

声明称：“我们继续承认此类云端硬盘引发的争议，但也想承认这为读者以及向我们提交作品的作者所带来的快乐。”声明还补充道：“我们的云端硬盘极具争议且非常公开，因此黑客很可能是一个普遍反对这类云端硬盘的人。”

Avery doesn’t mind her work circulating in private drives or being asked for copies of their work for fans to read—her concern is with her work being uploaded to giant drive libraries. But she says AO3’s breakdown has revealed entitlement among some readers, who’ve messaged her demanding to know where her fics are now available. “I just can’t wait for AO3 to go back up so that people can go back to normal,” she says. “It’s left a sour taste in my mouth.”

Avery 并不介意自己的作品在私人云端硬盘中流传，也不介意粉丝向她索要作品副本阅读——她担心的是自己的作品被上传到大型云端硬盘库中。但她说，AO3 的崩溃暴露了一些读者的特权心态，他们发消息要求她告知作品现在可以在哪里找到。“我只是迫不及待地想让 AO3 恢复正常，这样人们的生活也能回归常态，”她说。“这让我感到很不舒服。”

“Writers hating their readers this much will kill fandoms actually,” one user wrote on X in response to an artist expressing frustration about people who “don’t contribute anything to the fandom” weighing in on how work is shared. Another X user accused the same artist of being “self-righteous” and “entitled.”

“作者如此憎恨读者实际上会扼杀同人圈，”一位用户在 X 上写道，回应了一位艺术家的沮丧情绪，该艺术家曾抱怨那些“对同人圈毫无贡献”的人对作品分享方式指手画脚。另一位 X 用户则指责同一位艺术家“自以为是”且“充满特权感”。

Avery says some readers seem to feel authors’ feelings “are secondary to the readers.” “It feels like AO3 going down made so many people so crazy that they couldn’t get their fix, and I think the authors are most outspoken because it’s our work.”

Avery 表示，一些读者似乎认为作者的感受“次于读者”。“感觉 AO3 的下线让很多人因为无法获得‘精神食粮’而变得疯狂，我认为作者们之所以最直言不讳，是因为那是我们的作品。”

Riley, a writer with over 90,000 followers across X and Instagram, ran afoul of some fellow authors when they shared a Google doc cataloging some of the drives compiling fanfic on November 2. Some people claimed it was a privacy violation and could put AO3 users at risk of doxing. “It’s about basic respect,” one user wrote, while others demanded their fics be removed from the list.

Riley 是一位在 X 和 Instagram 上拥有超过 9 万粉丝的作家，他们在 11 月 2 日分享了一份整理了部分同人小说云端硬盘的 Google 文档，这引起了一些同行作者的不满。一些人声称这侵犯了隐私，并可能使 AO3 用户面临被“人肉搜索”的风险。“这是基本的尊重问题，”一位用户写道，而其他人则要求将他们的作品从列表中删除。

Riley, who did not want their real name used for fear of receiving more backlash, made their X account private soon after. “I did not anticipate the potential harmful effects of me compiling the document of stories, which is why I deleted it,” they tell WIRED.

Riley 因担心遭到更多反弹而不愿透露真实姓名，随后不久便将 X 账号设为私密。“我没有预料到我整理这份故事文档可能带来的负面影响，这就是我删除它的原因，”他们告诉《连线》。

Meanwhile, Ellipsus, a collaborative writing platform with an anti-AI stance that was founded in 2022, has seen a boom in interest since AO3 went down. Allegra Rosenberg, an Ellipsus employee and writer, says that new sign-ups are up more than 500 percent since the outage began and views of its public reader links—the ways in which authors can share work—have increased heavily.

与此同时，成立于 2022 年、持反 AI 立场的协作写作平台 Ellipsus 自 AO3 瘫痪以来，关注度激增。Ellipsus 的员工兼作家 Allegra Rosenberg 表示，自宕机开始以来，新注册用户增长了超过 500%，其公共阅读链接（作者分享作品的方式）的浏览量也大幅增加。

What Ellipsus offers is control. Authors can control who can see their work and how they can interact with it. They can turn on comments, and readers can leave kudos (called “props” on Ellipsus), as well as custom fonts and themes. Rosenberg says the company noticed that many of the users were fan fiction writers and adjusted features for them before the outage, for example, a button that exports work directly to AO3.

Ellipsus 提供的是控制权。作者可以控制谁能看到他们的作品以及如何与作品互动。他们可以开启评论功能，读者可以留下点赞（在 Ellipsus 上称为“props”），还可以使用自定义字体和主题。Rosenberg 说，公司注意到许多用户是同人小说作者，并在宕机前为他们调整了功能，例如增加了一个将作品直接导出到 AO3 的按钮。

“We know that people want a space to not only write but share new writing, in a way that feels safe for them,” says Rosenberg. Another reason the outage has felt so fraught is that the world of fan fiction has broken containment, becoming mainstream in part due to shows like Heated Rivalry and the adaptation of former fan fictions such as Alchemised by SenLinYu, which was originally published on AO3, into bona fide novels.

“我们知道人们不仅想要一个写作的空间，还想要一个能以让他们感到安全的方式分享新作品的空间，”Rosenberg 说。这次宕机之所以让人感到如此紧张，另一个原因是同人小说世界已经“破圈”，部分原因是像《Heated Rivalry》这样的作品，以及像 SenLinYu 原本在 AO3 上发表的《Alchemised》被改编成正式出版的小说，使得同人小说变得主流化。

“It’s the Tik-tokification of fanfic,” Avery says. “Everyone wants a snippet and a graphic and a one shot, and they can share it on Twitter, and then suddenly a thing I wrote years ago goes viral. Fan fiction never used to go viral.”

“这是同人小说的 TikTok 化，”Avery 说。“每个人都想要片段、图片和短篇，他们可以在 Twitter 上分享，然后我几年前写的东西突然就火了。同人小说过去从来不会病毒式传播。”

Rosenberg, who is writing a book on fandom, thinks that so much of the backlash comes from a mismatch between how big the community actually is and how small it still feels to many. The giant drives and libraries drew outrage due to their scale and a fear from many fans that their stories could be taken down. That fear has a real history within fandom spaces. “In the ’90s and 2000s, there were cease-and-desist notices and takedown controversies on Fanfiction.net and LiveJournal that led to AO3’s founding.”

正在撰写关于同人圈书籍的 Rosenberg 认为，如此多的反弹源于社区实际规模与许多人感受到的规模之间的错位。大型云端硬盘和图书馆因其规模以及许多粉丝担心自己的故事可能被删除而引发了愤怒。这种恐惧在同人圈中有着真实的历史。“在 90 年代和 2000 年代，Fanfiction.net 和 LiveJournal 上曾出现过停止侵权通知和下架争议，这最终导致了 AO3 的创立。”
