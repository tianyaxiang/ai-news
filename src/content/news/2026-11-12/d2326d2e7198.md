# Tim Peters has died

In Memoriam: Tim Peters 1952–2026 Core Development guido (Guido van Rossum) November 11, 2026, 5:36am 1 I am immensely sad to announce that Tim Peters has died earlier this year on July 15. It’s hard to overestimate Tim’s influence on Python. He’s probably best known for writing the Zen of Python (PEP 20), but to me he was a mentor and language co-designer long before that.

纪念：Tim Peters 1952–2026 核心开发 guido (Guido van Rossum) 2026年11月11日，凌晨5:36。我怀着无比悲痛的心情宣布，Tim Peters已于今年7月15日去世。很难高估Tim对Python的影响。他最出名的可能就是编写了《Python之禅》（PEP 20），但对我而言，他早在那个时候之前就是我的导师和语言共同设计者。

Tim found Python not long after its initial release. His first deed was to convince me that Python should support mixed arithmetic between int and float, because most users would expect them to work that way. And he never stopped contributing.

Tim在Python最初发布后不久就发现了它。他的第一项贡献是说服我，Python应该支持整数（int）和浮点数（float）之间的混合运算，因为大多数用户都期望它们能以这种方式工作。而且他从未停止过贡献。

Tim quickly became a major phenomenon on the Python mailing list (including later incarnations and spinoffs), where he earned the nickname “Timbot” by his innumerable, careful and patient answers to user questions. These he filled with mild humor (always marked by <wink> or <0.5 wink>) and often referred to as “channeling Guido”.

Tim很快成为了Python邮件列表（包括后来的版本和衍生列表）中的一个重要人物，他因无数次细心且耐心地回答用户问题而赢得了“Timbot”的绰号。他在回答中充满了温和的幽默感（总是以<wink>或<0.5 wink>标记），并经常称之为“传达Guido的思想”。

At the same time Tim participated in numerous in-depth design discussions, saving me or others from embarrassing design mistakes or solving deep problems, guided by his vast mathematical knowledge and compiler-building experience. In fact, Tim really set the tone for early core development, never outright dismissing people and explaining in detail why things could or couldn’t work.

与此同时，Tim参与了无数深入的设计讨论，凭借他渊博的数学知识和编译器构建经验，使我或其他人在设计中免于犯下尴尬的错误，或解决了深层次的问题。事实上，Tim确实为早期的核心开发定下了基调，他从不直接否定他人，而是详细解释事情为什么可行或不可行。

In 2002, Tim gave us Timsort: a new sorting algorithm he designed for Python lists, which was later adopted by Java and Android. mcdonc (Chris McDonough) 2026年11月11日，凌晨6:41。我很久以前写过关于Tim的内容：“我在21世纪初与Tim以及Python实验室的其他成员一起工作了几年。在我的整个Python职业生涯中，Tim对我产生了巨大的积极影响。在我与Tim交谈之前，他已经是传奇人物了。他曾在Cray Research为超级计算机编写编译器，也曾在Dragon Systems工作，在那个语音转文字任务被认为几乎不可能的年代，他编写了相关的软件。”

He took up Python on a lark in its very early days, becoming something like Python’s second real user after Guido. Guido came to rely on Tim heavily. Tim will demur about this, but as far as I can tell, Tim and Guido were largely partners in the design evolution of very early Python, and before most important features made it in to Python, you could be sure that they had been examined from every angle, shaken, stirred, and thunk about very hard by both of them.

“他在Python早期阶段出于好奇开始使用它，成为了继Guido之后Python的第二位真正用户。Guido非常依赖Tim。Tim对此会表示谦虚，但据我所知，Tim和Guido在早期Python的设计演进中很大程度上是合作伙伴。在大多数重要功能进入Python之前，你可以确信它们都经过了双方从各个角度的审视、推敲、搅拌和深思熟虑。”

Tim often played the role of gatekeeper and polisher. You have Tim to thank at least partially if you were a refugee from Java or Microsoft languages, which made excuses for their sprawling verbosity, complexity, and poor readability. Python, at the time, made none of those and had none of that (it now does). Tim was crucial in ensuring it stayed that way during his most active years.

“Tim经常扮演守门人和润色者的角色。如果你是从Java或微软语言转过来的，你应该至少部分地感谢Tim，因为那些语言总是为它们冗长的啰嗦、复杂性和糟糕的可读性找借口。当时的Python没有这些问题（现在有了）。在Tim最活跃的几年里，他对于确保Python保持那种状态至关重要。”

He will claim differently, but, by my observation, it is no accident that Python ballooned in complexity after his exit as day-to-day core developer. Perhaps this was more due to the resignation of Guido as BDFL and the new Steering Council model, which was also coincident, but not having Tim around didn’t help either.

“他会声称并非如此，但根据我的观察，在他退出日常核心开发后，Python的复杂性激增并非偶然。也许这更多是由于Guido辞去BDFL职务以及新的指导委员会模式的出现，这些事件恰好重合，但Tim不在身边确实没有起到帮助作用。”

It was pure joy to read Tim’s Usenet and mail list posts, which were full of humor and good nature and measured positivity and not a little winking darkness. They somehow resonated with me on my journey from becoming a new programmer to a not-so-new programmer and they lit a path to becoming one on which you could be dramatically competent without becoming cryptic and insufferable.

“阅读Tim在Usenet和邮件列表上的帖子是一种纯粹的快乐，它们充满了幽默、善良、适度的积极性，以及一点点眨眼式的黑暗。在我从一名新程序员成长为资深程序员的过程中，它们以某种方式与我产生了共鸣，并照亮了一条道路，让你可以在不变得晦涩难懂和令人难以忍受的情况下，变得极其出色。”

He was intensely knowledgeable, and unfailingly precise when arguing for or against some design change. But he was always gentle and patient and matter-of-fact, preferring to use humor and warmth rather than bile and authority as the currency in which he dealt. Tim penned “the Zen of Python” (“beautiful is better than ugly…”) which can be seen via “import this” to this day.

“他知识渊博，在支持或反对某种设计变更时总是极其精确。但他总是温和、耐心且实事求是，更喜欢用幽默和温暖，而不是尖刻和权威来作为他交流的方式。Tim撰写了《Python之禅》（‘优美胜于丑陋……’），至今仍可通过‘import this’看到。”

You can see a bit of his wonderful humor and style in the prelude to his introduction of the poem. His enthusiasm was infectious, and in the days when he was most involved, most everyone could be witnessed spouting Tim-isms (winks and “'lys” and whatnot). When I did get the chance to work with Tim, he was just as influential. He had absolutely no pretensions.

“你可以在他介绍这首诗的序言中看到他美妙的幽默感和风格。他的热情具有感染力，在他参与最深入的日子里，几乎每个人都能看到在引用‘Tim式语言’（眨眼和‘-ly’后缀等等）。当我真的有机会与Tim共事时，他同样具有影响力。他完全没有架子。”

If I hadn’t already known him to be a computer savant，I would have assumed he was a biker or mechanic due to his long grey locks, full mustache and working-class fashion sense. I would later find out that he had indeed worked at Harley-Davidson. I think he’s originally from Wisconsin, but an East- or West-Coaster not fluent in Midwest tongues could be excused for believing his disarming accent is Chicagoan.

“如果我之前不知道他是一位计算机天才，我可能会因为他灰色的长发、浓密的胡须和工人阶级的着装风格而认为他是一名摩托车手或机械师。后来我发现他确实曾在哈雷戴维森工作过。我想他最初来自威斯康星州，但对于不熟悉中西部口音的东海岸或西海岸人来说，误以为他那平易近人的口音是芝加哥口音是可以原谅的。”

He seemed just like a regular-looking neighborhood punch-the-clock guy, not unlike all the mechanics and deliverymen and cooks and dishwashers and event staff I’d worked around as a kid and in my 20s. Except he was pretty much the smartest guy in every room he entered.
