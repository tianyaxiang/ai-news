On Monday, the Supreme Court announced it would not hear an appeal of a lower court decision that blocked the government from canceling the grants of a handful of researchers whose funding had been revoked during the Trump administration’s purge of DEI-related work. While the decision applies to only seven researchers in California, the lower court had ruled that the government’s actions violated the First Amendment and so may have broad implications. The Supreme Court provided no discussion of its rejection, and no justices filed a dissent regarding the decision.

周一，最高法院宣布不会受理一项下级法院裁决的上诉，该裁决阻止政府取消少数研究人员的拨款，这些研究人员的资金在特朗普政府清理与多元化、公平和包容性（DEI）相关的工作期间被撤销。虽然该决定仅适用于加州的七名研究人员，但下级法院裁定政府的行为违反了第一修正案，因此可能具有广泛的影响。最高法院未就其驳回上诉做出任何讨论，也没有大法官对该决定提出异议。

The suit was initially filed by a large group of researchers at the University of California, Berkeley and UC San Francisco, contesting grants originally issued by many federal agencies, from the National Institutes of Health to the Department of Transportation. At issue were grants that had been terminated because the agencies decided they violated a Trump executive order blocking the use of DEI. This occurred despite the fact that the government hadn’t bothered to define DEI, and targeted grants were often identified by little more than a keyword search.

该诉讼最初由加州大学伯克利分校和加州大学旧金山分校的一大批研究人员提起，旨在对许多联邦机构（从国立卫生研究院到交通部）最初发放的拨款提出质疑。争议焦点在于那些被终止的拨款，因为相关机构认定这些拨款违反了特朗普政府禁止使用DEI的行政命令。尽管政府并未费心定义何为DEI，且目标拨款往往仅通过关键词搜索来确定，但上述情况依然发生了。

In the wake of a Supreme Court decision that held that most grant termination cases had to be heard by a different court, the case was narrowed considerably to just seven UC researchers and a handful of agencies: The Environmental Protection Agency, the National Science Foundation, and the National Endowment for the Humanities. The researchers argued that the grant cancellations were viewpoint discrimination that violated the First Amendment. A District Court agreed, issuing a preliminary injunction that would restore the grants given to these researchers.

在最高法院裁定大多数拨款终止案件必须由其他法院审理后，该案的范围大幅缩小，仅涉及七名加州大学的研究人员和少数几个机构：环境保护署、国家科学基金会和国家人文基金会。研究人员辩称，取消拨款属于违反第一修正案的观点歧视。地方法院表示同意，并发布了初步禁令，要求恢复给予这些研究人员的拨款。

That decision was upheld by a Court of Appeals panel. It ruled that, while the government has the option of eliminating grant programs that it determined were no longer appropriate, canceling individual grants that it disagreed with is an attempt at “suppression of dangerous ideas.” In support of this, it cited Supreme Court precedent regarding religious viewpoint discrimination. The court ruled that DEI fits in this framework because it “convey[s] the viewpoint that the exclusion of historically disadvantaged groups is undesirable.”

该裁决得到了上诉法院小组的支持。法院裁定，虽然政府有权取消其认为不再合适的拨款项目，但取消其不同意的个人拨款是对“危险思想的压制”。为了支持这一观点，法院引用了最高法院关于宗教观点歧视的先例。法院裁定，DEI符合这一框架，因为它“传达了排斥历史上处于弱势的群体是不可取的观点”。

As a result, “the government has not merely exercised its power to choose the programs it funds but has instead terminated individual grants in existing programs based on the perceived viewpoints of individual speakers.” Assuming this case continues, the Supreme Court may find other chances to intervene. But the fact that it hasn’t done so now is striking, given that it could easily have objected to everything from the venue to the decision that the government’s actions constituted viewpoint discrimination.

因此，“政府不仅行使了选择资助项目的权力，反而根据个人发言者被认为持有的观点，终止了现有项目中的个人拨款。”假设此案继续审理，最高法院未来可能还会找到干预的机会。但鉴于最高法院本可以轻易地对从审判地点到政府行为构成观点歧视的裁决提出异议，它目前没有采取行动的事实令人瞩目。

And its lack of intervention may be interpreted by other courts as a sign that the government’s anti-DEI efforts are on shaky legal ground.

其不干预的态度可能会被其他法院解读为政府的反DEI举措在法律基础上站不住脚的信号。
