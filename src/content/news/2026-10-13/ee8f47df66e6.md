# Writing a Demoscene Intro for the ZX Spectrum

Download Now View on pouet.net Intro In the late 80s, I spent an embarrassing number of hours hunched over my ZX Spectrum, typing in Sinclair BASIC programs and rudimentary assembler routines copied from magazines, trying to re-create the Amiga crack intros I’d seen round at a friend’s house. Obviously, they never came even slightly close - but now, many decades later, I’ve finally managed it! I’m now the proud author of a hand-rolled retro demoscene intro that runs on a stock 128Kb Spectrum. It features all those late 80s tropes: A smooth scrolltext (with ‘leet custom fonts!), a three-channel AY soundtrack, bouncing VU meters and the essential parallax starfield. It’s currently 10287 bytes of code, or 19552 bytes as a complete tape image - which, given I’ve been working on this since December 13th last year, means my average rate of code-production is a paltry ~ 455 bytes per week! This blog post is a technical overview of how I got there, some cool tricks I picked up, and everything I had to learn (and re-learn) about the Speccy and Z80 assembly along the way. It simultaneously rates as one of my favourite side-projects ever, and also the one that most often nearly triggered a foot put through my monitor in frustration. I’ll reflect on all that at the end, but for now if you just want to see the finished product, the download button is at the top of the article and the included .tap cassette tape image should load into any emulator. There’s also a YouTube capture, links to the production on pouet.net, and you can run it from within a web browser by visiting my TNFS site portal - check option 4 for recent uploads.

Be Kind, Rewind But let’s skip back briefly to the late 80s. Coming from the world of an 8-bit, cassette tape loading computer, seeing an Amiga for the first time was like encountering something from outer space. Like everyone who saw one back in the day, I was blown away by the amazing graphics and sound on display, not to mention the library of ground-breaking games. But I found myself even more captivated by the mysterious (and totally badass) “intros” displayed at the beginning of pirated games. Created by the software piracy groups that had cracked the copy protection and distributed the floppy-disk based games, they were mini works of art in their own right - music, graphics and usually some cool-looking effect to show off the group’s prowess. There’s a great collection of these intros (or “cracktros”) archived on YouTube. Watch a few of those videos and you’ll quickly notice the “design” tropes that almost every cracktro shares: a cool, edgy logo at the top of the screen (bonus points if it’s dripping blood or in any way “metal”), some boastful text displayed on top of an effect of some sort - usually a scrolling parallax starfield, or rotating 3d object - and a further scroll-text full of shout outs, greetings to fellow pseudonymous crackers and adverts for illegal BBS systems galore. Needless to say, to a kid in the 80/early 90s this was possibly the coolest thing ever.

倒带重播。但让我们简要回到80年代末。作为一个来自8位、使用磁带加载程序的计算机世界的人，第一次见到Amiga就像遇到了外星科技。和当时所有见到它的人一样，我被它展示的惊人图形和声音所震撼，更不用说那些开创性的游戏库了。但我发现自己更被盗版游戏开头显示的神秘（且极其酷炫）的“开场动画”所吸引。这些动画由破解了防拷贝保护并分发软盘游戏的软件盗版组织制作，它们本身就是微型的艺术品——包含音乐、图形，通常还有一些展示组织实力的酷炫特效。YouTube上存档了大量此类开场动画（或称“cracktros”）。观看其中几个视频，你很快就会注意到几乎每个cracktro都共有的“设计”套路：屏幕顶部有一个酷炫、前卫的Logo（如果带有滴血效果或任何“金属”风格则加分），在某种特效之上显示一些吹嘘的文字——通常是滚动的视差星空或旋转的3D物体——以及一段充满致敬、对其他匿名破解者的问候以及大量非法BBS系统广告的滚动文字。不用说，对于80年代或90年代初的孩子来说，这可能是最酷的事情了。

The demoscene grew out of these intros. The cracking eventually faded into the background, and coders, “graphicians” and musicians all contributed to feature-length productions that showcased their abilities, competed with other groups and pushed the machine to its limits. In amazing feats of ingenuity and pure “code as an art form”, the scene lives on today, with groups producing breathtaking productions like this and this - bear in mind, both of those are running on a 7Mhz home computer equipped with a floppy disk, 1Mb of memory and a sound chip from 1985. But much as I admire the sheer skill and dedication that takes, it’s the early “single screen” scrolly-text-and-starfield cracktros I’ve always found captivating. They took on a sort of mystical quality for me, hinting at a scene and a culture I could only ever experience from the outside - which is exactly why I spent all those hours in Sinclair BASIC, trying in vain to re-create them, and dreaming of the day I’d eventually have an Amiga of my own.

演示场景（Demoscene）正是从这些开场动画中发展而来的。破解行为最终退居幕后，程序员、“图形设计师”和音乐家们共同贡献了长篇作品，展示他们的能力，与其他组织竞争，并将机器推向极限。凭借惊人的独创性和纯粹的“代码作为艺术形式”，这一场景延续至今，各个组织制作出如这般和那般令人叹为观止的作品——请记住，这两者都是在一台配备软盘驱动器、1Mb内存和1985年产音效芯片的7Mhz家用电脑上运行的。尽管我非常钦佩这背后所需的纯粹技能和奉献精神，但我一直觉得早期的“单屏”滚动文字加星空效果的cracktros最令人着迷。它们对我来说带有一种神秘感，暗示着一个我只能从外部体验的场景和文化——这正是为什么我花费了那么多时间在Sinclair BASIC上，徒劳地试图重现它们，并梦想着有一天能拥有属于自己的Amiga。
