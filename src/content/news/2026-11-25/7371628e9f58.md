What follows is the text of the talk I gave at Linux Day 2026 in Modena, lightly adapted for the blog. The talk was in Italian, in front of a Linux audience - which explains a few of the jokes. Linux is one of the best-known players in the open source world, but it's not the only possible path. A journey through the BSDs, illumos and other alternative operating systems, to reflect on Open Source as an idea, a philosophy and a practice of sharing knowledge.

以下是我在摩德纳 Linux Day 2026 上演讲的文字稿，并针对博客进行了轻微改编。演讲是用意大利语进行的，听众是 Linux 用户，这也解释了其中的一些笑话。Linux 是开源世界中最知名的参与者之一，但它并非唯一的路径。通过探索 BSD、illumos 和其他替代操作系统，我们可以将开源视为一种理念、一种哲学以及一种分享知识的实践。

My name is Stefano Marinelli. I'm a sysadmin and consultant, and I run my own small company specialised in open source services and solutions. I like to call myself the "Barista" of the BSD Cafe and the illumos Cafe, online communities of people passionate about technology, open source and sharing. Today I'm not here just to talk about operating systems, but about freedom, sharing and knowledge. From free software to open source.

我叫 Stefano Marinelli。我是一名系统管理员和顾问，经营着一家专门从事开源服务和解决方案的小公司。我喜欢称自己为 BSD Cafe 和 illumos Cafe 的“咖啡师”，这两个在线社区汇聚了对技术、开源和分享充满热情的人们。今天我在这里不仅是为了谈论操作系统，更是为了谈论自由、分享和知识。从自由软件到开源。

My first contact with the Free Software world was in 1996, when I wasn't even 17 yet. I saw a box set advertised in a magazine: "a whole operating system, for free", at a very low price. Something like 30,000 lire. Shortly before, I'd paid roughly ten times as much for the Windows 95 upgrade edition. Dismissive and without a shred of trust, I decided to order it and give it a go. I mean: who would ever write something like that... and release the source code? But six CDs for 30,000 lire... let's try. Worst case, it'll be an experience.

我第一次接触自由软件世界是在 1996 年，当时我还没满 17 岁。我在一本杂志上看到一套盒装软件的广告：“一个完整的操作系统，免费”，价格非常低，大约 30,000 里拉。在此之前不久，我为 Windows 95 升级版支付了大约十倍的价格。虽然不屑一顾且毫无信任感，但我还是决定订购并试一试。我的意思是：谁会写出这样的东西……还发布源代码呢？但 30,000 里拉买六张 CD……试试看吧。最坏的情况，就当是一次经历。

It was love at first shell. That terminal opened up a whole universe, and to what I already knew (commercial licences, freeware, shareware) I added the concept of Free Software. But I didn't have enough hardware to run two operating systems, so I reinstalled Windows and put it all off. Still, I started thinking. Reflecting. Studying. In 1997 I got a new PC and started using Linux - or, as I was careful to say, GNU/Linux - for everything. Except games. For those, a small Windows partition survived, though not for long.

这是一见钟情的命令行体验。那个终端打开了一个全新的宇宙，在我已知的商业许可、免费软件和共享软件概念之外，我增加了自由软件的概念。但我没有足够的硬件来运行两个操作系统，所以我重新安装了 Windows，暂时搁置了这一切。尽管如此，我开始思考、反思和学习。1997 年，我买了一台新电脑，开始用 Linux（或者像我谨慎称呼的那样，GNU/Linux）处理所有事情。除了游戏。为了游戏，我保留了一个小的 Windows 分区，但没过多久也放弃了。

In 1998 I enrolled in Computer Science at the University of Bologna. Even back then, it was a faculty strongly oriented towards free software. Linux in the labs. I was one of the few who, in the first year, already knew what Free Software was (not free as in price, but...freedom!) and how to use the Linux workstations. That gave me a big advantage, especially from the second year onwards. Renzo Davoli was already pushing hard in that direction. I decided that would be my path. And so it was, and I started pushing too. Windows wasn't the "enemy" to me. The imposition was. Personally, I respect rules, but not impositions. Some people followed me, others watched from a distance. I didn't care. I was widening my horizons. In the early 2000s I also discovered the BSDs. And I convinced my parents that I needed a laser printer for "university material". In reality, I wanted to print the excellent BSD documentation. Maybe not university material, but the printed FreeBSD Handbook probably served me better than half of the theory exams I took back then. Thanks, mom and dad!

1998 年，我进入博洛尼亚大学攻读计算机科学。即使在当时，该学院也非常倾向于自由软件，实验室里到处都是 Linux。我是大一新生中极少数已经知道什么是自由软件（不是价格上的免费，而是……自由！）以及如何使用 Linux 工作站的人之一。这给了我很大的优势，尤其是在大二之后。Renzo Davoli 当时已经在极力推动这一方向。我决定这就是我的道路。事实确实如此，我也开始推动它。对我来说，Windows 并不是“敌人”，强加于人的做法才是。我尊重规则，但不尊重强加的命令。有些人跟随我，有些人则在远处观望。我不在乎。我正在拓宽我的视野。21 世纪初，我发现了 BSD。我甚至说服父母，我需要一台激光打印机来打印“大学资料”。实际上，我是想打印出色的 BSD 文档。虽然可能不是大学教材，但打印出来的《FreeBSD 手册》可能比我当时参加的一半理论考试对我更有帮助。谢谢，爸爸妈妈！

But let's get back to the beginning. Free Software was born out of a change. In those days, code circulated freely among people. Depending on their needs, they exchanged source code, modified it and shared it again. But at some point this began to change. There's an anecdote that, while not exhaustive, gives the idea: the printer Richard Stallman was using kept jamming, and nobody got notified. He wanted to modify the software so that he'd get a notification, as he'd done with the previous printer. Back then it was common practice, but this time he was refused. "I can't share the source code. It's under NDA." In other words, he had lost control over what he was using. He even wanted to improve the program, and instead he got a refusal.

但让我们回到最初。自由软件诞生于一种变革。在那个年代，代码在人们之间自由流通。根据需求，他们交换源代码、修改并再次分享。但在某个时刻，情况开始改变。有一个轶事虽然不能概括全部，但能说明问题：理查德·斯托曼（Richard Stallman）使用的打印机总是卡纸，却没有任何人收到通知。他想修改软件以便能收到通知，就像他之前在另一台打印机上做的那样。在当时这是一种常见的做法，但这一次他被拒绝了。“我不能分享源代码。它受保密协议（NDA）约束。”换句话说，他失去了对自己所用工具的控制权。他甚至想改进程序，结果却遭到了拒绝。

The rest is history. At least until a few years ago. Because thirty years ago we fought for Free Software to be taken seriously. When I started working, I was almost laughed at. "That's toy stuff you use at university. The real world uses something else." "Why don't you just use Windows? Everything's easier." And yet, the toys often worked better than the expensive proprietary solutions. Or at least just as well, at a fraction of the cost. "Let's try. Don't pay me until you're satisfied." They smiled, incredulous. Then they paid, happily. We could prove there was an alternative. Free, with no artificial locks. Something that anyone - even the broke but eager student - could study and set up. Technology, that kind of "enterprise" technology, was truly within everyone's reach. There was no need to buy expensive products, or recurring certifications just to have the "right" to work on them. You study, you apply yourself, nobody can stop you. I attended Linux Days. I spoke at Linux Days. For example, here I am, in Linux Day 2004.

剩下的就是历史了。至少在几年前是这样。因为三十年前，我们为让自由软件受到重视而奋斗。当我刚开始工作时，我几乎被嘲笑。“那是你在大学里玩的玩具。现实世界用的是别的东西。”“你为什么不用 Windows？一切都更简单。”然而，这些“玩具”往往比昂贵的专有解决方案工作得更好。或者至少效果相当，但成本却只有一小部分。“试试看吧。不满意不用付钱。”他们笑着，半信半疑。然后，他们愉快地付了钱。我们证明了存在一种替代方案。它是自由的，没有人为的锁定。任何人——即使是身无分文但渴望学习的学生——都可以研究和设置它。技术，那种“企业级”技术，真正触手可及。不需要购买昂贵的产品，也不需要为了获得工作“权利”而不断考取认证。你学习，你努力，没有人能阻止你。我参加过 Linux Day，也在 Linux Day 上发过言。例如，这是我在 2004 年 Linux Day 上的照片。

We won that battle. We won that war. Linux - chaotic Linux, built by countless external contributors, made of different and varied distributions - has reached everywhere. Open Source is everywhere. But something happened - something beautiful, but terrible: precisely because it reached everywhere, something changed in the spirit. "Too big to fail". And so the big players arrived. Today, Linux development is driven by corporate money: companies like Microsoft (the same Microsoft that once considered Linux its sworn enemy - or Red Hat itself) and other big tech players. In itself, this has been a good thing. In practice, though, it means that they steer its development, even if indirectly. Year after year, it has started to take the shape of a different kind of product. Is the user still at the centre, or is it more code to make Azure run better? Sure, it's Open Source, it's free. But it's increasingly close to the needs of the big companies funding its development and, at times, further from the ordinary user. That's not necessarily bad: many of these investments have given us extraordinary technologies. But I wonder whether, in the process, we're losing something.

我们赢得了那场战斗。我们赢得了那场战争。Linux——混乱的 Linux，由无数外部贡献者构建，由各种各样的发行版组成——已经无处不在。开源无处不在。但发生了一件事——既美好又可怕：正是因为它无处不在，其精神内核发生了变化。“大而不能倒”。于是，大玩家们入场了。今天，Linux 的开发由企业资金驱动：像微软（曾经将 Linux 视为死敌的微软，或者红帽本身）以及其他大型科技公司。从本质上讲，这是一件好事。但在实践中，这意味着他们正在引导其发展，即使是间接的。年复一年，它开始呈现出一种不同产品的形态。用户仍然是核心吗？还是说更多是为了让 Azure 运行得更好而编写的代码？当然，它是开源的，是免费的。但它越来越贴近资助其开发的大公司的需求，有时却离普通用户越来越远。这未必是坏事：许多投资为我们带来了非凡的技术。但我怀疑，在这个过程中，我们是否正在失去什么。
