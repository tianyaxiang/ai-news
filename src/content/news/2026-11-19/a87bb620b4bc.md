This smell startup says its AI nose is as good as a human’s. In a New Jersey lab, researchers are trying to sniff every odor on Earth. Their careful notes—“nutty,” “peachy,” “marine”—are cross-referenced with chemical analyses to train what AI startup Osmo calls the world’s first foundation model for smell. Its goal, says founder Alex Wiltschko, is “to give computers a sense of smell.” Wiltschko claims the model has already achieved “human-level olfactory intelligence” and is helping fragrance brands design new scents. But Osmo has bigger ambitions, from detecting counterfeit goods to diagnosing diseases. Not everyone is convinced its technology can deliver. How far could an AI-powered sense of smell really take us? —Amit Katwala

这家气味初创公司称其人工智能鼻子与人类一样灵敏。在新泽西州的一间实验室里，研究人员正试图嗅出地球上的每一种气味。他们仔细记录的“坚果味”、“桃子味”、“海洋味”等笔记，与化学分析进行交叉比对，以训练人工智能初创公司 Osmo 所称的全球首个气味基础模型。创始人亚历克斯·威尔奇科（Alex Wiltschko）表示，其目标是“赋予计算机嗅觉”。威尔奇科声称，该模型已经达到了“人类水平的嗅觉智能”，并正在帮助香水品牌设计新气味。但 Osmo 有着更宏大的抱负，从检测假冒商品到诊断疾病。并非所有人都相信其技术能够实现这些目标。人工智能驱动的嗅觉究竟能带我们走多远？——阿米特·卡特瓦拉（Amit Katwala）

Hydropower hit record lows in the US Southwest this summer. The US Southwest generated 28% less electricity via hydropower between April and August than the 10-year average, according to new data from the US Energy Information Administration. Most hydropower plants in the US rely on dams to hold back water, creating reservoirs. But an extreme, prolonged drought has left water levels dangerously low in the region's two largest reservoirs—and climate change is adding to the pressure. Find out how climate change is threatening a vital source of renewable power. —Casey Crownhart

今年夏天，美国西南部的水电发电量创下历史新低。根据美国能源信息署的最新数据，今年 4 月至 8 月期间，美国西南部的水电发电量比 10 年平均水平低 28%。美国大多数水电站依靠大坝蓄水形成水库。然而，极端且持续的干旱导致该地区两个最大水库的水位降至危险的低点，而气候变化正加剧这一压力。了解气候变化如何威胁这一重要的可再生能源。——凯西·克朗哈特（Casey Crownhart）

MIT Technology Review Narrated: How solid-state transformers could help power the AI boom. Transformers are a crucial but nearly invisible part of the US grid, helping move power efficiently across long distances. But they’re complicated and in short supply, and their basic design hasn’t changed much in about a century. As electricity demand rises from EVs, data centers, and more, plans are emerging to modernize these grid staples. Solid-state versions could help ease supply concerns and cut power losses. The technology is still in development, but it could become a crucial part of our future grid.
