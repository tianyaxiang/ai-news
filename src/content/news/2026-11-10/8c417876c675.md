As voters went to the polls across the US last week, I was in Ashburn, Virginia, part of what’s known as Data Center Alley—the world’s largest data center hub. It’s also where the backlash now sweeping the country arguably first began taking shape.

这篇报道最初发表在我们的 AI 每周通讯《算法》（The Algorithm）上。若想第一时间在收件箱中获取此类报道，请在此注册。上周美国各地选民前往投票站时，我身处弗吉尼亚州的阿什本，这里被称为“数据中心巷”（Data Center Alley），是全球最大的数据中心枢纽。如今席卷全国的抵制浪潮，可以说正是从这里开始成形的。

Driving through it is surreal. Concrete monolithic buildings loom along every major road, with names like Skybox and CloudHQ. It’s accepted wisdom in Big Tech that the war against data centers must be about something bigger than just data centers themselves. Two days after the midterms, OpenAI’s chief global affairs officer, Chris Lehane, wrote that data centers “have become a proxy for public unease about affordability, corporate power, and people losing control over their futures.” It’s a culture war, in other words.

驱车穿行其中，感觉十分超现实。巨大的混凝土建筑矗立在每条主干道旁，挂着 Skybox 和 CloudHQ 等招牌。大型科技公司普遍认为，针对数据中心的战争必然涉及比数据中心本身更宏大的议题。中期选举两天后，OpenAI 全球事务主管克里斯·勒汉（Chris Lehane）写道，数据中心“已成为公众对负担能力、企业权力以及人们失去对未来掌控感等不安情绪的代名词”。换句话说，这是一场文化战争。

But spending a few days in Data Center Alley left me wondering if it’s really the proxy that Lehane describes. I came away convinced that the data center backlash is more about electricity than Big Tech admits. The industry can improve its image, but it can’t escape the physical consequences of its enormous appetite for electricity. Here’s why.

但在“数据中心巷”待了几天后，我不禁怀疑它是否真的如勒汉所描述的那样只是一个代名词。我最终确信，针对数据中心的抵制更多是关于电力问题，而非大型科技公司所承认的那样。该行业可以改善其形象，但无法逃避其对电力巨大需求所带来的物理后果。原因如下。

There are two ways to power the sort of data center that AI companies want: connecting to the grid, or generating electricity onsite. In Virginia’s Loudoun County, where data centers are ubiquitous but have been built at a steadier pace than the breakneck expansion now underway elsewhere in the country, most are connected to the existing grid. One facility I went inside, operated by Equinix, has massive diesel generators onsite for backup but gets its power from the local utility, Dominion Energy. Much of Dominion’s electricity is generated by natural gas (which is cleaner than coal or oil, but worse than renewables).

AI 公司所需的数据中心有两种供电方式：接入电网或现场发电。在弗吉尼亚州的劳登县，数据中心随处可见，但其建设速度比目前全国其他地区疯狂的扩张要平稳得多，大多数数据中心都接入了现有电网。我参观的一家由 Equinix 运营的设施，现场配备了大型柴油发电机作为备用，但其电力来自当地公用事业公司 Dominion Energy。Dominion 的大部分电力由天然气产生（这比煤炭或石油更清洁，但不如可再生能源）。

The average data center operating in this part of Virginia today has a power capacity of 54 megawatts. That’s more than the power draw of the Denver International Airport. Elena Schlossberg, a prominent activist against data centers in Virginia, told me she’s “always banging my head against the wall” when the industry paints the fight as a culture war. “It was always about power,” she says—that is, electricity.

如今在弗吉尼亚州这一地区运营的数据中心，平均电力容量为 54 兆瓦。这比丹佛国际机场的用电量还要大。弗吉尼亚州著名的反数据中心活动人士埃琳娜·施洛斯伯格（Elena Schlossberg）告诉我，每当业界将这场斗争描述为文化战争时，她都感到“极其沮丧”。她说：“这始终是关于电力的问题。”

Some Virginia protesters appear more animated by a broader anti-tech populism than Schlossberg, to be fair. But it’s the particular consequences of drawing so much power from the grid that have done the most to shape how the backlash has played out. Recent protests have led to new rules from the state that data centers must pay fully for the cost of new transmission lines to the grid and make sure they’re not spread among everyone else. Data center developers told me they’re preparing for new rules that would cut them off from grid power during peak demand.

平心而论，一些弗吉尼亚州的抗议者似乎比施洛斯伯格更受广泛的反科技民粹主义情绪驱动。但正是从电网抽取大量电力所带来的具体后果，在很大程度上塑造了抵制浪潮的发展方式。近期的抗议活动促使州政府出台了新规：数据中心必须全额支付接入电网的新输电线路成本，并确保这些成本不会分摊给其他用户。数据中心开发商告诉我，他们正在为新规做准备，这些规定可能会在高峰需求期间切断他们的电网供电。

Schlossberg also worries about the downstream effects of a strained grid—for example, that data centers will run more diesel generators or that Dominion Energy will expand fossil fuel inputs rather than renewables to meet demand. The overwhelming majority of AI data centers being built elsewhere in the country are not designed this way. The scale desired is so massive that companies are setting up in rural areas where there is virtually no grid infrastructure to connect to.

施洛斯伯格还担心电网紧张带来的连锁反应——例如，数据中心会运行更多的柴油发电机，或者 Dominion Energy 为了满足需求而增加化石燃料投入，而非转向可再生能源。全国其他地区正在建设的绝大多数 AI 数据中心并非如此设计。其所需的规模如此巨大，以至于公司纷纷在几乎没有电网基础设施可供连接的农村地区选址。

Building there means bringing your own power (so-called “behind the meter” power), in the form of natural-gas-powered turbines. Companies also build diesel generators for backup. Anthropic, for example, has reportedly agreed to pay $3.4 billion for gas turbines in what’s described as the largest power generation purchase by an AI company. Even if grids were nearby, they’d be unlikely to serve the amount of power demanded.

在这些地方建设意味着要自带电力（即所谓的“表后”电力），形式通常是天然气涡轮机。公司还会建造柴油发电机作为备用。据报道，Anthropic 已同意斥资 34 亿美元购买燃气轮机，这被描述为 AI 公司有史以来最大规模的发电设施采购。即使附近有电网，它们也很难满足如此巨大的电力需求。

The smallest data centers attractive for AI training are 300 megawatts. It’s a lot of power for one industrial site, but not unprecedented: One data center developer told me he’s eyeing a former steel mill already equipped to draw that much electricity from the grid. But the ideal facilities are more like 6,000 megawatts. That’s the estimated draw of the city of Chicago, which is powered by more than 20 power plants within the city alone (and dozens farther out).

适合 AI 训练的最小型数据中心也需要 300 兆瓦的电力。对于一个工业场所来说，这确实是巨大的电力需求，但并非史无前例：一位数据中心开发商告诉我，他看中了一家前钢铁厂，该厂已经具备从电网抽取如此多电力的设施。但理想的设施规模更接近 6,000 兆瓦。这相当于芝加哥市的估计用电量，而芝加哥仅市内就有 20 多座发电厂（市外还有数十座）为其供电。

The notion of data center companies’ bringing their own power might seem attractive at first, because they wouldn’t be straining the grid. But generating electricity at this scale inevitably affects surrounding communities, and when public utilities build infrastructure of this magnitude, communities have traditionally expected a say in where it goes. Now, private companies are undertaking projects on a similar scale. Natural-gas turbines of any scale release air pollutants. When diesel generators are used, it’s even worse.

数据中心公司自带电力的想法起初似乎很有吸引力，因为它们不会给电网造成压力。但如此规模的发电必然会影响周边社区，而当公用事业公司建设这种规模的基础设施时，社区通常期望对选址拥有发言权。现在，私营公司正在进行类似规模的项目。任何规模的天然气涡轮机都会排放空气污染物。如果使用柴油发电机，情况则更糟。

And in the unprecedented rush to get these facilities operating, companies have become horrible neighbors (recall that Elon Musk violated air quality laws when he set up dozens of turbines to power a data center in Memphis, according to the Environmental Protection Agency). Big Tech’s tendency to chalk the backlash up to a culture war obfuscates one simple fact: There’s only so pleasant you can make a small city of computers to live around.

在争分夺秒让这些设施投入运营的过程中，公司成了糟糕的邻居（回想一下，根据美国环境保护署的说法，埃隆·马斯克在孟菲斯为数据中心供电而安装数十台涡轮机时，就违反了空气质量法规）。大型科技公司倾向于将抵制浪潮归咎于文化战争，这掩盖了一个简单的事实：在一个由计算机组成的小城市周围生活，其舒适度终究是有限的。

Sure, companies can pursue better deals that raise tax revenues, hire local workers, and get more community involvement, as the protests are leading them to do. But if a small town is asked to welcome a Walt Disney World, an international airport, a steel plant, and the power plants to run them all, should we be surprised by its trepidation? Again, that’s not to say this is the sole reason for opposition to data centers. Fears of job losses, existential risks, and concentrated corporate power all fuel the backlash, as does misinformation (most facilities use far less water than many people think).

当然，正如抗议活动所促使的那样，公司可以寻求更好的协议，以增加税收、雇佣当地工人并获得更多的社区参与。但如果一个小镇被要求同时接纳一个迪士尼乐园、一个国际机场、一家钢铁厂以及为它们供电的发电厂，我们还会对其不安感到惊讶吗？再次强调，这并不是说这是反对数据中心的唯一原因。对失业、生存风险和企业权力集中的担忧都在助长这种抵制，错误信息也是如此（大多数设施的用水量远低于许多人的想象）。
