So, I've always been somewhat annoyed at SSL/TLS. It requires everyone to go through a complex "ACME" dance just to put up a public website. Worse, certificates expire every few months, so it's not a one and done thing: even with automation, maintaining a certificate requires care to ensure the ACME client always has access to DNS records or the web server. ... and the whole system doesn't even work because certificates are issued over plaintext connections, and trusted certificate authorities use very secure keys like "4".

First, let's look at mathematical framework of RSA. To generate a key, pick two large prime numbers (p and q) and multiply them to yield the modulus (n). Pick a small number, e as public exponent. The RSA public key is composed of the modulus (n) and the public exponent.

Ok, back to vanity keys. The standard value for e is 65537 (0x10001), because the small number of set bits makes encryption faster. In fact, the CA I used will refuse to sign keys with a non-standard public exponent, so I can't do anything funny there. The modulus, n, is either 2,048 or 4,096 "user controlled" bits, so it's the ideal place to hide a message.

好了，回到虚荣密钥上来。e 的标准值是 65537 (0x10001)，因为设置的位数较少使得加密速度更快。事实上，我使用的证书颁发机构会拒绝签署非标准公钥指数的密钥，所以我无法在那里搞什么花样。模数 n 有 2048 或 4096 位“用户可控”空间，因此它是隐藏信息的理想场所。

However, to use the certificate, I'll need to find two large primes that multiply out to the vanity modulus. My method starts by picking two random primes and calculating the resulting n value. Since p and q are similar in size, a small change q only affects the least-significant half of the product. As long as the message is embedded into the upper half of n, it's likely to survive undamaged.
