Meet the ecologist revealing the invisible networks that revive rainforests. Tropical forests are our planet’s biggest terrestrial carbon sink, accounting for around 40% of carbon stored on land. Forest ecologist Daisy Dent, a senior scientist at ETH Zurich, is part of an international network of researchers working to decipher the networks of animal and plant interactions that enable some forests to recover after destruction. The goal is a low-cost, high-impact alternative to typical carbon-crediting schemes, which plant huge areas with a single species of tree. For over a decade, her research has helped show that these large “monocrop” programs could be expensive mistakes—and that more resilient forests can rebound naturally in just a few decades, re-creating ecosystems that suck up vast quantities of carbon dioxide. Learn more about Dent, her work, and her impact on how we understand the role rainforests play in regulating our climate. —Matthew Ponsford
