# Title: Your Model’s MSE Is Lying to You
# 标题：你的模型 MSE 正在欺骗你

Two models, one number, opposite realities. Imagine you have a sensor recording something you care about, for example seismic background at a detector site, electrical load on a grid, or strain in a bridge cable, and you've trained a model to forecast the next value. The model looks at the recent history, thinks for a moment, and gives you a single number: 0.5.
两个模型，一个数值，截然不同的现实。想象一下，你有一个传感器在记录你关心的事物，例如探测站的地震背景、电网的电力负荷或桥梁缆索的应变，并且你已经训练了一个模型来预测下一个数值。模型查看了近期历史，思考片刻后，给出了一个单一的数字：0.5。

There's a threshold $\tau = 1.0$ that fires an alarm. The question is: should you worry? You can't answer that. Not because you're missing information about the model, but because the model is missing a way to tell you what it knows. That single number 0.5 is all it can say. Let's see why that's a problem.
有一个触发警报的阈值 $\tau = 1.0$。问题是：你应该担心吗？你无法回答。这并不是因为你缺少关于模型的信息，而是因为模型缺少一种方式来告诉你它所知道的内容。那个单一的数字 0.5 是它唯一能表达的信息。让我们看看为什么这是一个问题。

Imagine you actually have two models, both watching the same signal, both predicting $\hat{x} = 0.5$ at the same timestep. They even post the same mean squared error on your test set, not approximately, but identically to three decimal places. By every standard ranking metric, they are interchangeable. Except they're not. Here's what's hiding underneath.
想象一下，你实际上有两个模型，都在观察同一个信号，并且在同一个时间步长都预测 $\hat{x} = 0.5$。它们在测试集上的均方误差（MSE）甚至相同，不是近似相同，而是精确到小数点后三位都完全一致。按照任何标准的排名指标，它们都是可以互换的。但事实并非如此。以下是隐藏在表象之下的真相。

Model A is looking at a moment where the true conditional distribution, the actual spread of values the signal could realistically take, given its recent history is very tight: $x \sim \mathcal{N}(0.5, 0.01^{2})$. If this notation is new to you: $\mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^{2})$ is a Gaussian distribution (the classic bell curve), where $\mu = 0.5$ is the center, and $\sigma = 0.01$ is the standard deviation, which controls the width. A small $\sigma$ means the values cluster tightly around the center. Here, 99.7% of the probability mass sits within $3\sigma = 0.03$ of the mean, roughly between 0.47 and 0.53. The threshold at 1.0 is 50 standard deviations away. That alarm will not fire in the lifetime of the experiment.
模型 A 观察到的时刻，其真实的条件分布（即在给定近期历史的情况下，信号实际上可能取值的范围）非常集中：$x \sim \mathcal{N}(0.5, 0.01^{2})$。如果你对这种符号感到陌生：$\mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^{2})$ 是高斯分布（经典的钟形曲线），其中 $\mu = 0.5$ 是中心，$\sigma = 0.01$ 是标准差，它控制着分布的宽度。较小的 $\sigma$ 意味着数值紧密地聚集在中心周围。在这里，99.7% 的概率质量位于均值的 $3\sigma = 0.03$ 范围内，大约在 0.47 到 0.53 之间。1.0 的阈值距离均值有 50 个标准差之远。在实验的整个生命周期内，该警报都不会触发。

Model B is looking at a moment where the true distribution is wide: $x \sim \mathcal{N}(0.5, 2.0^{2})$. Same center, but $\sigma = 2.0$. The bell curve is now extremely spread out. The threshold at 1.0 is only $\frac{\tau - \mu}{\sigma} = \frac{1.0 - 0.5}{2.0} = 0.25$ standard deviations away. That's nothing. Roughly 40% of the time, the signal will cross the alarm.
模型 B 观察到的时刻，其真实分布非常宽泛：$x \sim \mathcal{N}(0.5, 2.0^{2})$。中心相同，但 $\sigma = 2.0$。现在的钟形曲线极其分散。1.0 的阈值距离均值仅有 $\frac{\tau - \mu}{\sigma} = \frac{1.0 - 0.5}{2.0} = 0.25$ 个标准差。这几乎不算什么。大约 40% 的情况下，信号会超过警报阈值。

Same forecast. Same MSE. Same test set. But the actual risk of triggering the alarm is $\approx 0\%$ versus $\approx 40\%$. If you're deciding whether to evacuate, reroute power, or flag a detector event, those are opposite conclusions and the number you ranked both models with cannot tell you which is which.
同样的预测。同样的 MSE。同样的测试集。但触发警报的实际风险分别是 $\approx 0\%$ 和 $\approx 40\%$。如果你正在决定是否撤离、重新分配电力或标记探测器事件，这些是截然相反的结论，而你用来对这两个模型进行排名的那个数字，无法告诉你哪个是哪个。

Figure 1: Two models making the same point prediction of $\hat{x} = 0.5$ but with radically different underlying uncertainty. Model A (blue) knows the answer very precisely; Model B (orange) is essentially guessing. A deterministic loss function like MSE cannot distinguish them. Image by author.
图 1：两个模型做出了相同的点预测 $\hat{x} = 0.5$，但其潜在的不确定性截然不同。模型 A（蓝色）非常精确地知道答案；模型 B（橙色）本质上是在猜测。像 MSE 这样的确定性损失函数无法区分它们。图片由作者提供。

The problem isn't that either model is broken. Both predicted the correct mean. The problem is that a single number can't express "I'm sure" versus "I'm guessing" and the reason the model can't express that isn't a training bug or a missing feature. It's a direct, provable consequence of the loss function it was trained with.
问题不在于模型本身坏了。两者都预测出了正确的均值。问题在于，单一的数字无法表达“我很确定”与“我在猜测”之间的区别，而模型无法表达这一点的原因，既不是训练错误，也不是缺少功能。这是它所使用的损失函数直接且可证明的结果。

That's what this post unpacks. We'll see exactly why MSE hands you the mean and discards everything else, what to replace it with, and what that replacement costs once a real optimizer gets hold of it.
这就是本文要剖析的内容。我们将确切地看到为什么 MSE 只给你均值而丢弃了其他所有信息，应该用什么来替代它，以及一旦真正的优化器开始使用这种替代方案，会付出什么样的代价。

**What forecasting actually asks**
**预测的本质需求**

Let's set up the problem properly, because the assumption we're going to break is hiding in the setup itself. A time series is a sequence of numbers recorded in order over time. For instance, temperature every hour, stock price at market close each day, or displacement of a seismometer sampled at 100Hz. The key property is that the order carries information, the value at time $t$ tells you something about the value at $t+1$. Shuffle the sequence and that information is destroyed. That's what separates a time series from, say, a bag of independent measurements.
让我们正确地设定这个问题，因为我们要打破的假设就隐藏在设定本身之中。时间序列是按时间顺序记录的一系列数字。例如，每小时的温度、每天收盘时的股价，或以 100Hz 采样的地震仪位移。其关键属性是顺序携带信息，时间 $t$ 的值会告诉你关于 $t+1$ 时刻值的一些信息。打乱这个序列，信息就会被破坏。这就是时间序列与一组独立测量值之间的区别。

We write the observed sequence as: $x_{1}, x_{2}, \ldots, x_{T}$ where $x_{t}$ is the value at timestep $t$. Forecasting means predicting the future values: $x_{T+1}, x_{T+2}, \ldots, x_{T+H}$.
我们将观察到的序列写为：$x_{1}, x_{2}, \ldots, x_{T}$，其中 $x_{t}$ 是时间步 $t$ 的值。预测意味着预测未来的值：$x_{T+1}, x_{T+2}, \ldots, x_{T+H}$。

Two numbers control the setup: $T$ = context length: how far back the model looks. $H$ = forecast horizon: how far ahead the model predicts. The simplest case is $H = 1$, which means predicting only the very next value. That's where we'll focus. In practice, $H$ can be dozens or hundreds, but the argument about MSE versus NLL applies identically regardless of $H$.
两个数字控制着这个设定：$T$ = 上下文长度：模型回顾过去多远。$H$ = 预测范围：模型预测未来多远。最简单的情况是 $H = 1$，这意味着只预测下一个值。这就是我们将要关注的重点。在实践中，$H$ 可以是几十或几百，但关于 MSE 与 NLL（负对数似然）的论点无论 $H$ 为多少都同样适用。

Figure 2: The forecasting setup. The blue region is the observed context window; the red region is the forecast horizon we must predict. Open circles are the model's predictions. At this stage they are just single numbers per step, point predictions. Image by author.
图 2：预测设定。蓝色区域是观察到的上下文窗口；红色区域是我们必须预测的预测范围。空心圆圈是模型的预测。在此阶段，它们只是每个步骤的单一数字，即点预测。图片由作者提供。

Now here's the subtle part that most textbooks gloss over. When you write down your prediction as a single number $\hat{x}_{T+1}$, you've made a philosophical commitment without realizing it. You're treating the future as though it's determined by the past, as though knowing the history perfectly would tell you the next value exactly.
现在，这是大多数教科书都忽略的微妙之处。当你将预测写为一个单一数字 $\hat{x}_{T+1}$ 时，你在不知不觉中做出了一种哲学上的承诺。你将未来视为由过去决定的，仿佛完美地了解历史就能准确地告诉你下一个值。

Think about what that single number means. The model says the next value is 0.5, not probably around 0.5, not somewhere between 0.3 and 0.7, just 0.5, full stop. That format has no room for doubt. There is no field in the output for "by the way, I'm not sure about this one."
思考一下那个单一数字意味着什么。模型说下一个值是 0.5，不是“大概在 0.5 左右”，也不是“在 0.3 到 0.7 之间”，就是 0.5，句号。这种格式没有留出怀疑的空间。输出中没有一个字段可以写“顺便说一下，我不确定这个值”。

Nobody agrees to this assumption on purpose. You agree to it by picking a loss function. The loss decides what the model can and cannot express, and the standard loss, MSE, decides for you: the answer is a point, not a distribution.
没有人会故意同意这个假设。你是通过选择损失函数来同意它的。损失函数决定了模型能表达什么和不能表达什么，而标准的损失函数 MSE 为你做出了决定：答案是一个点，而不是一个分布。
