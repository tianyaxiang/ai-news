STARBASE, Texas—The black and silver ship fell out of the sky, emerging from the cloud deck near the very end of its flight. Incongruously, the rocket flew horizontal until the last moment when three Raptor rocket engines ignited and it swooped into a vertical position. Slowly, gently, haltingly, the Starship upper stage eased its way toward the waiting tower where two arms extended outward, awaiting expectantly.

德克萨斯州星际基地——那艘黑银相间的飞船从天而降，在飞行即将结束时从云层中显现出来。令人不可思议的是，火箭一直保持水平飞行，直到最后一刻，三台猛禽火箭发动机点火，它才猛地转为垂直姿态。星舰上面级缓慢、轻柔且迟疑地向等待中的发射塔靠拢，塔上的两只机械臂向外伸展，满怀期待地等待着。

The margins are so incredibly narrow. Two nubs on either side of the rocket, each protruding only about one foot, were the only purchase the rocket would have on the arms. Then, less than a minute after it came into view, the Starship upper stage was snagged by the launch tower, as snug as you like. And so it was all over. SpaceX, nearly a quarter of a century after its founding, had just taken a hugely significant step toward developing and optimizing a massive, rapidly reusable rocket.

容错空间极其狭小。火箭两侧各有两个仅突出约一英尺的凸起，这是火箭与机械臂接触的唯一着力点。随后，在它进入视野不到一分钟的时间里，星舰上面级就被发射塔稳稳地抓住了，严丝合缝。一切就这样结束了。在成立近四分之一世纪后，SpaceX 在开发和优化这种大型、可快速重复使用火箭的道路上，刚刚迈出了意义极其重大的一步。

We are dazzled This was a breathtaking feat; a brilliant technical achievement that required the best engineering minds to think through a million different, difficult problems. And on Saturday evening, following a handful of practice runs into the Indian Ocean, they did it before the entire world. Watching Starship launch from here a little less than a day earlier, and knowing it had orbited the planet multiple times, added to the sense of unreality in seeing this spaceship return to Earth under its own power, and be captured by the tower. It felt like stepping into the future.

我们被震撼了。这是一项令人叹为观止的壮举；这是一项辉煌的技术成就，需要最顶尖的工程大脑去思考数百万个不同且困难的问题。周六晚上，在经历了数次向印度洋的试飞后，他们在全世界面前完成了这一壮举。不到一天前在这里观看星舰发射，并知道它已经绕地球飞行了多次，这让亲眼目睹这艘飞船依靠自身动力返回地球并被发射塔捕获时，产生了一种超现实感。这感觉就像踏入了未来。

SpaceX has dazzled us before, by landing rockets on barges and returning humans to space with Crew Dragon. But this flight took things to another level. Starships are huge. They carry a huge amount of energy coming back from orbit. And until a few years ago, it seemed beyond fantastical to think about capturing a rocket with what are essentially outsized chopsticks. Nevertheless, it happened.

SpaceX 过去曾通过在驳船上回收火箭以及利用载人龙飞船将人类送入太空让我们感到惊叹。但这次飞行将一切提升到了一个新的高度。星舰非常巨大。它们从轨道返回时携带巨大的能量。直到几年前，用本质上是超大号筷子的装置来捕获火箭，听起来还像是天方夜谭。然而，它确实发生了。

Rapid reuse is firmly on the horizon Humans have built reusable spacecraft that flew into orbit before. In the 1970s, flush with confidence after the triumphant Moon landings, NASA designed and built several Space Shuttles. They had solid rocket boosters that were fished from the ocean and reused. The large orange external tank was not recovered after each flight, but the orbiters went to space for two weeks, or even longer, before returning to Earth and landing on a runway.

快速重复使用已指日可待。人类此前曾建造过能飞入轨道的重复使用航天器。20 世纪 70 年代，在登月成功后，充满信心的美国国家航空航天局（NASA）设计并建造了几架航天飞机。它们拥有从海洋中打捞并重复使用的固体火箭助推器。巨大的橙色外储箱在每次飞行后并未回收，但轨道器可以在太空中停留两周甚至更长时间，然后返回地球并在跑道上着陆。

The shuttles were engineering marvels. But during the return to Earth they acted as gliders, not igniting their engines. And their heat shields were delicate flowers, requiring painstaking refurbishment after each flight. At the outset of the shuttle program, the space agency contemplated sending dozens of humans into space at a time, and carrying cargo at a cost as low of $25 per pound. Reality never matched these rosy projections. The crew space capped out at seven astronauts, and the cost-per-pound was closer to $25,000.

航天飞机是工程学上的奇迹。但在返回地球的过程中，它们像滑翔机一样飞行，并没有点燃发动机。它们的隔热罩非常脆弱，每次飞行后都需要进行精细的翻新。在航天飞机计划之初，航天局曾设想一次将数十人送入太空，并将货物运输成本降低到每磅 25 美元。现实从未达到这些乐观的预测。载人上限仅为 7 名宇航员，而每磅成本更接近 25,000 美元。

Each orbiter cost an extraordinary amount of money to build; NASA only ever built five that went to space. SpaceX has five Starships in an advanced state of development at Starbase today; and its factory houses the components for a dozen more. The company can currently manufacture a Starship upper stage in its South Texas Starfactory every three weeks. The factory was sized to produce a ship every three days. When SpaceX founder Elon Musk discussed these build targets in an interview five years ago, he sounded mad. But he no longer does.

每架轨道器的制造成本都高得惊人；NASA 总共只建造了五架进入太空的航天飞机。如今，SpaceX 在星际基地有五艘处于高级开发阶段的星舰；其工厂内还存放着另外十几艘的组件。该公司目前每三周就能在其南德克萨斯州的“星际工厂”制造出一艘星舰上面级。该工厂的规模设计为每三天生产一艘。五年前，当 SpaceX 创始人埃隆·马斯克在一次采访中谈到这些制造目标时，他听起来像个疯子。但现在，他不再是了。

A proof of concept The Space Shuttle was nearly a fully reusable space launch system, but it fell far short of low-cost, rapid launch. Over 30 years the vehicle made 135 flights. Within a few years SpaceX aims to fly 135 times a year, then thousands of times. Such was the significance of Saturday. SpaceX aspires to fundamentally change the relationship between humanity and the heavens. The first step is to get the entire rocket back.

概念验证。航天飞机几乎是一个完全可重复使用的太空发射系统，但它远未实现低成本、快速发射的目标。在 30 年的时间里，该飞行器完成了 135 次飞行。SpaceX 的目标是在几年内实现每年飞行 135 次，随后达到每年数千次。这就是周六事件的意义所在。SpaceX 渴望从根本上改变人类与太空之间的关系。第一步就是找回整个火箭。

SpaceX has now returned both elements of a Starship rocket safely to its launch tower. It will take time to reach a high rate of success with such landings, but Saturday’s safe return offered a proof of concept. The landings will come with increasing frequency; and so will the launches. Over time SpaceX will perfect the heat shield that is likely to be the most difficult aspect of rapid reuse. And eventually, as difficult as it may be to believe, what we experienced with amazement this weekend will become routine. One day, we will take this all for granted. But not today.

SpaceX 现在已经将星舰火箭的两个部分安全地送回了发射塔。要实现此类着陆的高成功率还需要时间，但周六的安全回收提供了一个概念验证。着陆频率将会越来越高，发射频率也是如此。随着时间的推移，SpaceX 将会完善隔热罩，这很可能是快速重复使用中最困难的环节。最终，尽管难以置信，我们这个周末所经历的惊人时刻将成为常态。总有一天，我们会认为这一切理所当然。但不是今天。
