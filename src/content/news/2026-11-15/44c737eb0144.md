# No Pilots. Few Air Traffic Controllers. What Could Go Wrong?

Save this storySave this storyOn September 27, US transportation secretary Sean Duffy posted a video on X with the words “GAME CHANGER.” “Introducing ‘SMART’—the new air traffic management tool that saves YOU time, cuts back DELAYS, and makes your flying experience BETTER,” he wrote. This was a new AI-infused air traffic control system. Almost three weeks later, that post would get a new life, as a nervous joke, as the FAA issued a national groundstop on Friday the 13th that instantly threw the world’s airline schedules into chaos. The administration quickly announced that there was no evidence of a cyberattack, and no sign that SMART had anything to do with the communications failure at the heart of the government’s decision to suspend flights. But when asked on Friday night whether he could definitively rule out AI failure, Homeland Security secretary Markwayne Mullin could only say, “There’s nothing certain right now.”

9月27日，美国交通部长肖恩·达菲（Sean Duffy）在X平台上发布了一段视频，并配文称其为“游戏规则改变者”。他写道：“隆重推出‘SMART’——这款全新的空中交通管理工具能为您节省时间、减少延误，并让您的飞行体验更美好。”这是一个植入了人工智能的全新空中交通管制系统。近三周后，随着联邦航空管理局（FAA）在13号星期五发布全国性停飞令，导致全球航空时刻表瞬间陷入混乱，这篇帖子以一种令人不安的笑话形式重获“新生”。政府迅速宣布，没有证据表明发生了网络攻击，也没有迹象表明SMART与导致政府决定停飞的通信故障有关。但在周五晚上被问及能否明确排除人工智能故障的可能性时，国土安全部长马克韦恩·穆林（Markwayne Mullin）只能表示：“目前还没有什么是确定的。”

New systems like SMART are only the tip of the iceberg when it comes to the AI aviation revolution currently underway. Take, for instance, the Cessna Caravan that took off from Buchanan Field Airport in Concord, California, and flew south to Phoenix on a calm September morning. The plane, a single-engine turboprop, then turned east to Fort Worth, stopped at Shaw Air Force Base in South Carolina, and finally arrived at Dare County Regional Airport off the coast of North Carolina. The zigzag, coast-to-coast trip covered 3,200 miles; along the way, the aircraft navigated busy airspace and maneuvered to avoid storms.

像SMART这样的新系统，仅仅是当前正在进行的人工智能航空革命的冰山一角。以一架塞斯纳大篷车（Cessna Caravan）飞机为例，它在一个平静的九月早晨从加利福尼亚州康科德的布坎南机场起飞，向南飞往凤凰城。这架单引擎涡轮螺旋桨飞机随后转向东飞往沃斯堡，在南卡罗来纳州的肖空军基地停留，最后抵达北卡罗来纳州海岸外的戴尔县地区机场。这趟横跨海岸的曲折旅程全长3200英里；沿途，飞机在繁忙的空域中航行，并机动避开了风暴。

None of this would be remarkable, save for one fact: The entire journey took place without a single human hand touching the plane’s controls. According to Joby Aviation, the company that carried out the project, a safety pilot was aboard the Cessna at all times—but never needed to intervene. The pilotless trip marked a major milestone in aviation. The air travel industry has long been technologically conservative, and with good reason: When human lives are at stake, it’s better to be time-tested and a little dated than cutting-edge and unpredictable. But AI is making inroads into aviation with unprecedented speed. The effects now are hard to see, but the changes in the offing are huge, promising to remake the economics of air travel and transform how people and things move around in the sky. A big question remains, though: Can we trust this technology to be reliable and safe?

如果不是因为一个事实，这一切都不会引人注目：整个旅程中，没有任何人类的手触碰过飞机的操纵装置。据执行该项目的Joby Aviation公司称，塞斯纳飞机上始终有一名安全飞行员，但他从未需要进行干预。这次无人驾驶飞行标志着航空业的一个重要里程碑。航空旅行行业在技术上一直比较保守，这不无道理：当涉及人类生命时，经过时间考验且略显过时的技术，总比尖端但不可预测的技术要好。但人工智能正以空前的速度进入航空领域。目前其影响尚不明显，但即将到来的变革是巨大的，有望重塑航空旅行的经济模式，并改变人和物在天空中的移动方式。然而，一个巨大的问题依然存在：我们能相信这项技术是可靠且安全的吗？

Depends who you ask. “We're introducing all this new technology to improve the system, and it's going to be a disaster,” says a recently retired FAA employee. “This is literally a Pandora's box that we are going to regret.”

这取决于你问谁。“我们正在引入所有这些新技术来改进系统，但这将是一场灾难，”一位刚退休的FAA员工说。“这简直是一个我们会后悔打开的潘多拉魔盒。”

Aviation’s embrace of AI traces back to the early 2000s, when advances in batteries and microelectronics suddenly made drones much more capable and inexpensive. The surge convinced officials that robots and humans would eventually need to share the airways, and that new infrastructure would be necessary to allow that. At the time, virtually all aircraft were human-piloted, and there was no way for drones to avoid human-flown planes or each other.

航空业对人工智能的拥抱可以追溯到21世纪初，当时电池和微电子技术的进步突然使无人机变得更加强大且廉价。这一激增让官员们确信，机器人和人类最终将需要共享航道，而新的基础设施对于实现这一目标至关重要。当时，几乎所有的飞机都是由人类驾驶的，无人机无法避开人类驾驶的飞机，也无法避开彼此。

In 2007, the federal government rolled out a sweeping project called Next Generation Air Transportation System—NextGen, for short—that mandated broad changes to the air travel system over the decades to come. Among its provisions was the requirement that, by 2020, aircraft in the United States be equipped with ADS-B, a system that constantly sends radio signals telling everyone in the vicinity where you are and where you’re headed.

2007年，联邦政府推出了一个名为“下一代航空运输系统”（简称NextGen）的全面项目，要求在未来几十年内对航空旅行系统进行广泛的变革。其条款之一是要求到2020年，美国的飞机必须配备ADS-B系统，该系统会不断发送无线电信号，告知附近的所有人你的位置和航向。

Joby’s aircraft flying past Lower Manhattan, New York, in April. Courtesy of Joby AviationWith a road map to robot-friendly skies in place, big players like Amazon and Walmart began working on ambitious plans for drone delivery services. In 2014, Google unveiled Project Wing, a drone delivery offering that by 2018 was ferrying food, coffee, and medication to 164 homes near Canberra, Australia. The next year, the pilot program was expanded to Christiansburg, Virginia. Costs were high and service slow, but those early programs weren’t meant to become viable businesses. Instead, they were providing Google with operational experience for the years to come.

Joby的飞机于4月飞过纽约曼哈顿下城上空。（图片由Joby Aviation提供）随着通往机器人友好天空的路线图到位，亚马逊和沃尔玛等大公司开始制定雄心勃勃的无人机配送服务计划。2014年，谷歌推出了“翼计划”（Project Wing），这是一项无人机配送服务，到2018年已为澳大利亚堪培拉附近的164户家庭运送食品、咖啡和药品。次年，该试点项目扩展到弗吉尼亚州的克里斯琴斯堡。虽然成本高昂且服务缓慢，但这些早期项目并非旨在成为可行的商业业务。相反，它们是在为谷歌提供未来几年的运营经验。

Around the same time, a host of startups began working on a related kind of vehicle: electric vertical takeoff and landing aircraft, or eVTOLs, also known as electric air taxis. These are, essentially, oversized drones (with room for pilots, for now) designed to carry a small number of people a short distance. By 2022 there were 600 different models under development. Soon, their manufacturers promised, cheap, short aerial hops would be commonplace, Joby was among those leading the charge: In 2020, the company unveiled the design of its S4 model, which it said would be able to carry a pilot and four passengers 100 miles or more at speeds of up to 200 mph. The S4’s maiden flight took place last year.

大约在同一时间，许多初创公司开始研发一种相关的交通工具：电动垂直起降飞行器（eVTOL），也称为电动空中出租车。它们本质上是超大型无人机（目前有飞行员的空间），旨在运送少量人员进行短途飞行。到2022年，已有600种不同的型号在研发中。制造商承诺，很快，廉价的短途空中飞行将变得司空见惯。Joby是其中的领跑者之一：2020年，该公司公布了其S4型号的设计，称其能够搭载一名飞行员和四名乘客，以高达200英里/小时的速度飞行100英里或更远。S4的首次飞行于去年进行。

A commercial launch seemed just around the corner. But deadlines kept getting pushed back. As it turned out, engineering a flying autonomous robot was just part of the overall problem. You also needed to design an airspace that they could operate in, train humans to pilot or supervise them, develop an economic framework within which they could earn money, and so on. “It's a very important, safe, careful dance,” says Greg Bowles, Chief Policy Officer at Joby Aviation.

商业发布似乎指日可待。但截止日期却一再推迟。事实证明，设计一个飞行自主机器人只是整个问题的一部分。你还需要设计一个它们可以运行的空域，培训人类来驾驶或监督它们，开发一个它们可以盈利的经济框架，等等。“这是一场非常重要、安全且谨慎的舞蹈，”Joby Aviation的首席政策官格雷格·鲍尔斯（Greg Bowles）说。

Silicon Valley’s speed has long been in conflict with air travel norms. “Aviation regulation has been conservative and prioritizes reliability, because the consequences of failure are severe,” says Mary Schiavo, an aviation lawyer and former inspector general in the Department of Transportation. “Certification standards and airworthiness require proof and demonstrated safety, and that usually means incremental rather than radical change.” The FAA was in no rush to let unproven robots go careening around the national airspace.
