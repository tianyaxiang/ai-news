# Microsoft exec called AI scraping ‘the largest theft of labor in human history,’ new unredacted filings reveal
# 微软高管称 AI 抓取是“人类历史上最大规模的劳动窃取”，最新未删减文件披露

New unredacted information in the copyright lawsuit The New York Times brought against OpenAI and Microsoft three years ago reveals an admission that AI scraping was tantamount to theft, and that AI products pose a major threat to publications.
《纽约时报》三年前对 OpenAI 和微软提起的版权诉讼中，最新披露的未删减信息显示，相关方承认 AI 抓取等同于盗窃，且 AI 产品对出版机构构成了重大威胁。

Per the lawsuit, a top Microsoft executive privately described the companies’ AI training practices as “theft,” and OpenAI’s own leadership said its AI models posed an “existential threat” to the publishers and journalists whose work trained them.
根据诉讼文件，微软的一位高管曾私下将两家公司的 AI 训练行为描述为“盗窃”，而 OpenAI 的高层也表示，其 AI 模型对那些为其提供训练素材的出版商和记者构成了“生存威胁”。

The unsealed material also details how the companies allegedly obtained and used that content by bypassing paywalls undetected, building training datasets via mass scraping, and deliberately stripping copyright notices from training data.
这些解密材料还详细说明了这些公司如何通过绕过付费墙而不被发现、通过大规模抓取构建训练数据集，以及故意从训练数据中删除版权声明来获取和使用这些内容。

It’s worth noting that much of the new information comes from The Times’ own brief, not the underlying exhibits, which remain sealed. The quotes below are presented without their original context.
值得注意的是，大部分新信息来自《纽约时报》自己的陈述书，而非作为证据的原始附件，后者目前仍处于保密状态。以下引用的内容并未提供原始语境。

The unredacted filing is the latest escalation in the three-year-old lawsuit, in which The New York Times initially alleged the firms violated copyright law by training generative AI models on its content.
这份未删减的文件是这起三年诉讼案的最新升级。此前，《纽约时报》指控这些公司利用其内容训练生成式 AI 模型，违反了版权法。

The question of whether AI firms can legally use copyrighted material to train AI has no clear answer, but judges have been largely favorable to AI companies’ arguments that training constitutes “fair use.” This legal rule lets people use copyrighted work without permission in certain cases, like parody, news reporting, or criticism.
关于 AI 公司是否可以合法使用受版权保护的材料来训练 AI，目前尚无明确答案，但法官们在很大程度上倾向于支持 AI 公司的论点，即训练行为构成“合理使用”。这一法律规则允许人们在特定情况下（如模仿、新闻报道或评论）未经许可使用受版权保护的作品。

Earlier this month, the Trump administration contributed a brief in defense of OpenAI’s unlicensed use of copyrighted material to train its LLMs.
本月初，特朗普政府提交了一份陈述书，为 OpenAI 未经许可使用受版权保护的材料来训练其大语言模型（LLM）的行为进行辩护。

Several of the new admissions, however, run counter to OpenAI’s fair use defense, particularly the rule’s requirement that use doesn’t substitute or harm the market for the original work. For example, Microsoft’s own data shows its Copilot “answer engine” caused click-through rates for The New York Times’ domain to drop as much as 93% compared to traditional Bing search.
然而，其中几项新的承认内容与 OpenAI 的“合理使用”辩护相抵触，特别是该规则要求使用行为不得替代或损害原作品市场。例如，微软自己的数据显示，与传统的必应（Bing）搜索相比，其 Copilot “答案引擎”导致《纽约时报》域名的点击率下降了高达 93%。

An internal Microsoft presentation written by Microsoft’s director of Applied Science, Brent Hecht, in January 2024 describes the decline as a “doom loop” that would “hurt the performance of our models and the entire web at the same time.”
微软应用科学总监 Brent Hecht 在 2024 年 1 月撰写的一份内部演示文稿中，将这种下降描述为一种“厄运循环”，称其将“同时损害我们模型的性能和整个网络”。

“It is highly unusual that an end-product threatens the economic foundations of its essential suppliers, but that is the situation we have created for our LLM business with respect to its ‘content supply chain,’” reads the Microsoft document, as quoted in the filing.
“终端产品威胁到其核心供应商的经济基础是非常罕见的，但这正是我们为大语言模型业务在‘内容供应链’方面所造成的局面，”诉讼文件中引用的微软文档如是写道。

Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella also testified in a deposition earlier this year that “anything that is paywalled should be licensed by anyone who wants to use it…for grounding or training,” and made clear that, if he “had been made aware that OpenAI had scraped and trained on information that was behind a paywall,” he would have “invoked [Microsoft’s right to] require OpenAI to retrain its models.”
微软首席执行官萨提亚·纳德拉（Satya Nadella）今年早些时候在证词中也表示，“任何有付费墙的内容，任何想要使用它的人……无论是用于基础支撑还是训练，都应该获得许可。”他明确表示，如果他“早知道 OpenAI 抓取并训练了付费墙后的信息”，他会“行使（微软的权利）要求 OpenAI 重新训练其模型”。

Other admissions cut against different pillars of the fair-use test: OpenAI’s head of ChatGPT, Nick Turley, wrote in internal communication that publishers face an “existential threat” from products like the chatbot, which are “largely substitutive” and “will get more and more substitutive as they get better.”
其他承认内容则削弱了“合理使用”测试的其他支柱：OpenAI 的 ChatGPT 负责人 Nick Turley 在内部通讯中写道，出版商正面临来自聊天机器人等产品的“生存威胁”，这些产品“在很大程度上具有替代性”，并且“随着性能的提升，替代性会越来越强”。

OpenAI President Greg Brockman described the models as “excellent at news.” Nadella agreed under oath earlier this year that conversing with chatbots “has substituted … giving you the information right there on the website on the AI platform versus needing to go to the underlying source.” That kind of language speaks to how the technology could directly compete with, rather than transform, the original work.
OpenAI 总裁 Greg Brockman 将这些模型描述为“非常擅长新闻”。纳德拉今年早些时候在宣誓后也同意，与聊天机器人对话“已经取代了……直接在网站上获取信息，而无需访问原始来源”。这种表述说明了该技术如何直接与原作品竞争，而非对其进行转化。

A Microsoft document states that there is a “real risk” that generative AI could “significantly disrupt the employment of the very people who generated the data on which the foundation model was trained.”
一份微软文档指出，生成式 AI 存在一种“真正的风险”，即它可能“严重扰乱那些为基础模型提供训练数据的创作者的就业”。

The sheer scale of the copying is striking. The documents reveal for the first time that OpenAI’s mid-training datasets alone contain more than 91,692 copies of works published by the NYT, Daily News, and Center for Investigative Reporting. A Common Crawl-derived dataset included more than 2 million documents from nytimes.com alone.
复制的规模之大令人震惊。文件首次披露，仅 OpenAI 的训练中期数据集就包含了超过 91,692 份由《纽约时报》、《每日新闻》和调查报道中心发布的作品副本。一个源自 Common Crawl 的数据集仅从 nytimes.com 就抓取了超过 200 万份文档。

In a January 2023 internal memo, Hecht called it “an astonishing theft of unprecedented proportions” and “the largest theft of labor in human history.”
在 2023 年 1 月的一份内部备忘录中，Hecht 将其称为“一场规模空前的惊人盗窃”和“人类历史上最大规模的劳动窃取”。

The filing lays out in new detail how OpenAI and Microsoft went about acquiring the plaintiffs’ content, including scraping it from the Bing Index. “OpenAI delivered the entire GPT-3 training dataset to Microsoft, which Microsoft used to evaluate how to implement OpenAI’s models within its own commercial products,” the filing reads. “Microsoft similarly provided training data to OpenAI through initiatives called Project Taxi and Project Mango.”
该文件详细披露了 OpenAI 和微软获取原告内容的方式，包括从必应索引中进行抓取。“OpenAI 将整个 GPT-3 训练数据集交付给微软，微软利用该数据集评估如何在自己的商业产品中实现 OpenAI 的模型，”文件中写道。“微软同样通过名为‘出租车项目’（Project Taxi）和‘芒果项目’（Project Mango）的计划向 OpenAI 提供了训练数据。”

The companies allegedly assembled the Project Mango data into a training dataset that contains copies of at least 160,903 unique works from the news publishers. In order to get the most out of their scraping, OpenAI employees allegedly came up with a plan to circumvent paywalls without detection.
据称，这两家公司将“芒果项目”的数据汇编成一个训练数据集，其中包含了来自新闻出版商的至少 160,903 份独特作品的副本。为了最大限度地利用抓取成果，OpenAI 的员工据称还制定了一项绕过付费墙而不被发现的计划。

The filings show that when OpenAI researcher Nick Ryder told Brockman about a “hack to get around nytimes paywall,” Brockman replied: “ah nice.”
文件显示，当 OpenAI 研究员 Nick Ryder 向 Brockman 提到一种“绕过《纽约时报》付费墙的黑客手段”时，Brockman 回复道：“啊，不错。”

OpenAI employees also allegedly built training datasets like WebText and WebText2 that disproportionately relied on scraped news content. They also allegedly pulled millions of articles from Common Crawl, a free, open repository of web crawl data.
据称，OpenAI 员工还构建了 WebText 和 WebText2 等训练数据集，这些数据集过度依赖抓取的新闻内容。他们还据称从 Common Crawl（一个免费、开放的网络抓取数据存储库）中提取了数百万篇文章。

The findings also describe deliberate efforts to strip copyright notices from training data before it reached the model, since researchers “wouldn’t want model outputting” “copyright notices” to users.
调查结果还描述了在训练数据进入模型之前，研究人员故意删除版权声明的行为，因为他们“不希望模型向用户输出”“版权声明”。

“The evidence revealed here for the first time shows that OpenAI and Microsoft knew that what they were doing was wrong,” Steven Lieberman, counsel for the New York Daily News, said in a statement shared with TechCrunch. OpenAI and Microsoft did not return requests for comment.
“这里首次披露的证据表明，OpenAI 和微软知道他们的所作所为是错误的，”《纽约每日新闻》的律师 Steven Lieberman 在发给 TechCrunch 的声明中表示。OpenAI 和微软均未回应置评请求。
