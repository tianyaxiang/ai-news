# There's no such thing as Just a Tool
# 世上没有“仅仅是工具”这回事

**Author:** Iris Meredith  
**Date published:** 2026-08-29  
**作者：** Iris Meredith  
**发布日期：** 2026年8月29日

If you work or write in the social criticism of tech, you hear a wide range of views and opinions about tech, some of them startlingly hostile to the very idea that you might be doing what you're doing. Consequently, you very often find yourself experiencing a wide range of different criticism of your work, your ideas and your viewpoints, some of them extremely valid, others... less so.  
如果你从事科技社会评论的工作或写作，你会听到各种关于科技的观点和意见，其中一些对你所做的事情本身抱有惊人的敌意。因此，你经常会发现自己面临着针对你的工作、想法和观点的各种批评，有些极其深刻，而另一些……则不然。

A common refrain when critiquing specific technologies in this way is that engineers will say that a given technology is "just a tool". You hear it when talking about all kinds of things: shells, containerisation, reactive frameworks and most recently it comes up a lot in discussions of LLM technology. What this response seems to mean is the following:  
在以这种方式批评特定技术时，工程师们常挂在嘴边的一句话是：某项技术“仅仅是工具”。在讨论各种事物时你都会听到这种说法：Shell、容器化、响应式框架，最近在讨论大语言模型（LLM）技术时，这种说法也频繁出现。这种回应似乎意味着以下几点：

* The technology being discussed has no bearing on the attitudes or ideologies of the person holding it: use or non-use is ideologically neutral.  
* 所讨论的技术与使用者的态度或意识形态无关：使用或不使用在意识形态上是中立的。
* The technology being discussed can be effectively separated from the people using it: nothing can be inferred about a person from the technologies that they use.  
* 所讨论的技术可以有效地与使用者剥离：无法从一个人使用的技术推断出关于其本人的任何信息。
* The technology is used fully consciously: you are always fully aware that you are using a technology and have complete volitional freedom to use a different technology at any time.  
* 技术的使用是完全自觉的：你始终完全意识到自己正在使用某项技术，并且随时拥有使用其他技术的完全意志自由。
* The technology does not influence the kinds of things likely to be done with it: use of the technology doesn't tend to encourage one course of action over another ("guns don't kill people, people do").  
* 技术不会影响人们用它去做什么：使用该技术并不倾向于鼓励某种特定的行为方式（即“枪不会杀人，人会杀人”）。

The consequence of these statements being true is that the only valid criticism of a tool or technology becomes how well it performs the task that it's designed to perform. You cannot, for example, say that a tool teaches bad habits, because if it isn't explicitly a teaching tool what it teaches people becomes out-of-bounds for discussion. Similarly, you cannot discuss the fact that driving an SUV tends to encourage shitty driving behaviour on the road because, after all, the SUV is "just a tool" and there are some legitimate use cases for it: because this is the case, the fact that the SUV has ideological and behavioural consequences is something that we're not really allowed to discuss. This kind of folk understanding of tools is very common, but unfortunately it's almost all wrong.  
如果这些陈述成立，那么对工具或技术的唯一有效批评就变成了它执行设计任务的效果如何。例如，你不能说某种工具会教坏习惯，因为如果它不是明确的教学工具，它教给人们什么就成了讨论的禁区。同样，你不能讨论驾驶 SUV 往往会助长道路上的糟糕驾驶行为，因为毕竟 SUV “仅仅是工具”，而且它确实有一些合理的使用场景：正因如此，SUV 具有意识形态和行为后果这一事实，是我们实际上不被允许讨论的。这种对工具的民间理解非常普遍，但不幸的是，它几乎完全是错误的。

To explain why saying that something's "just a tool" is an illegitimate move, we shall have to rely on the work of notable Nazi (and unfortunately also very important 20th century philosopher) Martin Heidegger. While Heidegger's major work, Being and Time mostly discusses ontology and human being as being being-towards-death, a significant part of Heidegger's work there and elsewhere has to do with the nature and being of tools.  
为了解释为什么说某物“仅仅是工具”是一种不合理的论调，我们必须借助著名的纳粹分子（同时也是 20 世纪非常重要的哲学家）马丁·海德格尔的著作。虽然海德格尔的主要著作《存在与时间》主要讨论本体论和作为“向死而生”的人的存在，但他在该书及其他著作中，有很大一部分内容涉及工具的本质与存在。

Heidegger thinks about tools like so: a tool is something (a thing that has being in the Heideggerian sense, not necessarily an object) that represents an extension of human capabilities in some way. A tool is thus any kind of thing that lets you do something you wouldn't otherwise have been able to. The important (for us) conceptual leap here is that when a tool works well, or isn't broken, it develops what Heidegger describes as a "ready-to-hand" quality: the tool fades into the background as a kind of human-tool gestalt of a human with the additional capability afforded by a tool forms. As a simple example of this, consider eating with a fork. When the fork is well-designed and not broken, you don't explicitly have "I am using a fork" in your conscious mind while eating dinner. You simply eat, and the assumptions encoded into how you eat with a fork and what you might eat with a fork fade into the background and are taken as a given. You only explicitly notice the fork when it doesn't work: perhaps a tine is bent, or maybe you're trying to eat soup. Either way, the tool only becomes, in Heidegger's wording, present-at-hand when it becomes obtrusive, or an obstacle to doing what you want to do.  
海德格尔对工具的思考如下：工具是某种东西（在海德格尔意义上具有“存在”的东西，不一定指物体），它以某种方式代表了人类能力的延伸。因此，工具是任何能让你做到原本做不到的事情的东西。这里对我们而言重要的概念飞跃是：当工具运行良好或未损坏时，它会产生海德格尔所描述的“上手状态”（ready-to-hand）：工具会淡出背景，形成一种“人-工具”的完形（gestalt），即人与工具所赋予的额外能力融为一体。举个简单的例子，考虑用叉子吃饭。当叉子设计良好且没有损坏时，你在吃晚饭时并不会明确意识到“我正在使用叉子”。你只是在吃饭，而关于如何用叉子吃饭以及可以用叉子吃什么的假设，都已淡入背景并被视为理所当然。只有当叉子无法正常工作时，你才会明确注意到它：也许叉齿弯了，或者你正试图用它喝汤。无论哪种情况，用海德格尔的话说，只有当工具变得碍事，或者成为你达成目的的障碍时，它才会变成“在手状态”（present-at-hand）。

The human-tool gestalt that forms with a ready-to-hand tool, however, is not neutral. Rather, the tools that are ready-to-hand each create a different gestalt, with a different range of allowable and potential "things you could do" and, accordingly, different thought patterns, ideas and ideologies that go with that. Take the fork, for example: it is, notionally, "just a tool" and should thus be neutral. Except that it really, really isn't. If you need an example of this, go find some older or more culturally sheltered people from an Anglo-Saxon background (if these are difficult to find where you live, substitute people from a different food culture where you do) and take them to a Sichuan restaurant.  
然而，随着“上手”工具形成的“人-工具”完形并非中立的。相反，每一个“上手”的工具都会创造出不同的完形，带来不同范围的“可做之事”和潜在可能，并相应地伴随着不同的思维模式、观念和意识形态。以叉子为例：从概念上讲，它“仅仅是工具”，因此应该是中立的。但事实并非如此。如果你需要一个例子，去找一些来自盎格鲁-撒克逊背景、年纪较大或文化接触较少的人（如果你所在的地方很难找到这样的人，可以用来自不同饮食文化的人代替），带他们去一家川菜馆。

Put that way, the point is fairly clear: the affordances that eating with knife and fork allow are pretty radically different from the affordances that you have when eating with chopsticks. As a first point, the assumptions encoded about how food is prepared are completely different: you cannot feed diners eating with chopsticks a steak in the European fashion, and food in general has to be carefully cut so that it can be easily grasped with chopsticks.
