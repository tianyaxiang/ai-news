# Voyager Is a Light-Day Away. Here’s How Far That Really Is

The Voyager 1 spacecraft launched from Earth in September 1977. It’s not just old; it’s also really far away. In fact, it’s so far that it takes light one whole day to travel from the spacecraft to Earth and vice versa. That puts its distance at one light-day. The expanse separating it from Earth raises so many interesting physics questions about Voyager 1’s epic, well, voyage. Let’s get to the questions—and the answers.

旅行者1号航天器于1977年9月从地球发射升空。它不仅“高龄”，而且距离极其遥远。事实上，它远到光从航天器传播到地球（反之亦然）需要整整一天的时间。这意味着它的距离为一光日。将它与地球隔开的广袤空间引发了许多关于旅行者1号这段史诗般旅程的有趣物理学问题。让我们来探讨这些问题及其答案。

How Do We Measure Distance in Space? In science, we like to measure things, including distance. Surely, you’re familiar with meters. If you put a mass on a string and let it swing back and forth (like the pendulum of a clock), it will take about 1 second to go from side to side if the string is 1 meter long. But if you want to measure larger distances like the proximity of stuff in space, this unit quickly becomes silly because objects are so far apart. While kilometers can be helpful for measuring the distance between things like Earth and the International Space Station or the moon, even they become hard to comprehend at larger scales.

我们如何测量太空中的距离？在科学领域，我们喜欢测量事物，包括距离。你肯定熟悉米。如果你把一个重物系在绳子上让它来回摆动（就像时钟的摆锤），如果绳子长1米，它从一侧摆到另一侧大约需要1秒钟。但如果你想测量更大的距离，比如太空中物体的接近程度，这个单位很快就会变得荒谬，因为物体之间的距离太远了。虽然公里对于测量地球与国际空间站或月球之间的距离很有用，但在更大的尺度上，即使是公里也变得难以理解。

Enter the Astronomical Unit, or AU, which is the average distance from the sun to the Earth, or about 1.5 x 10^11 meters. Humans found this distance by observing the transit of Venus. This transit is like a solar eclipse, except instead of the moon passing in front of the sun, it’s the planet Venus. Looking at Venus’ movement from different locations on Earth, the planet creates a shadow at different times. If you know the radius of the Earth, you can find this distance from the Earth to sun.

于是引入了天文单位（AU），即太阳到地球的平均距离，约为1.5 x 10^11米。人类通过观测金星凌日发现了这个距离。这种凌日现象类似于日食，只不过不是月球从太阳前方经过，而是金星。从地球上不同的位置观察金星的运动，金星会在不同的时间投下阴影。如果你知道地球的半径，就可以算出地球到太阳的距离。

But what if you want to measure the distance to a star outside the solar system? AUs start to become a little unwieldy. Enter the parsec. No matter what Han Solo says in Star Wars, the parsec is a unit of distance and not the best time for the Kessel Run. And it’s a long distance: for comparison, 1 parsec is about 200,000 AUs. The parsec is a way to measure distance using parallax. You can get the mechanics of parallax by holding your thumb out with an extended arm in front of your face. Close one eye and line up your thumb with an object across the room. Now close that eye and open your other eye. You will notice that the thumb seems to shift its position. The size of the angular shift depends on both the distance between observation locations—the distance between your eyes—and the distance to the object. The farther the object, the less parallax shift you will observe.

但如果你想测量太阳系外恒星的距离呢？天文单位开始变得有些笨重。于是引入了秒差距（parsec）。无论韩·索罗在《星球大战》中怎么说，秒差距都是一个距离单位，而不是“凯塞尔航道”的最佳时间。这是一个很长的距离：作为对比，1秒差距约为20万个天文单位。秒差距是一种利用视差测量距离的方法。你可以通过伸直手臂将拇指放在脸前，来理解视差的原理。闭上一只眼睛，让拇指与房间对面的物体对齐。现在闭上那只眼睛，睁开另一只眼睛。你会发现拇指的位置似乎发生了偏移。角位移的大小取决于观测位置之间的距离（即你双眼之间的距离）以及到物体的距离。物体越远，你观察到的视差位移就越小。

OK, So What’s a Light-Day? As Earth orbits the sun, we have different observation locations while looking at stars. Imagine an observation shift of 1 AU (you would get a 2 AU shift going from December to June). If a star has a parallax shift of 1 arc-second, we say it has a distance of 1 parsec. At one point, astronomer Friedrich Bessel was using parallax to measure the distance to a star. He said that it was so far away that it would take light 10.3 years to reach the Earth. Boom, there’s your unit of distance, the light-year. It’s the distance light travels in one year, or 9.9 x 10^15 meters, or over 6 million AU. But we can use any unit of time to measure distance with light. One light-day is the distance that light travels in 24 hours, or 2.6 x 10^13 meters. That’s really far. In fact, no human-made object has gone farther. Let’s say that Voyager traveled at a constant speed and in a straight line (it didn’t). That would give it an average speed of 164 kilometers per second or about 36,000 mph.

那么，什么是光日？当地球绕太阳公转时，我们在观察恒星时会有不同的观测位置。想象一下1个天文单位的观测位移（从12月到6月你会得到2个天文单位的位移）。如果一颗恒星的视差位移为1角秒，我们就说它的距离是1秒差距。曾经，天文学家弗里德里希·贝塞尔利用视差测量一颗恒星的距离。他说它太远了，光需要10.3年才能到达地球。砰，这就是你的距离单位：光年。它是光在一年内传播的距离，即9.9 x 10^15米，或超过600万个天文单位。但我们可以用任何时间单位来测量光行距离。一光日是光在24小时内传播的距离，即2.6 x 10^13米。这真的很远。事实上，没有任何人造物体走得更远。假设旅行者号以恒定速度沿直线飞行（实际上并非如此），那么它的平均速度将达到每秒164公里，即每小时约36,000英里。

What Forces Affect Voyager 1? Voyager has felt a lot of different forces out in space. The first is the rockets that got it there. But let’s talk about the other ones it has encountered. First, there’s gravitational force. There is an attractive gravitational force between any two objects with mass. The force depends on the masses of the two objects and the distance between them. The force is there on any number of scales: Since your body has a mass (maybe around 70 kilograms) and your phone has a mass (about 200 grams), there is an attractive force. If the phone is about 50 centimeters from your body, the gravitational force the phone exerts on you is about 4 x 10^-9 newtons. In other words, it’s exceedingly tiny. (Depending on how much you love your phone, though, the attraction may feel stronger.)

什么力影响着旅行者1号？旅行者号在太空中感受到了许多不同的力。首先是将其送入太空的火箭推力。但让我们谈谈它遇到的其他力。首先是引力。任何两个有质量的物体之间都存在引力。这种力取决于两个物体的质量以及它们之间的距离。这种力在任何尺度上都存在：由于你的身体有质量（大约70公斤），你的手机也有质量（大约200克），所以它们之间存在引力。如果手机距离你的身体约50厘米，手机对你施加的引力约为4 x 10^-9牛顿。换句话说，它极其微小。（不过，取决于你有多爱你的手机，这种吸引力可能会感觉更强。）

For Voyager, there’s also a gravitational interaction with masses—like the outer planets it was originally launched to observe, as well as Earth. However, the really big gravitational influence is from the sun, since it has such a large mass (2 x 10^30 kilograms). Using the mass of Voyager 1 at 722 kilograms, there is an attractive force of 1.4 x 10^-4 newtons. That’s equivalent to the weight of 78 grains of salt on the surface of the Earth. The only other object that would have a substantial relationship to Voyager 1 is the planet Jupiter, with a mass of 1.9 x 10^27 kilograms. But it’s only when Voyager was fairly close to the planet way back in 1979 that this gravitational force would even partially compare to the interaction with the sun.

对于旅行者号来说，它还与一些质量体存在引力相互作用，比如它最初被发射去观测的外行星，以及地球。然而，真正巨大的引力影响来自太阳，因为它拥有巨大的质量（2 x 10^30公斤）。以旅行者1号722公斤的质量计算，它受到的引力为1.4 x 10^-4牛顿。这相当于地球表面78粒盐的重量。唯一与旅行者1号有实质性引力关系的另一个天体是木星，其质量为1.9 x 10^27公斤。但只有在1979年旅行者号相当靠近木星时，这种引力才会在一定程度上与太阳的引力相互作用相提并论。

There are other, more exotic interactions with the spacecraft, but these forces are also tiny. Solar winds—charged particles emitted from the surface of the sun—have blown past Voyager. Although they travel very fast, the mass of these particles is extremely small. The further you get from the sun, the more their intensity decreases as well. The venerable craft is also likely to feel the impacts of magnetic fields and other weird things as it moves from a region where the primary interactions are with the sun—the heliosphere—to interactions with other stars and stuff—the interstellar medium.

航天器还受到其他更奇特的相互作用，但这些力也非常微小。太阳风（从太阳表面发射的带电粒子）吹过了旅行者号。虽然它们传播速度很快，但这些粒子的质量极小。你离太阳越远，它们的强度也会随之减弱。当这艘功勋卓著的航天器从主要与太阳相互作用的区域（日光层）移动到与其他恒星等物质相互作用的区域（星际介质）时，它也很可能会感受到磁场和其他奇异事物的影响。
