EXECUTIVE SUMMARY Researchers in Australia claim they’ve kept a human liver alive, on life support, outside the body for seven weeks, opening up speculative new possibilities in biomedical engineering.

执行摘要：澳大利亚的研究人员声称，他们已成功将一颗人体肝脏在体外通过生命支持系统维持了七周的存活状态，这为生物医学工程开辟了新的推测性可能性。

The feat, whose details are pending publication, was carried out in Sydney after a team led by surgeon and inventor Carlo Pulitano connected a donated liver to a system of automated pumps and sensors that fed the organ nutrients, supplied it with blood, and whisked away waste.

这一壮举的细节尚待发表。该实验在悉尼进行，由外科医生兼发明家卡洛·普利塔诺（Carlo Pulitano）领导的团队将一颗捐赠的肝脏连接到一套自动泵和传感器系统上，该系统负责为器官提供营养、输送血液并清除代谢废物。

In an interview, Pulitano, a transplant surgeon at the Royal Prince Alfred Hospital and a professor at the University of Sydney, likened the life support system to “an artificial body.” Its official name is the Extended Organ Perfusion System.

在接受采访时，身为皇家阿尔弗雷德王子医院移植外科医生及悉尼大学教授的普利塔诺将这套生命支持系统比作“人造身体”。其正式名称为“扩展器官灌注系统”（Extended Organ Perfusion System）。

“Our group is the only one in the world to have maintained a human liver under fully normothermic, physiological conditions for seven weeks,” he says. Normothermic means body temperature, or about 37 °C.

“我们团队是世界上唯一一个在完全常温、生理条件下将人体肝脏维持了七周存活的团队，”他说道。常温是指体温，即约 37 摄氏度。

The previous record for keeping an internal organ alive outside the body was set in Switzerland several years ago. That organ—also a liver—lasted around two weeks.

此前在体外维持内脏器官存活的纪录是几年前在瑞士创下的。当时那颗器官同样是肝脏，存活了约两周。

Most organs destined for transplant survive only a few hours in special ice buckets and need to be raced to their destination. Over the last decade, however, surgeons have been switching to perfusion systems, which pump warm or cold fluids into an organ. This can double the time it can be held before it must be transplanted.

大多数用于移植的器官在特殊的冰桶中只能存活几个小时，必须争分夺秒地运送到目的地。然而，在过去十年中，外科医生们开始转向使用灌注系统，通过泵将温热或冷却的液体注入器官。这可以将器官在移植前的保存时间延长一倍。

Pulitano says the key to breaking the record was to automate the systems used to keep the organ thriving. During a meeting in Switzerland earlier this year, the Sydney team described perfusing 55 human livers, one after the other.

普利塔诺表示，打破纪录的关键在于实现维持器官活力的系统自动化。在今年早些时候于瑞士举行的一次会议上，悉尼团队描述了他们先后对 55 个人体肝脏进行灌注的过程。

Some were connected to a system that needed to be adjusted manually, but for those placed on the automated system—which produced the seven-week survivor—the average survival time tripled, to about four weeks.

其中一些肝脏连接的是需要手动调节的系统，但对于那些使用自动化系统的肝脏（包括那颗存活了七周的肝脏）来说，平均存活时间增加了两倍，达到约四周。

Perfusion has already enabled doctors to restart hearts from dead people and keep them beating outside the body. Paulo Martins, a transplant doctor at the Oklahoma University, says about half the kidneys he transplants are now perfused, even though the process can add $100,000 to the cost of a transplant.

灌注技术已经使医生能够重启已故者的心脏，并使其在体外持续跳动。俄克拉荷马大学的移植医生保罗·马丁斯（Paulo Martins）表示，他所移植的肾脏中约有一半现在都经过了灌注处理，尽管这一过程可能会使移植成本增加 10 万美元。

While the research awaits formal publication, the eventual goal is what Pulitano and colleagues call “time-unlimited liver preservation.” Scientists have already discovered possible medical benefits to keeping organs on life support longer than usual.

虽然这项研究尚待正式发表，但普利塔诺及其同事的最终目标是实现他们所称的“时间无限的肝脏保存”。科学家们已经发现，将器官维持在生命支持系统下的时间比通常情况更长，可能具有潜在的医学益处。

One, they say, is that perfused organs apparently start to repair themselves and become stronger after a day or so. That’s what led a Swiss team, in 2022, to transplant a damaged liver that had been kept on perfusion for three days—the longest time any internal organ had remained outside the body before a successful transplant, says Waldemar Hoffmann, the CEO and cofounder of Apersys, a startup formed to commercialize the technology.

他们表示，其中一个益处是，经过灌注的器官在一天左右后似乎会开始自我修复并变得更强健。Apersys 公司的首席执行官兼联合创始人瓦尔德马·霍夫曼（Waldemar Hoffmann）表示，这促使瑞士团队在 2022 年移植了一颗经过三天灌注的受损肝脏，这是内脏器官在成功移植前在体外停留的最长时间。Apersys 是一家旨在将该技术商业化的初创公司。

Pulitano says his own startup, Aeternum Life Technologies, will be helping with a planned trial in Sydney to transplant low-quality livers kept on perfusion for four days.

普利塔诺表示，他自己的初创公司 Aeternum Life Technologies 将协助在悉尼进行一项计划中的试验，旨在移植经过四天灌注的低质量肝脏。

What’s less clear, says Hoffmann, is whether any transplant surgeon would ever want, or need, to use an organ kept alive for nearly two months. He says people in the field believe Pulitano’s record is real, but they don’t know how healthy the organ was at the end or even what so much extra time is likely to achieve.

霍夫曼指出，目前尚不清楚是否有移植外科医生愿意或需要使用一颗存活了近两个月的器官。他说，业内人士相信普利塔诺的纪录是真实的，但他们不知道该器官在最后阶段的健康状况如何，甚至不清楚如此长的时间能带来什么实际成果。

“The key question is what the purpose for a seven-week perfusion would be,” he says. Yet that’s where things could start to get interesting, says Martins, the Oklahoma surgeon.

“关键问题是，进行七周灌注的目的是什么？”他问道。然而，俄克拉荷马州的外科医生马丁斯认为，这正是事情开始变得有趣的地方。

Long-term life support could enable far-out concepts like artificial wombs, new types of organ manufacturing, and the chance to experiment directly on human organs outside the body for extended periods.

长期生命支持系统可能实现一些前沿概念，如人造子宫、新型器官制造，以及在体外对人体器官进行长时间直接实验的机会。

For his part, Pulitano says long-term machine perfusion could help grow new organs. In living donations, surgeons commonly take half of a person’s liver to give someone else. After about two months, both parts will have grown back almost to their full volume.

普利塔诺认为，长期机器灌注有助于培育新器官。在活体捐献中，外科医生通常会切取捐献者一半的肝脏给他人。大约两个月后，两部分的肝脏都会生长回几乎完整的体积。

Pulitano says he’s tried dividing a liver and then attaching the sections to the machine. If that works, he says, it could be a way to multiply one donor liver into several full-size organs.

普利塔诺表示，他已经尝试过将肝脏分割，然后将这些部分连接到机器上。他说，如果成功，这可能成为将一颗捐赠肝脏转化为多个完整尺寸器官的方法。

“It’s important to have people thinking outside the box and pushing the limits and creating possibilities for things that, right now, sometimes we don’t even have a need for but are going to be needed in the future,” says Martins. “That’s how science moves.”

“重要的是要有人跳出思维定式，挑战极限，并为那些我们目前可能还不需要、但未来将会需要的技术创造可能性，”马丁斯说，“科学就是这样进步的。”

He’s not the only one who thinks so. For instance, a US company, Bexorg, has been reanimating human brains using perfusion pumps. The idea is to study what effects experimental drugs have on them. That company perfuses brains for about a day but has said it wants to extend the time to two weeks.

他并非唯一持此观点的人。例如，美国公司 Bexorg 一直在使用灌注泵复苏人体大脑，其目的是研究实验性药物对大脑的影响。该公司目前对大脑进行约一天的灌注，但已表示希望将时间延长至两周。

Similar experiments can be done on livers, says Pulitano, who has already tried adding gene therapies to the organs. “It gives us a living human organ model for translational research that no animal system can match, because the biology, the immunology, and the drug responses are human from the outset,” he says.

普利塔诺表示，类似的实验也可以在肝脏上进行，他已经尝试过将基因疗法应用于这些器官。“它为我们提供了一个转化研究的活体人体器官模型，这是任何动物系统都无法比拟的，因为其生物学、免疫学和药物反应从一开始就是人类的，”他说。

Meanwhile, in Silicon Valley, patents filed by Altos Labs, a billionaire-backed longevity startup, describe attempts to introduce reprogramming genes into animal livers maintained outside the body.

与此同时，在硅谷，由亿万富翁支持的长寿初创公司 Altos Labs 提交的专利描述了将重编程基因引入体外维持的动物肝脏的尝试。

Eventually, Altos scientists have speculated, they might temporarily disconnect a failing organ while it’s still in a person’s body, connecting it to a perfusion machine in order to douse it with ultra-powerful genetic treatments without risking the rest of the body.

Altos 的科学家推测，最终他们可能会在器官仍处于人体内时将其暂时断开，连接到灌注机上，以便在不危及身体其他部位的情况下，对其进行超强效的基因治疗。

Doctors probing the limits of organ life support have now formed two new scientific societies to explore the concept. One, founded by Martins and called the International Society for Organ Preservation and Perfusion Therapy, will hold its inaugural meeting this year in Italy.

探索器官生命支持极限的医生们现已成立了两个新的科学协会来研究这一概念。其中一个由马丁斯创立，名为“国际器官保存与灌注治疗学会”（International Society for Organ Preservation and Perfusion Therapy），将于今年在意大利举行首次会议。

The other, based in China, was launched in August to advance what doctors at the First Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-Sen University have termed “organ medicine”: research at the level of whole organs kept a

另一个协会总部位于中国，于八月成立，旨在推进中山大学附属第一医院医生所称的“器官医学”：即在保持器官整体存活水平上的研究。
