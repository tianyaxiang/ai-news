A human liver has been kept alive outside the body for a record seven weeks. Researchers in Australia claim they’ve kept a human liver alive on life support outside the body for seven weeks, smashing the previous record of about two weeks. They achieved the feat by connecting a donated liver to a system of automated pumps and sensors that fed the organ nutrients, supplied it with blood, and whisked away waste. The approach could open up new possibilities in biomedical engineering, from repairing damaged organs to growing new ones. Some researchers are even exploring artificial wombs. Take a closer look at the technology keeping human organs alive. —Antonio Regalado

一颗人类肝脏在体外存活了创纪录的七周。澳大利亚的研究人员声称，他们通过生命支持系统让一颗人类肝脏在体外存活了七周，打破了之前约两周的纪录。他们通过将捐赠的肝脏连接到一套自动泵和传感器系统来实现这一壮举，该系统为器官提供营养、输送血液并清除废物。这种方法可能为生物医学工程开辟新的可能性，从修复受损器官到培育新器官。一些研究人员甚至正在探索人造子宫。深入了解这项维持人类器官存活的技术。——安东尼奥·雷加拉多（Antonio Regalado）

What a billion-dollar deal says about the US battery market. After a tough few years littered with high-profile failures, there’s some positive news for next-generation batteries in the US: Sila has signed a deal with battery maker Panasonic Energy to supply silicon-carbon anode material that could be worth more than $1 billion. The agreement will help Sila scale production, potentially bringing smaller, lighter batteries to market. But it also reveals how battery politics have shifted. The factory is backed by a $1.4 billion loan from the Trump administration’s Defense Department—and the industry is increasingly focused on geopolitics rather than the energy transition. Find out what this deal reveals about the US battery industry. —Casey Crownhart

十亿美元的交易揭示了美国电池市场的现状。在经历了充满高调失败的艰难几年后，美国下一代电池领域传来了一些积极消息：Sila 与电池制造商松下能源（Panasonic Energy）签署了一项协议，供应价值可能超过 10 亿美元的硅碳负极材料。该协议将帮助 Sila 扩大生产规模，有望将更小、更轻的电池推向市场。但这同时也揭示了电池政治的转变。该工厂获得了特朗普政府国防部 14 亿美元的贷款支持，而该行业正日益关注地缘政治，而非能源转型。了解这笔交易揭示了美国电池行业的哪些信息。——凯西·克朗哈特（Casey Crownhart）

One more thing: China has approved the world’s first invasive brain-computer chip—here’s what’s next. Sitting in the courtyard of his house in China’s Henan province last October, Dong Hui decided to try holding a pen. Six years after a car accident left him paralyzed from the neck down, he slowly wrote his name, “Thank you,” and the date. The breakthrough was made possible by a brain implant called NEO. In March, it became the world’s first invasive brain-computer interface approved for use beyond clinical trials.
