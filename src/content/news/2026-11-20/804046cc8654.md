Over the past week, anyone could be forgiven for wondering whether a Diehard IV–style cyberwar had finally arrived in the US. Across just four consecutive business days, a mass Microsoft exchange outage broke IT services for thousands of companies, a Federal Aviation Administration tech screw-up grounded airplanes across the country, a software problem affecting JPMorgan Chase caused another round of disruption, a United Airlines system glitch delayed flights yet again, and an NSA classified network’s downtime left the agency’s staff sitting at home waiting to return to their office at Fort Meade.

在过去的一周里，如果有人怀疑《虎胆龙威4》式的网络战争是否已经在美国爆发，那是可以被原谅的。在短短四个连续的工作日内，微软Exchange的大规模故障导致数千家公司的IT服务中断，美国联邦航空管理局的技术失误导致全国航班停飞，摩根大通的软件问题引发了新一轮的混乱，联合航空的系统故障再次导致航班延误，而美国国家安全局（NSA）机密网络的中断则让该机构的工作人员只能待在家里，等待重返位于米德堡的办公室。

In the midst of a US war with Iran and rising fears of AI-enabled hacking, this still-unfolding comedy of IT errors colliding across American infrastructure felt to plenty of observers like more than a coincidence. Yet there’s still no evidence that any one of these clustered technological failures were related, much less caused by hackers. Their improbable, sequential timing left Americans anxiously doomscrolling for signs of foul play.

在美国与伊朗的战争背景下，以及对人工智能驱动的黑客攻击日益增长的担忧中，这场在美国基础设施上不断上演的IT错误闹剧，在许多观察家看来绝非巧合。然而，目前没有任何证据表明这些集中出现的技术故障之间存在关联，更不用说是由黑客造成的了。它们那不可思议的连续发生时间，让美国人焦虑地刷着手机，寻找恶意破坏的迹象。

But in cases like these, it’s the job of cybersecurity analysts (and reporters at WIRED) to determine—vibes aside—whether the latest tech outage was actually an accident or the result of a cyberattack. The baseline for that fact-finding, as anyone tasked with investigating these incidents will tell you, is that the vast majority of these digital breakdowns are not the result of malicious intent but rather casualties of IT admins’ constant war to keep computer systems from collapsing under the weight of their own complexity.

但在这种情况下，网络安全分析师（以及《连线》杂志的记者）的工作就是排除直觉，确定最近的技术故障究竟是意外还是网络攻击的结果。正如任何负责调查此类事件的人所言，事实调查的基准是：绝大多数此类数字故障并非出于恶意，而是IT管理员为了防止计算机系统在自身复杂性的重压下崩溃而进行的持续斗争中的牺牲品。

“I’ve just seen enough times where we thought maybe it’s a cyberattack and it turns out it’s not. Sometimes it will be, but typically not,” says Matthew Prince, CEO of the Internet infrastructure company Cloudflare. “Right now, most of what we’re seeing is the standard background noise. Stuff breaks, and that’s just the nature of complex systems.”

互联网基础设施公司Cloudflare的首席执行官马修·普林斯（Matthew Prince）表示：“我见过太多次我们以为可能是网络攻击，结果却并非如此的情况。有时确实是，但通常不是。目前，我们看到的大部分情况都是标准的背景噪音。东西坏了，这就是复杂系统的本质。”

So in the interest of soothing the country’s rattled nerves and helping us all find ground truth amid seemingly endless tech catastrophes, we at WIRED present our general guide to determining whether a tech outage is the result of a cyberattack. So take a breath—maybe open the bunker door to let in some fresh air—and read on.

因此，为了安抚全国紧张的神经，并帮助我们在看似无休止的技术灾难中找到真相，《连线》杂志特此提供了一份通用指南，用于判断技术故障是否由网络攻击引起。所以，深呼吸——也许可以打开地堡的门透透气——然后继续阅读。

1. Start with a presumption of innocence–or negligence
The process of determining the cause of an IT service outage should begin with the assumption that it was an accident, says cybersecurity researcher Doug Madory. As the head of Internet analysis at the network security firm Infoblox, Madory says he monitors as many as a dozen network outages a day. Only around “one in a thousand,” he says, turns out to have been caused by hackers. “One’s default position should be that it isn’t a cyberattack,” he says, “unless there is some concrete evidence to the contrary.”

1. 从无罪——或疏忽——推定开始
网络安全研究员道格·马多里（Doug Madory）表示，确定IT服务中断原因的过程应从假设其为意外开始。作为网络安全公司Infoblox的互联网分析主管，马多里称他每天监测多达十几起网络故障。他说，其中只有大约“千分之一”是由黑客造成的。“除非有确凿证据表明事实相反，否则一个人的默认立场应该是这并非网络攻击，”他说。

Given the rarity of cyberattacks compared to outages as a whole, it’s wise to look at other potential causes first. “Most big outages that look like attacks are self-inflicted,” says João Tomé, the Internet research editor at network data company IPinfo. (In response to WIRED’s questions, Tomé also wrote his own guide for distinguishing outages from cyberattacks.) The usual suspects, he says, include “a bad update, a configuration change, a cable cut, or a power failure.”

考虑到网络攻击在所有故障中所占比例极低，首先考虑其他潜在原因是很明智的。网络数据公司IPinfo的互联网研究编辑若昂·托梅（João Tomé）表示：“大多数看起来像攻击的大型故障都是自找的。”（在回应《连线》杂志的问题时，托梅也写了他自己区分故障与网络攻击的指南。）他说，常见的嫌疑因素包括“错误的更新、配置更改、电缆切断或电源故障”。

2. Look at status updates from organizations that might be involved
In the scramble to understand what’s really happening with a given outage, it can sometimes be difficult to tell which sites and services are actually down. And there can be a few reasons for this muddled picture. Sometimes, an outage occurs within a foundational service like a big cloud provider. Major downtime within Amazon Web Services, for example, can cause a cascade of other failures among the company’s customers, who are all dependent on that infrastructure.

2. 查看可能涉及的组织的最新状态
在试图弄清楚某次故障究竟发生了什么时，有时很难分辨哪些网站和服务真正瘫痪了。造成这种混乱局面的原因可能有几个。有时，故障发生在大型云服务提供商等基础服务内部。例如，亚马逊网络服务（AWS）的大规模停机可能会导致其客户出现一系列连锁故障，因为这些客户都依赖于该基础设施。

In other cases, multiple services can seem to be having outages and producing errors but are functioning normally and only having problems when they interact with an entity that’s down. This appears to have been the case in this week’s bank outage: Multiple banks appeared to be having issues, but they all seem to have been linked to certain inter-institutional transactions that involved JPMorgan Chase.

在其他情况下，多个服务看起来似乎出现了故障并产生错误，但实际上它们运行正常，只是在与已瘫痪的实体交互时才出现问题。本周的银行故障似乎就是这种情况：多家银行似乎都出现了问题，但它们似乎都与涉及摩根大通的某些机构间交易有关。

Another good example of this scenario is situations where it may appear that multiple wireless carriers are struggling, but all of the failed calls actually involve one network that’s having an outage. On top of these, there are also scenarios where one or more large services are down, and false (typically accidental or accidental or anecdotal) reports of additional outages create further confusion and opacity.

这种情况的另一个好例子是，当多家无线运营商看起来都在挣扎时，但所有失败的通话实际上都涉及同一个正在发生故障的网络。除此之外，还有一种情况是，一个或多个大型服务瘫痪，而关于额外故障的虚假（通常是偶然的或传闻的）报告造成了进一步的混乱和不透明。

It can all feel overwhelming, even impossible, to attempt to parse these situations yourself—but there is a concrete place to start. If you think a website or service from a major tech company may be down, look for acknowledgment of the situation on their verified social media accounts or in the “newsroom” press release section of their websites. And keep in mind that you can find these pages and social media accounts through search engines, so you definitely don’t need to be familiar with every company’s communications strategy. Use this same approach for government agencies and other institutions.

试图自己分析这些情况可能会让人感到不知所措，甚至是不可能的——但有一个具体的切入点。如果你认为某家大型科技公司的网站或服务可能瘫痪了，请查看其经过验证的社交媒体账户或其网站“新闻室”新闻稿部分对该情况的说明。请记住，你可以通过搜索引擎找到这些页面和社交媒体账户，所以你绝对不需要熟悉每家公司的沟通策略。对政府机构和其他机构也采用同样的方法。

Another concrete and vital source of information that you can find through search engines is what’s often known as “status dashboard” pages. Large digital platforms specifically offer these informational pages to provide transparency into whether different components of their service are functioning normally or dealing with issues. In a major incident, companies often thread incremental updates about system restoration on the status dashboard.
