There’s something about watching violent events that broadly appeals to us, whether it’s the latest John Wick movie, a video game, or a live UFC bout. Scientists have debated whether watching violent things could make us more aggressive, but it could also be an adaptive survival strategy.

观看暴力事件对我们有着广泛的吸引力，无论是最新的《疾速追杀》电影、电子游戏，还是现场直播的终极格斗冠军赛（UFC），皆是如此。科学家们一直在争论观看暴力内容是否会让我们变得更具攻击性，但这或许也是一种适应性的生存策略。

Male mice, for instance, who watched other mice fighting learned enough about how to fight to better protect themselves (compared to mice who didn’t watch fights) when pitted against stronger, more aggressive opponents, according to a paper published in the journal Nature. The team randomly assigned male mice to three groups—fighters, spectators, or novices—and then subjected each group to eight days of training with three 10-minute sessions per day.

例如，根据发表在《自然》杂志上的一篇论文，观察过其他小鼠打斗的雄性小鼠，在面对更强大、更具攻击性的对手时，学会了足够的打斗技巧以更好地保护自己（与未观看打斗的小鼠相比）。研究团队将雄性小鼠随机分为三组——格斗者、旁观者或新手——并对每组进行了为期八天的训练，每天进行三次10分钟的训练。

The novices played with stuffed mice in isolation, while the spectators watched various pairs of fighter mice battle from a raised platform behind a transparent barrier—a set-up chosen to let them smell and listen to the fights as well as observe them. The ninth day was “fight night,” when all the mice had to face a daunting opponent in three 4-minute rounds.

新手小鼠在隔离环境中与填充玩具鼠玩耍，而旁观者小鼠则在透明屏障后的高台上观看多对格斗小鼠的战斗——这种设置旨在让它们不仅能观察战斗，还能闻到和听到战斗的声音。第九天是“格斗之夜”，所有小鼠都必须在三轮4分钟的比赛中面对强大的对手。

Each fight was filmed from above so that the mouse behavior could be analyzed. To monitor corresponding changes in brain activity, the team engineered the rodents’ excitatory brain cells to glow green whenever they were activated, while inhibitory brain cells glowed red. They also implanted tiny optical fibers into 12 interconnected brain regions so they could track changing activity in every brain region simultaneously.

每场战斗都从上方拍摄，以便分析小鼠的行为。为了监测大脑活动的相应变化，研究团队对啮齿动物的兴奋性脑细胞进行了改造，使其在激活时发出绿光，而抑制性脑细胞则发出红光。他们还在12个相互连接的脑区植入了微小的光纤，以便同时追踪每个脑区的活动变化。

How did they get the mice to fight? “Mice are naturally territorial,” co-author Jorge Iravedra-Garcia of Princeton University told Ars. “A traditional behavioral assay that experimenters use is called resident intruder. You just drop mice that are bred to be slightly weaker, and a little bit more docile, into the home cage of strains we know are more on the aggressive side. Those strains are typically very good at just naturally evoking that behavior.”

他们是如何让小鼠打斗的呢？普林斯顿大学的合著者豪尔赫·伊拉维德拉-加西亚（Jorge Iravedra-Garcia）告诉《Ars》：“小鼠天生具有领地意识。实验人员使用的一种传统行为分析方法被称为‘居住者-入侵者’测试。你只需将培育得稍微弱小、性格温顺一点的小鼠放入我们已知更具攻击性的品系小鼠的笼子里。这些品系通常非常擅长自然地诱发这种行为。”

So the fights weren’t entirely fair. “We tip the scales in favor of having a clear winner and a clear loser by having the clear winner be at home, be bigger, and be more experienced,” co-author Annegret Falkner, also of Princeton, told Ars. “We really wanted to show that these changes are adaptive in some way. Animals don’t know what the outcome of a fight is before they enter it. So we set up a situation where the outcome is a little bit more uncertain for the mouse.”

因此，这些战斗并不完全公平。同样来自普林斯顿大学的合著者安内格雷特·福克纳（Annegret Falkner）告诉《Ars》：“我们通过让获胜者处于主场、体型更大且经验更丰富，从而使天平向产生明确的赢家和输家倾斜。我们确实想证明这些变化在某种程度上是适应性的。动物在进入战斗前并不知道结果如何。所以我们设置了一个对小鼠来说结果更具不确定性的情境。”

The results: The spectator mice ended up being better prepared for fight night—despite being weaker, less aggressive strains—because they watched fighter mice during the training sessions. Specifically, both spectators and fighters spent less time with their backs turned toward opponents than novice mice, and were also quicker to attack. They also used similar moves.

结果显示：旁观者小鼠在“格斗之夜”表现得准备更充分——尽管它们属于更弱小、攻击性更弱的品系——这是因为它们在训练期间观察了格斗小鼠。具体来说，与新手小鼠相比，旁观者和格斗者背对对手的时间更少，攻击速度也更快。它们还使用了相似的动作。

Granted, they weren’t quite as skilled as their fighter-mouse brethren; practical experience still mattered. But they learned enough through observation to perform better than the novices. That behavioral change was mirrored in the neural data: the brains of the spectator mice altered to more closely resemble the brains of the fighting mice across all 12 of the interconnected regions.

诚然，它们并不像格斗小鼠同伴那样熟练；实践经验仍然很重要。但它们通过观察学到了足够多的东西，表现得比新手更好。这种行为变化在神经数据中得到了反映：旁观者小鼠的大脑在所有12个相互连接的区域中，变得更接近格斗小鼠的大脑。

During training, the spectator inhibitory cells resembled a fighter’s, while the excitatory cells were closer to those of novice mice, but that changed on fight night, when both types of cells more closely resembled those of fighting mice. “When you’re watching a fight, you’re paying attention to many things at once,” said Iravedra-Garcia. “You’re not just simulating that aggressive behavior in your head, you’re also trying to gauge who’s dangerous, who’s not, who’s actually involved in the fight versus ‘Do I know them? Are they novel to me?’”

在训练期间，旁观者的抑制性细胞类似于格斗者，而兴奋性细胞则更接近新手小鼠，但在“格斗之夜”情况发生了变化，两种细胞都更接近格斗小鼠。伊拉维德拉-加西亚说：“当你观看一场战斗时，你同时关注着许多事情。你不仅是在脑海中模拟那种攻击行为，还在试图评估谁是危险的，谁不是，谁真正参与了战斗，以及‘我认识他们吗？他们对我来说是陌生的吗？’”

Witnessing aggression leaves a circuit trace that changes how an animal behaves in its next conflict, and that trace looks similar to the one left by fighting itself. “They can watch males walking around all they want and none of this happens,” said Falkner. “There’s something specifically salient and arousing about attack that makes them both want to attend to it and also drives this specific plasticity mechanism. That’s one of the open questions: why is attack so sticky? Probably because it creates these adaptive behavioral changes.”

目睹攻击行为会留下一种回路痕迹，改变动物在下一次冲突中的行为方式，而这种痕迹看起来与实际打斗留下的痕迹相似。福克纳说：“它们可以随意观看雄性小鼠走来走去，但这些都不会发生。攻击行为中存在某种特别显著且令人兴奋的东西，既让它们想要关注，又驱动了这种特定的可塑性机制。这是一个悬而未决的问题：为什么攻击行为如此‘粘人’？可能是因为它产生了这些适应性的行为变化。”

There have been similar studies, notably with macaques. According to Falkner, this latest study adds to that body of research by pointing to a specific mechanism for the learned behavior. “There’s this broad decrease in regions that are known to provide inhibitory control for some of these aggression control networks,” she said. “This one piece of the aggression network is really just the tip of the iceberg in terms of how global networks for aggression control respond during these observed activities. I think these subcortical subnetworks are really recurrent. It’s a tiny piece of a larger network.”

此前已有类似研究，特别是针对猕猴的研究。据福克纳称，这项最新研究通过指出学习行为的具体机制，丰富了该领域的研究成果。她说：“在已知为某些攻击控制网络提供抑制性控制的区域中，存在广泛的活性降低。就全球攻击控制网络在这些观察活动中的反应而言，攻击网络的这一部分仅仅是冰山一角。我认为这些皮层下子网络确实是循环的。这只是更大网络中的一小部分。”

It’s an open question whether such findings in mice could extend to humans. “Although it’s difficult to draw a one-to-one parallel, these circuits are extremely conserved, especially these brain regions that seem to show this robust change in inhibitory tone,” said Falkner. “We think about violence as being maladaptive in human culture, but if you are a mouse or an animal who’s around a lot of aggression, it’s actually highly adaptive for you to be ready to fight at any particular time.”

小鼠身上的这些发现是否能延伸到人类身上，还是一个悬而未决的问题。福克纳说：“虽然很难进行一一对应，但这些回路是非常保守的，尤其是那些似乎表现出抑制性基调强烈变化的大脑区域。我们在人类文化中认为暴力是不适应的，但如果你是一只小鼠或生活在充满攻击性环境中的动物，随时准备战斗实际上是高度适应性的。”

The same could be true of humans. The authors plan to extend their research to determine whether female mice watching male mice duking it out changes their mating preferences.
