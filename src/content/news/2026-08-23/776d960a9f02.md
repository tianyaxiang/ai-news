# The Rate Floor Doesn't Exist: Tech Contracting Has Become a Race the Market Never Agreed to Run
# 费率底线并不存在：科技外包已演变成一场市场从未同意参与的竞赛

Contractor rates are falling, contract durations are shrinking, and the freelance labor market is flooding with senior talent — and the problem isn't the market, it's that contractors keep letting companies define the terms.
外包费率正在下降，合同期限正在缩短，自由职业劳动力市场充斥着资深人才——问题不在于市场，而在于外包人员总是任由公司来定义条款。

A senior backend engineer — eight years of production experience, solid Go and Kubernetes chops, three reference clients — recently told a recruiter she was looking for £650 a day. The recruiter called back two days later to say the client had found someone at £450. The counter-offer was presented as good news.
一位资深后端工程师——拥有八年生产环境经验、扎实的 Go 和 Kubernetes 技术功底、三个推荐客户——最近告诉招聘人员，她期望的日薪是 650 英镑。两天后，招聘人员回电称，客户已经以 450 英镑的价格找到了人。这个还价甚至被当作好消息传达。

That's the state of independent tech work right now. Not a crisis, not a correction — something more mundane and more insidious: a slow, structural re-anchoring of what contractor labor is worth, driven less by any single market force than by the compound effect of layoff volumes, budget caution, and platform-mediated price visibility.
这就是目前独立科技工作的现状。这不是危机，也不是修正，而是一种更平庸且更阴险的现象：外包劳动力价值正在经历缓慢的结构性重估。这种重估并非由单一市场力量驱动，而是由裁员规模、预算谨慎以及平台中介带来的价格透明度共同作用的结果。

Rates are going down. Engagements are getting shorter. And the freelancers accepting this are — not entirely without blame — helping it stick. Here's the uncomfortable claim: the ongoing compression of tech contractor rates is as much a self-inflicted wound as a market inevitability. The conditions that caused it are real. But the capitulation that maintains it is a choice.
费率在下降，项目周期在变短。而接受这一切的自由职业者们——并非完全没有责任——正在助长这种趋势。这里有一个令人不安的观点：科技外包费率的持续压缩，既是市场必然，也是一种自残行为。导致这种情况的客观条件是真实的，但维持这种局面的妥协却是一种选择。

### How We Got Here: The Supply Side Exploded
### 我们是如何走到这一步的：供给侧的爆发

The overrecruitment of 2021 and 2022 didn't just hurt the permanent hiring market when the hangover hit. Software developer jobs saw the biggest boom and bust in vacancies of any sector. No other segment saw hiring more than double in 2022, and hiring has since fallen faster in software development than anywhere else.
2021 年和 2022 年的过度招聘在市场泡沫破裂时，不仅伤害了全职招聘市场。软件开发岗位的职位空缺经历了所有行业中最大的繁荣与萧条。没有其他领域在 2022 年的招聘人数翻了一倍以上，而此后软件开发领域的招聘下滑速度也比任何地方都快。

The engineers who got caught in that bust didn't all disappear. Many turned to contracting. More than 100,000 people were laid off in the technology industry in 2024 alone, and at least some of them are not heading back into exclusively full-time work. LinkedIn's Services Marketplace, launched in 2021 to catch exactly this cohort, saw 10 million people create pages on the platform, up 48% in the last year. That number sounds like opportunity. It's actually a description of supply pressure.
在萧条中被裁掉的工程师并没有全部消失，许多人转向了外包。仅 2024 年，科技行业就有超过 10 万人被裁，其中至少有一部分人不再寻求全职工作。LinkedIn 的“服务市场”（Services Marketplace）于 2021 年推出，旨在吸纳这部分人群，目前已有 1000 万人在平台上创建了页面，同比增长 48%。这个数字听起来像是机遇，实际上却是供给压力的写照。

The layoff wave increased the supply of highly skilled senior-plus engineers who used to be hard to hire — and as a result, many companies hired them as permanent employees. The ones who didn't land those roles went freelance, often reluctantly. Now you have the highest concentration of experienced contract-eligible talent in a decade competing for the smallest pool of contract budgets in the same period.
裁员潮增加了高技能资深工程师的供给，这些人过去很难雇佣——因此，许多公司将他们招为全职员工。而那些没能获得这些职位的人则被迫（通常是不情愿地）成为自由职业者。现在，十年来最密集的资深外包人才，正在争夺同期最小规模的外包预算池。

The numbers reflect exactly this. Contractor rates are dropping. Teams are hiring contractors as a last resort. There is less hiring than before, pay is slightly lower, and contract lengths that once ran 12-plus months have compressed to six months or fewer due to budget uncertainty. That last detail matters more than people acknowledge. A 12-month contract at a rate you negotiated once is a fundamentally different economic proposition than a six-month contract you have to renegotiate twice a year into a market that knows you need the work.
数据准确地反映了这一点。外包费率正在下降，团队将外包作为最后的手段。招聘需求减少，薪酬略有下降，且由于预算不确定性，曾经长达 12 个月以上的合同已压缩至 6 个月或更短。最后这一点比人们意识到的更为重要。一份协商好费率的 12 个月合同，与一份必须每年重新协商两次、且市场深知你急需工作的 6 个月合同，在经济本质上是完全不同的。

### The Platform Problem: Both Sides Are Losing
### 平台问题：双方皆输

The freelance platform layer — Upwork, Fiverr, and their peers — was supposed to be the infrastructure that made this market efficient. In the pandemic boom, the theory held. Share prices of Fiverr and Upwork were surging as a new class of knowledge workers opted to work more flexibly, and businesses leaned into on-demand models to fill their needs.
自由职业平台层——Upwork、Fiverr 及其同行——本应是使市场高效的基础设施。在疫情期间的繁荣中，这一理论似乎成立。随着新一代知识工作者选择更灵活的工作方式，企业倾向于按需模式来满足需求，Fiverr 和 Upwork 的股价一度飙升。

That story has since aged poorly. By 2024, freelancer marketplaces were recalibrating their business models after seeing declines in demand, increasing their take rates to keep revenues up as more people opted for steady employment or moved away from these platforms. This is the platform version of a price squeeze: demand falls, so the platform's answer is to extract more from each remaining transaction.
但这个故事后来变得不再动听。到 2024 年，自由职业市场在需求下降后开始重新调整商业模式，通过提高抽成比例来维持收入，因为越来越多的人选择了稳定就业或离开了这些平台。这是平台版的“价格挤压”：需求下降，平台的应对之策就是从每一笔剩余的交易中榨取更多利润。

Fiverr's marketplace revenue in Q2 2026 was $97.8 million compared to $108.6 million in Q2 2025 — a 10% year-over-year decrease — with marketplace revenue specifically declining 15.5% over the same period. What this means in practice is a double compression: rates go down from the demand side while the platform takes a growing cut from the supply side. The freelancer is being squeezed from both ends.
Fiverr 2026 年第二季度的市场收入为 9780 万美元，而 2025 年第二季度为 1.086 亿美元——同比下降 10%，其中市场业务收入同期下降了 15.5%。在实践中，这意味着双重挤压：需求侧导致费率下降，而平台侧则从供给侧抽取更多份额。自由职业者正受到两端的挤压。

### The Structural Trap: When "Flexible" Labor Becomes Permanently Cheap
### 结构性陷阱：当“灵活”劳动力变得永久廉价

There's a version of the contractor story that companies tell with a straight face: we use freelancers for flexibility, for specialist skills, for short-burst capacity we don't need permanently. This is sometimes true. It is also sometimes a polite description of an employment structure that offloads risk and benefits onto the worker while retaining the output.
公司总是面不改色地讲述着关于外包的故事：我们使用自由职业者是为了灵活性、专业技能，以及我们不需要长期投入的短期突发需求。这有时是真的，但有时也是一种委婉的说法，掩盖了这样一种雇佣结构：将风险和福利转嫁给劳动者，同时保留产出。

Layoffs often do not cut costs — there are many instances of laid-off employees being hired back as contractors, with companies paying a contracting firm to make it happen. The Stanford GSB observation is darkly funny: fire the employee, rehire the skill set, pay a middleman, present this as efficiency. The worker ends up with lower effective compensation, no benefits, and a renewable six-month contract. The company gets to claim headcount reduction.
裁员往往并不能削减成本——有许多案例显示，被裁员工以承包商身份被重新聘用，公司为此支付给外包公司中介费。斯坦福商学院（GSB）的观察令人感到一种黑色幽默：解雇员工，重新聘用其技能，支付中介费，并将其包装为“效率”。最终，劳动者获得了更低的实际报酬、没有福利，以及一份可续签的六个月合同，而公司则可以宣称实现了“裁员减负”。
