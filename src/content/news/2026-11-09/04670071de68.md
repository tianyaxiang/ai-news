# I Used NYT Cooking’s New AI-Powered Recipe Modifier, and It’s Actually Pretty Good

Save this story. In an aside in the New York Times Cooking video I watched recently, where Melissa Clark made her lemony white bean soup with turkey and greens, the food reporter and recipe columnist invited viewers to leave a comment telling her what they do with the kale stems. It highlighted this sweet, funny relationship between a media brand’s personalities and its audience, a warm-casual vibe.

保存这篇文章。在最近观看的一段《纽约时报》烹饪视频中，梅丽莎·克拉克（Melissa Clark）制作了她的柠檬白豆火鸡蔬菜汤。在视频的插曲中，这位美食记者兼食谱专栏作家邀请观众留言，分享他们如何处理羽衣甘蓝的茎。这突显了媒体品牌形象与受众之间那种甜蜜、有趣的关系，营造出一种温暖而随意的氛围。

I am not among the multitudes in the Cooking section's commenter army, but a few days later I was very surprised to see some of them in open revolt. I was even more surprised to see why: In an iOS-only beta, the Times is now using AI to allow subscribers to modify recipes.

我并不属于烹饪版块评论大军中的一员，但几天后，我非常惊讶地看到其中一些人公开反抗。更让我惊讶的是原因：在仅限 iOS 系统的测试版中，《纽约时报》现在开始利用人工智能允许订阅者修改食谱。

Below the article announcing the change on October 23, commenters loudly aired their grievances, threatening to cancel subscriptions, asking who wanted this, and how to turn it off. After four days and 172 almost exclusively angry comments, commenting for the story was turned off.

在 10 月 23 日宣布这一变更的文章下方，评论者们大声表达了他们的不满，威胁要取消订阅，质问谁需要这个功能，以及如何将其关闭。在经历了四天 172 条几乎全是愤怒的评论后，该报道的评论功能被关闭了。

The Times doesn't share Cooking's subscriber numbers, but NYT executive communications director Jordan Cohen said the site and app were visited 514 million times in 2025, and its YouTube channel has over 1.4 million subscribers. Reading over the section's AI plans and thinking about how many bungled attempts I'd seen to mush technology into places where it doesn't need to be in the kitchen, I guessed that I would side with the commenters. Then I tried the new AI feature, and found that it worked surprisingly well.

《纽约时报》并未公布其烹饪版块的订阅人数，但该报执行通讯总监乔丹·科恩（Jordan Cohen）表示，其网站和应用程序在 2025 年的访问量达到 5.14 亿次，其 YouTube 频道拥有超过 140 万订阅者。在阅读了该版块的人工智能计划，并想到我曾见过多少次将技术生硬地塞进厨房里那些并不需要它的地方的拙劣尝试后，我猜我会站在评论者这一边。然而，当我尝试了这项新的人工智能功能后，发现它的效果出奇地好。

My writing for WIRED over the past decade has shown me again and again that adding tech to a kitchen product is risky at best. I've repeatedly watched companies try and fail: Bluetooth connections for all the appliances, apps that lean too heavily on crummy recipes, toasters with optical sensors or touchscreens that still couldn't toast particularly well. Just about anything that was part of the "smart kitchen" tended not to be very smart at all.

过去十年里，我为《连线》（WIRED）撰稿的经历一次又一次地向我证明，在厨房产品中加入技术充其量是有风险的。我反复看到企业尝试并失败：为所有电器配备蓝牙连接、过度依赖劣质食谱的应用程序、带有光学传感器或触摸屏却依然烤不出好面包的烤面包机。几乎所有属于“智能厨房”的东西往往一点也不智能。

Developing dependable, delicious recipes is hard, expensive work that can require a whole staff. Yet skimping can hurt the brand. Two notable companies with a deep bench—The New York Times Cooking and America's Test Kitchen—are cooking content makers. Recipes, which is to say delicious, high-quality, tested recipes, are what they do.

开发可靠、美味的食谱是一项艰巨且昂贵的工作，可能需要整个团队的参与。然而，偷工减料会损害品牌形象。两家拥有深厚底蕴的知名公司——《纽约时报》烹饪版块和美国测试厨房（America's Test Kitchen）——都是烹饪内容的创作者。食谱，也就是那些美味、高质量、经过测试的食谱，正是他们的核心业务。

Since AI is often a lousy chef, giving it a role in the presentation of NYT's gold mine of content is a big risk. Earlier this year, the Times allowed AI to help with a recipe-scaling feature (modifying ingredient quantities so "serves 4" turns into "serves 6") and converting from imperial measurements to metric—cups, tablespoons, and Fahrenheit to grams, milliliters, and Celsius. Now, in the iOS-only beta, a "customize this recipe" field appears just above the ingredient list.

由于人工智能往往是个糟糕的厨师，让它参与《纽约时报》这一内容金矿的呈现是一项巨大的风险。今年早些时候，《纽约时报》允许人工智能辅助食谱缩放功能（修改配料数量，将“4 人份”变为“6 人份”），并进行英制到公制的转换——将杯、汤匙和华氏度转换为克、毫升和摄氏度。现在，在仅限 iOS 的测试版中，配料表上方出现了一个“自定义此食谱”的字段。

If you want to make banana bread but tell it you don't have a loaf pan, it'll ask you what kind of pans you do have, then modify the recipe to suit. All the changes throughout the recipe are noted in a visible red-orange text. Over several days of testing, I saw how you can do relatively simple things like swap ground pork for ground beef, or make more complicated demands like asking it to tailor things to your dietary needs. The modifications it makes are very visible throughout the recipe. If you ask it for something too out there—like turning Eric Kim's gochujang buttered noodles into a cookie—it gives you a "hard redirect" and points you to a better-suited recipe, which in this case is Kim's gochujang caramel cookies.

如果你想做香蕉面包，但告诉它你没有长方形烤盘，它会询问你有什么样的烤盘，然后相应地修改食谱。食谱中所有的改动都以醒目的红橙色文字标出。经过几天的测试，我发现你可以进行相对简单的操作，比如用猪肉末代替牛肉末，或者提出更复杂的要求，比如让它根据你的饮食需求进行调整。它所做的修改在整个食谱中非常显眼。如果你提出的要求过于离谱——比如想把埃里克·金（Eric Kim）的韩式辣酱黄油面做成饼干——它会给你一个“强制重定向”，并为你指向更合适的食谱，在这种情况下，就是金的韩式辣酱焦糖饼干。

"We put a lot of effort into trying to tell you when not to do something if it's no longer in the spirit of the recipe," says Camilla Velasquez, the general manager of NYT Cooking. For its recommendations, the LLM looks at the recipe itself, the Times’ ingredient substitution guide, user comments, and then what it calls AI "drawing on our expertise," in that order. Velasquez says the AI should be a helper, not the main act, noting, "You shouldn't be chatting with it."

“如果某种做法不再符合食谱的初衷，我们会投入大量精力去提醒你不要这样做，”《纽约时报》烹饪版块总经理卡米拉·贝拉斯克斯（Camilla Velasquez）表示。在提供建议时，大语言模型会依次参考食谱本身、《纽约时报》的配料替换指南、用户评论，以及它所谓的“借鉴我们的专业知识”。贝拉斯克斯表示，人工智能应该是一个助手，而不是主角，并指出：“你不应该和它聊天。”

She explains that when the Times built the AI functionality, recipe editors helped create the original rule set, then evaluated how it worked at the end. When I ask what this means for the jobs of 60 editors, writers, video journalists, and other employees—the team that creates the recipes people use and curates the vibe readers relate to—Velasquez stresses that NYT Cooking has been "hiring more than ever" and that it has "zero intention of publishing fewer recipes."

她解释说，当《纽约时报》构建这项人工智能功能时，食谱编辑们帮助创建了最初的规则集，并在最后评估了其运行效果。当我问及这对 60 名编辑、撰稿人、视频记者和其他员工的工作意味着什么——即那个创造人们使用的食谱并策划读者所共鸣的氛围的团队——贝拉斯克斯强调，《纽约时报》烹饪版块一直在“比以往任何时候都更多地招聘”，并且“完全没有减少发布食谱的打算”。

So far, she says people are mainly using it for ingredient and equipment substitutions, along with adjusting recipes for dietary needs. "Some people use the chatbot to scale, or they'll scale to match an ingredient," giving the example of someone who had only one cup of oil for a recipe that needed more. As a food writer whose career has taken some substantial turns due to AI recently, I wouldn't say I've been clamoring for this, but I find the Times’ measured approach works pretty well.

到目前为止，她说人们主要将其用于配料和设备的替换，以及根据饮食需求调整食谱。“有些人使用聊天机器人进行缩放，或者为了匹配某种配料而进行缩放，”她举例说，有人在食谱需要更多油的情况下，手里却只有一杯油。作为一名职业生涯最近因人工智能而发生重大转变的美食作家，我不会说我一直渴望这个功能，但我发现《纽约时报》这种审慎的方法效果相当不错。

I started testing it out by making chicken and dumplings using a five-star Alison Roman recipe with more than 6,000 ratings. I asked the AI tool to swap out bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs for boneless, skinless breasts. It thought about it for a moment—"simmering" in AI Cooking patois—and then offered up "Chicken Breast and Dumplings," with the "breast" in the title rendered in the red-orange color that indicates a modification. Just below the title, it said it was "Joseph's version made with AI. Not tested or endorsed by the original recipe's creators." Along with those visible changes, I could also toggle back and forth between my version and the original.

我开始测试时，制作了一道艾莉森·罗曼（Alison Roman）的五星级鸡肉饺子，该食谱有超过 6000 条评分。我要求人工智能工具将带骨带皮的鸡腿肉换成去骨去皮的鸡胸肉。它思考了一会儿——在人工智能烹饪术语中称为“炖煮”——然后提供了“鸡胸肉饺子”，标题中的“鸡胸肉”以表示修改的红橙色呈现。在标题下方，它注明这是“约瑟夫（Joseph）的人工智能版本。未经原食谱创作者测试或认可。”除了这些可见的更改外，我还可以随时在我的版本和原始版本之间切换。

Once I started cooking, things went smoothly. I'm a recipe follower, not an improviser, and the Times’ recipes are easy to follow. The modifications were too. I was also impressed by how the app flagged that the chicken breast simmering time would be reduced because it cooks faster than thighs, and since they don't render as much fat as skin-on thighs do, it added some butter to the roux.

一旦开始烹饪，一切都很顺利。我是一个遵循食谱的人，而不是即兴发挥的人，而《纽约时报》的食谱很容易遵循。这些修改后的食谱也是如此。我还对应用程序如何提示鸡胸肉的炖煮时间会缩短印象深刻，因为它比鸡腿肉熟得快；而且由于它们不像带皮鸡腿肉那样能析出那么多油脂，它还在面糊中增加了一些黄油。
