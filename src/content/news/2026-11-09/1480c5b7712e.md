The For You Page Trains Attention to React. What If It Could Train It to Aim? The EU accuses TikTok's 'rabbit hole' algorithm of breaching its content rules. That's the headline this week, and it's worth reading in full — regulators say the For You page needs to change how it works in order to protect users' mental health. You can debate the legal merits for hours. But underneath the regulatory language is a claim most of us already suspect is true: an endlessly optimized feed is very good at capturing attention and very bad at training it. Scrolling teaches your brain to react. It does not teach your brain to aim.

“为你推荐”页面训练的是反应力，如果它能训练专注力呢？欧盟指责 TikTok 的“兔子洞”算法违反了其内容规则。这是本周的头条新闻，值得全文阅读——监管机构表示，“为你推荐”页面需要改变其运作方式，以保护用户的心理健康。你可以花几个小时争论其法律依据，但监管语言背后隐藏着一个我们大多数人都怀疑是事实的观点：一个不断优化的信息流非常擅长捕捉注意力，却非常不擅长训练注意力。滑动屏幕教会了你的大脑如何反应，却没能教会它如何瞄准。

For most people that's an annoyance. For people with amblyopia, it's a missed opportunity on a much bigger scale — because amblyopia isn't a willpower problem. The two eyes send mismatched information to the brain, and so binocular coordination and depth perception never get the practice they need. The result is a lifetime of small hesitations: catching a ball, parking, judging a step, reading a room. I've been thinking about this less as a policy story and more as an engineering story. Because the interesting question isn't whether feeds should change. It's what an attention-training system would even look like if you built one on purpose — and why the tools in this space have lagged so far behind the attention economy that surrounds them.

对于大多数人来说，这只是个烦恼。但对于弱视患者而言，这是一个更大规模的错失良机——因为弱视并非意志力问题。双眼向大脑发送不匹配的信息，导致双眼协调能力和深度感知从未得到所需的锻炼。结果就是一生中充满了细小的迟疑：接球、停车、判断台阶、观察环境。我思考这个问题时，将其视为一个工程问题而非政策故事。因为有趣的问题不在于信息流是否应该改变，而在于如果你刻意构建一个注意力训练系统，它会是什么样子，以及为什么该领域的工具远远落后于围绕它们的注意力经济。

The tools have mostly been passive too Traditional approaches are effective but rarely engaging. An adhesive patch over the stronger eye. Red-blue glasses. Both ask for patience and deliver very little feedback. There's no telemetry, no loop, no signal telling you whether today's session moved anything. If your attention is already being competed for by every app on your phone, "sit still and wait" is a hard sell. That's the gap we built Amblyotube to fill. Amblyotube, developed by Seven Sports, is recreational and educational software for visual coordination and attention exercises in VR. It runs on Meta Quest, and its core idea is simple: show a different visual experience to each eye while you watch YouTube-style content.

The architectural bet is worth spelling out, because it's the part that took the longest to get right. A normal headset renders one stereo scene to two panels. Amblyotube renders two divergent pipelines — per-eye compositing with independent occlusion, blur, and highlight layers — and keeps them frame-locked so the brain receives a consistent mismatch instead of a flickering one. Any drift between the eyes and the fusion target falls apart. What actually happens in the headset Start by choosing your affected eye (left or right) and a severity preset — Mild, Moderate, or Severe — or run the LogMAR vision check. Then pick a video you actually like and launch it in a virtual room, or use Room View AR passthrough so you're still aware of your surroundings.

其架构上的投入值得详细说明，因为这是耗时最长、最难攻克的部分。普通的头显将一个立体场景渲染到两个面板上。而 Amblyotube 渲染两条不同的管线——通过独立的遮挡、模糊和高亮层进行单眼合成——并保持帧同步，这样大脑接收到的是一致的差异信息，而不是闪烁的画面。双眼与融合目标之间的任何偏差都会导致融合失败。头显内实际发生的情况是：首先选择你的患眼（左眼或右眼）和严重程度预设（轻度、中度或重度），或者进行 LogMAR 视力检查。然后选择你喜欢的视频并在虚拟房间中播放，或者使用 Room View AR 透视功能，以便你仍能感知周围环境。

From there, the app does three things at once: Dual-eye technology. Your dominant eye gets a gentle, adjustable digital blur via the Dominant Eye Shader. Your lazy eye gets the AI Sharpener (MFBF), which detects human figures in the video and marks them with a bold red outline and a yellow-green highlight, plus a pulsing flicker at a steady rhythm. Optional Wedge Occlusion blacks out part of each eye's view, so each eye sees unique portions of the scene — which cuts down binocular rivalry. Binocular fusion. A bright magenta focus cue appears in the lazy eye's view, and a subtle neutral grey dot appears in the dominant eye's view. As your tracking improves, the two merge into one cohesive 3D image. That merge is the rep that matters: it's the brain learning to fuse both eyes and process true depth. Think of it as a feedback controller where the error signal is binocular disparity, and the reward is convergence. Real-world carryover. After a session you take the headset off. No adhesive patches. No red-blue glasses. Just 30-40 minutes a day with content you'd probably have watched anyway.

Why this matters right now The regulatory fight over the For You page is really a fight about what attention is for. If the answer is "to be harvested," then the incentives will keep pointing that direction. If the answer is "to be trained," you need tools where the user is the one choosing the target. I keep coming back to a simple design principle: the feed should serve the training loop, not the other way around. That's the difference between an algorithm that optimizes for time-on-app and one that optimizes for a measurable skill. Same delivery surface, opposite objective function.
