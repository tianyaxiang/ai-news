Superconductors carry electricity without resistance, but most only work a few degrees above absolute zero. In the late 1980s, copper-based materials called cuprates were found to superconduct at higher temperatures—reachable with affordable liquid nitrogen. Yet nearly 40 years later, physicists still don’t agree on why. In a recent Science study, a team of physicists led by Milan Allan of Ludwig Maximilian University of Munich and Ingmar Swart of Utrecht University in the Netherlands has found some answers by listening to the faint electrical crackle of current flowing into a cuprate.

超导体在没有电阻的情况下传输电流，但大多数超导体只能在绝对零度以上几度时工作。20世纪80年代末，人们发现一种被称为铜氧化物的铜基材料可以在更高的温度下实现超导，而这种温度可以通过廉价的液氮达到。然而近40年过去了，物理学家们对于其中的原因仍未达成共识。在最近发表于《科学》杂志的一项研究中，由慕尼黑大学的米兰·艾伦（Milan Allan）和荷兰乌得勒支大学的英格玛·斯瓦特（Ingmar Swart）领导的物理学家团队，通过监听电流流入铜氧化物时产生的微弱电噪声，找到了一些答案。

Central to the mystery of cuprates’ high-temperature superconductivity is a strange state called the pseudogap. “The pseudogap is a phenomenon of missing electrons,” Allan said. Above the temperature at which cuprates become superconducting, some of their electrons seem to vanish—measurements find far fewer of them at the energies where they should be. “Everybody knows that from different probes, you see that there’s these vanishing electrons, but nobody knows why,” Allan said.

铜氧化物高温超导之谜的核心是一种被称为“赝能隙”（pseudogap）的奇异状态。“赝能隙是一种电子缺失现象，”艾伦说。在铜氧化物转变为超导态的温度之上，它们的部分电子似乎消失了——测量结果显示，在它们本应存在的能级上，电子数量远少于预期。“每个人都知道，通过不同的探测手段，你可以看到这些电子在消失，但没人知道原因，”艾伦说。

Over the decades, physicists have put forward two main competing explanations. The first hypothesizes that the electrons get stuck in some sort of structure. In this view, electrons freeze into patterns, such as charge density waves, and superconductivity develops separately. The alternative explanation tightly links this phenomenon to superconductivity. “Other people think they’re missing because they form pairs,” Allan said. Pairs of electrons, called Cooper pairs, are what carry current in a superconductor. If, as the material cools, these pairs appear before the material becomes superconducting, they would be a precondition of superconductivity, a waiting room of sorts, where pairs exist but can’t yet move together in lockstep.

几十年来，物理学家提出了两种主要的竞争性解释。第一种假设电子被困在某种结构中。在这种观点下，电子冻结成某种模式，例如电荷密度波，而超导性则是独立发展的。另一种解释则将这一现象与超导性紧密联系起来。“其他人认为它们缺失是因为它们形成了配对，”艾伦说。电子对被称为库珀对（Cooper pairs），是超导体中电流的载体。如果随着材料冷却，这些配对在材料进入超导态之前就已出现，那么它们将成为超导性的先决条件，就像一个候车室，配对虽然存在，但还不能同步移动。

The team decided to listen to shot noise—tiny fluctuations caused by the arrival of individual charges that form the stream of an electric current. Allan says it resembles rain drumming on the roof. “If you imagine that you have raindrops and then all of a sudden you make the drops twice as heavy, there’ll be more noise,” Allan said. “With the electrons it’s similar. If you have two electrons coming always together, because they’re associated with pairing, then it will be more noise.”

该团队决定监听“散粒噪声”（shot noise）——即由构成电流流的单个电荷到达时引起的微小波动。艾伦说，这就像雨点敲击屋顶。“如果你想象有雨点落下，然后突然让雨滴的重量增加一倍，噪声就会变大，”艾伦说。“电子的情况也类似。如果你有两个电子总是成对出现，因为它们与配对有关，那么产生的噪声就会更大。”

The team examined crystals of a cuprate known as BSCCO, cooled to a few degrees above absolute zero, and compared the noise to the energies at which electrons go missing. By adjusting the voltage between the tip of the needle and the sample, the team could choose the energy of the electrons they were listening to, a bit like turning a radio dial. If the pseudogap came purely from frozen patterns, the team reasoned, the extra noise of pairing should only kick in at energy levels associated with superconductivity. Instead, pairing showed up right at the pseudogap energy.

该团队检查了一种被称为BSCCO的铜氧化物晶体，将其冷却至绝对零度以上几度，并将噪声与电子缺失的能级进行了比较。通过调节针尖与样品之间的电压，研究小组可以选择他们所监听的电子能量，这有点像转动收音机的旋钮。研究小组推断，如果赝能隙纯粹源于冻结的模式，那么配对产生的额外噪声应该只会在与超导性相关的能级上出现。然而事实相反，配对现象直接出现在赝能隙的能级上。

The decisive check involved placing the samples in magnetic fields of up to six tesla, which suppresses pairing while leaving the overall pattern of missing electrons largely intact. As the magnetic field grew, the noise dropped back toward the single-electron level, exactly as expected if pairs were behind it. The strength of electron pairing can be expressed as the energy required to pull a pair apart. Lowering the temperature helps boost pairing because the warmer a material becomes, the more violently its particles Jostle and the more pairs get broken.

决定性的验证是将样品置于高达6特斯拉的磁场中，这会抑制配对，同时使电子缺失的整体模式基本保持不变。随着磁场增强，噪声回落至单电子水平，这与如果配对是背后原因时的预期完全一致。电子配对的强度可以用拆散一对电子所需的能量来表示。降低温度有助于增强配对，因为材料越热，其粒子碰撞越剧烈，配对就越容易被破坏。

In cuprates, Allan and Swart’s study points to a different sequence. The pairing energy the team measured is well above the temperature at which the material superconducts. “So the picture that emerges is that you first have pre-formed pairs, and then only at lower temperatures, phase coherence sets in,” Swart said. That suggests the superconductivity bottleneck is not getting electrons to pair, but getting the pairs to move together.

在铜氧化物中，艾伦和斯瓦特的研究指向了一个不同的序列。该团队测得的配对能量远高于材料实现超导的温度。“因此，呈现出的图景是：首先形成了预配对，然后只有在更低的温度下，相位一致性才会出现，”斯瓦特说。这表明超导性的瓶颈不在于让电子配对，而在于让这些配对同步移动。
