# A 55-Year-Old Math Problem Was Solved—This Time by Humans

The late Ronald Graham wore two hats. He was a renowned mathematician and, at one time, president of the American Mathematical Society. He was also a serious juggler and president of the International Jugglers’ Association. “He loved tricks,” said Fan Chung, a mathematician at UC San Diego who was married to Graham. “You know, spinning a ball, spinning a coat hanger, spinning several balls together, throwing pens against the wall.”

已故的罗纳德·格雷厄姆（Ronald Graham）拥有双重身份。他是一位著名的数学家，曾担任美国数学学会主席。同时，他也是一位资深的杂耍艺人，担任国际杂耍协会主席。“他热爱戏法，”加州大学圣地亚哥分校的数学家、格雷厄姆的妻子范·钟（Fan Chung）说，“你知道，转球、转衣架、同时转几个球，或者把笔扔向墙壁。”

Sometimes Graham wore both hats at once. “It’s interesting, in fact, that many mathematicians and computer scientists have an interest in juggling,” he said in a 1980 television interview. “I think it’s the search for patterns and structure that is responsible for this.”

有时，格雷厄姆会同时展现这两种身份。“事实上，许多数学家和计算机科学家对杂耍感兴趣，这很有趣，”他在1980年的一次电视采访中说道，“我认为这是因为他们对模式和结构的探索。”

He would go on to write numerous papers, some with Chung, on the mathematics of juggling. But back in 1971, decades before he made that connection explicit, he posed a question that some mathematicians now say might have been inspired by juggling, too.

后来，他撰写了大量关于杂耍数学的论文，其中一些是与范·钟合作完成的。但早在1971年，即他明确提出这种联系的几十年前，他就提出了一个问题，一些数学家现在认为这个问题可能也受到了杂耍的启发。

Start with a random set of different integers, not including zero. Can you always rearrange them so that if you add up the first two numbers, then the first three, then the first four, and so on, every “partial sum” turns out different? In the language of juggling, this would mean that if each ball stays in the air for a different amount of time, you can always find an order to throw them in such that two balls won’t come crashing down on the same beat—which would ruin the act.

从一组不包含零的随机不同整数开始。你是否总能重新排列它们，使得当你把前两个数相加，然后是前三个数，再是前四个数，以此类推时，每一个“部分和”都各不相同？用杂耍的语言来说，这意味着如果每个球在空中的停留时间不同，你总能找到一种投掷顺序，使得两个球不会在同一拍落下——那会毁了表演。

If the numbers are all positive, then the answer to Graham’s question is obviously yes: The sums will always grow larger as you add more numbers. Similarly, if there are both positive and negative numbers in the mix, the answer is also known to be yes. But what if the numbers live in a finite world—like numbers wrapped around a clock, which repeat after a certain count?

如果这些数字都是正数，那么格雷厄姆问题的答案显然是肯定的：随着你加入更多的数字，总和会不断变大。同样，如果混合了正数和负数，答案也被认为是肯定的。但如果这些数字存在于一个有限的世界中呢——就像绕着时钟排列的数字，在达到一定计数后会重复？

That’s what Graham wanted to know. He conjectured that the answer should still be yes. It often happens, he figured, that even when dealing with rigid constraints, you can still find enough flexibility to construct special patterns or structures—just as it’s usually possible to find a valid sudoku board or Latin square (another kind of puzzle) despite their many rules. “It fits nicely in all these questions about designs and about very symmetric structures,” said Noga Alon, a mathematician at Princeton University. But for decades, no one could prove Graham’s intuition to be true.

这正是格雷厄姆想要知道的。他推测答案应该仍然是肯定的。他认为，即使在处理严格的约束条件时，通常也能找到足够的灵活性来构建特殊的模式或结构——就像尽管数独或拉丁方阵（另一种谜题）有许多规则，但通常仍能找到有效的解法一样。“这非常契合所有关于设计和高度对称结构的课题，”普林斯顿大学的数学家诺加·阿隆（Noga Alon）说。但几十年来，没有人能证明格雷厄姆的直觉是正确的。

That changed recently, when several young mathematicians picked up the balls. In a proof that spanned four papers and various fields of mathematics, they finally resolved Graham’s rearrangement conjecture. The final paper, by Lisa Sauermann of the University of Bonn and Huy Tuan Pham of the University of Chicago, appeared in February 2026, officially closing the problem.

这种情况最近发生了改变，几位年轻的数学家接过了接力棒。通过跨越四篇论文和多个数学领域的证明，他们最终解决了格雷厄姆的重排猜想。由波恩大学的丽莎·绍尔曼（Lisa Sauermann）和芝加哥大学的范辉团（Huy Tuan Pham）撰写的最后一篇论文于2026年2月发表，正式终结了这个问题。

Across the papers, one theme prevailed: the power of randomness to draw out patterns. As Alon put it, “It’s the power of collaboration, the power of the young generation, the power of probabilistic methods” that solved the problem.

在这些论文中，一个主题贯穿始终：随机性在提取模式方面的力量。正如阿隆所言，“是合作的力量、年轻一代的力量、概率方法的力量”解决了这个问题。
