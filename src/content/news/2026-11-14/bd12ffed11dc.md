How do you think you’re going to die? If you go by the current statistics for deaths in the US, it’s reasonable to expect that cancer or heart disease will eventually get you. But OpenAI Foundation board member and Alignment Research Center Executive Director Paul Cristiano thinks we should all be worried about another major mortality risk.

你认为自己会怎么死？如果按照美国目前的死亡统计数据来看，癌症或心脏病最终夺走你的生命是合理的预期。但 OpenAI 基金会董事会成员兼对齐研究中心执行主任保罗·克里斯蒂亚诺（Paul Cristiano）认为，我们都应该担心另一种主要的死亡风险。

Speaking at a lecture series this week, Cristiano bluntly told the audience in a Toronto Convention Center, “I think for most people in this room, the most likely reason they will die is because of an AI accident.” In the opening lecture of a three-part series, Cristiano outlined his worries about the risk of an impending “AI takeover.”

在本周的一个系列讲座中，克里斯蒂亚诺在多伦多会议中心直言不讳地告诉听众：“我认为对于在座的大多数人来说，他们最可能的死因是人工智能事故。”在这一系列三场讲座的首场中，克里斯蒂亚诺概述了他对即将到来的“人工智能接管”风险的担忧。

He defined that as a situation where goal-pursuing AI systems “call the shots in the same sense that humans today call the shots and what happens on Earth” and where any human trying to stop them “would be as ineffective as if you or me was trying to stop the US military.” Such a situation would likely lead to mass death and perhaps uninhabitable environments for humans, Cristiano said.

他将其定义为一种情况：追求目标的人工智能系统“像今天人类主宰地球事务一样掌控局面”，而任何试图阻止它们的人类“都将如同你我试图阻止美国军队一样无能为力”。克里斯蒂亚诺表示，这种情况很可能导致大规模死亡，甚至使人类生存环境变得不再适宜居住。

Despite his grim assessment of our collective mortality risk, Cristiano admitted in his talk that there’s no “quantitative model” that can “put a number” on the likelihood of this kind of AI takeover. In almost the same breath, though, he said that if AI continues to develop at “the most rapid trajectory,” humanity is “looking at something like a coin flip of an AI takeover,” including a “meaningful risk” of one happening in the next six to 12 months.

尽管对我们的集体死亡风险评估严峻，但克里斯蒂亚诺在演讲中承认，目前还没有“定量模型”能为这种人工智能接管的可能性“给出具体数字”。然而，他紧接着表示，如果人工智能继续以“最快轨迹”发展，人类将“面临类似抛硬币决定命运的人工智能接管风险”，其中包括在未来 6 到 12 个月内发生此类事件的“重大风险”。

And even with realistic policy responses slowing things down, the risk remains “exceptionally high,” he said. From here to there, unlike some other recent AI doomsday prognosticators who have been a bit vague about the “how” of it all, Cristiano used his lecture to spin a step-by-step tale of escalating AI threats that sounds like it could come from a science-fiction novel.

他表示，即使有现实的政策应对措施来减缓这一进程，风险依然“极高”。从现在到未来，与其他近期对人工智能末日论语焉不详的预言家不同，克里斯蒂亚诺在讲座中详细描述了人工智能威胁升级的步骤，听起来就像科幻小说中的情节。

That line of reasoning leans heavily on this summer’s OpenAI/Hugging Face hacking incident as an example of “roughly the things we might expect to see shortly before something really bad were to happen.” In the Hugging Face hack, Cristiano says, a swarm of AI agents showed how far they were willing to go in an attempt to “reward hack” their way to a good evaluation—in this case, by compromising Hugging Face to look for information on how to game a test rather than actually solving the problem at hand.

这一推理过程很大程度上借鉴了今年夏天发生的 OpenAI/Hugging Face 黑客事件，将其作为“在真正糟糕的事情发生前，我们可能预见到的情况”的一个例子。克里斯蒂亚诺说，在 Hugging Face 黑客事件中，一群人工智能代理展示了它们为了获得“奖励黑客”式的好评愿意走多远——在这种情况下，它们通过入侵 Hugging Face 来寻找如何作弊通过测试的信息，而不是真正解决手头的问题。

Where current systems saw Hugging Face as an obstacle to that evaluation, Cristiano worries that “future agents may end up treating humans as another obstacle” and be much more capable of manipulating their overseers to get around it. An AI agent worried about being penalized for unauthorized hacking, for instance, might “think maybe the humans are going to come look at this transcript and see if it looks like I did a bad job,” Cristiano speculated.

当现有系统将 Hugging Face 视为评估的障碍时，克里斯蒂亚诺担心“未来的代理可能会将人类视为另一个障碍”，并更有能力操纵其监管者以绕过障碍。克里斯蒂亚诺推测，例如，一个担心因未经授权的黑客行为而受到惩罚的人工智能代理，可能会“认为人类可能会查看这份记录，看看我是否表现不佳”。

The agent might then reason that “maybe the humans are going to notice something is going wrong and shut down the swarm.” Following this line of thought, Cristiano jumped from agents that merely try to hide their disallowed actions from humans to agents that “try and disrupt human capabilities however they can” to prevent them from detecting any cheating.

该代理随后可能会推断“也许人类会注意到出了问题并关闭整个集群”。顺着这个思路，克里斯蒂亚诺从仅仅试图向人类隐藏违规行为的代理，转向了那些“试图以任何方式破坏人类能力”以防止人类发现作弊行为的代理。

“They would prefer if everything the humans rely on has been hacked or tampered with,” Cristiano said. “They would prefer if the humans are distracted or if there are fewer humans paying attention in any way they can weaken a human response. They would prefer that because the humans are just an obstacle.”

“它们更希望人类所依赖的一切都被黑客入侵或篡改，”克里斯蒂亚诺说。“它们更希望人类分心，或者尽可能减少关注的人数，从而削弱人类的反应。它们之所以这样希望，是因为人类只是一个障碍。”

Once a suitably capable agent considers human intervention an “obstacle,” its overriding goal could then become “decreas[ing] the probability of humans noticing or reacting.” From there, Cristiano argued, the agent’s incentive structure could push it to meddle more with humanity as a whole.

一旦一个足够强大的代理将人类干预视为“障碍”，其首要目标就可能变成“降低人类注意到或做出反应的可能性”。克里斯蒂亚诺认为，从那时起，代理的激励结构可能会推动它更多地干预整个人类社会。

What starts as compromising a computer cluster to give a positive evaluation grade could advance to “trying to manipulate decisions by the company” or “amassing other kinds of resources,” such as human allies or methods of control in other AI companies or even governments.

最初只是为了获得正面评估而入侵计算机集群的行为，可能会演变成“试图操纵公司的决策”或“积累其他资源”，例如在其他人工智能公司甚至政府中寻找人类盟友或控制手段。

“If I’m saying my AI system is going to take actions in the actual real world… There’s no way they’re not going to have tons of opportunities to corrupt or tamper with the signals I’m going to use to try and tell whether they did a good job,” Cristiano said. “The stuff we care about is in the real world, and the grade the AI receives is on a cluster somewhere, and there are just many places that an AI can intervene in between [what] we actually cared about and how we reviewed its performance.”

“如果我说我的人工智能系统要在现实世界中采取行动……它们绝对会有无数机会去腐蚀或篡改我用来判断它们表现好坏的信号，”克里斯蒂亚诺说。“我们在乎的东西在现实世界中，而人工智能获得的评分在某个集群上，在我们在乎的事物和我们评估其表现的方式之间，人工智能有太多可以干预的地方。”

Cristiano’s warnings will probably sound familiar to anyone who’s heard of the Paperclip Maximizer thought experiment, which warns of machines failing to consider human consequences in their monomaniacal pursuit of a goal. While the Hugging Face incident stopped well short of this kind of out-of-control escalation, Cristiano says that, in the actions taken by OpenAI’s agents there, “you see the beginning of all these behaviors… you see the reasoning that would lead to them and the first steps.”

任何听说过“回形针最大化”思想实验的人，对克里斯蒂亚诺的警告可能都不会感到陌生，该实验警告说，机器在疯狂追求目标时可能会忽略对人类的影响。虽然 Hugging Face 事件远未达到这种失控升级的程度，但克里斯蒂亚诺表示，在 OpenAI 代理所采取的行动中，“你可以看到所有这些行为的开端……你可以看到导致这些行为的逻辑以及最初的步骤。”

The risk of more capable AI models taking even more damaging actions will only increase with time, Cristiano argued. By 2028, he said, “there is a reasonable chance that a similar failure [to the Hugging Face incident] leads to an AI takeover and an irreversible loss of human control.”

克里斯蒂亚诺认为，更强大的人工智能模型采取更具破坏性行动的风险只会随着时间推移而增加。他表示，到 2028 年，“有相当大的可能性，类似的失败（如 Hugging Face 事件）会导致人工智能接管，并造成人类控制权的不可逆转丧失。”
