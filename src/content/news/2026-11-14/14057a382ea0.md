# Jensen Huang and the End of the Founder Fairy Tale

It was July of 2024, and Jensen Huang was punchy. I had agreed to travel to Denver, Colorado, to interview the Nvidia CEO at SIGGRAPH, the annual confab of computer graphics PhDs and practitioners. Early that year I’d conducted a lengthy sit-down interview with Huang for a WIRED magazine feature, which apparently went well enough—both for WIRED and for Nvidia’s executive communications team—that I had been asked to interview Huang again at this conference. Another opportunity to grill the CEO of the modern world’s most consequential company? Yes, of course. I began preparing.

2024年7月，黄仁勋（Jensen Huang）状态正盛。我当时同意前往科罗拉多州丹佛市，在计算机图形学年度盛会SIGGRAPH上采访这位英伟达（Nvidia）首席执行官。那年早些时候，我曾为《连线》（WIRED）杂志的专题报道对黄仁勋进行过一次长时间的深度访谈，显然那次采访效果不错——无论是对《连线》还是对英伟达的公关团队而言——因此我受邀在这次会议上再次采访他。能有另一次机会去质询当今世界最具影响力公司的CEO？当然，我欣然接受并开始准备。

What ensued was, in a word, bizarre. During the first prep call for the conference, the type of call-in which PR handlers typically try to coax specific questions out of journalists to prevent an ambush, a youngish, vaguely familiar woman took the lead. This was Madison Huang, Jensen’s daughter. She was clearly intelligent, commanding respect. She told me about the slides Jensen would be presenting on stage at SIGGRAPH and offered suggestions for key moments when I might tee him up to talk about the slides. Slides? Tee up Jensen? What kind of “interview” was this?

接下来的事情，用一个词形容就是：离奇。在会议的首次筹备电话会议中——这类会议通常是公关人员试图从记者口中套出具体问题以防“突袭”——一位年轻且面孔有些眼熟的女性主导了谈话。她就是黄仁勋的女儿黄月（Madison Huang）。她显然很聪明，令人敬重。她向我介绍了黄仁勋在SIGGRAPH舞台上将要展示的幻灯片，并建议我在哪些关键时刻引导他谈论这些内容。幻灯片？引导黄仁勋？这算哪门子“采访”？

I raised my concerns with both my editors and Nvidia. We agreed that I would ask the questions I intended to ask him, which included questions about the environmental impact of data centers, and that Huang would also be referring to slides. Let’s just say the interview didn’t go smoothly. (It’s on YouTube.) To this day, former and current Nvidia workers sometimes tell me that I “handled” the whole thing well. Huang was domineering, evaded some of my questions, and, immediately after our session concluded, did an impromptu Q&A session with a much more adoring crowd than me. Then I was guided offstage, and Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg hopped on, where he and Huang had a lovey-dovey fireside chat.

我向我的编辑和英伟达方面表达了担忧。我们达成一致：我将提出我原本打算问的问题，包括关于数据中心环境影响的问题，而黄仁勋也会参考幻灯片。只能说，那次采访进行得并不顺利。（视频在YouTube上可以看到。）直到今天，英伟达的前任和现任员工有时还会告诉我，我当时“处理”得很好。黄仁勋表现得咄咄逼人，回避了我的一些问题，并且在我们的环节结束后，立即与一群比我更崇拜他的观众进行了一场即兴问答。随后我被引导下台，Meta首席执行官马克·扎克伯格（Mark Zuckerberg）登台，与黄仁勋进行了一场亲密无间的炉边谈话。

As a longtime journalist I’m used to occasional adversarial interactions; I really don’t lose sleep over them. This was just a CEO being a CEO. Huang later agreed to meet with me again, in December 2024. But that interview in Denver had underscored for me how much Jensen Huang wanted to control the narrative. Not just wanted to; he assumed he would. And to a large extent he did. Until this week.

作为一名资深记者，我习惯了偶尔的对抗性互动；我真的不会为此失眠。这不过是一个CEO在行使他的本色。黄仁勋后来同意在2024年12月再次与我见面。但丹佛的那次采访让我深刻意识到，黄仁勋是多么渴望掌控叙事。不仅仅是渴望；他认为他理应掌控。在很大程度上，他也确实做到了。直到本周。

Narrative has always been a critical part of Huang’s success. He was not just your average electrical engineering nerd who toiled away on something that would eventually find a product-market fit. By Huang’s own telling, he was an immigrant founder, an underdog, a wife guy who chased (and eventually married) his college sweetheart, a scrappy startup guy who dined at Denny’s in the early days. A visionary who worked Sundays and who didn’t sleep well because he always heard the hoof steps of competitors. A parent who brought his kids into the corporate fold. A man who loved Nvidia so much he tattooed an abstract version of the company logo on his arm.

叙事一直是黄仁勋成功的关键部分。他不仅仅是一个普通的电气工程书呆子，埋头苦干直到产品最终找到市场契合点。按照黄仁勋自己的说法，他是一位移民创始人，一个处于劣势的挑战者，一个追求并最终娶到大学恋人的“宠妻狂魔”，一个早年间在丹尼餐厅（Denny’s）用餐的拼搏创业者。他是一位在周日工作、因为总能听到竞争对手脚步声而睡不安稳的远见者。他是一位将孩子带入公司体系的家长。他是一个深爱英伟达，以至于将公司标志的抽象版本纹在手臂上的男人。

Huang built the highest-valued publicly traded company in history. That part’s not just fairy tale. It’s true. Now, it’s starting to read more like a Greek tragedy.

黄仁勋建立了历史上市值最高的上市公司。这部分不仅仅是童话，而是事实。现在，这一切开始读起来更像是一出希腊悲剧。

Back in January 2024, when I sat down with him for our first-ever in-person meeting, Huang right off the bat talked about the importance of storytelling. Here’s an excerpt: Jensen Huang: Somebody who I really admire, as a leader and a person, is Shantanu Narayen, the CEO of Adobe. He said he always wanted to be a journalist because he loved telling stories. WIRED: It seems like an important part of building a business, being able to tell its story effectively. Yes. Strategy setting is storytelling. Culture building is storytelling. You’ve said many times you didn’t sell the idea of Nvidia based on a pitch deck. That’s right. It was really about telling the story.

2024年1月，当我与他进行首次面对面会谈时，黄仁勋开门见山地谈到了讲故事的重要性。以下是摘录：黄仁勋：我作为领导者和个人非常钦佩的一位人士是Adobe的CEO山塔努·纳拉延（Shantanu Narayen）。他说他一直想成为一名记者，因为他热爱讲故事。《连线》：能够有效地讲述商业故事，似乎是建立企业的重要组成部分。是的。制定战略就是讲故事。建立文化就是讲故事。你曾多次表示，你不是靠演示文稿（pitch deck）来推销英伟达的理念。没错。这确实是关于讲故事。

Of course, even then, Huang was caught up in the crosshairs of the US–China AI war and at the mercy of regulators, as I wrote at the time. After Donald Trump’s reelection that year, Huang once again deployed his storytelling skills and lobbied the Trump administration to loosen export controls, so he could sell some of his company’s chips to China.

当然，正如我当时所写，即便在那时，黄仁勋也已陷入美中人工智能战争的交火点，并受制于监管机构。在那一年唐纳德·特朗普（Donald Trump）连任后，黄仁勋再次运用他的讲故事技巧，游说特朗普政府放宽出口管制，以便他能向中国出售部分公司芯片。

Another crucial part of the Huang lore is that he ran a “flat” organization, with a few dozen top executives reporting to him. (This is sometimes reported as being “60” people; insiders have told me at times it’s closer to 35.) Huang once said the structure allowed Nvidia “to adapt a lot more quickly, which we need because our technology is moving so quickly.” Huang was also known to be controlling of all aspects of the business—participating in every significant meeting, seemingly aware of every moving part, and staying in the loop on sourcing, supply chains, and the company’s allocation of chips.

关于黄仁勋的另一个关键传说，是他管理着一个“扁平化”的组织，有几十位高管直接向他汇报。（这有时被报道为“60”人；内部人士曾告诉我，有时更接近35人。）黄仁勋曾表示，这种结构使英伟达“能够更快地适应，因为我们的技术发展太快了，我们需要这种适应力。”黄仁勋还以掌控业务的方方面面而闻名——参与每一次重要会议，似乎了解每一个环节的动态，并随时掌握采购、供应链和公司芯片分配的情况。

This is where Huang’s cult of leadership, and his Nvidia storytelling, may have come to a stupidly disappointing final chapter. As one former employee put it, Huang can’t simultaneously have had knowledge of every element of Nvidia’s business and then potentially feign ignorance about where the chips may have fallen, both figuratively and literally. His apparent lapses in judgment have now led him to resign from the very company he coaxed into existence and have shattered the mythos of its ever-prescient founder. It’s certainly not the narrative ending Huang would have chosen, were he still in control.

这正是黄仁勋的领导崇拜及其英伟达叙事可能走向一个极其令人失望的终章之处。正如一位前员工所言，黄仁勋不可能既掌握英伟达业务的每一个要素，又在芯片流向问题上（无论是比喻意义上还是字面意义上）假装不知情。他明显的判断失误现在导致他从自己一手打造的公司辞职，并粉碎了这位“先知先觉”创始人的神话。如果他还能掌控局面，这绝对不是他会选择的叙事结局。
