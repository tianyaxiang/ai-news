EXECUTIVE SUMMARY Scientists have been working on ways to decode brain activity for decades. Teams have developed brain implants that can be used to control robotic limbs and computers, and even to “speak.” The people who volunteer for trials of those devices play a vital role in that research, and they ought to be recognized for their contributions, says Ian Burkhart, who had one implanted in his own brain back in 2014. He’s been working on a set of guidelines for researchers developing brain-computer interfaces (BCIs).

执行摘要：几十年来，科学家们一直致力于研究解码大脑活动的方法。研究团队已经开发出可用于控制机械肢体、计算机甚至实现“说话”功能的脑植入设备。Ian Burkhart 于 2014 年在自己的大脑中植入了此类设备，他表示，参与这些设备试验的志愿者在研究中发挥了至关重要的作用，他们的贡献应当得到认可。目前，他正致力于为开发脑机接口（BCI）的研究人员制定一套指导方针。

Ethics guidelines for clinical trials already exist. But BCI trials are different, says Jennifer Chandler, a professor of law and neuroethics at the University of Ottawa who also worked on the guidelines. “These are people who have a device embedded in their brain, are in trials sometimes for years and years … [and contribute] a significant investment of time, of the body, and of intellectual effort.”

渥太华大学法学与神经伦理学教授 Jennifer Chandler 也参与了这些指导方针的制定，她指出，虽然临床试验已有伦理准则，但脑机接口试验有所不同。“这些人将设备植入大脑，有时需要参与长达数年的试验……他们投入了大量的时间、身体机能和智力劳动。”

Burkhart was left paralyzed from the chest down following a diving accident in 2010, when he was 19 years old. Around four years later, he volunteered to become the first person to trial a brain implant designed for hand control. He had an array of 96 electrodes inserted into the part of his brain that controls movement. The electrodes were connected to a computer via a cable that essentially plugged into his head.

2010 年，19 岁的 Burkhart 在一次跳水事故后胸部以下瘫痪。大约四年后，他自愿成为首位试验手部控制脑植入设备的人。他的大脑运动控制区域被植入了 96 个电极阵列。这些电极通过一根实际上插入他头部的电缆连接到计算机上。

Algorithms on the computer were designed to interpret those signals and deliver them to another sleeve of electrodes worn on Burkhart’s arm, allowing him to control it. The first movements were small, but Burkhart was ultimately able to use the system to pour beads from bottles, move small and delicate objects, and even play Guitar Hero, if only during lab visits. It was a scientific breakthrough.

计算机上的算法旨在解读这些信号，并将它们传输到 Burkhart 手臂上佩戴的另一个电极套筒，从而使他能够控制手臂。最初的动作幅度很小，但 Burkhart 最终能够利用该系统从瓶中倒出珠子、移动细小脆弱的物体，甚至在实验室访问期间玩《吉他英雄》。这是一项科学突破。

Burkhart took part in two years-long clinical trials over the seven-and-a-half-year period he had the electrode array implanted in his brain. “It was a fantastic experience,” he says. “But there’s certainly some things that, knowing what I know now, I would want to do a little differently.”

在电极阵列植入大脑的七年半时间里，Burkhart 参加了两次为期多年的临床试验。“这是一次美妙的经历，”他说，“但以我现在的认知，确实有些事情我希望能换种方式处理。”

In 2022, Burkhart cofounded BCI Pioneers Coalition—an organization through which people who have taken part in BCI trials can share their experiences, both with each other and with researchers. Over the last year, members of the group have been developing a set of guidelines based on those experiences. The idea is to help academics, clinicians, and companies run ethical trials that respect participants’ autonomy.

2022 年，Burkhart 共同创立了“脑机接口先锋联盟”（BCI Pioneers Coalition），这是一个让参与过脑机接口试验的人员能够相互交流并与研究人员分享经验的组织。过去一年里，该小组成员基于这些经验制定了一套指导方针，旨在帮助学术界、临床医生和企业开展尊重参与者自主权的伦理试验。

The guidelines, published on Tuesday, lay out a list of recommendations that fall within 13 categories. One, for example, focuses on recognizing and compensating trial participants. During the trials that Burkhart participated in, “I was going into the lab two or three days a week, for four hours at a time,” he recalls. “That’s [easily] a part-time job if you just count the hours you’re putting in.”

周二发布的指导方针列出了一系列建议，涵盖 13 个类别。例如，其中一项重点在于对试验参与者的认可与补偿。Burkhart 回忆道，在他参与试验期间，“我每周要去实验室两到三天，每次四个小时。如果只计算投入的时间，这完全就是一份兼职工作。”

As the trial progressed, Burkhart says, he learned more and was able to give more constructive feedback on what was working and what wasn’t. He says he was a key member of the research team. Others who find themselves in a similar position might appreciate being listed as a coauthor on scientific publications or publicly named as a research collaborator, he says. Burkhart and his colleagues acknowledge that not everyone will want this, however.

Burkhart 表示，随着试验的推进，他学到了更多知识，并能就哪些环节有效、哪些无效提供更具建设性的反馈。他认为自己是研究团队的关键成员。他说，其他处于类似处境的人可能希望在科学出版物中被列为合著者，或被公开命名为研究合作者。不过，Burkhart 和他的同事们也承认，并非每个人都有此意愿。

The guidelines also address the thorny question of privacy. That’s important: The public will always be interested in cutting-edge biotechnologies like these—and the guidelines say researchers also have a duty to prepare participants for the possibility of media attention. In some cases, it might be difficult to completely conceal the identity of trial participants.

指导方针还探讨了棘手的隐私问题。这一点很重要：公众总是对这类尖端生物技术充满兴趣，而指导方针指出，研究人员也有责任让参与者为可能面临的媒体关注做好准备。在某些情况下，完全隐匿试验参与者的身份可能很困难。

Things get even more complicated when it comes to the privacy of participants’ brain data. Research teams will need to access, analyze, and—at least temporarily—store this data. Burkhart wasn’t too bothered about that in his case, where scientists were collecting neural activity related to movement. But people who have brain implants designed for turning thoughts into words might feel differently.

当涉及参与者大脑数据的隐私时，情况会变得更加复杂。研究团队需要访问、分析并至少暂时存储这些数据。在 Burkhart 的案例中，科学家收集的是与运动相关的神经活动，他对此并不太介意。但那些植入旨在将思想转化为文字的脑植入设备的人，感受可能会有所不同。

The guidelines state that brain data “should be understood as the participant’s personal information.” They are pushing for participants to be able to access their own brain data whenever they want—something that has not always been possible—and want to make sure people aren’t prevented from sharing it with their own doctors because of embargoes or nondisclosure agreements (NDAs) designed to keep findings under wraps from competitors.

指导方针明确指出，大脑数据“应被视为参与者的个人信息”。他们主张参与者应能随时访问自己的大脑数据——这在过去并非总能实现——并确保人们不会因为旨在对竞争对手保密的禁令或保密协议（NDA）而无法与自己的医生分享这些数据。

“It’s not super common for there to be firm NDAs within a lot of the trials,” says Burkhart. “But it’s something where we want to make sure that that does not happen.” The guidelines also address what comes after a trial. Around five years after Burkhart had his device implanted, the research team ran into some issues with funding.

“在许多试验中，严格的保密协议并不常见，”Burkhart 说，“但我们希望确保这种情况不会发生。”指导方针还探讨了试验结束后的后续事宜。在 Burkhart 植入设备约五年后，研究团队遇到了一些资金问题。

When we last spoke a few years ago, he recalled how he was told he would have to have the implant removed and could have it put back in once the team had secured funding. (And we’re talking about brain surgery here.) Ultimately, he kept the implant until 2021, when it was removed after an infection started to develop at the point where the cable led into his scalp.

几年前我们上次交谈时，他回忆起自己被告知必须移除植入物，待团队获得资金后再重新植入。（要知道，这可是脑部手术。）最终，他一直保留着植入物，直到 2021 年，由于电缆进入头皮的部位开始出现感染，植入物才被移除。

But losing the device after all those years was difficult; he’d regained the ability to move his arm—and then lost it again. Rita Leggett, an Australian woman who had a device implanted in her brain to treat her epilepsy, has said she felt she “became one” with her implant. When the company that made it went bust two years later, the device was removed against her will—an act ethicists have described as a potential breach of human rights.

但在多年后失去该设备对他来说非常痛苦；他曾恢复了手臂的活动能力，却又再次失去。澳大利亚女性 Rita Leggett 曾在大脑中植入设备以治疗癫痫，她表示自己感觉与植入物“融为一体”。两年后，制造该设备的公司倒闭，她的设备被违背意愿地移除——伦理学家将此行为描述为潜在的人权侵犯。
