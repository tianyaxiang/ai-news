The self-styled visionaries of Silicon Valley, leaders like OpenAI chief executive Sam Altman and SpaceX boss Elon Musk, have built careers claiming to see the future. Long-term vision is currency: Meta’s Mark Zuckerberg built a 10-year road map for Facebook in 2016, and Google famously told investors before its public offering that it was unconcerned with short-term results.

硅谷那些自诩为远见卓识的人，比如 OpenAI 首席执行官萨姆·奥特曼（Sam Altman）和 SpaceX 老板埃隆·马斯克（Elon Musk），都是靠声称能预见未来而建立起职业生涯的。长远眼光就是一种货币：Meta 的马克·扎克伯格（Mark Zuckerberg）在 2016 年为 Facebook 制定了 10 年路线图，而谷歌在上市前也曾向投资者宣称，他们并不关心短期业绩。

And yet there they all were, a mere five weeks before the midterm elections, gathered at the White House to smile for photos with President Trump and sign on to his “Super Intelligence” agenda. Even by the diminished standards of today’s corporate governance, this was a shockingly foolish decision. Despite dozens of polls showing a rising tide of anti-Trump sentiment and bipartisan rejection of AI and data centers, the futurists of the tech elite cast aside long-term corporate interests for short-term political favor.

然而，就在中期选举前仅仅五周，他们却齐聚白宫，与特朗普总统合影留念，并签署了他的“超级智能”议程。即便以当今公司治理水平不断下降的标准来看，这都是一个极其愚蠢的决定。尽管数十项民意调查显示反特朗普情绪日益高涨，且两党对人工智能和数据中心持排斥态度，但这些科技精英中的未来学家们还是为了短期的政治利益而抛弃了长期的企业利益。

And now, their industry is very squarely in the new victors’ crosshairs. For an industry obsessed with pattern recognition, their decision to lash themselves to President Trump has proved to be a catastrophic misreading of the political landscape. Now, not only have they jeopardized their companies’ fates, but they’ve also raised questions about their vision and judgment. What were they thinking?

现在，他们的行业正处于新胜选者们的直接瞄准之下。对于一个痴迷于模式识别的行业来说，他们将自己与特朗普总统捆绑在一起的决定，被证明是对政治格局灾难性的误读。如今，他们不仅危及了各自公司的命运，还引发了人们对其远见和判断力的质疑。他们到底在想什么？

The people who work for them are just as confused as the rest of us. Tech workers I’ve spoken to at OpenAI, Meta, and other firms have noted an absence of commentary from their leaders post-election. Zuckerberg has yet to address the results internally, while an employee at another AI firm said that “leadership has been pretty silent on Slack.” Most employees share concern about potential anti-data-center legislation and are curious how leadership will adjust to a Democratic Washington, DC. But more than anything, they don’t understand how their bosses didn’t see what was so blindingly obvious to everyone else.

为他们工作的人和我们其他人一样感到困惑。我采访过的 OpenAI、Meta 和其他公司的科技员工都指出，选举结束后，他们的领导层没有任何评论。扎克伯格尚未在内部谈及选举结果，而另一家人工智能公司的员工则表示，“领导层在 Slack 上一直保持沉默”。大多数员工对潜在的反数据中心立法表示担忧，并好奇领导层将如何适应民主党治下的华盛顿特区。但最让他们不解的是，为什么他们的老板竟然没能看到对其他人来说显而易见的事情。

In Silicon Valley, the Gretzkyism “skate to where the puck is going, not where it has been” is a management maxim; it preaches anticipation and foresight over reactiveness to the present moment. Tech entrepreneurs love a good “pivot,” and companies that can’t change with the times get disrupted and die. Which is why I find it so strange that Zuckerberg, OpenAI cofounder Greg Brockman, and so many of Silicon Valley’s other prominent AI leaders and venture capitalists have put themselves in a potentially inescapable political bind.

在硅谷，“滑向冰球要去的地方，而不是它曾经所在的地方”这一格雷茨基名言是管理准则；它提倡预见性和远见，而非对当下的被动反应。科技创业者喜欢“转型”，而无法与时俱进的公司会被颠覆并走向消亡。这就是为什么我觉得扎克伯格、OpenAI 联合创始人格雷格·布罗克曼（Greg Brockman）以及硅谷其他许多知名人工智能领袖和风险投资家让自己陷入潜在的、无法逃脱的政治困境是如此奇怪。

In going all in on President Trump’s cultural and business agenda instead of playing both sides of the aisle as corporate America usually does, they’ve built identities they may not be able to pivot out of. Will anyone really believe that Palantir CEO Alex Karp is suddenly progressive after a year of promising “violence and death to our enemies” and telling investors that the company is “completely anti-woke”?

他们全力支持特朗普总统的文化和商业议程，而不是像美国企业界通常那样两边下注，这使他们建立起了可能无法轻易摆脱的身份标签。在承诺了一年“对敌人施以暴力和死亡”，并告诉投资者公司“完全反觉醒（anti-woke）”之后，还会有人真的相信 Palantir 首席执行官亚历克斯·卡普（Alex Karp）突然变得进步了吗？

I believe this is less a surprise than a very public exposure of a fundamental reality: that tech’s most visible leaders are not all-knowing visionary masterminds after all. Insights about social media or artificial intelligence do not make Zuckerberg or Altman experts on politics, the economy, or much else outside of technology. The instant gratification of presidential access, subsidies, and a say in their own regulation blinded them to the basics of political math that they clearly did not understand.

我认为这与其说是一个意外，不如说是一个基本事实的公开暴露：科技界最引人注目的领袖终究不是全知全能的远见大师。对社交媒体或人工智能的洞察力，并不能让扎克伯格或奥特曼成为政治、经济或技术领域之外其他方面的专家。获得总统接见、补贴以及在自身监管方面拥有发言权所带来的即时满足感，蒙蔽了他们的双眼，使他们忽视了自己显然并不理解的政治算术基础。

There’s a reason the chief executives of Chase, Boeing, and other Fortune 500 staples don’t go on podcasts to talk about “masculine energy,” or call the president from the stage of a MAGA-friendly AI summit to label fears about AI a “hoax” and a “scam.” This is the learned wisdom of decades operating in an environment where the customer changes every four years but contracts run for 10. Most companies play to both sides of the aisle because they know that their business models and multibillion-dollar projects must outlast the current administration.

摩根大通、波音和其他财富 500 强企业的首席执行官们之所以不会去播客上谈论“阳刚之气”，也不会在支持“让美国再次伟大”（MAGA）的人工智能峰会上打电话给总统，并将对人工智能的担忧称为“骗局”和“诈骗”，是有原因的。这是在一种客户四年一换但合同期限长达十年的环境中运营数十年所习得的智慧。大多数公司都会两边下注，因为他们知道自己的商业模式和数十亿美元的项目必须比现任政府更长久。

Instead, Big Tech tied itself to Trump and made its most important goals and investments—many related to grid access and data center permits—immediate targets for Democrats. In tech, you can always sunset a feature or update some code. But in politics, there is no undo button for failures that change public perception. The tech elites’ decision to visibly align their businesses with a temporary movement has left them no credible way to pivot now that the climate has changed.

相反，大型科技公司将自己与特朗普捆绑在一起，使其最重要的目标和投资——其中许多与电网接入和数据中心许可有关——成为了民主党的直接攻击目标。在科技领域，你总可以淘汰某个功能或更新代码。但在政治领域，对于改变公众认知的失败，没有“撤销”按钮。科技精英们决定将其业务与一场临时性的运动公开挂钩，这使得他们在气候发生变化后，已无法找到可信的转型方式。

As one Meta employee who has worked closely with Zuckerberg put it, “Mark is a long-term thinker, but he never charts a plan of how to get there. It’s spray and pray and see what happens.” As for whether employees expect the company to do a strategic postmortem? “Facebook has never really done that.” “Move fast and break things” might work in a startup, but it’s a terrible strategy for building reputational goodwill.

正如一位与扎克伯格密切合作过的 Meta 员工所言：“马克是一个长线思考者，但他从不制定如何实现目标的计划。这就像是‘广撒网，祈祷好运，看看会发生什么’。”至于员工是否期待公司进行战略复盘？“Facebook 从来没真正做过那种事。”“快速行动，打破常规”在初创公司或许行得通，但对于建立声誉商誉来说，这是一种糟糕的策略。

Previous technological leaps, like the rollout of fiber-optic technology and wireless infrastructure, avoided partisan politics. But by kowtowing to the Trump administration, Silicon Valley made artificial intelligence a culture-war issue. Now, when citizens vote on data center proposals from California to Florida, they are no longer considering only technical or economic concerns, but political ones. Tech leaders didn’t see this coming because they were too focused on the urgency of the AI race to understand how reputational damage could undermine the intangible goodwill—and ultimately the very tangible infrastructure—necessary to win it.

以往的技术飞跃，如光纤技术和无线基础设施的推广，都避免了党派政治。但通过向特朗普政府卑躬屈膝，硅谷将人工智能变成了一个文化战争议题。现在，当公民对从加利福尼亚到佛罗里达的数据中心提案进行投票时，他们考虑的不再仅仅是技术或经济问题，而是政治问题。科技领袖们没有预见到这一点，因为他们太专注于人工智能竞赛的紧迫性，以至于无法理解声誉受损如何会破坏赢得这场竞赛所必需的无形商誉，并最终破坏极其有形的实体基础设施。

The industry’s misreading of the moment has already punctured one myth Silicon Valley holds dear: that its leaders can always see what’s next.

该行业对当前形势的误读已经戳破了硅谷珍视的一个神话：即其领袖总是能预见未来。
