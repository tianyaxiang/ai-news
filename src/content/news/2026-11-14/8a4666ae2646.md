# In elephants, the resilience of communities has its limits

Elephants live in some of the most complex and resilient societies in the animal kingdom. Individuals die or move away, calves grow up, and newcomers join, but the local communities as a whole usually hold together. Long-term studies of African savanna elephants have shown that, even after poaching or other spikes in deaths, survivors tend to rebuild their lost connections over time. But a new study suggests that resilience may not keep the community intact when steady losses accumulate over time.

大象生活在动物界中最复杂、最具韧性的社会群体中。尽管个体可能会死亡或迁徙、幼象会长大、新成员会加入，但当地的社群通常能保持完整。针对非洲草原象的长期研究表明，即使在遭遇偷猎或其他导致死亡率激增的事件后，幸存者往往也能随着时间推移重建失去的联系。然而，一项新的研究指出，当损失随着时间推移不断累积时，这种韧性可能无法维持社群的完整。

Anastasia Madsen, a behavioral ecologist at the University of California, San Diego, and her colleagues analyzed 12 years of observations of wild Asian elephants in Sri Lanka. They found that, once losses began to outpace newcomers, the elephants’ society didn’t just get smaller. It fractured.

加州大学圣地亚哥分校的行为生态学家阿纳斯塔西娅·马德森（Anastasia Madsen）及其同事分析了对斯里兰卡野生亚洲象长达12年的观察记录。他们发现，一旦损失开始超过新成员的加入，大象的社会结构并不仅仅是缩小了，而是发生了分裂。

Udawalawe National Park in southern Sri Lanka was created in the 1970s around a reservoir on one of the country’s four major rivers. “Before that, it was basically a tropical deciduous forest,” said Shermin de Silva, an ecologist at UC San Diego and senior author of the study (de Silva started the Udawalawe Elephant Research Project in 2006). The reservoir had brought farming and settlers, some of whom lived inside what later became the park. When they were moved out, the land they had cleared for their crops turned into grassland that, according to de Silva, looks very much like an African savanna.

斯里兰卡南部的乌达瓦拉维国家公园（Udawalawe National Park）建于20世纪70年代，围绕着该国四大河流之一的一座水库而建。“在此之前，这里基本上是热带落叶林，”加州大学圣地亚哥分校的生态学家、该研究的资深作者谢尔敏·德·席尔瓦（Shermin de Silva）说（她于2006年发起了乌达瓦拉维大象研究项目）。水库带来了农业和定居者，其中一些人就住在后来成为公园的区域内。当他们被迁出后，他们为种植作物而开垦的土地变成了草地，据德·席尔瓦描述，这看起来非常像非洲草原。

With far more food than the original forest offered, the park became famous for its big elephant herds. But the savanna was only a stage in the forest’s comeback. Without fires and clearing, invasive plants like lantana took over. “In some places, they’re like an impenetrable thicket. Not even elephants really like to go there because there’s no food for them,” de Silva said. This is a problem because elephants tend to move where the grass is. A park with less forage, the team says, stops attracting elephants from outside while, due to limited food, fewer calves reach adulthood.

由于食物远比原始森林丰富，该公园因拥有庞大的象群而闻名。但草原只是森林恢复过程中的一个阶段。在没有火灾和清理的情况下，马缨丹等入侵植物占据了主导地位。“在某些地方，它们就像无法穿透的灌木丛。连大象都不太喜欢去那里，因为那里没有它们的食物，”德·席尔瓦说。这是一个问题，因为大象倾向于向有草的地方移动。研究小组表示，一个饲料减少的公园不再能吸引外来的大象，同时由于食物有限，能长到成年的幼象也变少了。

Her team watched this transformation from a vehicle, driving random routes along the park’s roads about three days a week. The team identified elephants mostly by their floppy ears, which collect holes, tears, and cuts with age. “Their ears are very individually distinct, so it’s like a fingerprint,” de Silva said. Between 2007 and 2018, the team identified 443 adult and subadult females that roamed the park.

她的团队坐在车里观察这种变化，每周大约有三天沿着公园的道路随机行驶。团队主要通过大象下垂的耳朵来识别它们，这些耳朵会随着年龄增长而留下孔洞、撕裂和伤痕。“它们的耳朵非常有辨识度，就像指纹一样，”德·席尔瓦说。在2007年至2018年间，该团队识别出了443头在公园内活动的成年和亚成年雌象。

The team focused on females because males lead more solitary lives, ranging widely in search of mates. Females live in a so-called fission-fusion society. “It’s kind of similar to humans,” Madsen said. “You might have your work friends, you might have your family, and you’re not always with those same individuals at the same time, but they’re all part of your social network.” Unlike African savanna elephants, where families follow older matriarchs, Asian elephants move more independently. This may be because Asia’s rainy environment ensures that food and water are rarely far apart and usually easier to find, so following an experienced individual brings less benefit.

研究团队将重点放在雌象身上，因为雄象过着更孤独的生活，为了寻找配偶会四处游荡。雌象生活在所谓的“裂变-融合”社会中。“这和人类有点像，”马德森说。“你可能有工作上的朋友，也可能有家人，你并不总是和同一群人待在一起，但他们都是你社交网络的一部分。”与非洲草原象不同（非洲草原象的家庭跟随年长的雌性首领），亚洲象的活动更加独立。这可能是因为亚洲多雨的环境确保了食物和水源通常不会相距太远，且更容易找到，因此跟随经验丰富的个体所带来的益处较小。

Madsen reconstructed the elephants’ social network for each year, measuring the strength of each relationship by how often two animals were seen together. “You can think of it as a probability that you will see those two individuals together,” she explains. An algorithm commonly used for analyzing human social networks then picked out communities—clusters of elephants with many strong ties to one another. For the first few years of the study, these communities seemed to hold very well. But then cracks started to appear.

马德森重建了每年大象的社交网络，通过两头动物被同时看到的频率来衡量每段关系的强度。“你可以把它看作是看到这两个个体在一起的概率，”她解释道。随后，一种常用于分析人类社交网络的算法被用来识别社群——即那些彼此之间有着许多紧密联系的大象集群。在研究的最初几年，这些社群似乎保持得非常好。但随后，裂痕开始出现。

Around 2012, the elephants that died or left began to outnumber the newcomers. By 2018, about 69 percent of the females seen at the start of the study were gone, even though Asian elephants can live 60 years or more. Soon after the balance tipped, communities grew smaller, more numerous and more isolated from each other. “As individuals are being lost from the network, there’s nobody filling it back in,” de Silva says.

2012年左右，死亡或离开的大象数量开始超过新加入的大象。到2018年，尽管亚洲象的寿命可达60年或更长，但研究开始时观察到的雌象中约有69%已经消失。在平衡被打破后不久，社群变得更小、数量更多，且彼此之间更加孤立。“随着个体从网络中流失，没有人能填补这些空缺，”德·席尔瓦说。

A shrinking population naturally makes for a smaller network, so the team checked whether this was just a numbers game. They looked at a population of 59 elephants seen every year of the study, which the team referred to as residents. “If it was just an artifact of the data, those core individuals should have fairly consistent relationships. But that’s not what we saw,” Madsen said. “They were changing their relationships with each other, even though they were still there.” Among these residents, the fracturing came one to three years after the decline began.

人口减少自然会导致网络缩小，因此研究小组核实了这是否仅仅是数字游戏。他们观察了研究期间每年都能见到的59头大象，团队称之为“常驻者”。“如果这只是数据造成的假象，那么这些核心个体应该保持相当稳定的关系。但我们看到的并非如此，”马德森说。“尽管它们依然存在，但它们彼此之间的关系却在发生变化。”在这些常驻者中，分裂现象在数量下降开始后的一到三年内出现。

Madsen suspects some of the lost elephants were the glue holding groups together. “If you go to a city hall meeting, it’s maybe the same group of people that constantly comes in. You’re not friends with every single other person in that group, though,” she said. “There could have been some key individuals that were lost that were holding these subgroups together.” The first ties to go were the weakest, the casual acquaintances. These weak relationships, Madsen says, could represent animals that merely tolerate each other: “Tolerance is really important in a society, especially in large animals. If they get into conflict, it could be serious.” These weak ties, researchers argue, may still be important because they can carry information between communities.

马德森怀疑一些流失的大象是维系群体的粘合剂。“如果你去参加市政厅会议，可能总是同一群人出席。但你并不是那群人中每个人的朋友，”她说。“可能有一些关键个体流失了，而它们正是维系这些亚群体的纽带。”首先消失的是最薄弱的联系，即泛泛之交。马德森说，这些薄弱的关系可能代表了仅仅是相互容忍的动物：“容忍在社会中非常重要，尤其是在大型动物中。如果它们发生冲突，后果可能会很严重。”研究人员认为，这些薄弱的联系可能依然很重要，因为它们可以在社群之间传递信息。

De Silva links this fracturing to the disappearing grass. “As the competition becomes more severe, your social sphere sort of shrinks,” she said. “It’s as if they’re emphasizing the quality over the quantity of the relationships.”

德·席尔瓦将这种分裂与草地的消失联系起来。“随着竞争变得更加激烈，你的社交圈会缩小，”她说。“就好像它们在强调关系的质量而非数量。”
