AI models flub these intelligence tests. Can you fare any better? Puzzles and games have always been central to AI development. The term “machine learning” was popularized in a 1959 article about an algorithm that learned to play checkers. Chess and Go are famous AI test beds too. Judged purely on its puzzling skills, AI is improving a lot—and quickly. In late 2024, even the best models could figure out only 18% of the infamous New York Times Connections puzzles; by early 2025, some could solve them nearly perfectly every time. But puzzles do more than highlight AI’s progress. Seeing where models succeed and fail, and where humans still beat them, offers a window into the technology’s strengths and weaknesses. We’ve gathered seven puzzles that have stumped models at one time or another—now it’s your turn to see how you fare. Take our test to find out whether you can outsmart AI. —Grace Huckins

How AI plotted an interstellar journey to Alpha Centauri
人工智能如何规划前往半人马座阿尔法星的星际之旅

A nonprofit organization called the Fermi Explorer Mission announced yesterday that it intends to launch a spacecraft to our nearest star system by the end of 2029. It’s a hugely ambitious mission—if all goes well, the spacecraft could take up to 80,000 years to arrive at Alpha Centauri, which is 4.4 light-years away. To get there, it will follow a novel trajectory discovered by an AI system developed by PSI, a physics research lab. Explore how AI found a new route to Alpha Centauri—and what the mission could tell us about alien life. —Michelle Kim
一个名为“费米探索任务”（Fermi Explorer Mission）的非营利组织昨日宣布，计划在 2029 年底前向我们最近的恒星系发射一艘航天器。这是一项极其宏大的任务——如果一切顺利，航天器可能需要长达 8 万年的时间才能抵达 4.4 光年外的半人马座阿尔法星。为了到达那里，它将遵循由物理研究实验室 PSI 开发的人工智能系统所发现的一条新颖轨道。探索人工智能如何找到前往半人马座阿尔法星的新路线，以及这项任务能告诉我们关于外星生命的哪些信息。——Michelle Kim

MIT Technology Review Narrated: the role of the astronaut is in flux
《麻省理工科技评论》播客：宇航员的角色正在发生变化

We go to space for geopolitical prestige, manifest destiny, spiritual fulfillment, scientific curiosity, and, increasingly, business opportunities. In the wake of Artemis II, a slew of new books suggest that these justifications are subsumed by one unifying fact: humans have itchy feet, and we are simply wired to roam. In The Ultraview Effect, space anthropologist Deana L. Weibel frames human space exploration as part of our need to embark on pilgrimages. In A Heart for Space, civilian astronaut Eiman Jahangir recounts one such voyage with Blue Origin. And in Dinner with an Astronaut, former NASA astronaut Leroy Chiao argues that people simply “need to know what’s on the other side.” “We go into space because we want to explore,” he says. “We want to see what it’s like to walk on another world.” But as our presence in space expands, we are bound to bring along the same human foibles that have stymied us on Earth.
我们进入太空是为了地缘政治声望、天命论、精神满足、科学好奇心，以及日益增长的商业机会。在“阿尔忒弥斯 2 号”任务之后，一系列新书表明，这些理由都被一个统一的事实所涵盖：人类天生好动，我们注定要流浪。在《超视效应》（The Ultraview Effect）一书中，太空人类学家 Deana L. Weibel 将人类太空探索视为我们进行朝圣需求的一部分。在《太空之心》（A Heart for Space）中，平民宇航员 Eiman Jahangir 讲述了与蓝色起源公司的一次此类航行。而在《与宇航员共进晚餐》（Dinner with an Astronaut）中，前 NASA 宇航员焦立中（Leroy Chiao）认为，人们只是“需要知道另一边有什么”。他说：“我们进入太空是因为我们想要探索。我们想看看在另一个世界行走是什么感觉。”但随着我们在太空中的存在不断扩大，我们必然会带去那些曾在地球上阻碍我们的同样的人性弱点。
