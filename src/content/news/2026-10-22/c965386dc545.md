Astrolab’s upcoming rover will run the first LLMs on the moon Large language models are set to go lunar. A computer capable of running AI software is one of the payloads aboard Astrolab’s FLIP lunar rover, which is slated to head to the moon as early as next month. The mission will test whether LLMs can enable greater autonomy and real-time intelligent decision-making in the extreme lunar environment. But the moon will present formidable challenges, from extreme temperatures to intense cosmic radiation. Find out how the lunar rover could change the future of space exploration. —Tereza Pultarova

How to study a swiftly melting climate disaster In Alaska’s Denali National Park, a 2013 lightning fire burned nearly 35,000 acres, stripping away the insulating layer that preserves permafrost. It’s given scientists a rare opportunity to study how fires reshape permafrost and carbon storage over time. The work is providing critical clues into the complex mechanisms driving cascading climate feedback effects. As warming temperatures fuel fires, more permafrost thaws, releasing greenhouse-gas emissions that threaten to fuel more warming, fires and permafrost loss. Their observations are revealing worrisome signs about what lies ahead. Discover what their fieldwork is revealing about a cascading climate disaster. —James Temple

如何研究一场迅速融化的气候灾难？在阿拉斯加的迪纳利国家公园，2013年的一场雷击火灾烧毁了近35,000英亩土地，剥离了保护永久冻土的绝缘层。这为科学家提供了一个难得的机会，来研究火灾如何随时间推移重塑永久冻土和碳储存。这项工作为驱动连锁气候反馈效应的复杂机制提供了关键线索。随着气温升高助长火灾，更多的永久冻土融化，释放出的温室气体排放威胁着进一步加剧变暖、火灾和永久冻土流失。他们的观察揭示了关于未来令人担忧的迹象。了解他们的实地考察揭示了关于连锁气候灾难的哪些信息。——James Temple

Cammarata claims that LLMs might be far easier to understand, and thus to monitor and control, than people previously thought. If true, it’s a huge deal. But not everyone is convinced. MIT Technology Review spoke to Cammarata the day after his post blew up.
