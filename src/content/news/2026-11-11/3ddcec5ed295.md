# Louis Rossmann Wants More People to Care About the Right to Repair

Save this story. I love my old iPhone 14, but I’m starting to face the facts: It’s going to die. The battery is about to go; apps take longer to load than they used to. Some of it I can fix. I can go to the Genius Bar and swap the battery. Maybe I could even do it myself; that’d buy me some time. But eventually, Apple won’t support this wildly expensive device at all, and there’s not much I can do about it.

保存这篇文章。我非常喜欢我的旧款 iPhone 14，但我不得不面对现实：它快要报废了。电池即将耗尽；应用程序加载的时间也比以前长了。有些问题我可以修复。我可以去天才吧（Genius Bar）更换电池。也许我甚至可以自己动手；这能为我争取一些时间。但最终，苹果公司将完全不再支持这款昂贵的设备，而对此我几乎无能为力。

Apple did start offering self-repair services a few years ago, but it involves renting nearly 80 pounds of equipment and requires you to use official parts. When all is said and done, it doesn’t save you that much money versus just going to the Apple Store. But if I buy a screen or a camera component from a third party, there's a chance my iPhone is gonna lock me out of certain features because it doesn't recognize it.

苹果公司几年前确实开始提供自助维修服务，但这需要租用近 80 磅重的设备，并要求使用官方零件。归根结底，这与直接去苹果商店维修相比，并不能省下多少钱。但如果我从第三方购买屏幕或摄像头组件，我的 iPhone 很可能会因为无法识别这些零件而锁定某些功能。

This problem goes far beyond iPhones. Manufacturers have put similar locks on all kinds of gadgets, from video game consoles to cars to smart home devices, making them hard to modify and even harder to restore after some firmware update renders a certain feature unusable.

这个问题远不止 iPhone 这么简单。制造商在各种电子产品上都设置了类似的锁，从游戏机、汽车到智能家居设备，这使得它们难以改装，而在某些固件更新导致特定功能无法使用后，想要恢复它们就更难了。

Not surprisingly, a lot of people think this isn't fair, that there should be a better way to do it. I think at this point it's safe to say it qualifies as a movement, one that unites under the banner of “right to repair.” Built around the radical idea that when you buy something you should be able to truly own it, right-to-repair advocates believe consumers have the right to maintain, fix, or modify any piece of hardware they’ve purchased. The movement has gained even more traction now that AI makes it even easier to hack the software powering consumers’ devices.

不出所料，许多人认为这不公平，应该有更好的解决方式。我认为现在可以肯定地说，这已经形成了一场运动，并团结在“维修权”（right to repair）的旗帜下。维修权倡导者基于一个激进的理念，即当你购买某样东西时，你应该能够真正拥有它；他们认为消费者有权维护、修理或改装他们购买的任何硬件。随着人工智能使破解消费者设备软件变得更加容易，这场运动现在获得了更大的推动力。

To find out what’s next for the right-to-repair movement, I went straight to the source: Louis Rossmann, a YouTuber and consumer rights advocate who recently organized the Own Your Shit tour, which went around the US earlier this year to call for the death of Section 1201 of the Digital Millennium Copyright Act, which prohibits users from bypassing digital restrictions put on devices by manufacturers.

为了了解维修权运动的下一步动向，我直接找到了消息源：YouTube 博主兼消费者权益倡导者路易斯·罗斯曼（Louis Rossmann）。他最近组织了“拥有你的东西”（Own Your Shit）巡回活动，今年早些时候在美国各地奔走，呼吁废除《数字千年版权法》（DMCA）第 1201 条，该条款禁止用户绕过制造商在设备上设置的数字限制。

Rossmann and I talked about more than just Section 1201. We also discussed the US midterm elections, putting out bounties to find product fixes, and what will happen as the right-to-repair movement collides with the rise of AI. This interview has been edited for length and clarity.

我和罗斯曼谈论的内容远不止第 1201 条。我们还讨论了美国中期选举、为寻找产品修复方案提供悬赏，以及当维修权运动与人工智能的兴起发生碰撞时会发生什么。本次采访为篇幅和清晰度进行了编辑。

BRIAN BARRETT: Louis, thank you so much for being here. You're someone who spends a lot of time not just evangelizing over “right to repair,’ but I would imagine introducing it to people who don't know what it is. How do you do that?

布莱恩·巴雷特：路易斯，非常感谢你能来。你花了很多时间不仅是在宣传“维修权”，我想你还在向那些不知道它是什么的人介绍它。你是怎么做到的？

LOUIS ROSSMANN: I try to avoid talking about it, and I try to show people. So instead of telling people, “Here’s why you should care about this issue,” which sounds like lecturing, what I’ll do is if I’m done fixing something for a customer and they say, “The Apple Store told me $2,000," and we could do it for $200, when they’re giving me the hug and saying, “Thank you for getting back all of our baby pictures” or something, I’ll say, “Here’s how close you were to this never happening.”

路易斯·罗斯曼：我尽量避免空谈，而是尝试向人们展示。与其告诉人们“这就是你为什么应该关心这个问题”（这听起来像是在说教），我通常会这样做：如果我为客户修好了一件东西，而他们说“苹果商店报价 2000 美元”，而我们只需要 200 美元，当他们拥抱我并说“谢谢你找回了我们所有的宝宝照片”之类的话时，我会说：“你差点就永远失去这些照片了。”

I like to tell them, “This is the document that I used to fix your device.” When they ask me what was wrong with it, I’ll tell them, “By the way, you should know that Apple tells the company that makes this chip not to sell it to me, and if everybody in that chain listened, I would not be able to fix this, and you wouldn’t have any of your data back.” So I always try to show people and get them excited about what it is I’m doing.

我喜欢告诉他们：“这就是我用来修复你设备的文档。”当他们问我设备出了什么问题时，我会告诉他们：“顺便说一下，你应该知道苹果公司告诉制造这块芯片的公司不要把它卖给我，如果链条上的每个人都听从了，我就无法修复它，你也找不回任何数据。”所以我总是尝试向人们展示，并让他们对我所做的事情感到兴奋。

Got it. I have over 1,000 repair videos on YouTube where I show people how to do what I do, and there are people that say, “I was working at Walmart for seven bucks an hour, and I picked up a soldering iron and started fixing boards on eBay, and now I make triple what I did before.”

明白了。我在 YouTube 上有 1000 多个维修视频，向人们展示如何做我所做的事情。有些人会说：“我以前在沃尔玛工作，时薪七美元，后来我拿起了烙铁，开始在 eBay 上修理电路板，现在我的收入是以前的三倍。”

There are people that fix their own things, so not just making careers for people, but maybe somebody saved themselves some money by doing a repair themself. Or when I fix something for somebody that otherwise wouldn't have been fixed or that they were told couldn’t be fixed. I always try to get people personally invested because lecturing people about it just doesn’t make it very fun.

有些人会自己修理东西，所以这不仅是为人们创造了职业机会，也许还有人通过自己动手维修省下了一些钱。或者当我为某人修复了原本无法修复、或者被告知无法修复的东西时。我总是试图让人们亲自参与进来，因为对他们进行说教并不会让事情变得有趣。

Your organization has a wall of shame, almost, of examples of things that companies have done to take away the ownership of a product. Because that’s really what it is, right? It's like you buy something, you expect to be able to own it and do what you want with it, but that's not always how it plays out. To your mind, what are some of the more egregious instances of companies not letting you know what you want?

你的组织有一个“耻辱墙”，上面列举了公司剥夺产品所有权的例子。因为这确实就是本质，对吧？就像你买了一样东西，你期望能够拥有它并随心所欲地使用它，但事实并非总是如此。在你看来，公司不让你如愿以偿的例子中，有哪些比较恶劣的？

Some of my favorites are some of these thermostat companies. One of them sent out a firmware update after the company went bankrupt that required that you pay $117 a year to get some of the functionality that you had beforehand. So it’s not even necessarily when a company has a device that has a specific date when it’s gonna turn off.

我最喜欢举的例子是一些恒温器公司。其中一家公司在破产后发布了一个固件更新，要求你每年支付 117 美元才能获得你之前拥有的部分功能。所以，这甚至不一定是因为公司设定的设备停用日期。

It’s when they realize, “We need to make more money, so we’re gonna change what it is we’re offering you after the sale.” When I started doing this, I was just talking about right to repair for Apple devices, which is what my small shop worked on. I saw it from my small lens. I focused on the items where I knew exactly what was going on, and I really thought, “This is a problem that only affects me.”

而是当他们意识到：“我们需要赚更多的钱，所以我们要改变售后提供给你的服务。”当我刚开始做这件事时，我只是在谈论苹果设备的维修权，这也是我小店的工作内容。我从自己的小视角来看待它。我专注于我完全了解的项目，我真的以为：“这是一个只影响我的问题。”

It wasn’t until about 2017 when I went to Nebraska for this legislative hearing that I realized that it really is not just something that affects my small field. I was in a room filled with farmers that were talking about all the problems they were having with their tractors, and I remember going, “Where’s the right-to-repair?” And someone says, “This is the right-to-repair room. Everybody has this problem now.” And that’s when I started opening my field of view up to everybody who has this issue. As time went on, my channel stopped just discussing right to repair, and it started discussing how right to repair is a small element under this...

直到 2017 年左右，我去内布拉斯加州参加立法听证会时，我才意识到这不仅仅影响我的小领域。我在一个房间里，里面坐满了农民，他们正在谈论拖拉机遇到的各种问题。我记得我当时问：“维修权在哪里？”有人说：“这就是维修权会议室。现在每个人都有这个问题。”那时我才开始将视野扩大到所有遇到这个问题的人。随着时间的推移，我的频道不再仅仅讨论维修权，而是开始讨论维修权是如何成为这之下的一个小要素……
