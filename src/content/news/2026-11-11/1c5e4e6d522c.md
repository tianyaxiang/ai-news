Our modern world runs on math, from social media algorithms, package delivery, and weather prediction to medical imaging and machine learning. The rise of computers helped fuel its dominance, enabling us to work with increasingly large numbers that would otherwise be beyond the capacity of our finite human brains. But with the rise of large language models, AI chatbots, and, soon, quantum computing, so-called “big math” is beginning to outstrip its creators entirely. What that means, and how we might adapt, is the focus of a new book, Big Math: The Hidden Codes That Run Our World, by applied mathematicians Steven Strogatz and Alex Townsend.

现代世界由数学驱动，从社交媒体算法、包裹递送、天气预报到医学成像和机器学习，无所不包。计算机的兴起推动了数学的主导地位，使我们能够处理日益庞大的数字，而这些数字原本超出了我们有限的人类大脑的处理能力。然而，随着大型语言模型、人工智能聊天机器人以及即将到来的量子计算的兴起，所谓的“大数据数学”（big math）正开始完全超越其创造者。应用数学家史蒂文·斯特罗加茨（Steven Strogatz）和亚历克斯·汤森（Alex Townsend）的新书《大数据数学：运行我们世界的隐藏代码》（Big Math: The Hidden Codes That Run Our World）探讨了这意味着什么以及我们该如何适应。

Strogatz is perhaps best known in his field for his co-authorship of a seminal 1998 paper with Duncan Watts on the collective dynamics of small-world networks. He has also studied chaos theory as applied to physics, the geometry of supercoiled DNA, and the biological collective oscillators, such as swarms of synchronously flashing fireflies and crickets that chirp in unison. In 2005, Strogatz extended his study of synchronicity to the crowds that caused the wobbling of London’s Millennium Bridge. An award-winning communicator, Strogatz wrote three series of columns on mathematics for The New York Times in 2010, 2012, and 2025 and hosted a podcast for Quanta magazine from 2020–2021. He has written four popular books: Sync (2003), The Calculus of Friendship (2009), The Joy of X (2012), and the New York Times bestseller Infinite Powers (2019).

斯特罗加茨在这一领域最为人所知的成就，是他与邓肯·瓦茨（Duncan Watts）在1998年合著的一篇关于小世界网络集体动力学的开创性论文。他还研究过应用于物理学的混沌理论、超螺旋DNA的几何结构，以及生物集体振荡器，例如同步闪烁的萤火虫群和齐声鸣叫的蟋蟀。2005年，斯特罗加茨将他的同步性研究扩展到了导致伦敦千禧桥晃动的人群。作为一名屡获殊荣的传播者，斯特罗加茨曾在2010年、2012年和2025年为《纽约时报》撰写了三个系列的数学专栏，并于2020年至2021年为《量子杂志》（Quanta magazine）主持过播客。他著有四本畅销书：《同步》（Sync，2003年）、《友谊的微积分》（The Calculus of Friendship，2009年）、《X的乐趣》（The Joy of X，2012年）以及《纽约时报》畅销书《无限的力量》（Infinite Powers，2019年）。

This time around, Strogatz has found a well-matched co-author in Townsend, a younger colleague recently hired at Cornell who studies numerical analysis and deep learning. As senior faculty, Strogatz was assigned to mentor Townsend, and the two men began regularly lunching together in Risley Hall. Strogatz was intrigued by the popular book potential of Townsend’s course on the top 20 algorithms of the 20th century and suggested co-authorship. That concept slowly morphed into a book about big matrix calculations, and then into what became Big Math.

这一次，斯特罗加茨找到了一位旗鼓相当的合著者——汤森。汤森是康奈尔大学新聘请的年轻同事，研究数值分析和深度学习。作为资深教员，斯特罗加茨被指派担任汤森的导师，两人开始定期在里斯利大厅（Risley Hall）共进午餐。汤森关于“20世纪20大算法”的课程引起了斯特罗加茨的兴趣，他认为这有写成大众读物的潜力，于是建议共同撰写。这一构思慢慢演变成一本关于大型矩阵计算的书，最终成为了现在的《大数据数学》。

“There are so many books about pure math, as if computers didn’t exist,” Strogatz told Ars. “Alex and I are both very applied mathematicians. We thought there should be a book on computers and math, especially the class of really big problems with billions of variables or parameters that you just can’t approach in your head. A lot of the math we’ve had for centuries perfectly scales up to this computer moment. What the abacus did for arithmetic, the fangcheng (“rectangular array”) board did for systems of linear equations. It was a matrix you could manipulate with your hand and bamboo sticks. So this externalizing of math outside of our heads into our tools is a 2,000-year-old idea.”

“市面上有很多关于纯数学的书，就好像计算机不存在一样，”斯特罗加茨告诉Ars。“亚历克斯和我都是非常务实的应用数学家。我们认为应该有一本关于计算机和数学的书，特别是针对那些拥有数十亿变量或参数、你根本无法在脑海中处理的超大型问题。我们几个世纪以来拥有的许多数学知识，完全可以扩展到这个计算机时代。算盘对算术的作用，就像‘方程’（矩形阵列）板对线性方程组的作用一样。那是一个你可以用手和竹筹来操作的矩阵。因此，将数学从我们的大脑中外化到工具中，是一个有着2000年历史的理念。”

“The mathematician in each of us will always want to know more,” Strogatz and Townsend wrote. “The human being must insist that knowledge serves something larger than itself. That might be the work of our century: letting our power grow without losing our soul. Big Math keeps getting bigger. So must we.”

“我们每个人心中的数学家总是想要了解更多，”斯特罗加茨和汤森写道。“人类必须坚持知识服务于比自身更宏大的事物。这可能是我们这个世纪的任务：在不丧失灵魂的情况下让我们的力量增长。大数据数学在不断变大，我们也必须如此。”
