Somewhere on an IRC channel, I ended up in a conversation about the The Filthy Human Hands (FHH) License. It wasn't the first time weird licenses were discussed there: Reticulum's sparked some interesting discussions as well. Let's start from the beginning.

在某个 IRC 频道上，我参与了一场关于“肮脏人类之手”（The Filthy Human Hands，简称 FHH）许可证的对话。这并不是那里第一次讨论奇怪的许可证：Reticulum 的许可证也曾引发过有趣的讨论。让我们从头说起。

If you don't want to read the Wikipedia article on Free Software, a couple of years ago, I gave a small presentation at school as part of the "Oral communication in English" course, which is a (shoddy) cake-based explanation of the topic. Long story short, a Free Software is a software with 4 freedoms: The freedom to run the program as you wish, for any purpose. The freedom to study how the program works, and change it to make it do what you wish. Access to the source code is a precondition for this. The freedom to redistribute copies so you can help others. The freedom to distribute copies of your modified versions to others. By doing this you can give the whole community a chance to benefit from your changes.

如果你不想阅读维基百科上关于自由软件的文章，几年前，我在学校的“英语口语交流”课程中做过一个小演讲，那是一个（粗糙的）以蛋糕为比喻的解释。长话短说，自由软件是拥有四项自由的软件：运行程序的自由（随心所欲，用于任何目的）；研究程序如何工作并进行修改以实现你意图的自由（访问源代码是前提）；重新分发副本以帮助他人的自由；以及分发修改版本副本的自由。通过这样做，你可以让整个社区有机会从你的修改中受益。

There is also the term Open Source, which is more or less Free Software but without the sociopolitical aspect of it, which is like a pizza without olives, or art without context: something devoid of meaning, a business-oriented product instead of a creation. Like in every sociopolitical endeavour, people sometimes disagree, leading to subtle but important variations. One of the most important one in the Free Software movement has been the question of the copyleft: should derivatives of Free Software be licensed under the same license? Some licenses, like the GPL says yes and this includes everything built on top of GPL code, the LGPL says that only code under LGPL needs to be released as LGPL, and others, called permissive licenses, like the BSD and MIT don't impose such restrictions.

还有一个术语叫“开源”，它或多或少就是自由软件，但剥离了社会政治层面，就像没有橄榄的披萨，或者没有背景的艺术：一种失去了意义的东西，是一个以商业为导向的产品，而非一种创造。正如在每一项社会政治事业中一样，人们有时会产生分歧，导致了微妙但重要的差异。自由软件运动中最重要的问题之一是“著佐权”（copyleft）：自由软件的衍生品是否应该在相同的许可证下发布？一些许可证（如 GPL）说是，这包括所有构建在 GPL 代码之上的内容；LGPL 则表示只有 LGPL 下的代码才需要以 LGPL 发布；而其他被称为许可型许可证的（如 BSD 和 MIT）则没有施加此类限制。

The Free Software movement started in 1986, so we're around 40 years later now, and the picture is pretty bleak: technically, one could argue that it won, as every computer-using company in the world depends on Free Software one way or the other: Firefox, Linux, VLC, Chrome, Ubuntu, Apache, nginx, Wikipedia, Open Street Map, Open Food Facts, … as well as more fundamental software components like curl, libxml, busybox, OpenSSL, GCC, clang, … Yet virtually none of them do contribute back to those commons, some of them being thanklessly maintained by a random person in Nebraska.

自由软件运动始于 1986 年，现在大约 40 年过去了，情况相当惨淡：从技术上讲，有人可能会说它赢了，因为世界上每一家使用计算机的公司都在以某种方式依赖自由软件：Firefox、Linux、VLC、Chrome、Ubuntu、Apache、nginx、维基百科、Open Street Map、Open Food Facts……以及更基础的软件组件，如 curl、libxml、busybox、OpenSSL、GCC、clang 等。然而，几乎没有公司回馈这些公共资源，其中一些项目仅仅由内布拉斯加州的一位普通人默默维护着。

Worse, a handful of computer-related companies ("cloud providers") are making ungodly amount of money renting infrastructure running Free Software, most smart™ devices are running Free Software, every single software producing/selling company depends on Free Software, and again, almost none contribute back meaningfully. A small number are trying to sponsor some projects they're depending on, but it often feels like a crass move for software that are maintained as a hobby: "Could you please garden a bit more efficiently, and add 150 apple trees to your small garden? We need it for our business, here is a 1000€ one-time payment"

更糟糕的是，少数几家计算机相关公司（“云服务提供商”）通过出租运行自由软件的基础设施赚取了惊人的利润，大多数智能设备也在运行自由软件，每一家软件生产/销售公司都依赖自由软件，但同样，几乎没有公司做出有意义的回馈。少数公司试图赞助他们所依赖的项目，但对于那些作为业余爱好维护的软件来说，这通常感觉像是一种粗鲁的举动：“你能更高效地打理花园，并在你的小花园里多加 150 棵苹果树吗？我们的业务需要它，这是一笔 1000 欧元的单次付款。”

With AI making everything worse, Free Software maintainers are now wasting their time dealing with unreviewable useless pull-requests, drowning into a sea of low-quality no-fix-provided bug reports, being demoralized by vibe-coded forks adding shiny features that are never going to be upstreamable and thus upstreamed. It really feels as if nobody is even contemplating making the pedestrian effort of trying to avoid murdering the golden goose.

随着人工智能让一切变得更糟，自由软件维护者现在正浪费时间处理无法审查的无用拉取请求，淹没在低质量、未提供修复方案的错误报告海洋中，并因那些添加了华丽功能但永远无法合并回上游的“感觉派”分支而感到沮丧。真的感觉好像没有人考虑过哪怕付出一点点努力，去避免杀掉那只下金蛋的鹅。

Copyleft does help to mitigate those behaviours a bit, as companies aren't usually big fans to having to release their secret source code. The Asahi Linux project recently re-licensed its software from MIT to LGPL, to make it, if only symbolically, harder for people to profit from the labor without giving anything back. Unfortunately, there are a handful of tricks to bypass copyleft: don't use GPL as all, use strong boundaries (like Android is doing: Linux for its kernel, but everything running on top of it can have whatever license), rent the software to the users (every Cloud is doing that, and most ISP too), provide a context-free dump of the modifications and prevent users from modifying the code their devices are running, simplify ignore the license as the odds of somebody suing you are low, …

著佐权确实有助于在一定程度上缓解这些行为，因为公司通常不喜欢被迫发布其秘密源代码。Asahi Linux 项目最近将其软件许可证从 MIT 改为 LGPL，即使只是象征性地，也是为了让人们更难在不回馈任何东西的情况下从他人的劳动中获利。不幸的是，有一些技巧可以绕过著佐权：根本不使用 GPL，使用强边界（就像 Android 所做的那样：Linux 作为内核，但运行在其之上的任何东西都可以使用任何许可证），将软件租给用户（每家云服务商和大多数 ISP 都在这样做），提供一份脱离上下文的修改转储并阻止用户修改其设备运行的代码，或者干脆无视许可证，因为被人起诉的几率很低……

If copyleft isn't the answer, maybe it's time to find better licenses? In my opinion, the freedom 0, being able to run the program for whatever purpose, might be the crux of the problem. Reticulum's license has the following paragraph, because of course a decentralised secure communication network could help killing people more efficiently: The Software shall not be used in any kind of system which includes amongst its functions the ability to purposefully do harm to human beings. This sounds reasonable in theory, but it means that it's not Free anymore. Do we care? Well, Linux distributions usually have strict policies (debian, fedora, …) when it comes to what software they package, with non-Free ones being either forbidden, on living in their own not-enabled-by-default dedicated repository.

如果著佐权不是答案，也许是时候寻找更好的许可证了？在我看来，“自由 0”（即能够出于任何目的运行程序）可能是问题的症结所在。Reticulum 的许可证包含以下段落，因为去中心化的安全通信网络当然可能被用来更高效地杀人：“本软件不得用于任何包含‘蓄意伤害人类’功能的系统。”这在理论上听起来很合理，但这意味着它不再是“自由”的了。我们在乎吗？嗯，Linux 发行版（如 Debian、Fedora 等）在打包软件时通常有严格的政策，非自由软件要么被禁止，要么只能存放在默认不启用的专用仓库中。

One step further is the The Filthy Human Hands (FHH) License, with: No part of a work under this license may be used or reproduced in any manner by organizations or businesses with more than fifty (50) employees. An exception is granted to allow ISPs and other hosting services to reproduce the work for the purposes of display and distribution. No part of a work under this license may be used or reproduced in any manner which promotes bigotry of any kind. This includes but is not limited to fascism, racism, sexism, misogyny, ableism, transphobia (including erasure and/or denial of nonbinary identities), homophobia, ageism, sane-ism (including denial of multiplicity and/or plurality), and discrimination against non-human identities. What's interesting here, from a technical point of view, is that this isn't really enforceable, legally speaking, as most terms are pretty vague. I think it's by design, and must be taken as a statement.

更进一步的是“肮脏人类之手”（FHH）许可证，其中规定：员工人数超过 50 人的组织或企业不得以任何方式使用或复制本许可证下的作品。ISP 和其他托管服务商为展示和分发目的而复制作品的情况除外。本许可证下的作品不得以任何宣扬偏见的方式使用或复制。这包括但不限于法西斯主义、种族主义、性别歧视、厌女症、残疾歧视、跨性别恐惧症（包括抹杀或否认非二元性别身份）、同性恋恐惧症、年龄歧视、健全主义（包括否认多元身份）以及对非人类身份的歧视。从技术角度来看，这里有趣的是，从法律上讲，这实际上是无法执行的，因为大多数条款都非常模糊。我认为这是有意为之，必须将其视为一种声明。
