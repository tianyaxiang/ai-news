# Michael Nielsen took down his book on neural nets

I wrote this book between 2011 and 2015. At the time, I expected AGI systems based on neural networks to be many decades away, but to bring remarkable benefits for humanity. I also expected significant challenges, but believed it likely that, as with other technologies, those challenges would be addressed as the capabilities were developed.

我在 2011 年到 2015 年间写了这本书。当时，我预计基于神经网络的通用人工智能（AGI）系统还需要几十年才能实现，但它们将为人类带来巨大的福祉。我也预料到了重大的挑战，但相信像其他技术一样，这些挑战会随着能力的提升而得到解决。

My views have changed. With the extraordinarily reckless behaviour of the frontier labs, the systems have raced ahead much faster than I expected, giving humanity far too little time to meet the challenges. Although the systems genuinely will bring — indeed, already are bringing — many wonders, they also pose a significant risk of catastrophe. The frontier labs are knowingly endangering all of humanity. I've written about why I believe this at length, most recently in “Which Future?”. Two brief thoughts are below.

我的观点已经改变了。由于前沿实验室极其鲁莽的行为，这些系统的发展速度远超我的预期，留给人类应对挑战的时间太少了。尽管这些系统确实将带来——实际上已经正在带来——许多奇迹，但它们也构成了巨大的灾难风险。前沿实验室正在明知故犯地危及全人类。我已经在“Which Future?”一文中详细阐述了我的理由。以下是两个简短的想法。

I don't wish to help (even in a very small way) train people to contribute to this work, and so I've taken the book down.

我不希望（哪怕是以非常微小的方式）帮助培训那些为这项工作做出贡献的人，所以我已经撤下了这本书。

Michael Nielsen November 2026

迈克尔·尼尔森，2026 年 11 月

What follows are two brief remarks: one on the nature of the threat posed by ASI, and another on the ethical justification for working on frontier AI.

接下来是两点简短的评论：一点关于超级人工智能（ASI）所构成的威胁本质，另一点关于从事前沿人工智能研究的伦理正当性。

The most common argument for AI existential risk, developed by Nick Bostrom and Eliezer Yudkowsky, is loss of control of the future to advanced AI systems. I believe this concern should be understood as one instance of a more fundamental issue. That issue is whether we will discover technologies latent in nature which are easy and inexpensive to develop, catastrophic in outcome, and beyond our capacity to defend against. If such “progress” in technology were happening slowly, our social institutions might successfully develop defences. But it seems likely that ASI will cause a sudden explosion of such destructive technologies, with insufficient time to adapt.

由尼克·博斯特罗姆（Nick Bostrom）和埃利泽·尤德科夫斯基（Eliezer Yudkowsky）提出的关于人工智能生存风险的最常见论点，是人类对未来的控制权被先进人工智能系统夺走。我认为这种担忧应被理解为一个更根本问题的表现。这个问题是：我们是否会发现自然界中潜藏的、易于开发且成本低廉、但后果具有灾难性且超出我们防御能力的技术。如果这种技术“进步”发生得缓慢，我们的社会机构或许能成功建立防御机制。但看起来，ASI 很可能会导致此类破坏性技术的突然爆发，使我们没有足够的时间去适应。

A historical example to help prime intuition is fire. It is easy to make and extremely difficult to defend against. We have a one-cent technology, the matchstick, which requires hundreds of billions of dollars each year to defend against, as well as a whole apparatus of social institutions, technology, infrastructure and law. AI systems will enable technologies which are far more destructive than matches. Understandably, people skeptical of catastrophic risks want these supposed threats spelled out in detail. This is intrinsically difficult, because we can't reliably anticipate future science and technology. While it is common to speculate about biothreats, I expect the worst threats will be technologies we haven't yet imagined. Nature is, after all, more imaginative than we.

一个有助于启发直觉的历史例子是火。火很容易制造，却极难防御。我们有一种价值一分钱的技术——火柴，但每年需要花费数千亿美元来防范火灾，还需要一整套社会机构、技术、基础设施和法律体系。人工智能系统将催生出比火柴更具破坏性的技术。可以理解，对灾难性风险持怀疑态度的人希望详细说明这些所谓的威胁。这本质上是困难的，因为我们无法可靠地预测未来的科学和技术。虽然猜测生物威胁很常见，但我预计最糟糕的威胁将是我们尚未想象到的技术。毕竟，大自然比我们更有想象力。

Now suppose that ASI enables a 100-times speed-up in technological progress, without a matching change in the rate of social progress or social defenses. To get a sense of what the impact of such a speed-up would be, imagine that all the technological problems that occurred in the 20th century had occurred in the year 1901: the atomic bomb, the hydrogen bomb, the ICBM, chemical weapons, biological weapons, the CFC-caused ozone hole, sulfuric acid rain, climate change, and many more. While we've dealt well with some of these problems, it required decades of concerted work to develop the social institutions and agreements needed for an effective response. Again, defence is often much harder than offence, and relies on different systems. Of course, such a speed-up would also have brought the century's wonders all at once. But imagine another such set of equally difficult problems arising in 1902, and then 1903, and then 1904, and so on. I believe it would have made the world extraordinarily unstable and eventually liable to collapse.

现在假设 ASI 使技术进步速度提高了 100 倍，而社会进步或社会防御的速度却没有相应改变。为了感受这种加速的影响，想象一下 20 世纪发生的所有技术问题都在 1901 年发生：原子弹、氢弹、洲际弹道导弹、化学武器、生物武器、氟利昂导致的臭氧层空洞、硫酸酸雨、气候变化等等。虽然我们很好地处理了其中一些问题，但这需要几十年的共同努力来发展有效的社会机构和协议。再次强调，防御往往比进攻难得多，且依赖于不同的系统。当然，这种加速也会同时带来那个世纪的所有奇迹。但想象一下，另一组同样困难的问题在 1902 年出现，接着是 1903 年、1904 年，以此类推。我相信这会使世界变得极其不稳定，并最终导致崩溃。

The issue with ASI, then, is the destructive power latent in nature. It doesn't matter whether that power is wielded by humans or by out-of-control machines. I make this point because many people mistakenly believe that loss of control to such rogue machines is the fundamental issue. This leads them to treat AI safety as principally a technical problem, which can be addressed by “aligning” individual systems. Unfortunately, it cannot. Furthermore, such a view makes it seem as though the labs are the primary places where safety can be addressed. But such technical alignment is mostly market-supplied safety, which makes the systems more palatable to consumers and governments. It speeds development, and thus makes the fundamental underlying problem worse, while only addressing an (important) subsidiary problem.

因此，ASI 的问题在于自然界中潜藏的破坏力。这种力量是由人类还是失控的机器所掌控并不重要。我提出这一点是因为许多人错误地认为，对这些流氓机器失去控制才是根本问题。这导致他们将人工智能安全主要视为一个技术问题，可以通过“对齐”单个系统来解决。不幸的是，事实并非如此。此外，这种观点让人觉得实验室是解决安全问题的首要场所。但这种技术对齐大多是市场提供的安全保障，使系统更容易被消费者和政府接受。它加速了开发，从而加剧了根本性的潜在问题，而仅仅解决了（一个重要的）次要问题。

My view isn't popular, in part because it makes the problem of technology about making the entire world safe, not only about aligning particular AI systems. Indeed, even in a future where humanity no longer existed, the same problem would afflict any successor AI civilization: it would have to defend itself against these destructive capacities. Similarly, it afflicts any model of the future based on human uplift, through brain-computer interfaces and similar augmentation: the issue isn't which substrate intelligence runs on, but the dual-use nature of deep understanding of reality. In some sense, it's a question of whether sentience in the cosmos is compatible with advanced understanding of nature. Of course, I hope it is compatible. But rushing the technology while the broader social response lags seems likely to result in failure on our particular planet.

我的观点并不受欢迎，部分原因是它将技术问题上升到了让整个世界变得安全的高度，而不仅仅是关于对齐特定的人工智能系统。事实上，即使在人类不再存在的未来，同样的问题也会困扰任何继任的人工智能文明：它必须保护自己免受这些破坏性能力的影响。同样，它也困扰着任何基于人类提升（通过脑机接口和类似增强技术）的未来模型：问题不在于智能运行在什么基质上，而在于对现实的深刻理解所具有的双重用途。从某种意义上说，这是一个关于宇宙中的感知能力是否与对自然的先进理解相兼容的问题。当然，我希望它是兼容的。但在更广泛的社会反应滞后的情况下仓促推进技术，似乎很可能导致我们这个星球的失败。

Many people inside the frontier AI labs genuinely believe AI systems may pose a catastrophic threat. Why then do they help build such systems? They often justify their participation by arguing that it's better they do it than someone else does. Dario Amodei, for example, has made versions of this argument often: “If one company does not build it, others will do so nearly as fast”; “Not building the technology deprives humanity of benefits or simply places AI in the hands of authoritarian powers”; and Anthropic itself gives, as its founding rationale, “if powerful AI is coming regardless, Anthropic believes it's better

许多前沿人工智能实验室内部的人确实相信人工智能系统可能构成灾难性威胁。那么，他们为什么要帮助构建这些系统呢？他们经常通过辩称“由他们来做比由别人来做更好”来为自己的参与辩护。例如，达里奥·阿莫代（Dario Amodei）经常提出这种论点的变体：“如果一家公司不开发，其他人也会以几乎同样的速度开发”；“不开发这项技术会剥夺人类的利益，或者仅仅是将人工智能置于威权力量手中”；而 Anthropic 公司本身给出的成立理由是：“如果强大的 AI 无论如何都会到来，Anthropic 认为最好……”
