Some years ago, my colleague Ben Southwood sent me a handful of grainy online pictures showing some recent buildings in Pakistan. I was struck by the assurance of the designs and the quality of the materials, craftsmanship, and detailing. They seemed to be the work of one Kamil Khan Mumtaz, a Pakistani architect widely admired in his home country but almost invisible abroad. I wondered whether Mumtaz might be the world’s best architect, overlooked because global architecture magazines rarely do photoshoots in Lahore. Last year, I met his assistant Sohaib Ahmed at a conference and immediately entreated him to send Works in Progress any photographs he had of the practice’s work. With Mumtaz’s support, Sohaib prepared a portfolio for us, most of it never before seen outside Pakistan.

几年前，我的同事本·索斯伍德（Ben Southwood）发给我几张模糊的网络照片，展示了巴基斯坦近期的一些建筑。这些设计所展现出的自信，以及材料、工艺和细节的质量，令我深感震撼。它们似乎出自卡米尔·汗·蒙塔兹（Kamil Khan Mumtaz）之手，这位巴基斯坦建筑师在国内备受推崇，但在国外却几乎鲜为人知。我曾怀疑蒙塔兹是否是世界上最优秀的建筑师，只是因为全球建筑杂志很少去拉合尔拍摄而遭到了忽视。去年，我在一次会议上见到了他的助手苏海布·艾哈迈德（Sohaib Ahmed），并立即恳请他将该事务所的作品照片发送给《进展》（Works in Progress）。在蒙塔兹的支持下，苏海布为我们准备了一份作品集，其中大部分内容此前从未在巴基斯坦境外公开过。

Kamil Khan Mumtaz was born in Calcutta in 1939 and grew up in what became Pakistan, taking British A-levels at Lahore’s elite Aitchison College. He then moved to London to study at the Architectural Association. Although he had never previously left South Asia, Mumtaz immediately felt at home in England. ‘It seemed completely familiar. I didn’t feel like a stranger at all’, he recalled in an interview with the historian Chris Moffat. ‘I think this is because of having been brought up on essentially English books, in English schools. So, the whole thing was like, “I know this place”.’ Mumtaz enjoyed his studies at the Architectural Association, which taught a standard modernist approach that Mumtaz accepted unquestioningly: ‘I just simply took it hook, line and sinker, and swallowed the whole thing.’

卡米尔·汗·蒙塔兹于1939年出生在加尔各答，在后来成为巴基斯坦的地区长大，并在拉合尔精英云集的艾奇逊学院（Aitchison College）修读了英国A-level课程。随后，他前往伦敦，在建筑联盟学院（Architectural Association）学习。尽管此前从未离开过南亚，但蒙塔兹在英国却感到宾至如归。“一切看起来都非常熟悉。我一点也不觉得陌生，”他在接受历史学家克里斯·莫法特（Chris Moffat）采访时回忆道，“我想这是因为我基本上是在英国书籍和英国学校的教育下长大的。所以，这一切就像是‘我了解这个地方’。”蒙塔兹很享受在建筑联盟学院的学习时光，那里教授的是标准的现代主义方法，而蒙塔兹对此全盘接受，毫无质疑：“我只是简单地全盘照收，囫囵吞枣地接受了这一切。”

Mumtaz returned to Pakistan in 1966 and began practicing as an architect. At around the same time, he became a communist activist, on account of which he was twice imprisoned by the military dictatorship. He remained resolutely modernist, although he acquired special interest in adapting modernism to the local climate and locally sourced building materials. He also became involved in architectural conservation. In the late 1970s, he prepared a report on the ancient Walled City of Lahore, highlighting its enormous heritage value and arguing for its preservation. ‘Our own bureaucrats only insisted that we should pull down the whole mess anyway, and build new, modern infrastructure and high-rise buildings, et cetera’, Mumtaz remembers. The Walled City was ultimately saved, and parts of it are now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

1966年，蒙塔兹回到巴基斯坦，开始从事建筑师职业。大约在同一时期，他成为了一名共产主义活动家，并因此两次被军事独裁政权监禁。他始终坚定地坚持现代主义，尽管他开始对将现代主义适应当地气候和本地建筑材料产生了浓厚兴趣。他还参与了建筑保护工作。20世纪70年代末，他撰写了一份关于拉合尔古老“围城”（Walled City）的报告，强调了其巨大的遗产价值，并主张对其进行保护。“我们自己的官僚们只是一味坚持要把这一切拆掉，去建造新的现代基础设施和高层建筑等等，”蒙塔兹回忆道。最终，这座围城得以保存，其部分区域现已被列为联合国教科文组织世界遗产。

Through the 1970s, Mumtaz began to question his approach. ‘I had this growing, nagging fear that I wasn’t communicating with my audience, as a visual artist or as an architect. This grew on me, almost like an obsession.’ In 1980, the Iranian architect Nader Ardalan gave him a copy of his book The Sense of Unity: The Sufi Tradition in Persian Architecture. Mumtaz initially paid no attention: ‘Flipping through it, I thought, “Oh yeah, this is traditional Iranian architecture, who cares!” I was totally uninterested.’ But eventually, frustrated by the sense that ‘I didn’t know the language of my own people’, he began to read. ‘From page one, it was mind-blowing. Absolutely. I thought, “My god, what have I been looking for? This is what I’ve been missing!” ... It shattered all of the beliefs I had, being a Marxist, a materialist, an atheist, a modernist.’

进入20世纪70年代，蒙塔兹开始质疑自己的方法。“我有一种日益增长的、挥之不去的恐惧，觉得作为一名视觉艺术家或建筑师，我无法与我的受众进行交流。这种感觉在我心中不断滋长，几乎成了一种执念。”1980年，伊朗建筑师纳德·阿达兰（Nader Ardalan）送给他一本自己的著作《统一感：波斯建筑中的苏菲传统》（The Sense of Unity: The Sufi Tradition in Persian Architecture）。起初，蒙塔兹并没有在意：“翻阅时，我想，‘哦，这是传统的伊朗建筑，谁在乎呢！’我完全不感兴趣。”但最终，由于感到“我不了解自己人民的语言”而倍受挫折，他开始阅读这本书。“从第一页开始，它就令人震撼。绝对震撼。我想，‘天哪，我一直在寻找什么？这就是我所缺失的东西！’……它粉碎了我作为马克思主义者、唯物主义者、无神论者和现代主义者所持有的所有信念。”

In the decades since, Mumtaz has worked to revive the older architectural traditions of Pakistan, and especially of Lahore, his home city. He has built a wide range of buildings, including homes, offices, mosques, and schools. His work has been received with warmth in Pakistan, though also with surprise. ‘I’ve found that there is a huge reservoir of desire, sympathy, and love for our traditional values, forms, architecture, and culture, altogether. But there’s a general kind of impression that these things can’t be done any more, there are no craftsmen who can do this.’

在此后的几十年里，蒙塔兹致力于复兴巴基斯坦，尤其是他家乡拉合尔的古老建筑传统。他建造了各种各样的建筑，包括住宅、办公室、清真寺和学校。他的作品在巴基斯坦受到了热烈欢迎，但也伴随着惊讶。“我发现，人们对我们的传统价值观、形式、建筑和文化有着巨大的渴望、同情和热爱。但普遍存在一种印象，认为这些东西已经无法再现了，没有工匠能做到这一点。”

The loss of craft skills is a problem for traditional architecture everywhere. Some elements of traditional architecture have been prefabricated in factories for centuries, but even then, skill is needed for installation. Without skilled craftspeople, important details are often badly executed, making buildings look strange or clumsy. This problem also exists in Pakistan, but its later industrialization meant premodern craft traditions had not quite vanished when Mumtaz began work. Mumtaz recalled that, when searching for skilled craftsmen, ‘immediately, you come into contact with very small remnants of these families, hereditary craftsmen, and so the relationship grows.’

工艺技能的丧失是各地传统建筑面临的共同问题。传统建筑的某些元素几个世纪以来一直在工厂中预制，但即便如此，安装仍需要技巧。如果没有熟练的工匠，重要的细节往往执行得很差，使建筑看起来奇怪或笨拙。巴基斯坦也存在这个问题，但其较晚的工业化进程意味着，当蒙塔兹开始工作时，前现代的工艺传统尚未完全消失。蒙塔兹回忆说，当寻找熟练工匠时，“你很快就能接触到这些家族中仅存的一小部分人，即世袭工匠，于是这种关系就建立起来了。”

Mumtaz’s work has become steadily more technically sophisticated as he and his craftsmen have built up expertise. ‘Together we have been able to rediscover a number of the practices that had become lost. Certain types of brickwork, the flat dome, the muqarnas [ornamental vaults], many things like this that hadn’t been done. As they tell me, “This has not been done for 300 years!” So, it was a great experience, a voyage of discovery, so much that we learnt from each other.’

随着蒙塔兹和他的工匠们积累了专业知识，他的作品在技术上也变得越来越成熟。“我们共同重新发现了一些已经失传的技艺。某些类型的砖砌工艺、平顶穹窿、穆卡纳斯（muqarnas，装饰性拱顶）等等，许多类似的东西以前都没有做过。正如他们告诉我的，‘这已经有300年没人做过了！’所以，这是一次伟大的经历，一次发现之旅，我们从彼此身上学到了很多。”

It is my great pleasure to introduce our readers to one of the most skilled and thoughtful architects working today.

我非常荣幸能向我们的读者介绍当今最娴熟、最具思想性的建筑师之一。

Now in his mid-eighties, Mumtaz continues to build. ‘It does feel like you’ve set the foundations for an alternative approach to contemporary urban environments’, an interviewer recently suggested to him. ‘I’m just keeping my fingers crossed! No idea what will happen’, Mumtaz replied.

如今已八十多岁的蒙塔兹仍在继续建筑创作。“感觉您确实为当代城市环境提供了一种替代性方案的基石，”一位采访者最近向他建议道。蒙塔兹回答说：“我只能祈祷好运！不知道未来会怎样。”
