Over the last few weeks, I’ve been talking to Alexander (Avi) Davidson. There are a lot of remarkable things about Avi. He sustained a horrific injury as a teenager, which left him paralyzed and without a left hand. Today, he uses his own experience to guide his practice as a trauma therapist. He’s also taking part in a clinical trial of some new tech that allows him to move—and feel with—a prosthetic hand.

在过去的几周里，我一直在与亚历山大（Avi）·戴维森交谈。Avi身上有很多非凡之处。他在青少年时期遭受了可怕的伤害，导致瘫痪并失去了左手。如今，他利用自己的经历来指导他作为创伤治疗师的工作。他目前还参与了一项新技术的临床试验，这项技术使他能够移动假肢，并能通过假肢产生触感。

Unlike standard prosthetics, this one connects directly to nerves in Avi's arm. He literally has wires coming out of his arm to plug it in with. Avi’s the ninth person to trial this tech. But he’s the first to take it home and use it independently, for things like drinking from a cup (which is not easy) and writing (also not easy).

与标准假肢不同，这种假肢直接连接到Avi手臂的神经上。他的手臂上确实伸出了电线，以便进行连接。Avi是第九位试验这项技术的人。但他却是第一位将其带回家并独立使用的人，用于像用杯子喝水（这并不容易）和书写（同样不容易）之类的事情。

What struck me the most over our conversations was Avi’s outlook and approach to not just his injuries and learning to use this cutting-edge technology, but to life itself. He has learned how to use something like seven different prosthetics in the 17 years since his accident. By taking part in this trial, he’s had to fit hundreds of hours of lab-based training and physical therapy around his demanding job. He had surgery to implant electrodes in his arm and will eventually need another to remove them. And he won’t even get to keep the latest prosthetic long term.

在我们的交谈中，最让我印象深刻的是Avi的观点，以及他不仅对待伤痛和学习使用这种尖端技术，而且对待生活的态度。自事故发生以来的17年里，他已经学会了使用大约七种不同的假肢。通过参与这项试验，他必须在繁重的工作之余，挤出数百小时进行实验室培训和物理治疗。他接受了手术，将电极植入他的手臂，最终还需要进行另一次手术将其取出。而且，他甚至无法长期保留这个最新的假肢。

“While it’s unfortunate at times, and irritating at times, I do not regret what has come from [the accident], or the fact that it happened,” he told me. “I am not going to benefit from this … I wanted to be a part of this in the context of being able to help push the technology forward.”

“虽然有时会感到不幸和烦恼，但我并不后悔（事故）带来的结果，也不后悔它曾经发生，”他告诉我。“我不会从中受益……我想要参与其中，是因为我希望能帮助推动这项技术的发展。”

The research team at the University of Utah that is running the trial hopes it will answer longstanding questions about the technology. How does it fare in a real-world setting, outside of the lab? What do people end up using it for? Where can improvements be made? There may be some surprises along the way. Avi has found that his prosthetic wrist can feel sore and stiff when it’s been overused, just like his flesh-and-blood wrist. Weirdly, that feeling can be relieved by stretching it out. What’s going on here? No one really knows.

负责这项试验的犹他大学研究团队希望它能回答关于该技术的长期疑问。它在实验室之外的现实环境中表现如何？人们最终会用它来做什么？哪些地方可以改进？过程中可能会有一些惊喜。Avi发现，当他的假肢手腕被过度使用时，会感到酸痛和僵硬，就像他真实的血肉手腕一样。奇怪的是，这种感觉可以通过伸展来缓解。这是怎么回事？没人真正知道。

Scientists don’t know whether Avi’s brain will change as a result of using the bionic hand, either. Before the trial started, they performed brain scans to see how his missing left hand was represented in his brain. Will that change? And what might that mean for people who are born without limbs—how might a bionic limb affect their brains?

科学家们也不知道Avi的大脑是否会因为使用仿生手而发生改变。在试验开始前，他们进行了脑部扫描，以观察他缺失的左手在大脑中是如何呈现的。这会改变吗？这对天生残疾的人意味着什么——仿生肢体可能会如何影响他们的大脑？

Avi will have the device until late January 2027 at the least, and potentially for another year. After that? Again, no one knows. It will depend on how the research is progressing, how Avi feels, and how much funding the team can secure. As a result, it’s not clear how long the device might even be expected to last in a person’s body. I’ll be following this study, though, and will keep you posted.

Avi至少会使用该设备到2027年1月底，甚至可能再多一年。那之后呢？同样，没人知道。这将取决于研究的进展、Avi的感受以及团队能获得多少资金。因此，目前还不清楚该设备在人体内预计能持续使用多久。不过，我会持续关注这项研究，并随时向大家通报最新情况。
