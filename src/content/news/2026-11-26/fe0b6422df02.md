AI is straining America’s open records systems. AI has intensified investors' interest in public records. Over the last few years, venture-backed startups have raised upwards of $100 million to collect data from thousands of agencies and turn it into AI-powered "signals" that help businesses sell to governments. At the same time, chatbots are making it easier for individuals to file requests. As agencies struggle to tell whether they’re dealing with a citizen, a chatbot, or a sales pipeline, some states are passing legislation that makes it easier to deny requests. That could potentially make public offices less transparent. Find out how AI is testing the limits of America's right to know. —Matthew King MIT Technology Review

人工智能正在给美国的公开记录系统带来压力。人工智能加剧了投资者对公共记录的兴趣。在过去几年里，风险投资支持的初创公司筹集了超过 1 亿美元，用于从数千个机构收集数据，并将其转化为人工智能驱动的“信号”，帮助企业向政府销售产品。与此同时，聊天机器人让个人更容易提交请求。由于各机构难以分辨他们面对的是公民、聊天机器人还是销售渠道，一些州正在通过立法，使拒绝请求变得更容易。这可能会使公共办公室的透明度降低。了解人工智能如何考验美国知情权的极限。——马修·金，《麻省理工科技评论》

One more thing: Kids outlearn AI—and we still don’t know why. Teaching a computer to use human language requires an inhuman amount of data. An LLM can easily churn through a hundred thousand times more words than a person will experience in the process of mastering their mother tongue. This yawning divide between children and machines is called the data efficiency gap. It raises a tantalizing question for researchers: How can children learn so much with so little? By reverse-engineering how they learn, scientists hope to create more efficient AI models—and perhaps settle enduring questions about language and children's developing minds. Here’s how they’re trying to close the data gap. —Elise Cutts
