# AI is straining America’s open records systems

In Panton, Vermont, population 660, town clerk Kyle Rowe did it all. He crafted budgets, paid vendors, handled property tax disputes, and served as the town’s zoning supervisor and presiding elections officer. During morning office hours at the town hall—a wood-frame structure built as a church in 1858—he also fielded public records requests. Recently, he got rather busy.

在佛蒙特州潘顿镇，人口仅 660 人，镇书记员凯尔·罗（Kyle Rowe）几乎包揽了一切。他负责编制预算、支付供应商款项、处理房产税纠纷，并担任该镇的规划主管和选举主持官员。在市政厅（一座建于 1858 年的木结构教堂）的办公时间内，他还要处理公共记录请求。最近，他变得非常忙碌。

Panton used to get a request every year or so from a company called SmartProcure for “all purchasing records,” covering everything from road salt to computers to toilet paper. Last year, those requests started coming quarterly. Then different companies began making more specific requests, like records of every purchase related to emergency road flares. These targeted inquiries started coming monthly or more.

过去，潘顿镇每年大约会收到一家名为 SmartProcure 的公司发来的一次请求，要求获取“所有采购记录”，内容涵盖从道路融雪盐到电脑再到卫生纸的各种物资。去年，这些请求开始变为每季度一次。随后，不同的公司开始提出更具体的请求，例如与紧急道路照明弹相关的所有采购记录。这些针对性的咨询开始每月甚至更频繁地出现。

“I’m happy to answer public records requests from people,” says Rowe, who recently moved into a position at a nearby town. “But answering it for these purposes—it feels like I’m doing unpaid opposition research on the taxpayers’ time.” Rowe’s experiences are being repeated across the US. While data brokers have mined public records for decades, AI has intensified investors’ interest.

“我很乐意回答人们提出的公共记录请求，”最近调往附近城镇工作的罗说，“但为了这些目的去回答——感觉就像是在用纳税人的时间做无偿的反对派调查。”罗的经历正在美国各地重演。虽然数据经纪人几十年来一直在挖掘公共记录，但人工智能加剧了投资者的兴趣。

Over the last few years, venture-backed startups have raised upwards of $100 million to collect data from thousands of agencies and turn it into AI-powered “signals” that help businesses sell to governments. At the same time, AI tools have made it easier for individuals to draft public records requests. Many places have seen double-digit annual increases in recent years.

在过去几年里，风险投资支持的初创公司筹集了超过 1 亿美元的资金，用于从数千个机构收集数据，并将其转化为人工智能驱动的“信号”，帮助企业向政府销售产品。与此同时，人工智能工具使个人更容易起草公共记录请求。近年来，许多地方的请求数量出现了两位数的年度增长。

While more activity is good for democracy, AI-drafted requests tend to be longer, and broader in scope—and the offices answering them haven’t always grown in head count or budget. As agencies struggle to tell whether they’re dealing with a citizen, a chatbot, or a sales pipeline, some states are passing legislation that makes it easier to deny requests, possibly making public offices less transparent.

虽然更多的活动对民主有益，但人工智能起草的请求往往更长、范围更广，而负责回复这些请求的办公室在人员编制或预算上并未同步增长。由于各机构难以分辨他们面对的是公民、聊天机器人还是销售渠道，一些州正在通过立法，使拒绝请求变得更容易，这可能会导致公共部门的透明度降低。

Transparency, monetized. The US Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) of 1966 established citizens’ right to know what their government is doing, and by now every state has written its own version into law. Greater transparency has led to cleaner drinking water, lower taxes, and better schools. But the system is far from perfect: Many requests linger for months, the process is often frustratingly bureaucratic, and rules and guidelines vary by state, county, and even town.

透明度，货币化。1966 年的美国《信息自由法》（FOIA）确立了公民了解政府行为的权利，如今每个州都已将各自的版本写入法律。更高的透明度带来了更清洁的饮用水、更低的税收和更好的学校。但该系统远非完美：许多请求被搁置数月，流程往往繁琐得令人沮丧，且规则和指南因州、县甚至城镇而异。

This is why businesses, with the time and resources to navigate this labyrinth, have often dominated FOIA since its early days. Margaret Kwoka, a law professor at Ohio State University and a recent member of the FOIA Advisory Committee, documented the trend in her 2016 study “FOIA, Inc.” Her analysis found that commercial requesters of all kinds—regulated companies, government contractors, information resellers—have accounted for 79% to 96% of public records requests at federal agencies like the Environmental Protection Agency and the Food and Drug Administration.

这就是为什么那些拥有时间和资源来应对这一迷宫的企业，自《信息自由法》实施初期以来就一直占据主导地位。俄亥俄州立大学法学教授、FOIA 咨询委员会前成员玛格丽特·克沃卡（Margaret Kwoka）在 2016 年的研究《FOIA, Inc.》中记录了这一趋势。她的分析发现，各类商业请求者——受监管公司、政府承包商、信息转售商——在环境保护署和食品药品监督管理局等联邦机构的公共记录请求中占比高达 79% 至 96%。

What Kwoka once described as a cottage industry of small shops reselling public records has become a lucrative market for “much more sophisticated and integrated database products,” she says. SmartProcure (which does business as GovSpend) pioneered the model 15 years ago, with a standardized approach for collecting government purchasing data. Its requests are “handled by real people with long-standing relationships,” says Trip Colden, GovSpend’s chief operating officer, and the company has preferential pricing for agencies.

克沃卡曾将这种转售公共记录的小作坊式产业描述为“家庭手工业”，而现在它已成为一个利润丰厚的市场，充斥着“更复杂、更集成的数据库产品”。SmartProcure（以 GovSpend 名义运营）在 15 年前开创了这种模式，采用标准化方法收集政府采购数据。GovSpend 首席运营官特里普·科尔登（Trip Colden）表示，其请求由“拥有长期合作关系的真人处理”，且该公司为各机构提供优惠定价。

The newer AI startups promise to make selling to government faster and fairer. Starbridge has raised $52 million, including a Series A led by Craft Ventures (the firm of David Sacks, the former White House AI and crypto czar), to build the equivalent of a “Bloomberg Terminal for public sector data,” a reference to the pricey data consoles that are a fixture on Wall Street trading desks. The company describes its mission as getting “the best technology into the hands of public leaders,” with public records as one input that helps match agencies with vendors.

较新的 AI 初创公司承诺让向政府销售产品变得更快、更公平。Starbridge 已筹集 5200 万美元，其中包括由 Craft Ventures（前白宫人工智能和加密货币主管大卫·萨克斯的公司）领投的 A 轮融资，旨在打造公共部门数据的“彭博终端”，这指的是华尔街交易桌上常见的昂贵数据终端。该公司将其使命描述为将“最好的技术交到公共领导人手中”，并将公共记录作为帮助机构匹配供应商的输入信息之一。

Pursuit, backed by investors including Bill Gurley and Jack Altman, has raised $25.5 million to help companies identify likely government buyers, drawing on board minutes, budgets, and what it calls “an industrial-scale FOIA operation.” CEO and cofounder Mike Vichich says Pursuit’s data will benefit the public, noting that many towns are seeing costs rise faster than revenues and “competition is what saves taxpayers money.”

在比尔·格利（Bill Gurley）和杰克·奥特曼（Jack Altman）等投资者的支持下，Pursuit 筹集了 2550 万美元，旨在利用董事会会议记录、预算以及所谓的“工业级 FOIA 操作”来帮助企业识别潜在的政府买家。首席执行官兼联合创始人迈克·维奇奇（Mike Vichich）表示，Pursuit 的数据将造福公众，并指出许多城镇的成本增长速度快于收入，“竞争才是为纳税人省钱的关键”。

While incumbents employ account teams to build a rapport with agencies, several clerks and county attorneys say newer startups often send many requests as they scramble to catch up on historical data. For example, PinPoint Analytics, which analyzes construction bid data, sent as many as 2,000 requests per day while building its dataset before settling closer to 500 today, says chief operating officer Mark Zurada. That mirrors the activity of SmartProcure and Pursuit, which send roughly 180,000 requests per year, according to their websites.

虽然老牌公司雇佣客户团队与各机构建立融洽关系，但几位书记员和县检察官表示，较新的初创公司在争相补齐历史数据时，往往会发送大量请求。例如，分析建筑投标数据的 PinPoint Analytics 的首席运营官马克·祖拉达（Mark Zurada）表示，该公司在构建数据集时每天发送多达 2000 次请求，如今已稳定在每天 500 次左右。这反映了 SmartProcure 和 Pursuit 的活动情况，据其网站显示，这两家公司每年发送约 18 万次请求。

But the numbers can be much higher. NationGraph, which serves midsize vendors such as janitorial service providers, claims to send about 10,000 requests per day on behalf of clients; the company did not respond to requests for comment. The requests that companies send are typically written with templates, not AI. But AI helps standardize what comes back—spreadsheets, PDFs, mailed packets—and analyze it all to create branded sales “signals,” or benchmarks to help the business clients set their prices.

但数字可能更高。为清洁服务提供商等中型供应商提供服务的 NationGraph 声称，其代表客户每天发送约 1 万次请求；该公司未回应置评请求。公司发送的请求通常使用模板编写，而非人工智能。但人工智能有助于标准化返回的内容（电子表格、PDF、邮寄包裹），并对其进行分析，从而创建品牌化的销售“信号”或基准，帮助商业客户设定价格。

In small towns, this activity can be an annoyance; in cities and counties, which get many times the volume, handling it is becoming a full-time job. Chautauqua County, New York, hired a paralegal this summer after seeing requests double—an increase its county attorney blamed on people using ChatGPT to generate requests and on others seeking records to resell.
