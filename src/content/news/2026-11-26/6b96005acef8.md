# Airplane Turbulence Is Extremely Hard to Predict, but Maybe Not for Long

In the early 1500s, an Italian polymath named Leonardo da Vinci took a good crack at exactly the phenomenon that makes nervous fliers clutch their airplane seats in fear and flight attendants push their carts back to the galleys with haste: turbulence.

16世纪初，一位名叫列奥纳多·达·芬奇的意大利博学家曾深入研究过一种现象，正是这种现象让紧张的乘客在飞机上惊恐地紧抓座椅，也让空乘人员匆忙将餐车推回厨房：这就是湍流。

Da Vinci was fascinated by swirling, chaotic eddies of water, movements so complex that he made hundreds of drawings of his observations. Why, exactly, did water—any fluid, really, be it atmospheric air or blood in the heart—move in that way?

达·芬奇对水流中混乱的漩涡着迷不已，这些运动极其复杂，以至于他为自己的观察绘制了数百幅草图。究竟为什么水——或者任何流体，无论是大气中的空气还是心脏里的血液——会以那种方式运动呢？

Now the world has ChatGPT and self-driving cars and solar farms, and it still doesn’t exactly know how turbulence works. “It’s the hardest remaining problem in classical physics, and it’s one we need to fly through,” says Michael Murillo, a professor of theoretical and computational physics at Michigan State University.

如今，世界已经拥有了ChatGPT、自动驾驶汽车和太阳能农场，但人类仍然无法确切地理解湍流的运作机制。密歇根州立大学理论与计算物理学教授迈克尔·穆里略表示：“这是经典物理学中遗留的最难问题，也是我们必须穿越的问题。”

Now a bumper crop of data-driven efforts to predict and avoid turbulence could get fliers closer to a smooth flight. Twenty terabytes of data are created by an airplane’s engine in one hour of commercial flying, according to the International Air Transport Association, with more broadcast by airplanes midflight: GPS position, altitude, speed.

现在，大量以数据为驱动的预测和规避湍流的尝试，可能会让乘客离平稳飞行更近一步。据国际航空运输协会统计，一架商用飞机飞行一小时，其引擎就会产生20太字节的数据，此外飞机在飞行中还会广播更多信息：GPS位置、高度和速度。

An “electronic flight bag,” basically a specialized iPad, is equipped with navigation charts and manuals and can transmit its own information, as long as the airplane’s internet connection cooperates. Satellites watch and collect it all.

“电子飞行包”（本质上是一种特制的iPad）配备了导航图和手册，只要飞机的互联网连接正常，它就能传输自身的信息。卫星则负责监测并收集所有这些数据。

“Air traffic has gone up, and communication costs and the costs of putting computers on aircraft have come down,” says Larry Cornman, a physicist with the National Science Foundation’s National Center for Atmospheric Research who has been studying turbulence for decades. “The time is now to work on these things because we can do them.”

“空中交通量增加了，而通信成本以及在飞机上安装计算机的成本却下降了，”美国国家科学基金会国家大气研究中心的物理学家拉里·科恩曼说，他研究湍流已有数十年。“现在是研究这些问题的时机，因为我们已经具备了实现的能力。”

This is good news for aviation safety. More than 200 people were seriously injured on US airplanes in turbulence-related incidents between 2009 and 2023, according to FAA data. Turbulence is also getting worse. Climate change alters the atmospheric conditions around the world’s jet streams, creating destabilizing wind shear.

这对航空安全来说是个好消息。根据美国联邦航空管理局（FAA）的数据，2009年至2023年间，有超过200人在美国航班的湍流相关事故中受重伤。此外，湍流状况正在恶化。气候变化改变了全球急流周围的大气条件，产生了不稳定的风切变。

From the cockpit of an airplane, this translates into sudden, clear-air turbulence, difficult to see or predict. Historically, pilots’ only method of avoiding turbulence is heeding the warning of those who accidentally went through it before them, and hoping that nothing had changed.

从驾驶舱的角度来看，这意味着突如其来的晴空湍流，这种湍流难以观测或预测。从历史上看，飞行员规避湍流的唯一方法就是听取那些不幸遭遇过湍流的前人的警告，并祈祷情况没有发生变化。

But just in the past two years, Cornman’s decades of work on turbulence has been put into the field in ways it couldn’t be before, part of several projects coordinated by the US federal government and the airline industry. One breakthrough came when Cornman and an FAA air traffic controller determined that aircrafts’ federally required broadcasts of their positions, altitude, and speed could be used to spot unexpected turbulence, without requiring new hardware or software onboard.

但在过去两年里，科恩曼数十年来对湍流的研究成果已以前所未有的方式投入应用，这是美国联邦政府与航空业协调开展的多个项目的一部分。一项突破性进展是，科恩曼与一名FAA空中交通管制员确定，飞机联邦强制广播的位置、高度和速度信息可用于识别突发湍流，而无需在机上安装新的硬件或软件。

Researchers can use this crowdsourced, public data to make predictions about when and where turbulence will pop up.

研究人员可以利用这些众包的公共数据，对湍流出现的时间和地点进行预测。

In September, the National Science Foundation announced a $30 million award to nine universities to stand up a new center dedicated to predicting turbulence—in the air, but also in the charged particles inside a fusion reactor, and in the gas clouds that stretch across space.

9月，美国国家科学基金会宣布向九所大学拨款3000万美元，用于建立一个新的中心，专门致力于预测湍流——不仅是大气中的湍流，还包括核聚变反应堆内带电粒子的湍流，以及横跨太空的气体云中的湍流。

“Turbulence is the reason we don’t have fusion energy yet,” says Murillo, who heads up the new center, called Transformative Explorations in Multi-Physics and Engineering of Scientific Turbulence, or Tempest. The chaotic swirling of superhot plasma inside a reactor allows heat to leak out, preventing the material from reaching the temperatures needed to allow nuclei to fuse. The US federal government wants to get closer to figuring that out.

“湍流是我们至今尚未实现核聚变能源的原因，”负责这个名为“科学湍流多物理与工程变革性探索”（Tempest）新中心的穆里略说。反应堆内超高温等离子体的混乱漩涡导致热量流失，阻碍了材料达到核聚变所需的温度。美国联邦政府希望在解决这一问题上取得进展。

“Closer,” because turbulence is impossible to perfectly predict, scientists say. For one thing, turbulence occurs at many different, small scales at the same time. Air swirling around an airplane breaks into eddies, then smaller eddies, and then even smaller eddies, with each interacting with each other—too many variables, happening too quickly, to mathematically account for.

科学家们说，只能是“更近一步”，因为湍流是不可能被完美预测的。首先，湍流同时发生在许多不同的微小尺度上。飞机周围旋转的空气会分解成漩涡，然后是更小的漩涡，再到更小的漩涡，每一个漩涡都在相互作用——变量太多且发生得太快，无法通过数学手段完全计算。

For another, tiny changes—wind speed, temperature—can eventually dramatically alter air movement, suddenly altering the turbulence once predicted. To make perfect forecasts, one would need a perfect picture of exactly what’s going on in the atmosphere at all times.

其次，风速、温度等微小的变化最终可能会剧烈改变空气运动，从而突然改变已经预测到的湍流。要做出完美的预测，就需要随时掌握大气中正在发生的每一件事的完美图景。

But increasing sophisticated applications of machine learning could allow scientists to get closer to knowing what’s going to happen when, says Murillo. And while brute, computational force can’t solve the turbulence problem, AI agents might give the effort a boost. OpenAI’s blockbuster announcement this fall that it had resolved the Navier-Stokes problem—equations that are the beginning of all turbulence models—suggests LLMs could help.

但穆里略表示，机器学习日益复杂的应用可能使科学家更接近于了解何时会发生什么。虽然单纯的计算能力无法解决湍流问题，但人工智能代理可能会推动这一努力。OpenAI今年秋天宣布其已解决纳维-斯托克斯问题（这是所有湍流模型的方程基础），这一重磅消息表明大语言模型（LLM）或许能提供帮助。

“Turbulence has been a problem only human minds could work on; as of this year, that's no longer true,” says Murillo.

“湍流曾经是一个只能由人类大脑来解决的问题；但从今年开始，情况不再如此了，”穆里略说。

To make flying better, the aviation industry doesn’t necessarily need to solve turbulence. It needs to get better at knowing what sort of conditions create it and not send airplanes there.

为了改善飞行体验，航空业并不一定非要彻底解决湍流问题。它需要更好地了解什么样的条件会产生湍流，并避免让飞机进入这些区域。

One company in Tel Aviv, SkyPath, uses those in-plane iPads and their accelerometers to collect and share crowdsourced data between different airplanes, even if they don’t fly for the same airline. (This sort of dating sharing can be a sensitive topic for competing companies; SkyPath says it assuages these concerns by ensuring that data is anonymized before it’s shared.)

特拉维夫的一家公司SkyPath利用机上的iPad及其加速度计，在不同飞机之间收集和共享众包数据，即使它们不属于同一家航空公司。（这种数据共享对于竞争公司来说可能是一个敏感话题；SkyPath表示，他们通过确保数据在共享前进行匿名化处理来缓解这些担忧。）

More than 40,000 of these devices have SkyPath installed on them, says CEO Maya Shpak; the company says that several major airlines, including American, use the technology. SkyPath combines that collected data with additional information, including weather forecasts, and uses machine intelligence to make predictions about the future state of the air. The app helps pilots avoid turbulent air and warn passengers and crew members that choppiness is coming, 10 to 15 minutes before it happens.

首席执行官玛雅·什帕克表示，已有超过4万台设备安装了SkyPath；该公司称，包括美国航空在内的几家大型航空公司都在使用这项技术。SkyPath将收集到的数据与包括天气预报在内的其他信息相结合，并利用机器智能对空气的未来状态进行预测。该应用程序可以帮助飞行员避开湍流区域，并在颠簸发生前10到15分钟提醒乘客和机组人员。
