Drone attacks on data centers. Amazon data centers came under military attack for the first time ever in the Middle East this year, and Russia and Ukraine exchanged an unprecedented flurry of drone strikes on each other’s data centers. To examine what such attacks on data centers mean for the future of cloud services that all modern societies depend upon, Ars hosted a live discussion.

针对数据中心的无人机袭击。今年，亚马逊的数据中心在中东首次遭到军事攻击，俄罗斯和乌克兰之间也针对彼此的数据中心进行了前所未有的无人机袭击。为了探讨此类针对数据中心的攻击对现代社会所依赖的云服务未来意味着什么，Ars 举办了一场直播讨论。

In a conversation with Ars Technica Tech Reporter Jeremy Hsu, Joseph Jarnecki at the Royal United Services Institute first talked about why public cloud data centers have increasingly become military targets during the 2026 Iran war and the ongoing full-scale Russian invasion of Ukraine: We see an increasing acknowledgement and fact of dual use in particularly the public cloud, but also just across the board in private cloud data centers as well. The dual use that I refer to is the utilization of compute functions within these data centers—within cloud services—by militaries, for example, to run battlefield management systems, to do their HR, to do their resource management. Once a military uses a piece of civilian infrastructure, at least under international law or in terms of the principles of targeting that are taken forward by militaries, it can sometimes become a military object and is therefore of interest to an adversary, and there’s an incentive to target it.

在与 Ars Technica 科技记者 Jeremy Hsu 的对话中，英国皇家联合军种国防研究所（RUSI）的 Joseph Jarnecki 首先谈到了为什么在 2026 年伊朗战争和正在进行的俄罗斯全面入侵乌克兰期间，公共云数据中心日益成为军事目标：“我们看到，人们越来越认可并意识到‘军民两用’的事实，这尤其体现在公共云中，但也普遍存在于私有云数据中心。我所说的军民两用，是指军队利用这些数据中心（即云服务）内的计算功能，例如运行战场管理系统、处理人力资源或进行资源管理。一旦军队使用了某项民用基础设施，至少根据国际法或军队所遵循的打击原则，它有时会成为军事目标，从而引起对手的关注，并产生攻击它的动机。”

Jarnecki also highlighted the fact that relatively cheap kamikaze drones can inflict disproportionate economic costs and create widespread cloud disruptions when striking data centers: The second reason that we’ve seen targeting particularly in Russia’s largest data centers or some of Russia’s largest public cloud data centers like Yandex, as well as American hyperscalers that are based in the Gulf, is to impose economic cost in a way that would be disadvantageous for an adversary and would otherwise be quite difficult to impose in other ways. So it’s relatively low cost of munition to high cost of disruption. If you think about the amount of investment that’s been put forward by AWS and other companies in the Gulf to build out these data center installations relative to the cost of the amount of Shahed drones that are manufactured, produced, and utilized by Iran to target these data centers, I would put it very, very conservatively at 100X or even more. The cost ratio is really very lucrative for the adversary, and it provides the opportunity to really degrade the economic outcomes of who you are targeting.

Jarnecki 还强调，相对廉价的自杀式无人机在袭击数据中心时，可以造成不成比例的经济损失并引发广泛的云服务中断：“我们看到针对俄罗斯最大数据中心或 Yandex 等俄罗斯大型公共云数据中心，以及位于海湾地区的美国超大规模云服务商的袭击，第二个原因是试图施加经济成本，这种方式对对手不利，且在其他方面很难实现。因此，这是以相对较低的弹药成本换取高昂的破坏成本。如果你考虑到 AWS 和其他公司在海湾地区为建设这些数据中心设施所投入的资金，与伊朗制造、生产并用于攻击这些数据中心的‘见证者’（Shahed）无人机的成本相比，我保守估计至少是 100 倍甚至更多。这种成本比率对对手来说非常划算，并提供了真正削弱目标经济成果的机会。”

Much of the discussion also focused on cloud resilience lessons for both governments and hyperscalers, with the latter including large cloud and data center operators such as Amazon, Google, Microsoft, and Meta. Jarnecki highlighted the example of how Ukraine took steps to safeguard its cloud infrastructure when Russian President Vladimir Putin ordered Russian military forces to launch the full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022: I think that Ukraine anticipated this problem set very effectively. They anticipated and understood that Russia would not be discriminating necessarily in its targeting of civilian digital infrastructure particularly, and therefore conducted a large-scale cloud migration at the onset of the full-scale war in part in reaction to the anticipated targeting but then also actual targeting that took place through kinetic means.

讨论的很大一部分还集中在政府和超大规模云服务商（包括亚马逊、谷歌、微软和 Meta 等大型云和数据中心运营商）的云韧性经验教训上。Jarnecki 重点介绍了乌克兰在 2022 年 2 月俄罗斯总统普京下令对乌克兰发动全面入侵时，如何采取措施保护其云基础设施的例子：“我认为乌克兰非常有效地预判了这一问题。他们预见到并理解俄罗斯在针对民用数字基础设施的打击中未必会有所区分，因此在全面战争爆发之初就进行了大规模的云迁移，这既是对预期打击的反应，也是对随后发生的动能打击的应对。”

Such lessons are important because 2026 is unlikely to be the last year in which data centers come under substantial military attack, Jarnecki said: Unfortunately, I think that this really does indicate a continuation of this kind of targeting, not just because data centers are, despite efforts to harden some of them, relatively squishy, relatively easy to find, and have the potential, because of their complexity, to induce cascading risks within the facility or more widely if they are hit particularly by kinetic munitions. So at present, I would say it’s a dangerous trend that is likely to crop up again.

Jarnecki 表示，这些教训非常重要，因为 2026 年不太可能是数据中心遭受重大军事攻击的最后一年：“不幸的是，我认为这确实预示着此类打击将持续下去。这不仅是因为数据中心——尽管人们努力对其进行加固——仍然相对脆弱、容易被发现，而且由于其复杂性，一旦受到动能弹药的打击，极有可能在设施内部或更广泛的范围内引发连锁风险。因此，目前我认为这是一个很可能会再次出现的危险趋势。”

Throughout the event, Jarnecki also addressed viewer questions about whether data centers can realistically be hardened or built underground to withstand physical attacks, and even how orbital data centers or low-Earth orbit satellite constellations such as Starlink could provide backup options from space.

在整个活动中，Jarnecki 还回答了观众关于数据中心是否能够切实加固或建在地下以抵御物理攻击的问题，甚至探讨了轨道数据中心或像“星链”（Starlink）这样的低地球轨道卫星星座如何能提供来自太空的备份方案。
