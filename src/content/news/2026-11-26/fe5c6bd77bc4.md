# The Iowa Lead Detonation Happened. Now the Real Work Begins to Study It

On Wednesday morning, the US Army detonated nearly five tons of degraded lead azide outside of Middletown, Iowa. Environmental Protection Administration chief Lee Zeldin told CNN shortly after the explosion that preliminary monitoring from the site detected no lead in the air around the site.

周三上午，美国陆军在爱荷华州米德尔敦郊外引爆了近五吨退化的叠氮化铅。美国国家环境保护局局长李·泽尔丁在爆炸后不久告诉美国有线电视新闻网（CNN），现场的初步监测显示，该地点周围的空气中未检测到铅。

But the full extent of the fallout may not be certain for at least a few days. A barrage of government, university, and citizen monitoring and testing projects are underway. Air, soil, and water samples they’ve collected will determine the extent of lead exposure, if any, in Middletown and the surrounding area.

但放射性尘埃的全部影响可能至少需要几天时间才能确定。目前，政府、大学和民间机构正在开展一系列监测和测试项目。他们收集的空气、土壤和水样本将确定米德尔敦及周边地区是否存在铅暴露及其程度。

The lead azide has been on the base near Middletown for decades, but recently the Army deemed it unstable and too dangerous to move and remediate. Poor communication between the Army and towns in the path of the fallout led to a public uproar. While the federal government stepped up its mitigation measures—the Army dug a dam around the detonation site, while NASA stepped in with research jets and satellites for monitoring—universities had already been swinging into action due to the chaotic rollout.

这些叠氮化铅在米德尔敦附近的基地存放了数十年，但陆军最近认为其性质不稳定，移动和处理过于危险。陆军与受影响城镇之间沟通不畅引发了公众的强烈抗议。虽然联邦政府加强了缓解措施——陆军在引爆点周围挖掘了堤坝，美国国家航空航天局（NASA）也介入并使用研究喷气机和卫星进行监测——但由于前期工作混乱，各大学早已采取了行动。

Several departments at the University of Iowa kicked into gear to begin to set up air and soil monitoring stations late last week. “Initially we did not know that [the] EPA or Army or anybody else was going to be doing anything,” says Peter Thorne, a professor of environmental health at the university.

爱荷华大学的多个部门在上周晚些时候迅速行动起来，开始建立空气和土壤监测站。该校环境健康学教授彼得·索恩表示：“起初我们并不知道环保局、陆军或其他任何人会采取任何行动。”

Scientists at Purdue University, nearly 300 miles away, also put out a call for soil samples from Middletown. “We learned about the detonation late last week; once we learned of the initial delay in detonation date we decided to put forth the call,” says Ellen Wells, a professor of public health at Purdue who is helping lead the testing.

近300英里外的普渡大学的科学家们也发出了收集米德尔敦土壤样本的呼吁。正在协助领导这项测试的普渡大学公共卫生教授艾伦·威尔斯说：“我们上周晚些时候才得知引爆的消息；一旦得知引爆日期最初有所推迟，我们就决定发出呼吁。”

Surrounding towns were notified in late October that the Army wanted to conduct pre- and post-detonation soil sampling for “an intentional, controlled detonation” of a “quantity of lead azide”—seemingly the only notification that many towns in the initial modeled path of the fallout say they received before a press release issued by the Army days before the planned explosion.

周边城镇在10月下旬收到通知，称陆军希望对“一定数量的叠氮化铅”进行“有意的、受控的引爆”，并进行引爆前后的土壤采样。据称，在计划爆炸前几天陆军发布新闻稿之前，许多处于初步模拟尘埃路径上的城镇收到的似乎只有这一份通知。

The lack of communication has, understandably, instilled a lot of public distrust in the federal government, including agencies’ monitoring programs. Thorne says that efforts like the University of Iowa’s are not meant to displace federal monitoring, but rather to supplement and support it—and hopefully reassure the public.

沟通的缺失理所当然地导致公众对联邦政府产生了极大的不信任，包括对各机构的监测项目。索恩表示，像爱荷华大学这样的努力并非旨在取代联邦监测，而是为了补充和支持它，并希望能让公众放心。

“We and the EPA are doing similar exposure assessment efforts. If they agree, they can give us some confidence,” he says. “For people who might be concerned about the veracity of what the government tells them, if their findings are in alignment with ours, then I think that helps quell some of those concerns.”

“我们和环保局正在进行类似的暴露评估工作。如果他们的结果一致，就能给我们带来一些信心，”他说。“对于那些可能担心政府所言真实性的人来说，如果他们的发现与我们的相符，那么我认为这有助于平息一些担忧。”

Thorne says that the soil samples the University of Iowa scientists had measured before the detonation had low levels of lead. “If I saw something like a 10 fold increase in the amount of lead in the soil 20 miles downwind, I’d be concerned with that,” he says.

索恩表示，爱荷华大学科学家在引爆前测量的土壤样本中铅含量很低。“如果我看到下风向20英里处的土壤中铅含量增加了10倍，我会感到担忧，”他说。

Some soil testing can be done instantaneously with devices that can image the soil’s surface, using a gun device that fires X-ray beams into a sample and measures the specific electromagnetic signature of lead atoms that bounce back. While Wells and her team don’t have those instruments in the field, they are planning on using them to analyze the samples they receive. More sensitive lab equipment can provide even more granular data, though Thorne estimates those results could take about a week.

一些土壤测试可以通过能够对土壤表面成像的设备即时完成，这种设备使用一种枪式装置，向样本发射X射线束，并测量反弹回来的铅原子的特定电磁特征。虽然威尔斯和她的团队在现场没有这些仪器，但他们计划用它们来分析收到的样本。更灵敏的实验室设备可以提供更精细的数据，尽管索恩估计这些结果可能需要大约一周的时间。

In addition to monitoring being done by the Army and the EPA, which is maintaining a live website of air quality results, University of Iowa scientists are monitoring the plume to figure out where the highest concentration of lead may settle. They’re also collecting data on particulate matter in the air around the detonation site for analysis, sending air filters to the lab for more detailed results.

除了陆军和环保局（该局正在维护一个空气质量结果的实时网站）进行的监测外，爱荷华大学的科学家们正在监测烟羽，以确定铅浓度最高的地方可能在哪里沉降。他们还在收集引爆点周围空气中颗粒物的数据以进行分析，并将空气过滤器送往实验室以获取更详细的结果。

“As these data are compiled over the holiday week to the early next week, I think we'll start to have a bigger picture of roughly how much loading we have of the soil at various places downwind and what we can expect from that,” says Thorne.

“随着这些数据在假期周到下周初汇总，我认为我们将开始对下风向各地土壤的铅负荷量以及我们可以预期的结果有一个更全面的了解，”索恩说。

A lot of concern online has centered around how additional lead in the soil may impact crops in Iowa, one of the central agricultural hubs for soybeans and corn. Jeffrey Catalano, a professor of earth and environmental sciences at Washington University in Saint Louis, says that there’s a large amount of research showing that plants like corn are actually pretty bad at absorbing lead.

网络上的许多担忧集中在土壤中额外的铅如何影响爱荷华州的农作物，该州是大豆和玉米的主要农业中心之一。圣路易斯华盛顿大学地球与环境科学教授杰弗里·卡塔拉诺表示，大量研究表明，像玉米这样的植物实际上吸收铅的能力很差。

“The hazard is more like, if soil with lead in it gets stuck to leaves, and you eat leaves that have lead-rich soil on them,” he says. “If you take some corn crops next year versus this year and measure their lead content, I don’t think you would see anything other than random variations. I’m not worried about food produced in Iowa.”

“危险更像是，如果含有铅的土壤粘在叶子上，而你吃了这些带有富含铅土壤的叶子，”他说。“如果你拿明年的玉米作物和今年的进行对比，测量它们的铅含量，我认为除了随机变化外，你不会看到任何其他东西。我不担心爱荷华州生产的食物。”

Municipal drinking water is also unlikely to be affected because most treatment plants are designed to remove lead from water supplies, says Patrick McNamara, a professor of environmental engineering at Marquette University. It’s more worrisome for private wells or streams on people’s property where livestock may drink. McNamara suggests that owners use at-home lead water tests for safety.

马凯特大学环境工程教授帕特里克·麦克纳马拉表示，市政饮用水也不太可能受到影响，因为大多数处理厂的设计目的就是从供水中去除铅。更令人担忧的是私人水井或人们财产上的溪流，牲畜可能会在那里饮水。麦克纳马拉建议业主使用家用铅水测试来确保安全。

While the detonation and haphazard announcements leading up to it raised alarm bells, both Thorne and Catalano say that there are communities dealing with legacy pollution around the country that are exposed to dangerous levels of lead every day that deserve attention—and remediation.

虽然引爆及其前期的混乱公告敲响了警钟，但索恩和卡塔拉诺都表示，全国各地都有社区正在处理遗留污染问题，他们每天都暴露在危险水平的铅环境中，这些问题值得关注和补救。

“I've done work in places like East Chicago, Indiana, where they built a housing project and a school on the grounds of a former lead smelting plant—we were seeing soil lead levels that are roughly 10,000-fold higher than what we've measured in Iowa this week,” Thorne says. “There are a lot of places around the country where there's a much greater lead hazard than I'm going to anticipate what we’ll end [up finding in Iowa].”

“我曾在印第安纳州东芝加哥等地工作，他们在一家前铅冶炼厂的场地上建造了一个住房项目和一所学校——我们看到的土壤铅含量大约是我们本周在爱荷华州测量结果的10,000倍，”索恩说。“全国有很多地方的铅危害远比我预期的（我们在爱荷华州最终发现的）要大得多。”
