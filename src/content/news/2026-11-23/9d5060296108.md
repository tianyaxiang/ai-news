One-off projects are the feast-or-famine machine. You land a great project, invoice it, celebrate — and then you are back at zero, hunting for the next one. The proposal treadmill never stops, and every slow month feels like starting over. There is a quieter alternative that more experienced freelancers are choosing in 2026: turn one good client into a monthly retainer. Not a vague "let's work together more" — a fixed monthly fee for a defined set of ongoing work. Here is how to do it, step by step.

一次性项目是“饱一顿饥一顿”的根源。你接了一个大项目，开具发票，庆祝一番——然后又回到了零点，继续寻找下一个目标。提案的跑步机永不停歇，每个业务淡季都感觉像是从头开始。2026年，经验丰富的自由职业者们正在选择一种更从容的替代方案：将一个优质客户转化为月度留存合同（Retainer）。这不仅仅是模糊的“让我们多合作”，而是针对一系列持续性工作设定的固定月费。以下是具体的操作步骤。

Why retainers are winning in 2026 Three trends point the same way: Retainers are replacing one-off gigs. Fractional roles and monthly retainers ($3,000–$15,000/month for 10–20 hours a week) are now a normal way experienced freelancers work, and clients increasingly prefer them because they get continuity instead of re-onboarding someone new every quarter. Platforms keep getting more expensive to depend on. Upwork takes 10% of your earnings; Fiverr takes 20%. Direct clients outside the platforms routinely pay 30–50% more for the same work — and a retainer is the simplest form of a direct relationship.

为什么2026年留存合同大受欢迎？三个趋势指向同一方向：留存合同正在取代一次性零工。分时职位和月度留存合同（每周10-20小时，月薪3,000-15,000美元）已成为资深自由职业者的常态。客户也越来越倾向于这种模式，因为他们能获得持续的服务，而不必每季度重新培训新人。依赖平台的成本越来越高。Upwork抽取你收入的10%，Fiverr抽取20%。平台之外的直接客户通常愿意为同样的工作多支付30-50%的费用，而留存合同正是建立直接合作关系最简单的方式。

Rate compression is real for one-off work. The median creator campaign payout dropped 14% in 2026 even as total creator-economy spending grew 26%. Single posts and single projects are getting cheaper. Packaged, ongoing value is not. A retainer does not fix everything. But one retainer that covers your rent changes the whole shape of your month: the floor rises, and every project on top becomes optional instead of desperate.

一次性工作的费率压缩是真实存在的。尽管创作者经济的总支出增长了26%，但2026年创作者活动的平均报酬却下降了14%。单篇帖子和单个项目的价格正在走低，但打包的持续价值却并非如此。留存合同不能解决所有问题，但一份能覆盖房租的留存合同会改变你整个月的状态：你的收入底线提高了，在此之上的每一个项目都变成了“可选”，而不是“救命稻草”。

Step 1: Pick the right client (you already have one) Do not pitch retainers to strangers first. Look at your last 12 months and find the client who: Has hired you at least three times, or for one project that kept growing. Has a recurring need — content every week, design every month, maintenance, reporting, support. Pays on time and respects your boundaries. That client already trusts you. A retainer is just a formal version of what you are already doing for them, with a predictable price instead of a new negotiation every time. If no client fits yet, that is useful information too: it means your next project should be chosen for repeat potential, not just for the fee.

第一步：选择合适的客户（你已经有了）。不要先向陌生人推销留存合同。回顾过去12个月，找到符合以下条件的客户：至少雇佣过你三次，或者有一个不断扩展的项目；有持续的需求——每周的内容、每月的排版、维护、报告或支持；按时付款并尊重你的底线。该客户已经信任你。留存合同只是将你们现有的合作形式化，用可预测的价格取代每次重新谈判。如果目前还没有合适的客户，这也是有用的信息：这意味着你下一个项目的选择标准应侧重于重复合作的潜力，而不仅仅是报酬。

Step 2: Package ongoing value, not hours The pitch that fails sounds like this: "Pay me $2,000 a month for 20 hours." The pitch that works sounds like this: "For $2,000 a month, I will keep your blog publishing twice a week, your newsletter going out on the 1st and 15th, and your analytics reviewed every month — so you never have to think about it." Hours are a cost. Outcomes are a purchase. Name the deliverables, the cadence, and the result. A good retainer package has three parts: What gets delivered — the recurring outputs, with a number attached (2 articles, 4 social posts, 1 report). How often — weekly or monthly, on fixed dates. What is included, and what is not — revisions, turnaround time, and what counts as extra work billed separately. Write it down in one page. If you cannot describe the package in one page, it is not a package yet.

第二步：打包持续价值，而非工时。失败的推销是这样的：“每月付我2000美元，我为你工作20小时。”成功的推销是这样的：“每月2000美元，我负责你博客每周两次的发布、每月1号和15号的通讯发送，以及每月的分析报告——让你完全无需操心。”工时是成本，成果才是商品。明确交付物、频率和结果。一个好的留存合同包包含三部分：交付内容（带有具体数量的持续产出，如2篇文章、4篇社交媒体帖子、1份报告）；频率（每周或每月，固定日期）；包含与不包含的内容（修改次数、周转时间，以及哪些属于额外收费的工作）。把它写在一页纸上。如果你无法用一页纸描述清楚，那它还算不上一个成熟的方案。

Step 3: Price it so the floor is real A retainer should be priced from your floor rate, not from a discount. Work it out like this: Your monthly minimum: the amount you must earn to cover expenses, tax set-aside, and a small buffer. (If you have never calculated this, do it first — guessing is how retainers quietly lose money.) The hours the package actually takes, including calls, revisions, and admin. Most freelancers underestimate this by 30–50%. Fee = hours × your rate, rounded to a clean monthly number. Then add one rule that protects you: a scope cap. "Up to X deliverables per month; additional work at $Y per item." Without a cap, a retainer slowly becomes unlimited work for a fixed fee — the exact trap that makes freelancers quit retainers. A simple starting point: price the retainer at 80–90% of what the same work would cost as separate projects. The client gets a small discount for commitment; you get predictable income. Both sides win, and neither side is being generous.

第三步：定价要确保底线真实。留存合同的定价应基于你的底线费率，而不是打折。计算方法如下：你的月度最低收入（覆盖开支、预留税款和小额缓冲的金额；如果从未计算过，请先做这件事，盲目猜测是留存合同亏钱的原因）；该方案实际耗费的工时（包括通话、修改和行政工作，大多数自由职业者会低估30-50%）。费用 = 工时 × 费率，取整为一个整洁的月度数字。然后增加一条保护你的规则：范围上限。“每月最多交付X项；额外工作按每项Y美元收费。”没有上限，留存合同会慢慢变成固定费用下的无限工作——这正是导致自由职业者放弃留存合同的陷阱。一个简单的起点：将留存合同定价为同样工作作为独立项目收费的80-90%。客户因承诺而获得小额折扣，你获得可预测的收入。双方共赢，且谁也不吃亏。

Step 4: The pitch (copy, adapt, send) You do not need a sales call to start. A short email to an existing client is enough: Subject: A simpler way to work together in 2027 Hi [Name] — we've done [X projects] together this year, and the work keeps coming back every [week/month]. Instead of a new quote each time, I'd like to propose a monthly arrangement: [package in one sentence], for $[fee]/month, starting [date]. It includes [deliverables], and anything beyond that is billed separately at $[rate]. We can review it after 3 months and adjust. Would you like to try it for one quarter? Three months is the right trial length. One month is too short to prove value; twelve months is too long to commit to an untested arrangement. A quarterly review date — written into the agreement — is what makes both sides comfortable saying yes.

第四步：推销（复制、调整、发送）。开始时不需要销售电话，给现有客户发一封简短的邮件就足够了：主题：2027年更简单的合作方式。你好[姓名]——今年我们一起完成了[X个项目]，工作需求也一直在[每周/每月]出现。与其每次都重新报价，我想提议一种月度合作安排：[用一句话描述方案]，每月[费用]美元，从[日期]开始。它包含[交付物]，超出部分按[费率]单独计费。我们可以在3个月后进行评估并调整。你想尝试一个季度吗？三个月是合适的试用期。一个月太短无法证明价值，十二个月对于未经测试的安排来说太长。写进协议的季度评估日期，是让双方都能安心点头的关键。

Step 5: Track the three numbers that keep a retainer profitable A retainer only works if you watch it. Once a month, check three numbers: Effective hourly rate — monthly fee ÷ hours actually spent. If it drops below your floor rate for two months in a row, the scope has crept and the fee needs renegotiating. Utilisation — hours used vs. hours included. Consistently over 100% means you are giving work away; consistently under 60% means the package is too big and the client may cancel. Renewal date — when the retainer comes up for review. Never let one renew by inertia. A review is your chance to raise the fee, trim the scope, or walk away cleanly. Put these three numbers in a spreadsheet and check them on the same day every month — the 1st, or the day you invoice. Fifteen minutes, once a month, is the difference between a retainer that pays your rent and one that quietly eats your weekends. Start with one You do not need five retainers. One is enough to change the math: it covers the floor, it ends the zero-income month, and it gives you the calm to choose your next project.

第五步：追踪三个数字，保持留存合同的盈利性。留存合同只有在被监控时才有效。每月检查三个数字：有效时薪（月费 ÷ 实际投入工时）。如果连续两个月低于你的底线费率，说明工作范围扩大了，需要重新谈判费用。利用率（已用工时 vs. 包含工时）。持续超过100%意味着你在免费赠送劳动；持续低于60%意味着方案太大，客户可能会取消。续约日期（留存合同评估的时间）。永远不要让合同因惯性自动续期。评估是你涨价、缩减范围或体面退出的机会。将这三个数字放入电子表格，每月同一天（如1号或开票日）检查。每月15分钟的投入，决定了留存合同是帮你付房租，还是悄悄吞噬你的周末。从一个开始吧，你不需要五个留存合同。一个就足以改变你的财务状况：它覆盖了底线，终结了零收入的月份，并让你有底气从容选择下一个项目。
