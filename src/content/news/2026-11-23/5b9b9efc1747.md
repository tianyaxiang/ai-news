From value creation to value extraction

从价值创造到价值提取

By Tim O’Reilly  November 20, 2026 • 32 minute read

作者：Tim O’Reilly，2026 年 11 月 20 日 • 阅读需 32 分钟

On October 16, 2026, I delivered the second Annual Margaret Levi Lecture in Political Economy in conjunction with the AI and History conference at Johns Hopkins University in Baltimore. This is my original lecture as written, which incorporates material from many pieces I’ve written over the years. As delivered, I departed significantly from the script, as you can see from the embedded video below or the transcript that you can see at the link above.

2026 年 10 月 16 日，我在巴尔的摩约翰霍普金斯大学举办的“人工智能与历史”会议期间，发表了第二届年度 Margaret Levi 政治经济学讲座。这是我撰写的讲座原文，其中整合了我多年来所写文章中的素材。正如您在下方嵌入的视频或上方链接的文字记录中所见，我在实际演讲时与讲稿内容有显著出入。

It’s an honor to give a lecture in Margaret Levi’s name. I’ve been asked to reflect on the changes in the political economy of Silicon Valley throughout my 40-plus year career in and about it, and I can’t imagine a better framing for my remarks than Margaret’s vision.

能以 Margaret Levi 的名义发表演讲是我的荣幸。有人请我回顾在我 40 多年的职业生涯中，硅谷政治经济所发生的变化，而我无法想象比 Margaret 的愿景更适合作为我演讲框架的内容了。

Margaret understood that advances in our ability to work together are essential to a better future. Cooperation is the human superpower. She also asks what happens when the systems of cooperation we have constructed over time stop holding up their end of the bargain.

Margaret 理解，我们协作能力的进步对于更美好的未来至关重要。合作是人类的超能力。她也提出了一个问题：当我们长期构建的合作体系不再履行其应尽的职责时，会发生什么？

That is very much the question that Silicon Valley is facing today. An industry that pioneered advances in collective intelligence at scale used to engender love from its customers because of the way it solved new problems in their lives. It now too often inspires fear and loathing. How did things go so wrong?

这正是硅谷今天所面临的问题。一个曾经在规模化集体智能领域开创先河的行业，过去因其解决人们生活中新问题的方式而深受客户喜爱。而如今，它却常常引发恐惧和厌恶。事情怎么会变得如此糟糕？

But let me start with my own history. I started my company more than 40 years ago with a $500 initial investment. We took in no venture capital, and we built a business that now generates hundreds of millions of dollars in profitable annual revenue, funded by customers who love our products and pay us for them. We got there the slow way, through steady organic growth. That is quite anathema to the rush to the top of whatever appears to be the latest hill that seems to characterize Silicon Valley today. Even when climbing that hill traps a company at a local maximum, the hope is to get to payday before the industry moves on to the next hill.

但还是让我从我自己的经历说起吧。40 多年前，我以 500 美元的初始投资创办了我的公司。我们没有接受任何风险投资，却建立起了一项如今每年能产生数亿美元盈利的企业，其资金来源是那些喜爱并愿意为我们的产品付费的客户。我们通过缓慢、稳健的有机增长达到了这一目标。这与当今硅谷那种急于攀登任何看似最新高峰的潮流截然相反。即使攀登那座山峰会将公司困在局部最优解中，人们也希望在行业转向下一座山峰之前先拿到报酬。

My original corporate goal was stated as simply “Interesting work for interesting people.” That evolved into two principles: “Change the world by spreading the knowledge of innovators,” and “Create more value than you capture.” Those principles shaped not only my company but what I expected the internet to be. When we launched GNN, the Global Network Navigator, in 1993, it was the first web portal and the first ad-supported site on the World Wide Web, and for two years it was the place people went to find other sites. What we built was a machine for pointing you at the best of what other people had made, and then getting out of your way.

我最初的公司目标简单地表述为“为有趣的人做有趣的工作”。后来演变为两条原则：“通过传播创新者的知识来改变世界”和“创造的价值要大于你获取的价值”。这些原则不仅塑造了我的公司，也塑造了我对互联网的期望。1993 年，当我们推出全球网络导航器（GNN）时，它是万维网上的第一个门户网站，也是第一个由广告支持的网站。在两年时间里，它是人们寻找其他网站的首选之地。我们构建的是一台引导您发现他人杰作的机器，然后便不再干预您的探索。

Even our idea of advertising was not what you think of today. It was inspired by direct mail advertising, where you’d read articles in a print magazine or look at accompanying ads, and then you’d call an 800 number or circle a number on a “bingo card” that you’d send in to the publisher so that the advertiser could send you a brochure. I realized that the web would be a good replacement for that awkward system, that every company ought to have its own website that provided more information about its products and services. What we invented was not the “grab your attention” kind of advertising. It was all about utility, and sharing information that customers really wanted and needed. In short, it was the commercial web, which no one any longer thinks of as advertising, even though it is the most durable and useful kind.

即使是我们对广告的构想也与您今天所想的不同。它的灵感来源于直邮广告：您阅读印刷杂志上的文章或查看随附的广告，然后拨打 800 电话，或者在“宾果卡”上圈出一个号码寄给出版商，以便广告商给您寄送宣传册。我意识到网络将是取代那种笨拙系统的绝佳方案，每家公司都应该拥有自己的网站，以提供更多关于其产品和服务的信息。我们发明的不是那种“吸引您注意力”的广告，而是关于实用性，以及分享客户真正想要和需要的信息。简而言之，这就是商业网络，尽管它是最持久、最有用的广告形式，但现在已没人将其视为广告了。

The commercial web was only a small part of what the web became. The web was the playground of amateurs, not entrepreneurs. It was a marvelous collective work of human imagination, creativity, sharing, and ultimately (though that became toxic only later), competition for attention. Our hand-curated catalog of the best websites was soon surpassed by algorithmic solutions like those provided by Google. The amount of content produced was far larger than purely human curation could manage. It was an early example of what Richard Sutton was later to call “the bitter lesson” in his analysis of AI training.

商业网络只是后来网络发展的一小部分。网络曾是业余爱好者的游乐场，而非企业家的领地。它是人类想象力、创造力、分享精神的伟大集体结晶，最终（尽管后来变得有些病态）演变成了对注意力的争夺。我们人工精选的最佳网站目录很快就被谷歌等提供的算法解决方案所超越。产生的内容数量远远超过了纯人工策展所能管理的范畴。这是 Richard Sutton 在其人工智能训练分析中后来称之为“苦涩的教训”的早期案例。

In my 2005 essay “What Is Web 2.0?” I described how the companies that survived the dotcom bust had all, in one way or another, figured out how to help people navigate the resulting information explosion at scale. As Herbert Simon noted in 1971, an abundance of information leads to a scarcity of attention. In the face of increasing information abundance, he predicted that we would use machines to help us better allocate our time and attention. And so it has transpired: Information has become so abundant that it defies manual curation. Instead, powerful, proprietary algorithmic systems use the data they collect to match users with the answers, news, entertainment, products, applications, and services they seek. Today’s AI is in many ways a continuation of this arc.

在 2005 年的文章《什么是 Web 2.0？》中，我描述了那些在互联网泡沫破裂中幸存下来的公司是如何以各种方式找到方法，帮助人们大规模地应对随之而来的信息爆炸。正如 Herbert Simon 在 1971 年指出的那样，信息的丰富导致了注意力的稀缺。面对日益增长的信息过剩，他预测我们将利用机器来帮助我们更好地分配时间和注意力。事实确实如此：信息变得如此丰富，以至于无法进行人工策展。取而代之的是，强大的专有算法系统利用它们收集的数据，将用户与他们寻求的答案、新闻、娱乐、产品、应用程序和服务进行匹配。当今的人工智能在许多方面都是这一发展轨迹的延续。

I called this “harnessing collective intelligence.” All of it was, in economic terms, a set of remarkable advances in networked, data-enabled market coordination. It felt like a public good even when it was privately owned.

我称之为“驾驭集体智能”。从经济学角度来看，这一切都是网络化、数据驱动的市场协调方面的一系列显著进步。即使在私有制下，它也感觉像是一种公共产品。

Hal Varian, formerly Google’s chief economist, once told me that only about 6% of Google search result pages carry any advertising or are otherwise monetized by Google. The other 94% were served up by search in a glorious demonstration of abundance, the surplus of value created over value captured. And while many of the pages themselves were commercial, even more were the product of the joyful exuberance of humanity, people creating and sharing for the joy of it. Google’s algorithm matched that creation to the people who wanted it, with no price involved.

前谷歌首席经济学家 Hal Varian 曾告诉我，只有约 6% 的谷歌搜索结果页面带有广告或以其他方式被谷歌货币化。其余 94% 的页面由搜索呈现，这是一种丰饶的壮丽展示，体现了创造的价值超过了获取的价值。虽然许多页面本身是商业性的，但更多页面是人类快乐激情的产物，是人们为了乐趣而创造和分享的成果。谷歌的算法将这些创作与需要它们的人进行了匹配，且不涉及任何费用。
