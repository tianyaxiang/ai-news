# How This Year’s Super El Niño Will Worsen Global Hunger

The world is now in the grip of a super El Niño: a Godzilla-sized version of a natural climate phenomenon that has already shriveled rivers, upended aquaculture, and flattened crops with drought. But the global organizations that keep an eye on food security are warning of even tougher times ahead.

目前，全球正处于一场“超级厄尔尼诺”现象的笼罩之下：这是一种规模堪比“哥斯拉”的自然气候现象，它已经导致河流干涸、水产养殖业陷入混乱，并因干旱摧毁了农作物。然而，负责监测全球粮食安全的组织警告称，未来还将面临更加严峻的挑战。

The impacts of an El Niño can be far-reaching and catastrophic. In the late 19th century, one of the strongest El Niños on record helped to fuel multiyear droughts across Asia, Brazil, and Africa that spurred a global famine: Some 50 million people, or 3 percent of the global population, died. Thankfully, the ways we plan for bad weather and move food around the planet have improved massively since then, with organizations working hard to provide relief where it’s needed. But the impacts of bad weather on crops are still hard to avoid. After a strong El Niño hit in 2015, cereal yields across Southern Africa were 20 percent below normal; crop yields in parts of Ethiopia triggered by 90 percent.

厄尔尼诺现象的影响可能是深远且灾难性的。19世纪末，有记录以来最强劲的厄尔尼诺现象之一加剧了亚洲、巴西和非洲各地的多年干旱，引发了一场全球性饥荒：约5000万人（占当时全球人口的3%）因此丧生。值得庆幸的是，自那时以来，我们在应对恶劣天气和全球粮食调配方面的能力已大幅提升，相关组织也在努力向受灾地区提供援助。但恶劣天气对农作物的影响依然难以避免。2015年强厄尔尼诺现象发生后，南部非洲的谷物产量比正常水平低了20%；埃塞俄比亚部分地区的农作物产量则下降了90%。

This year’s El Niño has already, by one measure, topped those previous record holders in strength. And it’s still growing. By the time the phenomenon peaks in early winter, sea-surface warming in the tropical Pacific could be higher than anything seen in a millennium. In August, the World Food Programme warned that this El Niño might push an additional 49 million people into acute food insecurity by the end of 2027, taking the total number to 274 million. Worryingly, this year’s event comes as global aid dollars are pinched and wars are making it hard to plug holes in global food systems.

从某种衡量标准来看，今年的厄尔尼诺现象在强度上已经超过了以往的纪录保持者，而且它仍在持续增强。当这一现象在初冬达到顶峰时，热带太平洋的海面升温幅度可能会达到千年来的最高水平。今年8月，世界粮食计划署警告称，到2027年底，这场厄尔尼诺现象可能会导致额外4900万人陷入严重的粮食不安全境地，使总人数达到2.74亿。令人担忧的是，今年的事件发生时，全球援助资金正处于紧缩状态，而战争也使得填补全球粮食系统漏洞变得愈发困难。

“It’s a triple whammy of high fertilizer costs, high energy costs, and now massive droughts across the tropics,” says Berkeley Earth climatologist Zeke Hausfather, who is watching the situation unfold. “It’s not a great story.”

“这是化肥成本高昂、能源成本高昂，以及现在热带地区大规模干旱带来的三重打击，”正在密切关注事态发展的伯克利地球（Berkeley Earth）气候学家泽克·豪斯法瑟（Zeke Hausfather）表示，“这绝非好消息。”

El Niño is one phase of a natural climate pattern called the El Niño–Southern Oscillation, which has a dramatic impact on global rainfall patterns. El Niño years are marked by unusually warm waters in the tropical Pacific, which weaken winds across that patch and alter global patterns of rising and falling air pressure. The results are complicated and variable, but they tend to bring dry conditions to some tropical areas—including the Amazon and parts of Australia and Africa—and rain to other places, including Florida. The other side of the oscillation, La Niña, is marked by cooler ocean temperatures and a different pattern of wet and dry. The planet swings between the two in irregular intervals of years, sometimes with neutral periods in between.

厄尔尼诺是被称为“厄尔尼诺-南方涛动”（ENSO）这一自然气候模式的一个阶段，它对全球降雨模式有着巨大的影响。厄尔尼诺年份的特征是热带太平洋海域出现异常温暖的海水，这会削弱该区域的风力，并改变全球气压升降的模式。其结果复杂且多变，但往往会给包括亚马逊地区以及澳大利亚和非洲部分地区在内的一些热带区域带来干旱，同时给包括佛罗里达州在内的其他地区带来降雨。该涛动的另一面是拉尼娜现象，其特征是海洋温度较低，并呈现出不同的干湿模式。地球在两者之间以不规则的年份间隔进行摆动，有时中间还会出现中性期。

Both sides of this cycle can harm crops, though one prominent 2014 study found that El Niño affects a larger swath of the globe’s harvested land. While some places get more water, and some crops thrive, in many spots drought withers production. That same study found that an El Niño tends to improve global soybean production by up to 5 percent but suppresses maize, rice, and wheat by up to 4 percent. The numbers in any one country can be much more dramatic than that: Since 1980, South Africa’s maize output, for example, has averaged about 28 percent below normal during El Niño years.

这一周期的两个阶段都可能损害农作物，尽管2014年一项著名的研究发现，厄尔尼诺现象影响的全球耕地面积更为广泛。虽然有些地方降水增加，部分作物生长旺盛，但在许多地方，干旱却导致产量枯竭。同一项研究发现，厄尔尼诺现象往往能使全球大豆产量提高多达5%，但会使玉米、水稻和小麦的产量下降多达4%。在某些国家，这些数字可能会更加惊人：例如，自1980年以来，南非在厄尔尼诺年份的玉米产量平均比正常水平低约28%。

Cereal disruptions, even if small, can hit hard if they come on top of other economic strains, says Stanford agricultural economist David Lobell. “When markets are tight, then if there’s a drought somewhere, that’s a much bigger deal.”

斯坦福大学农业经济学家大卫·洛贝尔（David Lobell）表示，谷物供应的中断，即使规模很小，如果叠加在其他经济压力之上，也可能造成沉重打击。“当市场供应紧张时，如果某地发生干旱，那将是一个严重得多的问题。”

And the markets are, indeed, tight. Global average food prices have been trending upwards for about 20 years, says Lobell, thanks to climate blows stacking on top of each other. And this year has brought additional strains. When the U.S. and Israel launched an attack on Iran at the end of February, marine traffic through the Strait of Hormuz ground to a near halt for months. The transport of oil was choked, energy prices spiked, and the supply of fertilizer, which is most often made with hydrogen sourced from cheap natural gas, was severely pinched. The Middle East usually produces a quarter of the world’s fertilizer. Meanwhile, the ongoing war in Ukraine continues to play havoc with the cost of fuel and restrict the transport of wheat from a major hub on the Black Sea.

市场确实处于紧张状态。洛贝尔指出，由于气候冲击不断叠加，全球平均食品价格在过去约20年里一直呈上升趋势。而今年又带来了额外的压力。当美国和以色列在2月底对伊朗发动袭击时，通过霍尔木兹海峡的海上交通几乎停滞了数月。石油运输受阻，能源价格飙升，而通常由廉价天然气制氢生产的化肥供应也受到了严重挤压。中东地区通常生产全球四分之一的化肥。与此同时，持续的乌克兰战争继续扰乱燃料成本，并限制了从黑海主要枢纽运输小麦的能力。

As of October, the warming in the mid-tropical Pacific that’s used to measure the strength of an El Niño hit 3.5 degrees Celsius—breaking the previous record of about 2.75 degrees Celsius—and is on its way to an unprecedented peak of around 4 degrees, according to Hausfather. “That’s scary,” he says. Broadly speaking, he says, the more powerful the El Niño, the more powerful its effects on weather events, including droughts.

据豪斯法瑟称，截至10月，用于衡量厄尔尼诺强度的热带太平洋中部升温幅度已达到3.5摄氏度，打破了此前约2.75摄氏度的纪录，并正朝着约4摄氏度的前所未有的峰值迈进。“这很可怕，”他说。从广义上讲，他认为厄尔尼诺现象越强，其对包括干旱在内的天气事件的影响就越强烈。

Unusually, this year’s El Niño is the third “strong” El Niño event to hit within a relatively short span of 11 years, which means some areas have been hit repeatedly with drought. The higher-than-usual frequency of El Niños may itself be caused by climate change, some researchers have suggested—though this is still an area of hot debate, Hausfather says.

不同寻常的是，今年的厄尔尼诺现象是11年相对较短的时间内发生的第三次“强”厄尔尼诺事件，这意味着一些地区已多次遭受干旱袭击。一些研究人员认为，厄尔尼诺现象频率高于往常可能本身就是由气候变化引起的——尽管豪斯法瑟表示，这仍是一个激烈争论的领域。

All these factors act together, with cascading impacts that can take months to develop and last for years. “The confluence of drought and conflict drives some of the worst outcomes we see,” says Richard Choularton, director of climate and resilience for the World Food Programme in Rome. A farmer can’t afford fertilizer; then his fields face drought. In anticipation of a bad year, he plants less and hires fewer people. The river used to transport his crop dries up, and the port that takes it overseas has been bombed out of commission. His cows have little to eat. A year later, he can’t afford to buy groceries or fuel his farming equipment. The lack of rain allows seawater to seep into aquifers, so fresh water gets rationed.

所有这些因素共同作用，产生了连锁反应，其影响可能需要数月时间显现，并持续数年之久。“干旱与冲突的交织导致了我们所见的一些最糟糕的结果，”位于罗马的世界粮食计划署气候与复原力主任理查德·乔拉顿（Richard Choularton）说。一位农民买不起化肥，随后他的田地又遭遇干旱。为了应对糟糕的一年，他减少了种植面积并削减了雇工。用于运输作物的河流干涸了，负责出口的港口也因轰炸而瘫痪。他的牛没有东西吃。一年后，他买不起食品杂货，也无法为农用设备提供燃料。降雨的缺乏导致海水渗入含水层，淡水供应因此受到限制。
