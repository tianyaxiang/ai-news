On Monday evening, San Diego’s city council voted to end its contracts with two surveillance equipment vendors, Flock and Ubicquia. San Diego is one of the largest cities to end its relationship with Flock, the startup with an estimated valuation of $8.3 billion.

周一晚上，圣地亚哥市议会投票决定终止与两家监控设备供应商 Flock 和 Ubicquia 的合同。圣地亚哥是终止与 Flock 合作关系的最大城市之一，这家初创公司的估值约为 83 亿美元。

“We fucking won!” said Lilly Irani of the Transparent and Responsible Use of Surveillance Technology (TRUST) SD Coalition, speaking in her personal capacity. She and her colleagues have been among the loudest critics in recent years. “The national energy creating left and right and center coalitions was the major cause [of the political victory],” she said, noting that it was only last month that one of the councilmembers revoked his support, creating an opening for a political majority to halt the surveillance.

“我们他妈的赢了！”以个人身份发言的“透明与负责任地使用监控技术”（TRUST）圣地亚哥联盟的莉莉·伊拉尼（Lilly Irani）说道。近年来，她和她的同事一直是该项目最激烈的批评者。“在全国范围内建立左翼、右翼和中间派联盟所产生的能量是（此次政治胜利的）主要原因，”她说。她指出，就在上个月，其中一名议员撤回了支持，这为政治多数派叫停监控创造了机会。

That council member, Stephen Whitburn, said in an October press conference that he wanted his fellow citizens to “feel safe.” “If Flock cameras are causing many San Diegans to feel unsafe, then they have lost effectiveness as a public safety tool,” he said at the time.

该议员斯蒂芬·惠特伯恩（Stephen Whitburn）在 10 月的新闻发布会上表示，他希望市民们能“感到安全”。他当时说：“如果 Flock 摄像头让许多圣地亚哥人感到不安全，那么它们作为公共安全工具就已经失去了效力。”

After the Monday vote, San Diego Mayor Todd Gloria said in a social media post that he “respect[ed] the Council’s decision” and would not veto the move to stop operation of the cameras across the city. Andrew Smith, a spokesperson for Flock, told Ars in an email on Tuesday that the company also “respect[s] the city’s decision.” But Flock isn’t happy about it.

周一投票结束后，圣地亚哥市长托德·格洛里亚（Todd Gloria）在社交媒体上发文称，他“尊重市议会的决定”，并且不会否决在全市范围内停止使用摄像头的举措。Flock 发言人安德鲁·史密斯（Andrew Smith）周二在给 Ars 的电子邮件中表示，该公司也“尊重该市的决定”。但 Flock 对此并不高兴。

“When a tool that is actively helping solve violent crimes is removed, public safety moves backward,” Smith wrote. “That has real consequences: cases will take longer to solve, organized retail theft crews will operate with fewer obstacles, a missing child may not be returned home, and victims may wait longer, or indefinitely, for justice.” At Monday’s city council meeting, police representatives said there has been a 20 percent decline in vehicle theft since 2023, with 689 stolen vehicles recovered as a result of the Flock cameras.

“当一个积极帮助侦破暴力犯罪的工具被移除时，公共安全就会倒退，”史密斯写道。“这会产生实际后果：案件侦破时间将延长，有组织的零售盗窃团伙将更畅通无阻，失踪儿童可能无法回家，受害者可能需要等待更久，甚至无限期地等待正义。”在周一的市议会会议上，警方代表表示，自 2023 年以来，车辆盗窃案下降了 20%，得益于 Flock 摄像头，已有 689 辆被盗车辆被找回。

The vote is a remarkable turnabout from just three years ago, when the mayor and other municipal leaders urged the city council to approve the program. The city’s wholesale rejection of these devices included Ubicquia’s so-called “smart streetlights” that worked in concert with Flock’s AI-driven automatic license plate reader (ALPR) software.

这次投票与三年前相比是一个显著的转变，当时市长和其他市政领导人还敦促市议会批准该计划。该市对这些设备的全面拒绝包括了 Ubicquia 所谓的“智能路灯”，这些路灯与 Flock 的人工智能驱动的自动车牌识别（ALPR）软件协同工作。

This cessation of smart streetlights marks the second time in less than a decade that San Diego has experimented with and then ultimately rejected this technology. At one point, San Diego purchased thousands of smart streetlight systems from a different vendor, GE Current, but a previous mayoral administration shuttered them in 2020. After that, Ubicquia acquired the smart city platform and proposed a newer installation of 500 cameras in 2023 in partnership with Flock—and supported by police—which was installed in 2023.

此次叫停智能路灯标志着圣地亚哥在不到十年内第二次尝试并最终拒绝了这项技术。圣地亚哥曾从另一家供应商 GE Current 购买了数千套智能路灯系统，但上一届市政府在 2020 年将其关闭。此后，Ubicquia 收购了该智慧城市平台，并于 2023 年与 Flock 合作，在警方的支持下提议安装 500 个新摄像头，并于 2023 年完成安装。

At the time, a local watchdog agency, the City of San Diego Privacy Advisory Board, asked the city council to vote down the plan. One of its board members, Pegah Parsi, wrote on LinkedIn that she is now feeling “vindication.” “A reminder for every city, agency, and institution: if you create an advisory board of experts, listen to them!” she wrote. “Actually, earnestly listen to them. It’s cheaper than learning the hard way. Do better.”

当时，当地监督机构“圣地亚哥市隐私咨询委员会”曾要求市议会否决该计划。其董事会成员之一佩加·帕西（Pegah Parsi）在领英上写道，她现在感到“平反了”。“给每个城市、机构和组织提个醒：如果你成立了一个专家咨询委员会，就听听他们的意见！”她写道。“真正地、认真地听取他们的意见。这比吃一堑长一智要划算得多。做得更好吧。”

That sentiment is echoed by Secure Justice, an Oakland-based advocacy group, which has been studying and monitoring Flock’s issues nationwide. “We’ve documented at least 513 Flock contracts that have ended in red and blue states, in large and small cities,” Brian Hofer, the group’s executive director, emailed Ars. “It’s pretty clear that Americans do not consent to their government tracking them as we go about our daily lives.”

总部位于奥克兰的倡导组织“安全正义”（Secure Justice）也表达了同样的观点，该组织一直在研究和监测 Flock 在全国范围内的问题。“我们记录了至少 513 份在红色州和蓝色州、大型和小型城市中终止的 Flock 合同，”该组织执行董事布莱恩·霍弗（Brian Hofer）在给 Ars 的电子邮件中写道。“很明显，美国人不同意政府在我们进行日常生活时追踪我们。”
