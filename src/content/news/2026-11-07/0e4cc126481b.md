EXECUTIVE SUMMARY I’m not the sort of person who typically pays for a house cleaner. I’m too frugal, and I kind of enjoy putting on a podcast and going to town on my tub with a Magic Eraser. But when I saw an X post about a new humanoid-robot cleaning service in San Francisco, I immediately signed up for the waitlist. I wanted my apartment to feel like The Jetsons, even if just for an hour.

执行摘要：我通常不是那种会花钱请家政清洁工的人。我太节俭了，而且我挺享受一边听播客，一边用魔术擦把浴缸刷得干干净净。但当我看到一篇关于旧金山一项新型人形机器人清洁服务的 X 帖子时，我立刻注册了候补名单。我想让我的公寓感觉像《杰森一家》里的场景，哪怕只有一个小时也好。

I was also motivated by journalistic interest. In order to ensure an authentic experience, I didn’t tell Tau Robotics, the startup behind the X post, that I was a journalist—though I did use my real name, so it’s possible someone looked me up. Tau’s long-term goal is to make autonomous cleaning robots a reality, but they’re not quite there yet: For now, the robots are piloted remotely by humans, who watch what’s going on via cameras mounted in the robots’ heads and wrists.

我同时也出于新闻工作者的兴趣。为了确保体验的真实性，我没有告诉发布那篇帖子的初创公司 Tau Robotics 我是一名记者——尽管我用了真名，所以他们也有可能查过我的背景。Tau 的长期目标是实现自主清洁机器人，但他们目前还没达到那个阶段：现阶段，这些机器人由人类远程操控，操作员通过安装在机器人头部和手腕上的摄像头观察现场情况。

As the robots clean, they collect data that Tau will use to train up their more independent descendants. Every counter successfully wiped and T-shirt successfully folded will help the models that power the robots learn to do better on the next go-round. Because this data is so valuable to Tau, the company charges less for cleaning than one might expect for such a cutting-edge product.

机器人在清洁时会收集数据，Tau 将利用这些数据来训练其更具独立性的后代机型。每一次成功擦拭的台面和成功折叠的 T 恤，都能帮助驱动机器人的模型在下一次任务中表现得更好。由于这些数据对 Tau 来说非常有价值，该公司提供的清洁服务收费比人们对这种尖端产品所预期的要低。

At the time I booked, Tau was offering $30 for an hourlong cleaning with a single robot; now the charge is $60 per weekly cleaning with two robots, or $100 for a one-time visit. But even at the cheaper rate of $30 per hour, Tau’s robots aren’t a great bargain. They are thorough but slow, so if you, like me, are tempted to stand around watching them for their entire visit, it would probably be a better use of your time to just take care of the cleaning yourself.

在我预订时，Tau 提供单台机器人一小时 30 美元的清洁服务；现在收费标准是两台机器人每周清洁一次 60 美元，或单次上门 100 美元。但即使按每小时 30 美元的低价计算，Tau 的机器人也不是特别划算。它们打扫得很彻底但速度很慢，所以如果你像我一样，忍不住站在旁边看它们工作一整小时，那么把时间花在自己动手清洁上可能更有效率。

But the robots are physically capable of cleaning, and they’re undeniably cute. Tau has armed each four-and-a-half-foot-tall robot with a 3D-printed “head” with antennae that allow it to communicate with Tau’s headquarters, as well as two 3D-printed claw “hands” (the more humanlike alternatives apparently aren’t durable enough, and claws do surprisingly well for most cleaning tasks). Mine was wearing a Tau Robotics shirt, and grippy socks so that it didn’t scratch hardwood floors.

不过，这些机器人确实具备清洁的物理能力，而且它们不可否认地很可爱。Tau 为每个四英尺半高的机器人配备了一个 3D 打印的“头部”，上面有天线，可以与 Tau 总部通信，还有两只 3D 打印的爪状“手”（更像人手的替代品显然不够耐用，而爪子在大多数清洁任务中表现出奇地好）。我那台机器人穿着一件 Tau Robotics 的衬衫，还穿了防滑袜，以免刮伤硬木地板。

With all the human support they need, the robots are closer to radio-controlled cars than Waymos. Many tasks, such as making the bed and folding laundry, require what’s called kinematic guidance: The human pilot performs the necessary motions, and sensors transmit those movements to the robot, which copies them. These kinematically guided tasks produce especially valuable training data.

由于需要大量的人工支持，这些机器人更像是遥控车，而不是 Waymo 无人驾驶汽车。许多任务，例如铺床和折叠衣物，需要所谓的运动学引导：人类操作员执行必要的动作，传感器将这些动作传输给机器人，机器人再进行模仿。这些通过运动学引导完成的任务会产生特别有价值的训练数据。

Tau’s AI models might not have solved laundry folding yet, but learning from the examples of robots that have completed the task in the real world is how they’ll get there. But according to Tau cofounder Cornelia Weinzierl, to whom I came clean as a journalist a few weeks after the cleaning, the robot can already complete some other tasks—such as vacuuming and mopping—without any human help.

Tau 的人工智能模型可能还没完全攻克折叠衣物的难题，但通过学习现实世界中机器人完成任务的案例，它们终将实现这一目标。不过，据 Tau 联合创始人 Cornelia Weinzierl（我在清洁服务几周后向她坦白了我的记者身份）称，机器人已经可以在没有任何人工帮助的情况下完成一些其他任务，例如吸尘和拖地。

If Tau succeeds in using all the data it is collecting to train truly autonomous cleaning robots, they could even become San Francisco’s newest tech tourist attraction as Waymos become more commonplace countrywide. The cleaning robot that came to my building last month was already a bit of a spectacle. It arrived in a blue minivan, out of which it was helped by Weinzierl and another Tau employee.

如果 Tau 成功利用所收集的所有数据来训练出真正的自主清洁机器人，随着 Waymo 在全国范围内变得越来越普遍，它们甚至可能成为旧金山最新的科技旅游景点。上个月来到我大楼的那台清洁机器人已经引起了一些围观。它坐着一辆蓝色小型货车抵达，由 Weinzierl 和另一名 Tau 员工将其从车上扶下来。

That’s when the robot encountered its first obstacle: the eight stairs leading up to the building’s front door. I’d come down to greet Weinzierl and the robot, so I had a front-row seat to its struggles with my steps. It climbed as if it were drunk. Each successful step was as likely as not to be followed by a stumbling backward stagger that erased all its progress—though, impressively, it never entirely lost its footing.

就在那时，机器人遇到了第一个障碍：通往大楼前门的八级台阶。我下楼去迎接 Weinzierl 和机器人，所以近距离目睹了它在台阶上的挣扎。它爬楼梯的样子就像喝醉了一样。每迈出成功的一步，紧接着很可能就是一次踉跄后退，抵消了它所有的进展——尽管令人印象深刻的是，它从未完全失去平衡。

When I later discussed my experience with Weinzierl, she explained that the robot sometimes walks strangely because it is operated remotely from Tau’s headquarters, and the short signal transmission delay can affect its coordination. “The amount of work and time that we spend just optimizing, for example, timings and connectivity is crazy,” she said.

后来我和 Weinzierl 讨论我的体验时，她解释说机器人有时走路姿势奇怪，是因为它是从 Tau 总部远程操作的，微小的信号传输延迟会影响其协调性。“我们仅仅在优化时间安排和连接性等方面所花费的工作量和时间简直疯狂，”她说。

Inside, the carpeted staircase leading up to my apartment defeated the robot entirely, and Weinzierl had to lug it up the last few steps. This, it turned out, was not typical. As the robot cleaned, it repeatedly stalled out, something Weinzierl attributed to it overheating in the unseasonably hot weather—until she heard from one of her colleagues that the heat had shut down not the robot but the equipment that its human controller was using to watch its progress through my apartment.

在室内，通往我公寓的地毯楼梯彻底难倒了机器人，Weinzierl 不得不把它扛上了最后几级台阶。事实证明，这并非典型情况。当机器人进行清洁时，它反复停滞，Weinzierl 将其归因于反常的高温天气导致过热——直到她从同事那里得知，高温关闭的不是机器人，而是人类操作员用来监控其在我公寓内工作进度的设备。

Without remote assistance, the robot couldn’t clean, and so it, and Weinzierl, departed before my booked hour was up. (Back in September, Weinzierl or another Tau representative remained with the robot throughout its visit, but she says that’s no longer necessary.) You can’t fault Tau on customer service, though. Weinzierl quickly emailed me to apologize, comp the visit, and offer me another on the house (minus whatever price I assign to my data privacy)—and that was all before I told her I was a journalist.

没有远程协助，机器人就无法工作，因此它和 Weinzierl 在我预订的一小时结束前就离开了。（早在九月份，Weinzierl 或其他 Tau 代表会在机器人访问期间全程陪同，但她说现在已经没必要了。）不过，你不能在客户服务方面指责 Tau。Weinzierl 很快给我发邮件道歉，免除了那次访问的费用，并免费提供了一次额外服务（扣除我个人对数据隐私的估值）——而这一切发生在我告诉她我是记者之前。

I scheduled the next cleaning for about a month later, after a couple of trips out of town, and it went much more smoothly—the robot got up my stairs without much assistance, though still with the drunken stagger. In the hour it was there, it did a creditable job of cleaning my kitchen (though it didn’t brave the stove) and even saved me my most dreaded cleaning task, the toilet.

我在大约一个月后安排了下一次清洁，当时我刚出差回来，过程顺利多了——机器人没怎么帮忙就爬上了楼梯，尽管依然带着那种醉酒般的踉跄。在那一小时里，它出色地完成了厨房的清洁工作（虽然没敢碰炉灶），甚至帮我解决了最令我头疼的清洁任务：马桶。

That was its most impressive performance: Three times the head fell off its toilet cleaning wand, and three times it managed to reattach it by poking around with the handle until it made contact at just the right angle. The robot is still rough around the edges, which is to be expected for a product in beta. 这是它最令人印象深刻的表现：它的马桶刷头掉了三次，它三次都通过用手柄四处戳动，直到以恰当的角度接触，成功将其重新装好。这个机器人目前仍有瑕疵，对于一款处于测试阶段的产品来说，这是意料之中的。它多次让我的水龙头滴水，打翻了一瓶（密封的）橄榄油，而且无法扶正一台空气净化器。
