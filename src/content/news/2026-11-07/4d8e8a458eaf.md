In the Grand Canyon, there is a line in the rock where time seems to have gone missing. Ancient crystalline rocks mark its bottom, while sandstones laid down as life started to diversify during the Cambrian explosion mark the top. “It’s called the Great Unconformity, and it’s a gap in the geologic record,” says Nicolas Flament, a geoscientist at the University of Wollongong, Australia. “This gap can be anywhere between 200 million years, which is not that impressive, to more than a billion years.”

在大峡谷的岩层中，有一条界线仿佛让时间消失了。底部是古老的结晶岩，而顶部则是寒武纪生命大爆发时期生命开始多样化时沉积的砂岩。“这被称为‘大不整合面’，是地质记录中的一个缺口，”澳大利亚伍伦贡大学的地球科学家尼古拉斯·弗拉门特（Nicolas Flament）说，“这个缺口的时间跨度从2亿年（这并不算太惊人）到超过10亿年不等。”

In a recent Nature study, Flament and his colleague Santosh Dhungana argue that this gap was carved with help from deep within the planet. According to their models, slow currents of hot rock in Earth’s mantle pushed entire continents up for hundreds of millions of years, letting glaciers grind them down and then let them sink, allowing fresh sediments to bury the scar. The largest hole in the rock record might be a fingerprint of processes happening deep in the planet’s interior.

在最近发表于《自然》杂志的一项研究中，弗拉门特和他的同事桑托什·邓加纳（Santosh Dhungana）认为，这个缺口的形成离不开地球深处的助力。根据他们的模型，地幔中缓慢流动的热岩流在数亿年间将整个大陆抬升，使冰川得以将其磨平，随后大陆下沉，让新的沉积物掩盖了这道伤痕。岩石记录中最大的这个空缺，或许正是地球内部深处地质过程留下的指纹。

The oldest sediments resting on the Great Unconformity in North America are between 635 and 482 million years old. Geologists have traced it across most of North America and parts of northern Europe and northern China. “Something seems to have happened, in particular in North America,” Flament says. The leading explanation was glaciation. Between roughly 720 and 635 million years ago, glaciers reached the tropics, and some scientists think the entire planet froze over, a scenario called Snowball Earth.

北美大不整合面上方最古老的沉积物距今约6.35亿至4.82亿年。地质学家已在北美大部分地区以及北欧和中国北部的部分地区追踪到了这一现象。“似乎发生了一些事情，特别是在北美，”弗拉门特说。目前主流的解释是冰川作用。在大约7.2亿至6.35亿年前，冰川延伸到了热带地区，一些科学家认为整个地球当时处于冰封状态，即所谓的“雪球地球”假说。

“Ice sheets are very good at grinding, scouring, scraping the surface,” Flament explains. He said that glaciers are about 10 times more effective than rivers at eroding rock away. But if the whole planet froze, the Great Unconformity should be found everywhere. And it’s not. Other explanations included rifting, pulling and stretching of Earth’s crust, and a global drop in sea levels. But rifting acts mostly on the edges of continents, and a sea level change should have affected all continents alike.

“冰盖非常擅长研磨、冲刷和刮擦地表，”弗拉门特解释道。他说，冰川侵蚀岩石的效率大约是河流的10倍。但如果整个地球都冻结了，那么大不整合面应该随处可见，事实却并非如此。其他的解释包括裂谷作用（地壳的拉伸和扩张）以及全球海平面的下降。然而，裂谷作用主要发生在大陆边缘，而海平面变化应该对所有大陆产生相同的影响。

“There’s no doubt that there was extensive glaciation. There’s no doubt that there have been sea-level changes throughout time. The question is, was that enough to put the Great Unconformity puzzle together?” Flament says. A fourth idea held that the mantle itself was moving continents up and down, a process called dynamic topography. “It had been proposed, but it hadn’t been quantitatively tested,” Flament says. So, his team set out to do so.

“毫无疑问，当时存在大规模的冰川作用。毫无疑问，地质史上一直存在海平面变化。问题在于，这些因素是否足以拼凑出大不整合面的谜题？”弗拉门特说。第四种观点认为，地幔本身在推动大陆上下移动，这一过程被称为“动力地形”。“这个观点之前就被提出过，但从未经过定量测试，”弗拉门特说。于是，他的团队着手进行了这项研究。

From roughly 100 to 200 kilometers beneath the surface down to nearly 3,000 kilometers, Earth’s mantle slowly convects. “An analogy would be a lava lamp, where some stuff comes up and some stuff comes down,” Flament explains. “Cold rock sinks and hot rock rises.” The mantle is solid, yet a piece of rock can slowly travel from the surface to the deep Earth over some 200 million years. That motion tugs on the surface.

从地表下约100到200公里处一直到近3000公里深处，地球的地幔在缓慢地进行对流。“可以把它比作熔岩灯，一些物质上升，一些物质下降，”弗拉门特解释道，“冷的岩石下沉，热的岩石上升。”地幔虽然是固态的，但一块岩石可以在约2亿年的时间里从地表缓慢移动到地球深处。这种运动会牵引地表。

Where cold slabs of ocean floor sink into the mantle at subduction zones, the ground gets pulled down; where hot mantle rises, it gets pushed up. The amplitude of this up and down motion is still debated, though. “The short of it is several hundreds of meters, and it’s still to be worked out exactly what that [precise value] is,” Flament says. Dhungana and Flament ran six simulations of mantle convection going back 1 billion years, driven by reconstructions of how tectonic plates moved over that time.

在俯冲带，寒冷的洋底板块沉入地幔，地面会被拉低；而在热地幔上升的地方，地面会被推高。不过，这种上下运动的幅度仍存在争议。“简而言之，幅度大约有几百米，但确切的数值还有待研究，”弗拉门特说。邓加纳和弗拉门特运行了六次地幔对流模拟，回溯了过去10亿年的历史，这些模拟由当时构造板块运动的重建数据驱动。

Then they grouped regions where the ground rose and fell in similar ways to find where the unconformity should have formed, and where it should have survived to the present. Flament, however, says the starting configuration of these simulations remains uncertain. Magnetic records in ancient rocks reveal how far continents were from the equator, but not their longitude. “It’s possible that some continents may be in the wrong hemisphere,” Flament admits.

随后，他们将地面升降方式相似的区域进行分组，以寻找不整合面应该形成的位置，以及它在何处能够保存至今。然而，弗拉门特表示，这些模拟的初始配置仍存在不确定性。古老岩石中的磁记录揭示了大陆距离赤道的远近，但无法确定其经度。“有些大陆可能处于错误的半球，”弗拉门特承认。

The ancient ocean floor, long since recycled into the mantle, had to be invented entirely from scratch in the models. Still, the simulations produced a consistent story. Laurentia, the ancient core of North America, had little or no subduction along its margins between about 930 and 510 million years ago, much like Africa today, so it sat above some rising mantle and was pushed up. Five of the six models showed this pattern of Laurentia seeing long uplift followed by sinking in the Cambrian.

早已被循环回地幔的古老洋底，在模型中必须完全从零开始构建。尽管如此，模拟结果还是得出了一个一致的结论。劳伦大陆（北美洲的古老核心）在大约9.3亿至5.1亿年前，其边缘几乎没有或完全没有俯冲作用，这与今天的非洲非常相似，因此它位于上升的地幔之上并被推高。六个模型中有五个显示出劳伦大陆经历了长期的抬升，随后在寒武纪发生了下沉。

For northern Europe and North China, this pattern showed up in four. But the uplift alone, Flament’s team concluded, was too gentle to strip away enough rock to create the Great Unconformity. “The uplift could create a tilt for the whole continent, but it’s not great at creating a steep slope,” Flament says. The missing piece of the puzzle, he argues, were glaciers that ground through rock even on low ground and gentle slopes.

对于北欧和华北地区，这种模式在四个模型中出现。但弗拉门特的团队得出结论，仅靠抬升作用过于平缓，不足以剥离足够的岩石来形成大不整合面。“抬升可以使整个大陆产生倾斜，但并不擅长形成陡峭的坡度，”弗拉门特说。他认为，拼图缺失的一块是冰川，它们即使在低地和平缓的斜坡上也能磨蚀岩石。

The models show the uplift was still building when Snowball Earth hit. “This gentle uplift that’s continuous, and then that glacial erosion that comes on top, work together,” Flament explains. “They’re not coupled. They just happen to coincide in time.” When the continents sank in the Cambrian, seas flooded in and buried the eroded surface. The models made by Flament’s team also explain why the gap is so obvious in North America but hard to find elsewhere.

模型显示，当“雪球地球”来临时，抬升作用仍在持续。“这种持续的平缓抬升，加上随后的冰川侵蚀，两者共同作用，”弗拉门特解释道，“它们并非耦合，只是在时间上恰好重合。”当大陆在寒武纪下沉时，海水涌入并掩埋了被侵蚀的地表。弗拉门特团队制作的模型还解释了为什么这个缺口在北美如此明显，而在其他地方却难以发现。

Compared with records of sediment preserved on each continent, North America and Eurasia show one long rise followed by one long fall, which the researchers interpret as the ideal recipe for the Great Unconformity. South America and Australia, on the other hand, went through two cycles. “This would form potentially two unconformities, and then the Great Unconformity would be more difficult to see,” Flament says. Africa was a different case altogether—hemmed in by mountain belts at the time, its erosion and sediment were shaped by the mountains rather than by the mantle beneath.

与各大陆保存的沉积记录相比，北美和欧亚大陆显示出一次长期的上升和随后的长期下降，研究人员将其解读为形成大不整合面的理想条件。另一方面，南美洲和澳大利亚经历了两个周期。“这可能会形成两个不整合面，从而使大不整合面更难被观察到，”弗拉门特说。非洲的情况则完全不同——当时它被山带包围，其侵蚀和沉积作用更多是由山脉而非下方的地幔塑造的。
