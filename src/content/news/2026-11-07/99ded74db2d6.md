计算机科学 > 人工智能 arXiv:2611.06972 (cs) [提交于 2026 年 11 月 4 日] 标题：KDASO：面向责任裁决任务的知识-数据双驱动自动化技能优化。作者：Zi-Jian Cheng, Weiming Wu, Jie Meng, Zhen Peng, Shan Huang, Qun Li, Guobing Wu, Lan-Zhe Guo。

Abstract: Liability adjudication tasks (e.g., legal charge determination) require analyzing event descriptions against domain-specific skills to determine whether a party should bear liability. While Large Language Models (LLMs) have shown promise in this area by leveraging executable skills for interpretable reasoning, existing approaches face three critical limitations: skill libraries become stale as real-world scenarios dynamically evolve, skill descriptions often suffer from a semantic execution gap where LLMs deviate from intended expert logic, and growing skill collections exceed context budgets causing critical skill omission.

摘要：责任裁决任务（例如法律指控判定）需要根据领域特定技能分析事件描述，以确定当事人是否应承担责任。尽管大语言模型（LLMs）通过利用可执行技能进行可解释推理在该领域展现出潜力，但现有方法面临三个关键局限：随着现实场景的动态演变，技能库会变得陈旧；技能描述常因语义执行鸿沟导致 LLMs 偏离预期的专家逻辑；不断增长的技能集合超出了上下文预算，导致关键技能遗漏。

To address these challenges, we propose Knowledge-Data Dual-Driven Automated Skill Optimization (KDASO), which continuously evolves a skill library through neighborhood-conditioned induction from recent adjudication data, execution-aligned refinement based on LLM trajectory analysis, and context-budgeted routing for scalable skill selection.

为了解决这些挑战，我们提出了知识-数据双驱动自动化技能优化（KDASO），它通过基于近期裁决数据的邻域条件归纳、基于 LLM 轨迹分析的执行对齐优化，以及用于可扩展技能选择的上下文预算路由，持续演进技能库。

Experiments across ride-hailing adjudication, legal liability determination, and logical reasoning judgment demonstrate that KDASO significantly outperforms current general-purpose and domain-specific baselines. KDASO further achieves 8.96% improvement when transferring skills to concept-similar scenarios and maintains consistent performance gains through continuous optimization, underscoring the high transferability and stability of generated skills.

在网约车裁决、法律责任判定和逻辑推理判断等方面的实验表明，KDASO 显著优于当前的通用和领域特定基准。KDASO 在将技能迁移到概念相似的场景时进一步实现了 8.96% 的提升，并通过持续优化保持了稳定的性能增益，凸显了所生成技能的高可迁移性和稳定性。
