# Man unexpectedly recovers from a knife wound through his eye and brain

A man in his 20s showed up to a UK hospital with the hilt of a knife sticking out of his face. The 15 mm-wide blade hidden within went completely through his right eye and through his brain, with the tip of the blade hitting the top of his skull. Despite the ghastly injury, the man was alive and conscious when he arrived. He could still talk and move all four limbs. His doctors described him as being in a state of agitation.

一名20多岁的男子出现在英国一家医院，刀柄从他的脸上伸出来。隐藏在其中的15毫米宽的刀刃完全穿过了他的右眼和大脑，刀尖触及了他的颅骨顶部。尽管伤势骇人，但这名男子到达时仍然活着且神志清醒。他还能说话并移动四肢。医生形容他处于一种焦躁不安的状态。

On the Glasgow Coma Scale, he scored a 12 (E3 V4 M5), indicating a “moderate” brain injury, just one point above “mild.” He could open his left eye when told, but otherwise kept it closed (E3 score); he could talk and answer questions, but was confused (V4); and he intentionally moved away when pressure was applied to his body (M5). In a report published Friday in BMJ Case Report, his doctors said they were concerned that in his confused, agitated state he may try to pull the knife out on his own. So, they swiftly sedated and intubated him. They put him on prophylactic treatments for bacterial infections, including tetanus. Then they used images to see what was going on in his head.

在格拉斯哥昏迷量表上，他得了12分（E3 V4 M5），表明是“中度”脑损伤，仅比“轻度”高出一分。当被要求时，他能睁开左眼，但平时保持闭合（E3评分）；他能说话并回答问题，但感到困惑（V4）；当身体受到压力时，他会下意识地避开（M5）。在周五发表于《英国医学杂志病例报告》的一份报告中，医生表示担心他在困惑和焦躁的状态下可能会试图自己拔出刀子。因此，他们迅速对他进行了镇静和插管。他们为他提供了包括破伤风在内的细菌感染预防性治疗。随后，他们利用影像检查了他的头部情况。

“Pull-and-see” The blade was entirely through the right eye socket and eyeball. It traversed the left frontal and temporal lobes of his brain, passing through in front of—and without apparently injuring—the corpus callosum, which connects the two sides of the brain and is full of nerve fibers. The blade also missed the left internal capsule, which holds another bundle of nerve fibers.

“边拔边看”：刀刃完全穿过了右眼眶和眼球。它穿过了大脑的左额叶和颞叶，从连接大脑两侧且充满神经纤维的胼胝体前方穿过，且显然没有造成损伤。刀刃还避开了包含另一束神经纤维的左侧内囊。

In terms of damage to major blood vessels, doctors found that the blade was close to the left internal carotid artery, a major blood vessel, but didn’t seem to have damaged it. Doctors did find, however, that a branch of the middle cerebral artery looked to be indented by the knife. After consulting with various medical specialties, the man’s doctors moved to emergency neurosurgery. They opted to remove part of his skull to directly see into the brain, but used a conservative “pull-and-see” method to slide the blade out rather than invasively excavate it. After it was out, the doctors saw no signs of significant hemorrhaging. The brain wound was cleaned and drained before surgeons patched up his skull. His right eye, however, couldn’t be saved. Given its extensive damage, it was removed.

在主要血管损伤方面，医生发现刀刃靠近左侧颈内动脉这一主要血管，但似乎并未造成损伤。然而，医生确实发现大脑中动脉的一个分支看起来被刀压凹了。在咨询了多个医学专科后，该男子的医生进行了紧急神经外科手术。他们选择切除部分颅骨以直接观察大脑，但采用了保守的“边拔边看”方法将刀刃滑出，而不是进行侵入性挖掘。取出后，医生没有发现明显出血的迹象。在外科医生修补颅骨之前，脑部伤口经过了清理和引流。然而，他的右眼无法挽救。鉴于其损伤严重，右眼被摘除。

The man then moved to intensive care, where he recovered rapidly. A day after surgery, doctors were able to remove his breathing tube. He developed some minor right-side weakness, but it resolved two days later. Scans of his brain showed steady improvement—a little bleeding and swelling, and there was some air in his brain tissue from where the knife was. But otherwise, it appeared to be healing. He developed no infections and had no seizures.

随后，该男子被转入重症监护室，恢复迅速。术后一天，医生就拔掉了他的呼吸管。他出现了一些轻微的右侧无力，但在两天后消失了。脑部扫描显示病情稳步好转——有一些出血和肿胀，且由于刀子的位置，脑组织中存留了一些空气。但除此之外，伤口似乎正在愈合。他没有发生感染，也没有癫痫发作。

Despite the “dramatic intracranial trajectory,” his language function was preserved, and he had no loss of movement or coordination, they reported. He was discharged from the hospital and at follow-up appointments remained “neurologically well,” with ongoing eye and mental health care.

据报告，尽管“颅内轨迹惊人”，但他的语言功能得以保留，且没有出现运动或协调能力丧失。他已出院，在随访中保持“神经系统良好”，并继续接受眼科和心理健康护理。

Lucky recovery As for how the injury happened, his doctors still don’t know. “The mechanism is unclear,” they wrote. However, they noted he had a history of depression. The doctors reflected on how lucky the man was to have survived and to have such a full recovery. Such injuries that go through the eye and the brain are rare. When they do occur, they’re usually from “high-velocity mechanisms” such as gunshots or other ballistic injuries. Knives, rods, and nails fit into the rarer “low-velocity mechanisms.”

幸运的康复：至于受伤是如何发生的，医生们仍然不得而知。他们写道：“受伤机制尚不明确。”然而，他们指出他有抑郁症病史。医生们感叹这名男子能活下来并完全康复是多么幸运。这种穿过眼睛和大脑的损伤非常罕见。当它们发生时，通常是由枪击或其他弹道损伤等“高速机制”造成的。刀、杆和钉子则属于更罕见的“低速机制”。

“Such low-velocity penetrating brain injuries carry substantial morbidity and mortality because of hemorrhage, vascular injury, abscess, meningitis, cerebrospinal fluid leak, seizures and neurological deficit,” they wrote. But such fortunate recoveries aren’t unheard of; they cited reports of other remarkably unexpected recoveries from seemingly devastating brain injuries. That includes the famous case of Phineas Gage, a railroad foreman who survived an 1848 accident that sent a large iron rod directly through his head. The rod entered below his left cheekbone and exited the top of his skull. (Although he survived and recovered, Gage’s personality was said to have changed after the accident. The doctors in the knife-wound case did not mention any such personality changes.) “Echoing classic and contemporary examples of severe frontal lobe penetration.

他们写道：“这种低速穿透性脑损伤由于出血、血管损伤、脓肿、脑膜炎、脑脊液漏、癫痫和神经功能缺损，具有很高的发病率和死亡率。”但这种幸运的康复并非闻所未闻；他们引用了其他从看似毁灭性的脑损伤中意外康复的显著案例。其中包括著名的菲尼亚斯·盖奇（Phineas Gage）案，这位铁路工头在1848年的一场事故中幸存下来，当时一根大铁棒直接穿过了他的头部。铁棒从他左颧骨下方进入，从颅骨顶部穿出。（尽管他幸存并康复了，但据称盖奇在事故后性格发生了改变。刀伤病例中的医生没有提到任何此类性格变化。）“呼应了经典和当代严重额叶穿透的案例。
