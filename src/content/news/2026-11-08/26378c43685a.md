If there’s any incoming congressional freshman who understands how voter anger about data centers supercharged this week’s election, it’s Will Lawrence.

如果说有哪位新当选的国会议员了解选民对数据中心的愤怒是如何助推本周选举的，那一定是威尔·劳伦斯（Will Lawrence）。

A former member of the Democratic Socialists of America who helped found the Sunrise Movement, Lawrence centered his campaign on opposition to data centers from the beginning, months before many other candidates had caught on. It helped him win a challenging primary in Michigan’s 7th Congressional District against much-better-funded opponents, and this week, he defeated incumbent Republican Tom Barrett in one of the most expensive House races in the country. (Elon Musk’s America PAC chipped in $340,000 against Lawrence.)

作为美国民主社会主义者协会的前成员，同时也是“日出运动”（Sunrise Movement）的创始人之一，劳伦斯从竞选之初就将反对数据中心作为其核心议题，这比许多其他候选人意识到这一问题早了数月。这一策略帮助他在密歇根州第七国会选区的激烈初选中击败了资金雄厚的对手。本周，他在全国最昂贵的众议院选战之一中击败了现任共和党议员汤姆·巴雷特（Tom Barrett）。（埃隆·马斯克的“美国政治行动委员会”曾投入34万美元试图阻击劳伦斯。）

For this last edition of Power Play, I caught up with Lawrence about what the future holds for data center developers and for voters concerned about them, and what his plans are when he gets to Washington, DC, next year.

在“权力游戏”的最后一期节目中，我采访了劳伦斯，探讨了数据中心开发商及相关担忧选民的未来，以及他明年进入华盛顿特区后的计划。

MOLLY TAFT: I’ve seen some post-election takes that data centers weren’t as big of a factor for voters as media coverage may suggest. I’m curious as to what you heard from voters in the last weeks of the campaign about how data centers may be competing with other big issues in this election.

莫莉·塔夫特：我看到一些选后评论认为，数据中心对选民的影响并不像媒体报道的那样大。我很好奇，在竞选的最后几周，您从选民那里听到了什么？他们认为数据中心在本次选举中与其他重大议题相比处于什么位置？

WILL LAWRENCE: What are the two headlines on the election? It's been about costs, and it’s about corruption. Data centers speak directly to costs and to the legalized corruption that's become ordinary business in our rigged economy. They’re taking tax breaks, they’re sucking up water, they’re proposing to use double or triple the total capacity of the grid in Michigan, they’re threatening people’s home values.

威尔·劳伦斯：这次选举的两个头条是什么？是生活成本和腐败问题。数据中心直接关系到成本，也关系到在我们这种被操纵的经济体系中已成为常态的“合法腐败”。它们享受税收减免，消耗大量水资源，提议使用密歇根州电网总容量的两到三倍，并且威胁到人们的房产价值。

All of this is being done because the playing field is fundamentally slanted away from local communities and towards these mega corporations. People have attempted to exert community control and local oversight, and they've been challenged in court by these Silicon Valley companies.

这一切之所以发生，是因为竞争环境从根本上偏向了这些巨型企业，而非当地社区。人们曾试图行使社区控制权和地方监督权，却遭到了这些硅谷公司的法律挑战。

You see an economy and a political order that is favoring the wealthy few over the many. It really makes a mockery of democracy itself. Why is it that we keep having to play whack-a-mole against these individual projects? It shows that people in Silicon Valley and Wall Street and DC have made these decisions for us, and it speaks of the lack of economic democracy.

你所看到的经济和政治秩序，是在偏袒少数富人而非大多数民众。这简直是对民主的嘲弄。为什么我们必须不断地像玩“打地鼠”游戏一样去应对这些个别项目？这表明硅谷、华尔街和华盛顿的人们已经为我们做出了决定，这反映了经济民主的缺失。

There have been a lot of announcements from big tech companies in recent weeks, making promises to be more transparent, to use less water, to not use NDAs—OpenAI released a bunch of commitments the day after the election. Do you think these compromises are enough to sway people?

最近几周，大型科技公司发布了许多公告，承诺提高透明度、减少用水量、不再使用保密协议（NDA）——OpenAI 在选举后的第二天就发布了一系列承诺。您认为这些妥协足以改变人们的看法吗？

I haven’t seen any of the specific proposals. But if they’re feeling the heat, that’s good. What they've offered so far has been completely inadequate, and it's going to take a lot to win back trust.

我还没有看到任何具体的提案。但如果他们感受到了压力，那是好事。他们目前提供的方案完全不够，要赢回信任还需要付出很多努力。

There was just a new hyperscale data center proposed in the last week before the election in Delhi Township, south of Lansing, close to Mason. There was an announcement made by a local economic development agency and a would-be developer. They said, “This is going to be different. We're going to do a six-month community engagement process before we even decide if we are going to build a data center, but if we do, it's going to have all these protections. And we want to hear from you.”

就在选举前的最后一周，兰辛以南、靠近梅森的德里镇（Delhi Township）提议建设一个新的超大规模数据中心。当地一家经济发展机构和一位潜在开发商发布了公告。他们说：“这次会有所不同。在决定是否建设数据中心之前，我们将进行为期六个月的社区参与流程；如果最终决定建设，我们将提供所有这些保护措施。我们希望能听到你们的声音。”

The township board scrambled [to put together] an emergency meeting. Two hundred fifty people showed up, probably more than that—it was overflowing. And people said, OK, the answer is still no. It’s going to take a lot of work [from the companies] to convince anybody that anything has actually changed.

镇委员会匆忙召开了紧急会议。有250人到场，甚至可能更多——现场人满为患。人们的回答依然是：不行。公司需要付出巨大的努力，才能让人们相信情况真的发生了改变。

Have any tech companies reached out to you?

有科技公司联系过您吗？

I haven’t spoken to any of these companies.

我没有与这些公司中的任何一家交谈过。

When you go to Washington in January, what are some of the first policies you’re going to try and work on around data centers?

当您一月份前往华盛顿就职时，您打算在数据中心方面优先推动哪些政策？

I'm going to fight for a federal data center moratorium, and along with it, a pause on advanced AI development. The risks simply do not match up to the benefits at this time. We've got to have a conversation across all of the different stakeholders to determine what an appropriate regulatory agenda looks like.

我将争取实施联邦层面的数据中心建设暂停令，同时暂停先进人工智能的开发。目前的风险与收益完全不成正比。我们必须与所有利益相关方进行对话，以确定合理的监管议程。

Would that be a pause similar to what Senator Bernie Sanders has proposed, where it's contingent on AI safety regulations?

这是否类似于伯尼·桑德斯（Bernie Sanders）参议员提出的那种以人工智能安全法规为前提的暂停？

Yes. Safety here also means making sure that we're not sacrificing people and the planet to build data centers. We need to have appropriate regulations on both of those aspects.

是的。这里的“安全”也意味着确保我们不会为了建设数据中心而牺牲民众和地球。我们需要在这两个方面制定适当的法规。

What about using oversight or investigatory levers?

那么，利用监督或调查手段呢？

Certainly—it makes sense. We build cars here in Michigan. If GM put a car on the market that had a 1 percent chance of exploding on the highway and killing everybody inside, it would never go to market. If it did, they would be sued out of existence.

当然，这很有道理。我们在密歇根州制造汽车。如果通用汽车（GM）投放市场的一款汽车有1%的概率在高速公路上爆炸并导致车内人员死亡，它绝不会获准上市。如果真的上市了，他们会被起诉到破产。

But these Silicon Valley companies, they tell us that they're actively developing a product that has a 10 percent chance of killing everybody on planet Earth. I mean, what are we talking about? This is obviously reckless endangerment, according to any existing standard of consumer protection law. There should be investigations into that, and there should be suits filed to provide a sanction against companies for engaging in unacceptable risks that are a public hazard.

但这些硅谷公司却告诉我们，他们正在积极开发一种有10%概率杀死地球上所有人的产品。我的意思是，我们在谈论什么？根据任何现行的消费者保护法标准，这显然是鲁莽的危害行为。应该对此进行调查，并应提起诉讼，对那些制造不可接受风险、构成公共危害的公司进行制裁。
