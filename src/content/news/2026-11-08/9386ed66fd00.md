It’s not easy to extract iron from its ore. But the peoples of the ancient city of Great Zimbabwe—the capital of a thriving kingdom in southern Africa between 1000 to 1700 AD—were masters. At sites near the city’s stone ruins, clusters of iron-smelting furnaces survive today as mounds of rubble.

从矿石中提炼铁并非易事。然而，古城大津巴布韦（公元1000年至1700年间非洲南部一个繁荣王国的都城）的居民却是这方面的行家里手。在大津巴布韦石质遗迹附近的遗址中，成群的炼铁炉如今已化作一堆堆瓦砾。

At Mashava, a village 25 miles northwest, fragments bear fingermarks of those who long ago smoothed the structures’ clay interiors. Some of the furnaces were nearly an adult’s arm span in width, with walls thick and strong to contain the heat of more than 2,100° Fahrenheit needed to smelt iron.

在西北25英里处的马沙瓦村，一些碎片上留有很久以前人们抹平炉内粘土壁时留下的指纹。有些炉子的宽度几乎相当于一个成年人的臂展，炉壁厚实坚固，足以承受炼铁所需的超过2100华氏度的高温。

Unlike other furnaces that required workers to force air into them with bellows, these were designed to suck air in, then out through a chimney, no human labor required. This would have made it significantly easier to produce the iron, which was fashioned into hoes, axes, and arrowheads.

与那些需要工人用风箱鼓风的炉子不同，这些炉子被设计成能够自动吸入空气，再通过烟囱排出，无需人力参与。这使得铁器的生产变得容易得多，这些铁被加工成锄头、斧头和箭头。

“The number and size of these furnaces, together with the use of natural-draft technology, indicate intensive and technologically sophisticated iron production,” says archaeometallurgist Ezekiel Mtetwa of Chicago’s Field Museum of Natural History, who has long studied the furnaces.

芝加哥菲尔德自然历史博物馆的考古冶金学家以西结·姆特瓦（Ezekiel Mtetwa）长期研究这些炉子，他表示：“这些炉子的数量和规模，加上自然通风技术的应用，表明当时存在着密集且技术先进的铁器生产。”

Scientists are extremely interested in the peoples who created this and other iron-powered societies across eastern and southern Africa. Research points to an adaptable and innovative group from western Africa as the masterminds. Known as Bantu speakers, these communities began gradually expanding across the continent’s southern half, starting from 6,000 to 4,000 years ago.

科学家们对在非洲东部和南部创造了这种及其他铁器文明的民族非常感兴趣。研究指出，来自西非的一个适应力强且富有创新精神的群体是这些文明的幕后推手。这些被称为班图语族（Bantu speakers）的群体，从6000到4000年前开始，逐渐向非洲大陆的南半部扩张。

In doing so, they introduced a fundamentally new, sedentary way of life—characterized by farming, raising livestock, pottery-making, and metalworking—to regions previously dominated by hunter-gatherer communities. This migration transformed not only the region’s history and technology, but also its languages: Today, around 350 million Africans speak one of roughly 500 distinct Bantu tongues, from southern African Zulu to eastern African Swahili.

在此过程中，他们将一种全新的定居生活方式——以农业、畜牧业、制陶业和金属加工为特征——引入了此前由狩猎采集群体主导的地区。这次迁徙不仅改变了该地区的历史和技术，还改变了其语言：今天，约有3.5亿非洲人使用约500种不同的班图语言，从非洲南部的祖鲁语到东非的斯瓦希里语。

And at least as many Africans—as well as many African Americans and Afro-Latinos—carry Bantu ancestry in their DNA. Reconstructing this expansion has been a tough puzzle, especially given the region’s sparse written historical records and limited research funding.

至少有同样数量的非洲人，以及许多非裔美国人和非裔拉丁美洲人，在他们的DNA中携带班图人的血统。重建这一扩张过程一直是一个难题，特别是在该地区书面历史记录稀少且研究资金有限的情况下。

But a unique blend of scientists—linguists, archaeologists, and geneticists—have teamed up to unearth clues about the deep origins of the Bantu-speaking people, their path across Africa, and their relationships with other cultures. Although much debate and uncertainty remain, little by little the work is uncovering one of history’s largest and longest human migrations.

但由语言学家、考古学家和遗传学家组成的独特科学家团队联手合作，挖掘关于班图语族起源、他们穿越非洲的路径以及他们与其他文化之间关系的线索。尽管仍存在许多争论和不确定性，但这项工作正一点一点地揭开人类历史上规模最大、持续时间最长的一次迁徙的面纱。

“It completely reshaped the demography in sub-Saharan Africa, and we are really interested in trying to understand it,” says population geneticist Sandra Beleza of the United Kingdom’s University of Leicester.

英国莱斯特大学的群体遗传学家桑德拉·贝莱扎（Sandra Beleza）说：“它彻底重塑了撒哈拉以南非洲的人口结构，我们非常有兴趣去了解它。”
