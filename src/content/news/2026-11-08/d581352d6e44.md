**NYT Cooking’s AI assistant saved my weeknight chicken, but almost ruined my cake**

《纽约时报》烹饪频道的 AI 助手拯救了我的工作日晚餐，却差点毁了我的蛋糕

I tested the new AI-powered tool on ingredient swaps, recipe scaling, and a missing Bundt pan. by Jennifer Pattison Tuohy Nov 7, 2026, 3:50 PM UTC. The NYT Cooking app now has a built-in AI assistant.

我测试了这款全新的 AI 工具，涵盖了食材替换、食谱缩放以及缺少烤盘等场景。作者：Jennifer Pattison Tuohy，2026 年 11 月 7 日，世界标准时间下午 3:50。《纽约时报》烹饪应用现已内置 AI 助手。

Around 7PM each weeknight, I find myself standing in front of the fridge, trying to decide on something for dinner that won’t make my husband complain, “Chicken again?” Yes, I should meal plan more, but I don’t, so this is where I’m at. A nightly battle in which the chicken usually wins.

每天工作日的晚上 7 点左右，我都会站在冰箱前，试图决定晚餐吃什么，才不会让丈夫抱怨：“又是鸡肉？”是的，我应该多做些膳食计划，但我没做，所以现状就是这样。这是一场每晚都会上演的战斗，而鸡肉通常是赢家。

Recently, my best weapon in this fight has been AI. Between Alexa Plus in the Echo smart display on my kitchen counter, the Samsung Food app on my fridge, and even an AI-powered cooking robot, AI has become my go-to sous chef — albeit one I need to keep a close eye on. It’s helped me swap ingredients, generate ideas, fix things when I mess up, and yes, even cook better. So I was intrigued when my go-to recipe app, NYT Cooking, introduced an AI assistant.

最近，我在这场战斗中最好的武器是人工智能。从厨房台面上 Echo 智能显示屏里的 Alexa Plus，到冰箱上的三星食品应用，甚至是人工智能烹饪机器人，AI 已经成为我首选的副主厨——尽管我需要密切关注它。它帮助我替换食材、激发灵感、在我搞砸时进行补救，甚至确实让我厨艺见长。因此，当我常用的食谱应用《纽约时报》烹饪频道推出 AI 助手时，我感到非常好奇。

Many, including some NYT Cooking users and recipe purists, are skeptical of this new tool (available to subscribers on iOS only at launch), and I get why. One of the things I like most about NYT Cooking is that it features thousands of recipes from dozens of reputable sources — such as the New York Times archives, restaurants, cookbooks, and food columnists — and real people have tested them. (I’ve tried some purely AI-generated recipes and they were not good).

许多人，包括一些《纽约时报》烹饪频道的用户和食谱纯粹主义者，都对这个新工具（发布时仅限 iOS 订阅用户使用）持怀疑态度，我理解原因。我最喜欢《纽约时报》烹饪频道的一点是，它汇集了来自数十个权威来源的数千份食谱——例如《纽约时报》档案、餐厅、烹饪书和美食专栏作家——并且经过了真人的测试。（我尝试过一些纯 AI 生成的食谱，味道并不好）。

Another great feature of the service, which I’ve been paying for for several years, is the reader comments. Here, I’ve often found solutions to cooking dilemmas, like what to substitute for makrut lime leaves — there’s always one slightly bougie ingredient in an NYT Cooking recipe that I don’t have on hand.

该服务另一个很棒的功能是读者评论，我已经为此付费多年。在这里，我经常能找到烹饪难题的解决方案，比如用什么来代替青柠叶——《纽约时报》的食谱里总有一种我手头没有的、稍微有点“高级”的食材。

However, reading through hundreds of comments to find answers at 7PM on a weeknight isn’t always practical. And this is where I’ve found the NYT Cooking assistant helpful. Among other things, it was trained on user comments, and in my few days testing it, it provided practical substitutions and solid cooking guidance for the new ingredients. It also won’t unquestionably agree to crazy requests (I tried!). As with all AI tools, it’s not perfect — it almost made me put half the required butter in my cake — but as a tool, it is useful and feels like a solid application of AI.

然而，在工作日晚上 7 点阅读数百条评论来寻找答案并不总是切实可行的。这正是《纽约时报》烹饪助手发挥作用的地方。除其他功能外，它还接受了用户评论的训练。在我测试的几天里，它为新食材提供了实用的替代方案和可靠的烹饪指导。它也不会盲目同意疯狂的请求（我试过了！）。和所有 AI 工具一样，它并不完美——它差点让我往蛋糕里只放一半所需的黄油——但作为一个工具，它很有用，感觉是 AI 的一次扎实应用。

The assistant appears in the app in a small text box on the recipe page with a prompt: “Customize this recipe…” From here, you type in a question to adapt it to your needs. This can be as simple as “What can I substitute for this ingredient?” to “Scale this recipe to feed seven people, and make it vegan and gluten-free.”

该助手出现在应用食谱页面的一个小文本框中，并带有提示：“自定义此食谱……”。在这里，你可以输入问题以根据需要进行调整。这可以简单到“我可以用什么代替这种食材？”，也可以是“将此食谱调整为七人份，并使其成为纯素且无麸质的。”

Last Friday night, instead of staring aimlessly into the fridge, I turned to the Chicken folder in my NYT Cooking app Recipe Box and found a One-Pot Chicken and Rice With Ginger I’d been wanting to try. Only I didn’t have chicken thighs or half the ingredients.

上周五晚上，我没有漫无目的地盯着冰箱看，而是打开了《纽约时报》烹饪应用食谱库中的“鸡肉”文件夹，找到了我一直想尝试的“一锅姜味鸡肉饭”。但我没有鸡腿肉，也没有一半的配料。

I typed my dilemma into the assistant box on the recipe page: “I have chicken tenders, fresh ginger, garlic, rice, broth, limes, but no chard or cilantro or scallions. I do have green beans and red onions.” Within seconds, it created a new version of the recipe, with a new title: One-Pot Chicken and Rice With Ginger and Green Beans. It suggested a shorter browning time for the tenders, cooking the red onion alongside the chicken, and steaming the green beans on top of the rice for the last eight minutes instead of the chard.

我在食谱页面的助手框中输入了我的困境：“我有鸡柳、鲜姜、大蒜、米饭、肉汤、青柠，但没有甜菜叶、香菜或葱。我有四季豆和红洋葱。”几秒钟内，它就创建了一个新版本的食谱，并起了一个新标题：姜味四季豆鸡肉饭。它建议缩短鸡柳的煎制时间，将红洋葱与鸡肉一起烹饪，并在最后八分钟将四季豆蒸在米饭上，而不是使用甜菜叶。

I noticed it hadn’t adjusted for the lack of cilantro, saying it was optional. So I asked if I could use Thai basil, which I happen to have, and it agreed, highlighting how it would change the flavor. While I could have figured out a lot of these steps myself, maybe with the help of Google, it was nice to have validation that my modifications wouldn’t make the dish taste terrible and nice not to have to swipe out of the app.

我注意到它没有针对缺少香菜的情况进行调整，称其为可选。于是我问是否可以用我正好有的泰国罗勒，它同意了，并强调了这会如何改变风味。虽然我可能可以自己想出很多这些步骤，也许在谷歌的帮助下也能做到，但能得到确认——即我的修改不会让这道菜变得难吃——感觉很好，而且不必退出应用也很方便。

The resulting dish — which took the same amount of time to cook as the original — was delicious and got my husband’s seal of approval (he just had the leftovers for lunch). It was very different from the recipe Yasmin Fahr wrote, and I can see the purists’ point. But the rewritten recipe was much easier to use and produced a better dish than winging it with my own subs gleaned from the comments section, which I usually do with varying degrees of success.

最终做出的菜肴——烹饪时间与原版相同——非常美味，并得到了我丈夫的认可（他午餐刚吃了剩菜）。它与 Yasmin Fahr 写的食谱大不相同，我能理解纯粹主义者的观点。但与我通常从评论区搜集信息进行即兴替换（成功率参差不齐）相比，重写后的食谱使用起来容易得多，做出的菜肴也更好。
