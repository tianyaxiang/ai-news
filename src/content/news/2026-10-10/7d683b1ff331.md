Philosophy, Taxonomy “Robot” Is A Social Construct: Why Your Dishwasher Is Not A Robot By Mikell · October 8, 2026

哲学、分类学：“机器人”是一个社会建构：为什么你的洗碗机不是机器人 —— Mikell · 2026年10月8日

The most effective way to nerd snipe a roboticist is to present them with a controversial definition of a “robot”. When I served on an industrial safety standards development committee, we once spent a memorable two full days trying to define “robot” sufficiently broadly so as to ensure the standard would apply to all reasonably forseeable uses of industrial robotic arms in industrial automation, but sufficiently narrowly so as to ensure we did not accidentally force newly developed types of industrial robots into a standard that would wrongly constrain them. This was, I say with all honesty, was an immensely enjoyable two days. I love this kind of thing. I will be pedantic all day long.

想要让机器人专家陷入纠结，最有效的方法就是给他们抛出一个关于“机器人”的争议性定义。当我在工业安全标准制定委员会任职时，我们曾有过难忘的两天，试图给“机器人”下一个足够宽泛的定义，以确保标准适用于工业自动化中所有可预见的工业机械臂用途，同时又要足够狭窄，以确保我们不会意外地将新型工业机器人强行纳入一个会错误限制它们的标准中。说实话，那两天过得非常愉快。我热爱这类事情，我可以整天钻牛角尖。

So of course, my esteemed colleague Tina decided to lob a grenade at me by asserting that a dishwasher is a robot. There are two ways I could womansplain to her just how wrong she is. One is to meet her at her level by debating the technical definition. My primary technical issue with her explanation is that I do not agree with how she describes the “manipulation” capabilities of a dishwasher. The spray arms and soap release mechanisms are just that, mechanisms – they do not handle objects, and manipulation is about handling objects. Dishwashers are absolutely automated systems, and increasingly smart ones (though I side-eye the manufacturer that makes my dishwasher calling it “fully autonomous” because it cracks it open on completion to let everything cool). But they’re not robots. They fall short of the technical definition Tina used.

所以，我那位受人尊敬的同事蒂娜（Tina）决定向我投下一枚“手榴弹”，断言洗碗机就是机器人。我有两种方式可以向她解释她错得有多离谱。一种是在她的层面上，通过辩论技术定义来反驳。我对她解释的主要技术异议在于，我不同意她描述洗碗机“操纵”能力的方式。喷淋臂和洗涤剂释放装置仅仅是机械装置——它们并不处理物体，而操纵的本质是处理物体。洗碗机绝对是自动化系统，而且越来越智能（尽管我对那家称我的洗碗机为“全自动”的制造商持怀疑态度，因为它只是在完成后自动打开门让餐具冷却）。但它们不是机器人，它们达不到蒂娜所使用的技术定义。

But the alternative I prefer is to inform Tina that she is wrong because robots are not actually defined by their technology, so arguing that way is meaningless. The word, and how we choose to apply it, is entirely a social construct. “Robot” as a term is an invention of science fiction – and specifically, of Karel Čapek in his 1920 play Rossum’s Universal Robots. Robots continued to be explored through science fiction for decades before the first thing called a robot became a product you could buy. Automated machines not called robots existed well before the word was coined, and continue to exist today. And that’s because “robot” is not actually a descriptor of technical functionality; it is used to describe an automated machine we feel some kind of way about.

但我更倾向于另一种方式，即告诉蒂娜她错了，因为机器人的定义实际上并非取决于技术，所以那样争论毫无意义。这个词以及我们如何使用它，完全是一个社会建构。“机器人”作为一个术语是科幻小说的发明——具体来说，是卡雷尔·恰佩克（Karel Čapek）在他1920年的剧作《罗素的万能机器人》（R.U.R.）中提出的。在第一个被称为机器人的产品可以购买之前的几十年里，机器人一直通过科幻小说进行探索。在“机器人”这个词被创造出来之前，不被称为机器人的自动化机器就已经存在，并且今天依然存在。这是因为“机器人”实际上并不是技术功能的描述符，它被用来描述一种让我们产生某种情感的自动化机器。

It’s specifically because the word and the concept were invented and developed through science fiction that this is the case. Science fiction spends a lot of time using the idea of aliens, robots, and artificial intelligence as a lens through which to view ourselves as humans. It’s why Spock and Data and The Doctor and Seven of Nine are all such compelling characters in Star Trek – they exist as narrative constructs through which to ask the question “what does it mean to be human?”

正是因为这个词和概念是通过科幻小说发明和发展的，所以才会出现这种情况。科幻小说花费大量时间利用外星人、机器人和人工智能的概念作为透镜，来审视我们人类自身。这就是为什么史波克（Spock）、Data、医生（The Doctor）和七之九（Seven of Nine）在《星际迷航》中都是如此引人注目的角色——他们作为叙事结构存在，通过他们来探讨“身为人类意味着什么”这个问题。

So that’s why we choose to apply the term “robot” to some automated machines and not others. We use the term for technologies which feel new, a little scary, and make our squishy biological brains feel a little bit weird about what this technology might mean for us. We might be worried about what this technology having some particular capability means about our jobs, or our relationships with other people, or our areas of expertise or talent. Technology that looks like the human form or appears to imitate aspects of human consciousness might make us question a religious belief we may hold about how life is created.

这就是为什么我们选择将“机器人”一词应用于某些自动化机器，而不是其他机器的原因。我们用这个词来形容那些感觉新颖、有点可怕，并让我们柔软的生物大脑对这项技术可能对我们意味着什么感到有些不安的技术。我们可能会担心这项技术具备某种特定能力对我们的工作、我们与他人的关系，或者我们的专业领域或才能意味着什么。看起来像人形或似乎模仿人类意识某些方面的技术，可能会让我们质疑自己关于生命如何创造的宗教信仰。

Our interactions with something that looks and sounds human might make us think uncomfortable thoughts about how we (individually or as a society) treat other human beings. Or we might realize that public discourse about how great it would be to have something that looks like a human, sounds like a human, and has all the capability of a human, but without any rights or bathroom breaks or pay in exchange for its labor feels pretty gross, actually, if you’ve thought literally at all about how our history of slavery has had an impact on so many aspects of modern America.

我们与看起来和听起来像人类的事物之间的互动，可能会让我们对我们（个人或社会）如何对待其他人产生不舒服的想法。或者我们可能会意识到，关于拥有一个看起来像人、听起来像人、具备人类所有能力，但没有任何权利、无需上厕所、也不用支付劳动报酬的东西是多么棒的公众讨论，实际上让人感到非常恶心——如果你稍微思考过我们的奴隶制历史对现代美国诸多方面的影响的话。

We see this in the way what we call a “robot” has changed and evolved over time, and the way that change is so tightly correlated with the novelty of the technology and the degree to which we feel we understand the potential societal implications of it. Outside of sci fi, “robot” used to just mean robotic arms used in assembly lines. When I attended the RoboBusiness conference circa 2007, I remember the conference information specifically stating it was NOT for industrial robotic arms because they didn’t consider those robots anymore. Meanwhile, Roombas were state of the art and everyone was excited about wheeled mobile robots.

我们从我们所称的“机器人”随时间的变化和演变中看到了这一点，这种变化与技术的创新程度以及我们对它潜在社会影响的理解程度紧密相关。在科幻小说之外，“机器人”曾经只意味着装配线上的机械臂。大约在2007年我参加RoboBusiness会议时，我记得会议资料明确指出它不针对工业机械臂，因为他们不再认为那些是机器人。与此同时，Roomba扫地机器人是当时最先进的技术，每个人都对轮式移动机器人感到兴奋。

A decade later, industrial robotic arms were allowed back in because power- and force-limited cobots and advanced vision systems attached to robot arms opened up novel new applications and capabilities that people had only just started to contemplate being automatable. These days, many folks are happy to laugh off the Roomba and robotic lawn mowers as “basically a toy” and humanoid robots are (unfairly) scaring the crap out of everyone about their job prospects (or safety in the face of someone’s robot army). All of these things meet the ISO definition of robot that Tina used, but as a culture, we’ve moved on to calling something new a “robot” and starting to demote the other stuff because we’re not impressed with (read: scared of) it anymore.

十年后，工业机械臂又被重新接纳，因为功率和力受限的协作机器人以及安装在机械臂上的先进视觉系统，开启了人们才刚刚开始考虑可以自动化的新颖应用和能力。如今，许多人乐于将Roomba和割草机器人嘲笑为“基本上就是个玩具”，而人形机器人则（不公平地）让每个人对自己的就业前景（或面对某人的机器人大军时的安全）感到极度恐惧。所有这些东西都符合蒂娜所使用的ISO机器人定义，但作为一个文化群体，我们已经转向将新事物称为“机器人”，并开始贬低其他东西，因为我们不再对它们感到惊叹（换句话说：不再感到害怕）了。

In short, a machine is a robot when it makes us sit with uncomfortable truths about ourselves. And my dishwasher does not force me to examine my own existential purpose. QED. Not a robot.

简而言之，当一台机器让我们不得不面对关于我们自身的令人不安的真相时，它就是机器人。而我的洗碗机并没有强迫我审视自己存在的意义。证毕。它不是机器人。
